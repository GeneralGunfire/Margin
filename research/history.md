## 1. Subject overview
History develops source analysis (extraction, usefulness, reliability, bias, limitations), essay writing (argument, PEEL paragraphs, line of argument) and understanding of cause/effect, change/continuity, multiple perspectives. Gr 10 (legacy) covers the Mapungubwe/Ancient world, the transatlantic slave trade, colonial expansion after 1750, the South African War and Union, etc. Gr 12 covers the Cold War, independent Africa, civil society protests, the coming of democracy and the 1994 transition.

## 2. Assessment (legacy CAPS, Gr 11)
- SBA 25% (100 marks of year mark) + final exam 75% (300 marks).
- Term 1: Task 1 source-based and/or essay (50 marks, reduced to 10%); Task 2 standardised Test 1 with source-based and essay questions (100 marks, reduced to 20%).
- Term 2: Task 3 research or oral history task (at least 50 marks, reduced to 20% in the CAPS table; the 2023/24 ATP lists it at 50); Task 4 mid-year exam: one 3-hour paper, 150 marks, three questions of 50 marks (ATP: 3 source-based and 3 essay questions offered).
- Term 3: Task 5 source-based or essay (50 marks, 10%); Task 6 standardised Test 2 (100 marks, 20%).
- Term 4: final exam, 2 papers × 3 h × 150 marks. Each paper: learners answer three 50-mark questions: one source-based (Section A), one essay (Section B) and one more from either.
- Cognitive levels Gr 11 (legacy CAPS table): Level 1 30%, Level 2 50%, Level 3 20%.
- Paper 1 content (ATP): Communism in Russia (Lenin or Stalin), Capitalism in USA (Great Depression or New Deal), Ideas of race (Australia or Nazi Germany). Paper 2 content: Nationalisms (two case-study choices from African, Afrikaner, Middle East, Ghana) and Apartheid South Africa.
- Gr 12 final is external (2 papers × 150 marks). Source-based and essay skills carry over directly.

## 4A. Term outline, LEGACY CAPS and 2023/24 ATP (default assumption)
CAPS suggested time: Topics 1, 2, 3, 5: 6 weeks each; Topic 4: 10 weeks; research assignment: 1 week.

### Term 1. Task 1 (source or essay, 50) + Test 1 (100)
- Overview of Gr 11, revision of skills: working with sources, visual and written sources, paragraph writing
- **Topic 1: Communism in Russia 1900-1940.** What is communism; Marx's writings (background); February and October 1917 revolutions (background, source-based); Civil War and war communism; Lenin seizes control; Marxism-Leninism and the NEP; Lenin's death and the power struggle; Stalin's interpretation of Marx (essay focus).
- **Topic 2: Capitalism in the USA 1900-1940.** American dream (background); 1920s boom; Wall Street Crash 1929 (in detail, source-based); election of FDR and the New Deal; analysis and assessment of the New Deal; outbreak of WW2 (background); responses to the crisis of capitalism.
- Research project planned (topic given, key question, assessment criteria).

### Term 2. Task 3 research (50) + mid-year exam (150)
- **Topic 3: Ideas of race.** Unpack the concept of racism (not to justify it). 19th-century race hierarchies; eugenics; modern understanding (Human Genome Project); practices of race and eugenics in USA, Australia, Namibia, South Africa. Case study choice: Australia and indigenous Australians (colonisation; racial suicide/decay debates; white immigration policies; the stolen generation) OR Nazi Germany and the Holocaust (Hitler's consolidation from 1933, Nazi racial ideology, creation of a racial state, targeted groups, choices people made).
- **Topic 4: Nationalisms** (provinces choose ANY TWO case studies; source: ATP):
  - Nationalism concept, origins, Italy and Germany (initiation), "imagined community" theory
  - Case 1: African nationalism in South Africa (effects of Union and 1913 Land Act; professionals and traditional leaders; WW2 influence; Atlantic Charter and AB Xuma's African Claims; types of nationalism; ANC/PAC split)
  - Case 2: Afrikaner nationalism (rise in 1920s-30s; Volk; class and race, education, labour, religion; nationalism in power toward apartheid)
  - Case 3: The Middle East (Arab and Jewish nationalism/Zionism, Balfour Declaration, state of Israel and 1948 War, perspectives, Arab-Israeli conflict, Palestine)
  - Case 4: From Gold Coast to Ghana (early nationalism, WW2 influence, mass movements, Nkrumah, Pan-Africanism, CPP and independence)
- Mid-year exam: Section A source-based on race (Australia OR Nazi Germany) and two nationalism case studies; Section B essays.

### Term 3. Task 5 (50) + Test 2 (100)
- Recovery content (2023/24 ATP): South African War 1899-1902 and Union 1910
- **Topic 5: Apartheid South Africa (1940s-1960s).** Global racism and segregation in the 1920s-30s; segregation after Union; how segregation laid the foundation for apartheid; challenges of Indians, Coloured resistance, Black resistance; National Party victory 1948; what apartheid was and how it differed from segregation; why NP adopted it; creation of the apartheid state (laws against multiracial labour, banning of the CPSA).

### Term 4. Final exam
- Topic 5 continued: overcoming/resistance to apartheid (programme of action, mass mobilisation, alliances, state responses); no formal task on this part.
- Revision and skills; Paper 1 and Paper 2 exams.
