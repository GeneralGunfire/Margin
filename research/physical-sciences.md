## 1. Subject overview
Physical Sciences combines Physics and Chemistry. Grade 11 builds on Grade 10 kinematics, forces, bonding and stoichiometry basics. It adds 2D vectors, Newton's laws with friction and inclines, gravitation, geometrical optics and diffraction, Coulomb's law and electric fields, electromagnetism/Faraday, Ohm's law and power, molecular shape and intermolecular forces, ideal gases, stoichiometry, energy changes, reaction types (acid-base, redox) and the lithosphere. Grade 12 then covers momentum, projectiles, work-energy, Doppler effect, circuits with internal resistance, electrodynamics, photoelectric effect, organic chemistry, rates, equilibrium, acids/bases, electrochemistry, fertilizers. Time: 4 hours/week (40 hrs per term, 160 hrs/year, 75% theory and practical, 25% exams/tests).

## 4. Term outline (CAPS Gr 11; hours as given)

### Term 1. Formal assessment: Newton's second law experiment + control test
**[PHY] Mechanics (~23 h)**
1. Vectors in two dimensions: resultant of perpendicular vectors, resolving into x and y components (Rx = R cos θ, Ry = R sin θ), graphical (tail-to-head) and component method, up to four vectors, closed vector diagrams
2. Forces: weight, normal, friction (static and kinetic, max static friction, on horizontal and inclined planes), applied force, tension
3. Force diagrams and free-body diagrams; resolving weight on an incline
4. Newton's first law (seatbelts), second law (Fnet = ma, applied separately in x and y), third law (action-reaction pairs)
5. Applying Newton's laws: single object on horizontal/inclined plane (frictionless and rough), connected objects (pulleys, strings), apparent weight, equilibrium vs non-equilibrium
6. Newton's law of universal gravitation: F = G m1 m2 / r²; weight as gravitational force; g = GM/R²; mass vs weight; weightlessness; weight on other planets
**[CHEM] Matter & Materials: atomic combinations (~hours not summed)**
7. Chemical bond as net electrostatic attraction; models in science; Lewis diagrams of atoms and molecules; covalent and dative (coordinate) bonds, multiple bonds
8. Molecular shape by VSEPR (linear, trigonal planar, tetrahedral, trigonal bipyramidal, octahedral; effect of lone pairs: e.g. H2O, NH3, SO2)
9. Electronegativity, bond polarity, polar vs non-polar molecules (polar bonds do not always give polar molecules)
10. Bond energy and bond length; relationship to bond strength and number of bonds
11. Intermolecular forces (~10 h): ion-dipole, dipole-dipole, hydrogen bonds, induced-dipole (London/dispersion); intermolecular vs interatomic; effect on density, melting/boiling points, thermal expansion, conductivity; molecular size trend (dispersion forces)
12. The chemistry of water: shape, polarity, hydrogen bonding, high heat capacity and heat of vaporisation, ice density, climate role

### Term 2. Formal assessment: intermolecular forces experiment + mid-year exam
**[PHY] Waves, Sound & Light (~13 h)**
13. Geometrical optics: reflection (revision), refraction, refractive index n = c/v, optical density, frequency constant while speed and wavelength change (~10 h overall for refraction block)
14. Snell's law, critical angle, total internal reflection, optical fibres and endoscopes
15. 2D and 3D wavefronts; Huygens' principle; diffraction through a single slit (qualitative, w = slit width relationship); evidence for wave nature of light
**[CHEM] Matter & Materials: ideal gases (~12 h block with kinetic theory)**
16. Kinetic molecular theory; ideal vs real gas; conditions for real gases approaching ideal; temperature as average kinetic energy; pressure from collisions
17. Gas laws: Boyle, Charles, Gay-Lussac; Kelvin scale; combined and ideal gas law (pV = nRT as per CAPS Gr 11 text; verify in Pass 2)
**[CHEM] Chemical Change: quantitative aspects of chemical change (~12 h)**
18. Molar volume of gases, concentration of solutions (mol/dm³)
19. Stoichiometry: limiting reagents, percent yield, percent composition/purity, empirical and molecular formula
20. Volume relationships in gaseous reactions (airbags, explosives, mining)

### Term 3. Formal assessment: project (physics OR chemistry) + control test
**[PHY] Electricity & Magnetism (~20 h)**
21. Electrostatics: Coulomb's law (1D and 2D, three charges in a right angle), superposition
22. Electric field: definition E = F/q, field lines, field of point charges E = kQ/r², F = qE
23. Electromagnetism: magnetic field of straight wire, loop and solenoid (Right Hand Rule); environmental impact of overhead cables
24. Faraday's law: flux Φ = BA cos θ, induced emf, Lenz's law (direction of induced current)
25. Electric circuits: Ohm's law, ohmic vs non-ohmic conductors, series and parallel (max four resistors)
26. Electrical power and energy: P = VI = I²R = V²/R, E = Pt, kWh and cost
**[CHEM] Chemical Change (~12 h)**
27. Energy and chemical change: bond energy changes, exothermic and endothermic reactions, examples and applications
28. Activation energy and activated complex; energy graphs
29. Types of reaction: acid-base (Arrhenius, Brønsted-Lowry, conjugate pairs, ampholytes, indicators, salts, qualitative titration), precipitation/gas-forming (as in overview text)

### Term 4. Formal assessment: final exam (Paper 1 physics, Paper 2 chemistry, 3 h each)
**[CHEM] Chemical Change**
30. Redox reactions: oxidation numbers, electron transfer, oxidising/reducing agents, balancing with ion-electron method (~5 h)
**[CHEM] Chemical Systems (~8 h)**
31. Lithosphere: mining and mineral processing (gold case study with cyanide process, others), extraction of metals, corrosion of iron, environmental impact of mining, fossil fuels and global warming
Then revision and examinations.
