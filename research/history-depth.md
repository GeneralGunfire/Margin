# History Grade 11 — PASS 2: DEPTH (companion to history.md)

Covers the LEGACY five-topic Gr 11 (default assumption). The DRAFT amended CAPS topics (Atlantic World, slavery and Haiti, southern African kingdoms, Scramble for Africa) are NOT given depth until the school confirms; see history.md. Dates and facts below are standard well-established history; anything I am less sure of is marked UNVERIFIED. No sources or exam questions are copied. Resources: none verified yet (Pass 3). Each topic has an "essay vs source-based" angle as in the 2023/24 ATP.

## Skills spine (all topics; Term 1 start)
- Source skills: extraction (quote/use evidence), define terms, interpret/explain, compare sources, usefulness (purpose, author, date, audience), reliability, bias, limitations. Cognitive split Gr 11: 30% L1, 50% L2, 20% L3.
- Essay skills: read the question, plan, introduction that answers the question, PEEL paragraphs, line of argument, conclusion linked to introduction; chronology.
- Mistakes: narrating without answering; copying the source; judging usefulness without a reason; no balance.
- Lessons: L1 "Reading a source"; L2 "Usefulness and reliability"; L3 "Essay planning"; L4 "PEEL paragraphs".
- Visuals: source-analysis template; essay planning frame; timelines.

## Topic 1: Communism in Russia 1900-1940 (Paper 1) — rebuilt from the Via Afrika guide, pp. 2-42
Source: Via Afrika Gr 11 History Study Guide (see resources-history-via-afrika.md). Key question the guide poses: how was communism applied in Russia under Lenin and Stalin. Dates are the guide's unless flagged. "CHECK" marks a date or claim that disagrees with standard references (see the date-check table in resources-history-via-afrika.md).

### Unit 1 (guide pp. 3-8): Communism, Marx, 1905
- **Key ideas:** socialism vs communism (communal ownership, no classes or state in principle); Marx (born 1818): Communist Manifesto 1848 (with Engels), Das Kapital 1867; bourgeoisie vs proletariat, class struggle, expected workers' revolution.
- **Russia before 1905:** autocratic Tsar, multinational empire, mostly peasants; serfs freed 1861 (Alexander II); rapid industrialisation after 1860; Alexander II killed 1881 and reforms stopped under Alexander III; Marxist ideas spread in the 1890s; the party split in 1903 into Bolsheviks (Lenin, small disciplined elite party) and Mensheviks. CHECK: the guide says the Social-Democratic Party formed in "1861"; the standard date is 1898.
- **1905:** defeat by Japan (war from Feb 1904), then Bloody Sunday (massacre of a peaceful petition march to the Winter Palace, led by Father Gapon), strikes and mutinies, October Manifesto (civil rights, a Duma), December Moscow uprising crushed, Fundamental Laws leave the Tsar a veto. CHECK: the guide says the revolution broke out "February 1905"; Bloody Sunday was 22 January 1905 (9 January old style).
- **Link 1905 to 1917:** soviets (workers' councils) tried out; Trotsky chaired the St Petersburg Soviet; Marxists learned the power of mass strikes; theory revised to allow a socialist revolution in backward Russia (Trotsky's "permanent revolution").
- **Skills practised:** timeline, cause and effect, interpreting a Marx extract, defining terms (Duma, zemstvo, soviet, Bolshevik).

### Unit 2 (pp. 9-21): February and October 1917
- **Calendar note (useful and correct):** Russia used the Julian calendar, 13 days behind, until February 1918, hence "February/March" and "October/November".
- **Causes (political, economic, social):** Tsar refused real reform after 1905 (Stolypin PM 1906-11, repressive, assassinated Sept 1911); Tsar took army command in 1915 and Tsarina Alexandra with Rasputin ran the government; WWI defeats, food and fuel shortages, inflation, strikes, overcrowded housing in Petrograd.
- **February revolution:** unplanned; International Women's Day protests grew into a mass strike; troops joined the crowds; Duma formed a Provisional Government; Tsar abdicated (guide: 14 March; CHECK, standard 15 March new style / 2 March old style). The guide's "by 28 October 1917 over 80 000 troops mutinied" sits in the February narrative and is almost certainly a slip; treat as CHECK.
- **Provisional Government failure:** Petrograd Soviet had equal power (dual power); Order Number 1 undermined army discipline; no land reform, war continued, elections postponed. The guide names Kerensky as leader from the start; standard accounts have Prince Lvov first, Kerensky from July (the guide's own question 1.10 lists Lvov).
- **Lenin's return (April 1917) and April Theses:** "Peace, Land, Bread", all power to the soviets; July Days (Bolsheviks blamed, Lenin hid, Trotsky jailed); Kornilov affair (August) boosted the Bolsheviks; Bolshevik majorities in Petrograd and Moscow soviets by September.
- **October revolution:** planned; Trotsky's Military-Revolutionary Committee; key buildings taken 24-25 Oct (old style), Winter Palace taken, cruiser Aurora; decrees on land and peace; Constituent Assembly met once in January 1918 and was dissolved; Brest-Litovsk (March 1918) took Russia out of WWI; Cheka set up.
- **People:** Nicholas II, Alexandra, Rasputin, Stolypin, Kerensky, Lvov, Kornilov, Lenin, Trotsky, Stalin (Commissar of Nationalities), Gapon. CHECK: the guide says "the Tsar had Rasputin killed in 1916"; he was killed by nobles (Dec 1916).
- **Skills practised:** causes and consequences, sequencing, comparing the two revolutions (spontaneous vs planned), reading the 1917 proclamation and the Rodzianko telegram as sources.
- **Essay angles:** why did the Provisional Government fail; why could the Bolsheviks take power; to what extent was October a coup.

### Unit 3 (pp. 22-28): Civil war, War Communism, NEP, Lenin's Marxism, Lenin's death
- **Civil war 1918-21:** Reds (Trotsky) vs Whites with Western backing; Cheka terror; Tsar's family killed 1918; Red victory helped by White disunity.
- **War Communism:** nationalised industry, grain requisitioning, labour conscription, banned private trade; result: economic collapse, 1921 famine (guide: five million died), Kronstadt mutiny (March 1921) crushed.
- **NEP (1921):** a compromise: grain tax replaced requisitioning; small private trade and factories allowed (Nepmen, kulaks); state kept banks and heavy industry; output recovered (1926 grain near 1913 level) but unemployment and poor transport remained; some Bolsheviks saw it as a betrayal of Marxism.
- **Lenin's interpretation of Marxism:** revolution possible in a peasant country; elite vanguard party; dictatorship of the proletariat; one-party state; USSR name (guide: 1923; CHECK, formed December 1922). CHECK: the guide's summary says the revolutionaries overthrew the Tsar and Provisional Government "in 1921"; it was 1917.
- **Women:** February protests; Provisional Government gave the vote; Bolsheviks gave legal equality, divorce on request, Zhenotdel; later rolled back under Stalin.
- **Lenin's death (1924):** Trotsky (internationalism, permanent revolution) vs Stalin (Socialism in One Country); the troika (Stalin, Kamenev, Zinoviev) isolated Trotsky; Stalin then removed the others; Trotsky exiled and later murdered (1940; the guide gives no date).
- **Key terms:** vanguard, dictatorship of the proletariat, War Communism, NEP, kulak, Nepmen, Cheka, Zhenotdel, troika.
- **Skills practised:** compare War Communism, NEP and the Five-Year Plans in a table (guide Q6); evidence on Kronstadt.

### Unit 4 (pp. 29-36): Russia under Stalin
- **Stalin's interpretation:** Socialism in One Country, economic modernisation before world revolution, strong bureaucracy.
- **Collectivisation from 1929:** kolkhoz (collective) vs sovkhoz (state farm); kulaks resisted (burned crops, killed livestock), arrested or sent to gulags; famine 1930 and 1932-33 (guide: 6 million; estimates in the literature vary, so present as an estimate); by 1941 almost all land collectivised.
- **Five-Year Plans:** Gosplan; First 1928-33 (heavy industry), Second 1933-37, Third 1938-41 (interrupted by war); industrial superpower but poor consumer goods, housing shortage, forced labour.
- **Terror:** Kirov murdered 1934; show trials 1934-38 (Kamenev and Zinoviev executed 1936); NKVD head Yezhov from 1936, later replaced; Red Army purge from 1937; the guide says 20 million sent to gulags with half dying (guide's estimate; flag as uncertain); censorship, cult of personality, apparatchiks.
- **Women under Stalin:** more education and work (28% of industrial workers in 1927, 40% in 1937) but abortion banned, divorce harder.
- **WWII:** Molotov-Ribbentrop pact 1939; Operation Barbarossa 22 June 1941. CHECK: the p. 39 summary says Stalin "broke pact with Nazi Germany in 1942"; Germany broke it in 1941 (the guide contradicts itself).
- **Skills practised:** analyse a propaganda poster (guide Q7), compare policies in a table, evidence for and against "success" of the plans.
- **Essay angles:** how far were the Five-Year Plans a success; was Stalinism a continuation of Leninism; why did Stalin win the succession struggle.

### Common mistakes (Topic 1)
Mixing Marx, Lenin and Stalin; using the 13-day calendar difference carelessly; treating February and October as one event; saying NEP ended communism instead of being a temporary compromise; quoting casualty figures as exact.

### Lesson breakdown (Topic 1)
L1 Marx, communism and the 1905 revolution (pp. 3-8); L2 February 1917 and the Provisional Government (pp. 9-18); L3 Lenin, October 1917 and the Bolsheviks in power (pp. 16-21); L4 Civil war, War Communism, NEP and Lenin's death (pp. 22-28); L5 Stalin: collectivisation, plans and terror (pp. 29-36); summary and questions on pp. 37-42.

### Guide features useful for Margin

## Topic 2: Capitalism in the USA 1900-1940 (Paper 1) — rebuilt from the Via Afrika guide, pp. 43-76
Key question in the guide: how did the Great Depression bring about a crisis of capitalism. "CHECK" = disagrees with standard references.

### Unit 1 (pp. 44-54): Nature of capitalism, the American Dream, the 1920s boom
- **Background:** USA overtook Britain and Germany in industrial output early 1900s; WWI made it a supplier and lender to the Allies; entered the war in 1917 after German unrestricted submarine warfare; Wilson (1913-21) helped design the League of Nations but the Senate did not ratify Versailles, so the USA stayed out and turned isolationist.
- **1920s politics:** Harding (1920, "return to normalcy"), Coolidge from 1923/24 ("the business of America is business"); laissez-faire, tax cuts favouring the rich, high tariffs, anti-union action; Immigration Act 1924 (quotas, cut southern/eastern Europe).
- **American Dream:** rags-to-riches, rugged individualism; reality of class, race and ethnic inequality.
- **Boom drivers:** mass production (Model T "Tin Lizzie"), advertising and hire purchase/credit, rising share prices, buying shares on the margin (about 10% down).
- **Weak spots under the boom:** tariffs provoked foreign retaliation; wages lagged behind profits; monopolies (e.g. US Steel); agricultural overproduction and falling farm prices (about 6 million pushed off farms per the guide); top 5% took about a third of income (guide's figure, source not given).
- **Society in the "Roaring Twenties":** jazz, radio, flappers, women's vote but little power, rising divorce; African American Great Migration, Harlem, Jim Crow in the South, Ku Klux Klan (about 5 million members mid-1920s per guide); Prohibition (1920-33), bootlegging, speakeasies, Al Capone. CHECK: the guide says "1931: end of Depression, states realised liquor tax would bring revenue"; the Depression did not end in 1931 (likely meant the Depression's depth was clear).
- **Skills practised:** cause and effect (boom causes vs hidden weaknesses), cartoons/photographs of the 1920s, comparing who benefited (guide Q1 mind map).

### Unit 2 (pp. 55-58): Wall Street Crash 1929
- **Events:** 24 October 1929 (panic selling, 13 million shares), Black Tuesday 29 October.
- **Causes:** speculation and margin buying; weak banks lending to speculators; overproduction and falling spending power; uneven wealth; tariffs and collapsing trade; laissez-faire (no regulation).
- **Impact:** output about 30% down, prices 25% down, national income halved by 1933 (guide); bank failures (650 in 1929, 4 000 in 1933 per guide); unemployment, evictions, Hoovervilles, breadlines, food riots, Bonus Marchers; no social security; Hoover's late and limited measures.
- **Skills practised:** interpreting graphs and photographs, explaining "causes vs trigger".

### Unit 3 (pp. 59-66): The New Deal
- **Why FDR won (1932):** anger at Hoover; promised a "New Deal for the forgotten man"; Brains Trust; first 100 days (15 major laws); fireside chats from 1933.
- **Three Rs:** relief, recovery, reform.
- **First New Deal (1933-mid 1935):** Emergency Banking Relief Act (bank holiday, deposit guarantee), FERA, alphabet agencies, NIRA with PWA and NRA ("Blue Eagle"), NRA ruled unconstitutional 1935.
- **Second New Deal (1935-40):** WPA (over 3 million jobs by 1938), Social Security Act 1935, Wagner Act 1935 (collective bargaining), "soak the rich" tax.
- **Opposition:** wealthy and business (taxes, unions, "socialism"), conservative Supreme Court (NRA 1935, AAA 1936), Huey Long, Father Coughlin, communists, Southern Democrats (racism; 1936 walkout).
- **Weaken or strengthen capitalism?** Strengthened: restored confidence, regulated abuses, saved capitalism from revolution; weakened (according to critics): bigger state, big labour and big government at the table; not a cure: 1937-38 recession after spending cuts; excluded many African Americans and women.
- **Essay angles:** "To what extent did the New Deal strengthen capitalism?"; "Was the New Deal a success?"; compare Hoover and FDR.

### Unit 4 (pp. 67-72): WWII and the world
- **Recovery:** 1937 spending cuts caused a new slump (guide: 5 million lost jobs, CHECK: figures in standard sources are lower, quote with care); Neutrality Acts 1935-37; rearmament from 1938/39, drafting 1940, Pearl Harbor December 1941 ended the slump.
- **Global impact:** USSR (cut off from the world economy, claimed immunity), Latin America (export collapse), Germany (loans recalled, bank collapse, Nazis and communists gained), Japan (military control, deficit spending, recovery), South Africa (off the gold standard in 1932, gold boom, Afrikaner poor-white problem, Carnegie Commission on Poor Whites, link to Afrikaner nationalism).
- **Conclusion:** the boom-bust cycle; the lesson that some state regulation was needed.
- **Skills practised:** link to Topic 1 (USSR avoiding the Depression) and Topic 4 (SA effects), evaluating sources on the global crisis.

### Common mistakes (Topic 2)
Treating the Crash as the sole cause of the Depression; mixing Hoover and FDR policies; saying the New Deal ended the Depression; leaving out the 1937-38 recession; omitting African Americans and women from the "who benefited" question.

### Lesson breakdown (Topic 2)
L1 American capitalism and the 1920s boom (pp. 44-50); L2 Society in the 1920s: women, race, Prohibition (pp. 51-54); L3 The Crash and the Depression (pp. 55-58); L4 FDR and the New Deal (pp. 59-66); L5 WWII and global impact, essay practice (pp. 67-72).

### Guide features useful for Margin
Summary box per unit; questions: mind maps of causes and consequences, define terms in paragraph form (start/end date, background, outcome, meaning), complete a New Deal agency diagram (relief, recovery, reform), answers from p. 195.

## Topic 3: Ideas of race (Paper 1)
- Covers: nineteenth-century hierarchies of race; eugenics; modern understanding (Human Genome Project: no biological basis for race categories); practices in USA, Australia, Namibia, South Africa; case study choice: Australia (colonisation, racial suicide/decay debates, white immigration policies, child migrants, the stolen generation) OR Nazi Germany (consolidation from 1933, Nazi racial ideology, racial state, targeted groups, choices people made).
- Key concepts: race, racism, eugenics, social Darwinism, genocide, segregation, assimilation.
- Examined: source-based on Australia OR Nazi Germany; essay "creation of a racial state" etc.
- Mistakes: treating race as biological; excusing racist thinking as "of its time" without analysis; mixing up Nuremberg laws and later events; chronological errors.
- Lessons: L1 "Concept of race and racism"; L2 "Eugenics and race science"; L3 "Case study part 1"; L4 "Case study part 2"; L5 "Practices in SA and Namibia".
- Sensitive content: handle Holocaust and genocide material with care (flag for the Margin content team).
- Visuals: propaganda images/posters; map of Australia or Europe.

## Topic 4: Nationalisms (Paper 2; provinces/schools choose ANY TWO case studies)
- Core: what nationalism is; origins; Italy and Germany as initiation; "imagined community" theory.
- Case 1 African nationalism in South Africa: effects of Union (1910) and the Land Act (1913); professionals and traditional leaders; WWII influence, the Atlantic Charter and A. B. Xuma's African Claims; ANC Youth League and the split into ANC and PAC (Africanism).
- Case 2 Afrikaner nationalism: rise in the 1920s and 1930s; Volk; class, race, education, labour, religion; Broederbond; nationalism in power toward apartheid.
- Case 3 The Middle East: Arab nationalism and Zionism; Balfour Declaration; state of Israel and the 1948 War; Palestinian and Israeli perspectives; later conflicts (Six-Day War 1967, Yom Kippur War 1973, occupation of the West Bank, intifada). Note multi-perspective, politically sensitive.
- Case 4 Gold Coast to Ghana: early nationalism, WWII influence, mass movements, Nkrumah and the CPP, Pan-Africanism, independence (1957).
- Examined: Paper 2 questions on two chosen cases (source-based and essay).
- Mistakes: confusing African and Afrikaner nationalism; presenting one perspective in the Middle East; mixing dates of Israel's creation and the wars.
- Lessons per case: L1 "Context and origins"; L2 "Key organisations/leaders"; L3 "Turning points"; L4 "Essay practice"; plus L1 "Defining nationalism" for the core.
- Visuals: maps (Middle East borders, Ghana), timeline, party/organisation family tree.

## Topic 5: Apartheid South Africa, 1940s-1960s (Paper 2)
- Covers: segregation in the 1920s-30s after Union; how segregation laid the foundation; Indian, Coloured and African resistance (1946 passive resistance; Congress Alliance, Defiance Campaign 1952, Freedom Charter 1955, Treason Trial, Women's march 1956, Sharpeville 1960, banning of ANC/PAC, rise of armed struggle: dates UNVERIFIED against the textbook); National Party 1948 victory; how apartheid differed from segregation; legislation creating the apartheid state (Population Registration, Group Areas, Mixed Marriages, Immorality, Separate Amenities, Bantu Education, pass laws; labour laws; Suppression of Communism Act and banning of the CPSA); overcoming/resistance to apartheid and state responses (Term 4, no formal task).
- Key concepts: segregation, apartheid, Africanism, Congress Alliance, mass mobilisation, Satyagraha, universal suffrage, state of emergency.
- Examined: source-based (segregation and the NP victory; legalising apartheid) and essay (creation of apartheid state; resistance), Test 2/3 on apartheid.
- Mistakes: dating the laws wrongly; treating all resistance as one organisation; ignoring women's and Indian community roles.
- Lessons: L1 "Segregation 1910-1948"; L2 "1948 and NP policy"; L3 "Apartheid laws"; L4 "Resistance in the 1950s"; L5 "Sharpeville and state response".
- Visuals: timeline of laws and campaigns; photographs/political posters; map of Group Areas/homelands (map image).
