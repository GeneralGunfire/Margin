## How the guide marks source-based questions and essays
(Own-words summary of the guide's exam section, pp. 214-243.)
- **Format:** 3 hours, 150 marks; three questions of 50 marks; at least one source-based and one essay; two questions on the same theme allowed.
- **Source-based questions:** numbered parts with mark notation like "2 x 2 (4)" meaning two valid points at two marks each; the memo lists "any two/any three" so extra valid points are credited. Skills tested: extract evidence, explain terms in context, make deductions, compare sources, judge usefulness and reliability (reasons such as propaganda, purpose, author, exaggeration), explain why one source contradicts another, then a short paragraph answer.
- **Paragraph answers (8 marks):** a 3-level holistic rubric: weak (0-3: evidence not from the source, illogical argument, informal style); competent (4-6: structured argument, competent use of evidence, sound interpretation); strong (7-8: logical and cohesive argument, expert use of evidence, thorough understanding). A 7-mark question uses a similar 3-level rubric (not achieved 1-2, achieved 3-5, achieved with excellence 6-7).
- **Essays (50 marks):** each memo gives a synopsis (what the candidate must show), then "introduction with a clear line of argument; main points; conclusion that closes the argument with no new information". The guide does not show a marking matrix and does not mention PEEL.
- **What earns marks per the guide:** a line of argument in the introduction, relevant facts for each main point, use of evidence from the source, a conclusion tied to the question.

| Aspect | Via Afrika guide | EC Nov 2024 memo |

| Stance | Not stated | "To what extent" questions need a stance for any marks |
