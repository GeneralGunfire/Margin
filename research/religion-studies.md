## 1. Subject overview
Religion Studies is an academic, non-confessional study of religion as a universal human phenomenon (not religious instruction). CAPS has four topics: (1) Variety of religions, (2) Common features of religion as a generic and unique phenomenon, (3) Topical issues in society, (4) Research into and across religions. Gr 10 introduces clusters of religions, their beginnings, statistics and definitions of religion; Gr 11 goes to developments, interdependence with social factors, inter-religious dialogue, symbols, theories, myth, rituals, religion and the state/politics/environment, co-responsibility and leisure; Gr 12 adds conceptual distinctions, internal differentiation within religions, unique features, central teachings and normative sources, and a set of social problems announced in annual exam guidelines.
Religions named in CAPS Gr 11 content: African religion, Christianity, Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism, Judaism, Baha'i Faith, Sikhism, African Initiated Churches. How many are studied in depth is a school/textbook decision: UNCONFIRMED.
Time: 4 hours/week (160 hours). Gr 11: Topic 1 8 weeks (32 h), Topic 2 11 weeks (44 h), Topic 3 8 weeks (32 h), Topic 4 6 weeks (24 h); contact time 33 weeks (132 h) + exams 7 weeks (28 h). (CAPS table.)

## 2. Assessment (CAPS Gr 11)
- Seven internal tasks; in Gr 10 and 11 six tasks make 25% of the year mark and the end-of-year exam is 75%.
- Term 1: Task 1 open-book extended writing (100 marks) + Task 2 test (100). Term 2: Task 3 source-based task (100) + Task 4 mid-year exam (300). Term 3: Task 5 project (100) + Task 6 test (100). Term 4: Task 7 end-of-year exam (300).
- Gr 11 exams (mid-year and end-of-year): two 2-hour papers of 150 marks. Paper 1: one compulsory short-question section (50) plus two of three or four optional 50-mark questions, source-based (texts, maps, graphs, comparative questions). Paper 2: three extended-writing questions of 50 marks each (choose three of four or five): take and argue a position from a religious perspective, with critique; a source may be a stimulus. Gr 11 exams are "similar to the external examination".
- Test: 1 h 30, 100 marks: 30 source-based, 30 short questions, 40 extended writing.
- Cognitive levels: recall 30%, comprehension 40%, analysis/application/evaluation/synthesis 30%.
- Project forms: research assignment, oral assignment, enrichment assignment, investigation; given before end of Term 2, submitted in Term 3; essay with sub-headings.
- Gr 12: seven tasks (25%) + external exam (75%, two papers 150 + 150 as per CAPS table); Gr 12 social problems set in annual exam guidelines.

## 4. Term outline (CAPS Gr 11)

### Term 1. Formal: open-book extended writing + test
1. **Topic 1 Variety of religions. Weeks 1-2: Main developments of religions** (developments in South Africa and worldwide; major influences on development).
2. **Weeks 3-4: Mutual interdependence of religion and social factors:** hunter-gatherer societies; early food producers; early state societies; societies of more advanced technology; modern technological society; postmodern society.
3. **Weeks 5-6: Influence and adaptation between religions:** very strong mutual influence (Sikhism; African Initiated Churches); missionary religions (Christianity, Islam, Baha'i, Buddhism) vs non-missionary religions (African religion, Judaism, Hinduism); distinguishing concepts: mission, evangelism, proselytisation, revitalisation, ecumenism, syncretism, religious colonialism/imperialism.
4. **Weeks 7-8: Approaches aimed at inter-religious dialogue:** attitudes of religions to one another; how religions interpret one another.
5. **Topic 2 Common features. Weeks 9-10: Symbols:** what a symbol is; importance; roles in representing and presenting; central symbols of various religions (origin, significance, use); change of meaning over time.

### Term 2. Formal: source-based task + mid-year exam (weeks 8-10 exams)
6. **Weeks 1-2: Theories about religion:** what a theory is; theory in a religious context; functionalist and conflict theories; morality and ethics in religion.
7. **Weeks 3-4: Nature and role of narrative and myth in religion:** narrative; myth and its kinds; roles of myth; analysis of narratives and myths from religions.
8. **Topic 4 Research into and across religions. Weeks 5-7: Interviews on gender issues:** structured interviews considering gender, religion, culture and economic background; identify interviewees, write questions, conduct, report objectively, discuss in class.

### Term 3. Formal: project + test
9. **Topic 2 Weeks 1-2: Types of rituals and their role:** concept; origin and significance; common characteristics; kinds; roles; link to religions; ritual as representation of beliefs/principles.
10. **Weeks 3-4: Faith, worship, prayer, meditation, mysticism and spirituality; artistic expressions of religion** (meaning and occurrence in various religions; religion in specific works of art and their interpretation).
11. **Topic 3 Topical issues in society. Weeks 5-6: Religion and the state:** no differentiation, theocracy, state religion, secularism, co-operative model; how beliefs shape state policy.
12. **Weeks 7-8: Religion and politics:** views about politics; influence on political life; influence of politics on religion; colonialism, imperialism, liberation, transformation.
13. **Weeks 9-10: Religions and the natural environment:** influence both ways; perspectives on the greenhouse effect and alternative energy; religious views, ethical principles, practical involvement, environmental justice.

### Term 4. Formal: end-of-year exam (weeks 7-10 exams)
14. **Topic 3 Weeks 1-3: Co-responsibility and co-operation of religions:** shared responsibility for quality of life; religious resources; examples of co-operation; impact on society.
15. **Topic 4 Weeks 4-6: Religion and leisure from an ethical point of view:** work vs leisure; recreation in individual and community life and in religions; advertisements and sponsorship related to leisure (compile and analyse); ethics of the leisure industry (evaluative report).
