## 1. Subject overview
LO develops the learner's personal, social, intellectual, emotional and physical growth. Six topics: (1) Development of the self in society, (2) Social and environmental responsibility, (3) Democracy and human rights, (4) Careers and career choices, (5) Study skills, (6) Physical Education. Gr 11 moves from Gr 10's goal setting and basics to relationships, financial assistance for study, democratic participation, environmental health, lifestyle risk, job-application skills and gender power relations. Gr 12 completes careers/study/exam preparation and adds citizenship issues.
Time: 2 hours per week; 66 contact hours in Gr 10 and 11 (80 hours scheduled across 40 weeks including exams). A fixed Physical Education period per week must be on the timetable.
Gr 11 topic hours (CAPS table): Development of the self 11; Social and environmental responsibility 3; Democracy and human rights 7; Careers and career choices 8; Study skills 4; Physical Education 33; total 66. (The table was read from a column-shifted layout, so the exact number-to-topic mapping is UNVERIFIED; the term plan below shows PE as 33 hours total and the others consistent with these.)

## 2. Assessment (CAPS Gr 11)
- Five internal tasks a year. Term 1: Task 1 written task (80 marks) + PET (20). Term 2: Task 2 mid-year exam (80) + PET (20). Term 3: Task 3 project (80) + PET (20). Term 4: Task 4 end-of-year exam (80) + PET (20). Each term is recorded out of 100 (80 + 20) and reported as 100; each term is 25% of the year mark (no external exam in Gr 11).
- Exams: at least 90 minutes each, twice a year. Mid-year: Terms 1-2 content. End-of-year: Terms 1-4. 80 marks in 3 sections: Section A 20 (compulsory, short, what/why/list/describe/explain, multiple choice, true/false with justification); Section B 30 (compulsory, short open-ended, scenario, source and case-study questions); Section C 30 (three 15-mark or four 10-mark questions, answer two or three; application, problem solving, decision making, advice, extended writing). Includes Physical Education content.
- PET (Physical Education Task): assessed every term out of 20: frequency of participation (10 marks) + movement performance (10 marks). Marks recorded each term.
- Project: Term 3 (80 marks), bound or stapled. Written task: Term 1.
- Gr 12: written task, mid-year, project, PET (40 in Term 2) set internally; final exam (80 marks) set by the province/DBE.
- No PAT in LO (PAT belongs to Tourism etc.).

## 4. Term outline (CAPS Gr 11)

### Term 1 (formal: written task + PET)
**Weeks 1-3**
1. **Development of the self in society (3 h):** plan and achieve life goals: types of goals (short, medium, long term), steps in goal-setting, problem-solving, perseverance; prioritising life goals (family, marriage, parenting, career, relationships); links between personal values, choices and goal-setting. Relationships and their influence on well-being: types, changing nature; relationships that contribute or harm; rights and responsibilities; social and cultural views; media's impact on values and beliefs about relationships.
2. **Physical Education (3 h):** programmes to improve current personal fitness and health; safety in fitness activities.
**Weeks 4-6**
3. **Careers and career choices (3 h):** requirements for admission to additional and higher education: NSC requirements for certificate, diploma and degree studies; evaluating options; Admission Point Scores and programme admission requirements; options for financial assistance (bursaries, study loans, scholarships, learnerships, SETAs); obligations in financial arrangements.
4. **PE (3 h):** fitness programmes and movement performance.
**Weeks 7-10**
5. **Democracy and human rights (4 h):** principles, processes and procedures for democratic participation (public participation and petitions, governance, law-making, rule of law, transparency, representation, accountability); democratic structures (national, provincial, local government, community structures, traditional authorities, parties, interest groups, business); local civil society structures; how structures function and change (constitutions, elections, mandates, lobbying, advocacy, running meetings); civil society participation in governance and law-making. Role of sport in nation building: participant and spectator behaviour, triggers, positive-behaviour programmes, impact on teams, community and nation.
6. **PE (4 h).**

### Term 2 (formal: mid-year exam + PET)
**Weeks 1-4**
7. **Study skills (4 h):** apply own study skills, styles, strategies; how learning takes place and its effectiveness; study styles vs strategies; examination writing skills and the assessment process; time management and annual study plan; personal development goals for study, health and fitness.
8. **PE (4 h):** self-designed and structured community/playground/indigenous games; own umpiring, administrative, organisational and leadership skills; safety.
**Weeks 5-7**
9. **Social and environmental responsibility (3 h):** environmental issues that cause ill-health (harmful substances in food production, inhumane farming); impact of degradation (soil erosion, pollution, radiation, floods, fires, wind damage, loss of open space, lack of infrastructure); depletion of resources (fishing stocks, firewood, land); personal responses (attitudes, safety, first aid, coping with disasters); climate change causes, impact on development, mitigation and adaptation; community service on a contemporary environmental issue showing which sectors suffer more.
10. **PE (3 h).**
**Weeks 8-10:** examinations.

### Term 3 (formal: project + PET)
**Weeks 1-5**
11. **Development of the self in society (5 h):** healthy and balanced lifestyle (physical, psychological, social, emotional, spiritual); negative factors: accidents, risky behaviour (personal safety, road use, substance abuse, sexual behaviour, pregnancy risk, teenage suicide, hygiene and diet, STIs, HIV and AIDS, peer pressure), socio-economic environment; positive factors (role models, parents and peers, values, belief system, religion, media, culture, economics); impact of unsafe practices; individual responsibility and seeking support; role of nutrition in health and physical activity.
12. **PE (5 h):** leadership roles in recreational and relaxation group activities; safety.
**Weeks 6-10**
13. **Careers and career choices (5 h):** competencies, abilities and ethics for securing a job: studying advertisements, application letters and forms; CV (experience, testimonials, job shadowing, informal jobs); managing meetings and projects, office administration; interview skills; ethics (transparency, accountability). Personal expectations: expectancy vs reality, chances of success and satisfaction, suitability audit. Self-knowledge vs world-of-work demands (skilled, semi-skilled, unskilled, physical labour); studies needed for careers.
14. **PE (5 h).**

### Term 4 (formal: end-of-year exam + PET)
**Weeks 1-3**
15. **Development of the self in society (3 h):** gender roles and effects on health and well-being; unequal power relations (physical abuse, incest, cultural practices such as different mourning periods, domestic and sexual violence/rape, sexual harassment at work); negative effects on health; addressing power inequality.
16. **PE (3 h):** modified traditional and/or non-traditional sports; umpiring, administration, leadership; safety.
**Weeks 4-6**
17. **Democracy and human rights (3 h):** contributions of South Africa's diverse religions and belief systems to a harmonious society and own belief system; clarify own values; analyse moral and spiritual dilemmas (right to life, euthanasia, cultural practices, economic and environmental issues); respect differing opinions.
18. **PE (3 h).**
**Weeks 7-10:** examinations.
