## PHYSICS

### 1. Vectors in two dimensions [PHY][CAPS+ATP]
- Covers: adding vectors by tail-to-head and component methods; resolving a vector into x and y components; resultant of up to four forces; closed vector diagram (equilibrium).
- Key: Rx = R cos θ, Ry = R sin θ; R = √(Rx² + Ry²); direction tan θ = Ry/Rx (state the reference axis and quadrant); closed diagram ⇒ resultant zero.
- Prereqs: Gr 10 vectors/scalars, Pythagoras, trig ratios (Pure Maths 6).
- Examined: component method with a sketch; direction as an angle from a stated axis; mostly integrated into Newton's laws questions (3 to 6 marks stand-alone).
- Mistakes: wrong sign for components; using θ from the wrong axis; adding magnitudes; not giving direction.
- Ideas: force-board experiment; two ropes pulling a boat; practice with 3 and 4 vectors.
- Lessons: L1 "Resultant of perpendicular vectors"; L2 "Resolving into components"; L3 "Component method for several vectors"; L4 "Closed vector diagrams".
- Visuals: draggable vectors with live components (interactive canvas).

### 2. Forces, friction and free-body diagrams [PHY][CAPS+ATP]
- Covers: weight, normal, friction (static, kinetic), applied, tension; force vs free-body diagrams; resolving weight on an incline.
- Key: w = mg; f_s,max = μ_s N; f_k = μ_k N; on an incline, w∥ = mg sin θ, w⊥ = mg cos θ (N = mg cos θ if no other vertical force); static friction adjusts up to its maximum.
- Prereqs: 1; Gr 10 forces.
- Examined: draw labelled free-body diagrams (marks for every force and labels); friction calculations; 3 to 8 marks.
- Mistakes: normal force = weight always; friction in the wrong direction; mixing static and kinetic; extra forces (e.g. "force of motion").
- Ideas: block on a rough incline; push at an angle.
- Lessons: L1 "Types of forces"; L2 "Friction"; L3 "Free-body diagrams"; L4 "Inclined planes".
- Visuals: incline simulator with sliders for angle and μ (interactive).

### 3. Newton's laws and applications [PHY][CAPS+ATP]
- Covers: Newton 1 (inertia, seat belts), Newton 2 (Fnet = ma, applied along x and y separately), Newton 3 (action-reaction pairs), equilibrium vs non-equilibrium, connected objects (pulleys, strings), lifts/apparent weight.
- Key: statements must be recalled exactly as worded in CAPS: net force and direction; Fnet = ma; action and reaction act on different objects, equal magnitude, opposite direction, same type of force.
- Prereqs: 1, 2.
- Examined: 8 to 15 marks, often the largest Paper 1 question; "state Newton's second law", "calculate the tension/acceleration"; derive from a free-body diagram first.
- Mistakes: taking the wrong direction as positive; same acceleration for connected bodies not applied; weight and normal as an action-reaction pair; tension equal on both sides when pulley friction is stated.
- Ideas: two blocks over a pulley; truck pulling a trailer; lift accelerating up/down.
- Lessons: L1 "First law and inertia"; L2 "Second law in 1D"; L3 "Third law pairs"; L4 "Connected objects"; L5 "Inclines and lifts".
- Visuals: animation of two-body systems (graph/physics library); Atwood machine.

### 4. Newton's law of universal gravitation [PHY][CAPS+ATP]
- Key: F = G m1 m2 / r² (G = 6,67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²·kg⁻²); g = G M / R²; w = mg; weight vs mass; weightlessness.
- Examined: calculate force between masses, change in force when r or m changes (ratio reasoning), g on another planet; 4 to 8 marks.
- Mistakes: r measured from the surface instead of centres; squaring the wrong quantity; kg vs N.
- Lessons: L1 "Inverse-square law"; L2 "g and weight".
- Visuals: force vs distance curve (graph library).

### 5. Geometrical optics [PHY][CAPS only; absent from 2023/24 ATP]
- Covers: refraction, refractive index n = c/v, optical density, Snell's law (n1 sin θ1 = n2 sin θ2), critical angle (sin θc = n2/n1), total internal reflection, optical fibres.
- Key: frequency stays constant; speed and wavelength change; light bends toward the normal in a denser medium.
- Examined: Snell's law calculation (3 to 5 marks); critical angle; ray diagrams; practical (find critical angle).
- Mistakes: angles measured from the surface not the normal; wrong n ratio; claiming frequency changes.
- Lessons: L1 "Refraction and n"; L2 "Snell's law"; L3 "Critical angle and TIR".
- Visuals: ray-box simulation with draggable angle (interactive).

### 6. Wavefronts and diffraction [PHY][CAPS only; absent from 2023/24 ATP]
- Covers: wavefront, Huygens' principle, diffraction at a slit/edge, single-slit pattern; evidence of wave nature.
- Key: more diffraction when slit width is comparable to wavelength.
- Examined: short conceptual questions 2 to 4 marks; diagram of wave pattern.
- Lessons: L1 "Wavefronts and Huygens"; L2 "Diffraction through a slit".
- Visuals: ripple-tank animation (interactive).

### 7. Electrostatics: Coulomb's law and electric field [PHY][CAPS+ATP]
- Key: F = k Q1 Q2 / r² (k = 9,0 × 10⁹ N·m²·C⁻²); E = F/q = kQ/r²; F = qE; field lines point away from positive charges; superposition with signs from direction.
- Prereqs: Gr 10 charge, vectors (1), Newton 3.
- Examined: 1D and 2D with three charges (right-angled arrangement); force on the charge at the right angle; field at a point; 6 to 12 marks.
- Mistakes: putting charge signs into the formula then losing direction; r not in metres; using wrong r in 2D; forgetting Newton 3 between charges.
- Lessons: L1 "Coulomb's law (1D)"; L2 "Coulomb's law (2D)"; L3 "Electric field and field lines"; L4 "Field of several charges".
- Visuals: 2D field-line plotter with movable charges (interactive).

### 8. Electromagnetism and Faraday's law [PHY][CAPS+ATP]
- Covers: magnetic field of a straight wire, loop and solenoid (right-hand rule); Faraday's law ε = −N ΔΦ/Δt, Φ = BA cos θ; Lenz's law direction.
- Key: induced emf proportional to rate of change of flux; induced current opposes the change.
- Examined: qualitative (direction, what happens when a magnet is pushed in) and short calculations; 3 to 6 marks. Whether the ATP restricts to qualitative Faraday: UNVERIFIED.
- Mistakes: confusing flux with field; ignoring the sign (Lenz); right-hand rule vs left hand.
- Lessons: L1 "Fields around current-carrying conductors"; L2 "Flux and Faraday's law"; L3 "Lenz's law".
- Visuals: 3D vectors for right-hand rule (3D helpful); magnet-in-coil animation.

### 9. Electric circuits: Ohm's law, power and energy [PHY][CAPS+ATP]
- Key: R = V/I; series R = R1 + R2 + …; parallel 1/R = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + …; P = VI = I²R = V²/R; E = Pt; 1 kWh = 3,6 MJ; ohmic vs non-ohmic conductors; cost = kWh × tariff.
- Prereqs: Gr 10 circuits.
- Examined: series-parallel networks (max four resistors), ammeter/voltmeter readings, power, cost of electricity; 8 to 12 marks.
- Mistakes: adding parallel resistances; using wrong V across a resistor; units (W vs kWh); ignoring voltmeter placement.
- Lessons: L1 "Ohm's law"; L2 "Series and parallel"; L3 "Power and energy"; L4 "Cost of electricity".
- Visuals: circuit builder (interactive, e.g. circuit simulator).

## CHEMISTRY

### 10. Atomic combinations: Lewis, bonding, VSEPR, polarity [CHEM][CAPS+ATP]
- Covers: bond as net electrostatic attraction; Lewis diagrams; covalent/dative bonds; molecular shape (linear, trigonal planar, tetrahedral, trigonal bipyramidal, octahedral and lone-pair derived shapes, e.g. bent, trigonal pyramidal); electronegativity and bond polarity; polar vs non-polar molecules; bond energy and length.
- Key: shape from number of structural electron pairs; polarity needs polar bonds and asymmetric shape; bond energy higher for shorter, multiple bonds.
- Examined: draw Lewis diagram, predict shape and polarity, explain, 4 to 10 marks.
- Mistakes: ignoring lone pairs; calling every polar bond a polar molecule; mixing intra- and intermolecular.
- Lessons: L1 "Lewis diagrams"; L2 "VSEPR shapes"; L3 "Electronegativity and polarity"; L4 "Bond energy and length".
- Visuals: 3D molecule viewer (3D needed).

### 11. Intermolecular forces [CHEM][CAPS+ATP]
- Covers: London (dispersion), dipole-dipole, hydrogen bonds, ion-dipole; effect on boiling point, melting point, solubility, viscosity, vapour pressure, surface tension.
- Key: stronger IMFs ⇒ higher boiling point; London forces increase with molar mass/surface area; H-bonding needs H bonded to N, O or F.
- Examined: rank substances by boiling point and explain, 4 to 8 marks; prescribed experiment (Term 2).
- Mistakes: boiling breaks covalent bonds; comparing across different types without reasoning; "bond" vs "force".
- Lessons: L1 "Types of IMF"; L2 "Properties and IMF"; L3 "Explaining boiling points".
- Visuals: particle diagrams of liquids; molecule animation.

### 12. The chemistry of water [CHEM][CAPS only]
- Covers: polarity and hydrogen bonding; high heat capacity and heat of vaporisation; ice less dense than water; climate role.
- Lessons: L1 "Hydrogen bonding in water"; L2 "Unusual properties".

### 13. Ideal gases [CHEM][CAPS+ATP]
- Key: Boyle p1V1 = p2V2; Charles V1/T1 = V2/T2; Gay-Lussac p1/T1 = p2/T2; combined p1V1/T1 = p2V2/T2; pV = nRT (R = 8,31 J·K⁻¹·mol⁻¹); T in kelvin (K = °C + 273); kinetic theory: pressure from collisions, temperature = average kinetic energy.
- Examined: gas-law calculations, graphs (V vs T gives a line through −273 °C), explain in terms of kinetic theory; 5 to 8 marks. Whether pV = nRT is in Gr 11 or Gr 12 CAPS: UNVERIFIED for Gr 11 exam.
- Mistakes: Celsius not converted; units of p and V inconsistent; "volume of molecules" arguments.
- Lessons: L1 "Kinetic theory"; L2 "Boyle's law"; L3 "Charles and Gay-Lussac"; L4 "Combined and ideal gas law".
- Visuals: p-V-T simulation (interactive graph); particle animation.

### 14. Quantitative aspects of chemical change [CHEM][CAPS+ATP]
- Key: n = m/M; n = V/V_m (22,4 dm³·mol⁻¹ at STP); c = n/V (mol·dm⁻³); limiting reagent; percent yield = actual/theoretical × 100; percent purity/composition; empirical and molecular formula; gas volume ratios.
- Prereqs: Gr 10 mole concept, balanced equations.
- Examined: 10 to 15 marks, multi-step stoichiometry; limiting reagent, percent yield; empirical formula from masses.
- Mistakes: unbalanced equations; mole ratio ignored; using mass instead of moles; M units; volume in cm³ vs dm³.
- Lessons: L1 "Mole, molar mass, molar volume"; L2 "Concentration"; L3 "Stoichiometry"; L4 "Limiting reagent and yield"; L5 "Empirical and molecular formulae".
- Visuals: mole-roadmap diagram; no 3D.

### 15. Energy and chemical change [CHEM][CAPS+ATP]
- Key: exothermic (ΔH < 0, products lower) vs endothermic (ΔH > 0); activation energy; activated complex; energy-profile graphs; bond breaking absorbs energy, bond forming releases energy.
- Examined: draw/interpret energy graph, classify reaction, 3 to 6 marks.
- Mistakes: sign of ΔH; showing ΔH from the wrong levels; thinking exothermic needs no activation energy.
- Lessons: L1 "Exo/endothermic"; L2 "Energy graphs and activation energy".
- Visuals: interactive energy-profile graph.

### 16. Types of reaction: acid-base [CHEM][CAPS+ATP]
- Key: Arrhenius and Brønsted-Lowry definitions; conjugate pairs; ampholytes; neutralisation (acid + base → salt + water; acid + carbonate → salt + water + CO2); indicators; qualitative titration.
- Examined: write balanced equations, identify conjugate pairs; 4 to 8 marks.
- Mistakes: unbalanced neutralisation; conjugate base from the wrong species; confusing strong/concentrated.
- Lessons: L1 "Acid and base definitions"; L2 "Conjugate pairs and ampholytes"; L3 "Neutralisation and salts".

### 17. Types of reaction: redox [CHEM][CAPS+ATP]
- Key: oxidation = loss of electrons (oxidation number increases); reduction = gain; reducing/oxidising agents; balance using half-reactions (ATP names the Table of standard reduction potentials).
- Examined: assign oxidation numbers, identify agents, balance half-reactions, 5 to 8 marks.
- Mistakes: reversing which species is oxidised; not balancing charge; mixing up agent vs process.
- Lessons: L1 "Oxidation numbers"; L2 "Oxidation and reduction"; L3 "Balancing redox equations".
- Visuals: electron-transfer animation.

### 18. The lithosphere and mining [CHEM][CAPS only]
- Covers: mining and mineral processing (gold case study), metal extraction, corrosion of iron, environmental impact, fossil fuels and global warming.
- Examined: short discussion/essay-type parts; 3 to 6 marks.
- Lessons: L1 "Metals from ores"; L2 "Mining and its impact".
