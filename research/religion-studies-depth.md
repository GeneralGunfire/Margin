# Religion Studies Grade 11 — PASS 2: DEPTH (companion to religion-studies.md)

Numbering follows religion-studies.md Section 4. Religion Studies is academic and non-confessional: describe and analyse religions respectfully and evenly. The school's textbook and chosen religions are UNCONFIRMED, so examples below are generic and drawn from the religions named in CAPS (African religion, Christianity, Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism, Judaism, Baha'i Faith, Sikhism, African Initiated Churches). Specific doctrinal claims are kept to widely agreed basics; anything detailed is marked UNVERIFIED. Exam format: Paper 1 (short questions 50 + two source-based 50s), Paper 2 (extended writing, three 50-mark essays arguing a position and critiquing it). No exam questions copied. Resources: none verified yet. Lessons L1 to L5.

## Answer skills (all topics)
- Source-based: read the source, extract, compare religions, link to concepts; answers range from one word to paragraphs.
- Extended writing: state a position from a religious perspective, argue it with examples, critique it; use correct terms; be balanced and respectful.
- Mistakes: confessional language ("the true religion"); generalising "all Muslims/Christians"; confusing a religion's teachings with a society's practices.

## 1. Main developments of religions — Term 1
- Covers: major developments in South Africa and worldwide and the influences behind them (migration, colonisation, trade, reform movements).
- Prereqs: Gr 10 clusters and beginnings of religions.
- Examined: short explanations and source comparisons, 6 to 12 marks.
- Lessons: L1 "Major developments globally"; L2 "Developments in South Africa".
- Visuals: timeline; map of religion spread (map image).

## 2. Religion and social factors — Term 1
- Covers: relationship between religion and kinds of society: hunter-gatherer, early food producers, early states, more advanced technology, modern technological, postmodern.
- Key: religion both shapes and is shaped by social conditions.
- Mistakes: treating religion as unchanging; assuming "primitive vs advanced".
- Lessons: L1 "Religion in early societies"; L2 "Religion in modern/postmodern societies".
- Visuals: society-type vs religion chart.

## 3. Influence and adaptation between religions — Term 1
- Covers: mutual influence (Sikhism; African Initiated Churches); missionary (Christianity, Islam, Baha'i, Buddhism) vs non-missionary (African religion, Judaism, Hinduism); concepts: mission, evangelism, proselytisation, revitalisation, ecumenism, syncretism, religious colonialism/imperialism.
- Key: define each concept precisely and give an example.
- Mistakes: syncretism vs ecumenism confusion; calling every conversion proselytisation.
- Lessons: L1 "Influence and adaptation"; L2 "Missionary and non-missionary religions"; L3 "Key concepts".

## 4. Inter-religious dialogue — Term 1
- Covers: attitudes of religions to one another; how they interpret one another; approaches to dialogue.
- Lessons: L1 "Attitudes and interpretations"; L2 "Dialogue in practice".

## 5. Symbols — Term 1
- Covers: what a symbol is; importance; roles (representing/presenting); central symbols of religions (origin, significance, use); how meaning changes over time.
- Mistakes: describing the symbol without its meaning; treating symbols as only decoration.
- Lessons: L1 "What is a symbol"; L2 "Symbols across religions".
- Visuals: symbol table (image licensing UNVERIFIED).

## 6. Theories about religion — Term 2
- Covers: what a theory is; theory in a religious context; functionalist (religion serves social functions: cohesion, meaning) and conflict (religion linked to power, class, inequality) theories; morality and ethics in religion.
- Key: apply each theory to an example.
- Mistakes: reading the theories as claims about whether a religion is true; mixing functionalist and conflict.
- Lessons: L1 "What is a theory"; L2 "Functionalist theory"; L3 "Conflict theory"; L4 "Morality and ethics".

## 7. Narrative and myth — Term 2
- Covers: narrative; kinds of myth; roles of myth (explain origins, justify practices, teach values); analysing narratives and myths.
- Key: "myth" in Religion Studies does not mean "false".
- Mistakes: calling a sacred story "just a myth" in the everyday sense.
- Lessons: L1 "Narrative and myth"; L2 "Roles of myth"; L3 "Analysing examples".

## 8. Interviews on gender issues (research) — Term 2
- Covers: structured interviews considering gender, religion, culture and economic background; identify participants; write questions; conduct; report objectively; class discussion.
- Skills: leading vs neutral questions; consent and confidentiality; summarising without bias.
- Lessons: L1 "Designing interview questions"; L2 "Conducting and reporting".

## 9. Rituals — Term 3
- Covers: concept and origin; significance in relation to historical events; common characteristics; kinds (e.g. rites of passage, worship, festivals); roles; link to religions; ritual as representation of beliefs.
- Lessons: L1 "What is a ritual"; L2 "Kinds and roles"; L3 "Rituals across religions".

## 10. Faith, worship, prayer, meditation, mysticism, spirituality, art — Term 3
- Covers: meaning of each concept and how it occurs in various religions; religion reflected in works of art and their interpretation.
- Lessons: L1 "Faith and worship"; L2 "Prayer, meditation and mysticism"; L3 "Religious art".

## 11. Religion and the state — Term 3
- Covers: relationship models: no differentiation, theocracy, state religion, secularism, co-operative model; examples in history; how religious beliefs influence state policy.
- Mistakes: equating secularism with atheism or hostility to religion; mislabelling co-operative models.
- Lessons: L1 "Five models"; L2 "Examples and policy influence".
- Visuals: model comparison table.

## 12. Religion and politics — Term 3
- Covers: religions' views on politics; religious influence on political life and politics' influence on religion; colonialism, imperialism, liberation, transformation.
- Mistakes: one-sided view; ignoring both directions of influence.
- Cross-link: History (apartheid, nationalisms).
- Lessons: L1 "Religion and politics"; L2 "Colonialism, liberation and transformation".

## 13. Religions and the natural environment — Term 3
- Covers: influence of environment on religion (sacred places, seasons) and religion on environment (stewardship, practices); perspectives on the greenhouse effect and alternative energy; religious views, ethical principles, practical involvement, environmental justice.
- Cross-link: Life Orientation, Tourism, Physical Sciences.
- Lessons: L1 "Environment and religion"; L2 "Religious responses to environmental issues".

## 14. Co-responsibility and co-operation of religions — Term 4
- Covers: why religions share responsibility for quality of life; religious resources; examples of co-operation; impact on society.
- Lessons: L1 "Shared responsibility"; L2 "Examples of co-operation".

## 15. Religion and leisure (ethics) — Term 4
- Covers: work and leisure; forms of recreation in individual and community life and across religions; analysing adverts and sponsorship related to leisure; ethics of the leisure industry (evaluative report).
- Lessons: L1 "Work and leisure"; L2 "Leisure in religions"; L3 "Ethics of leisure advertising".

## Projects and tasks
- Term 1 open-book extended writing; Term 2 source-based task; Term 3 project (research, oral, enrichment or investigation forms); tests of 100 marks (30 source-based, 30 short, 40 extended writing). Skills: key-question formulation, evidence, argument, bibliography.
