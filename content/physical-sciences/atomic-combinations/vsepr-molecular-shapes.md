> **What you'll learn:** how Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion (**VSEPR**) theory predicts the 3D shape of any molecule straight from its Lewis diagram, why lone pairs squeeze bond angles smaller, and all seven core CAPS molecular shapes you must know.
>
> **Prerequisites:** Lewis diagrams (counting bonding domains and lone pairs on the central atom).
>
> **Why this matters:** shape is NOT just memorising names — molecular shape determines whether bond dipoles cancel or add up (polarity), which determines intermolecular forces and boiling points!

## The idea in plain language

Imagine tying balloons together at their knots:
- Two balloons push straight apart to opposite sides (**$180^\circ$, linear**).
- Three balloons spread evenly into a flat triangle (**$120^\circ$, trigonal planar**).
- Four balloons do NOT sit flat in a $90^\circ$ cross — in 3D space they spread into a pyramid with equal **$109{,}5^\circ$ angles (tetrahedral)**!

Electron pairs in the outer shell of a central atom behave just like those balloons: because electron clouds are all negatively charged, **they repel one another as far apart as 3D space allows**. That simple principle is **VSEPR (Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion) theory**.

### Two crucial VSEPR rules that unlock every shape

1. **Double and triple bonds count as ONE single electron region ("domain")** when deciding geometry! In $\text{CO}_2$ (`O=C=O`), carbon has **two** electron regions (left double bond and right double bond) $\implies$ **linear ($180^\circ$)**!
2. **Lone pairs take up MORE space than bonding pairs!** Why? A bonding pair is stretched tightly between *two* positive nuclei, whereas a lone pair is held by only *one* nucleus and puffs out wider. So lone pairs push nearby bonding pairs slightly closer together:
   - $\text{CH}_4$ (4 bonding, 0 lone): **$109{,}5^\circ$** (**tetrahedral**)
   - $\text{NH}_3$ (3 bonding, 1 lone): **$\approx 107^\circ$** (**trigonal pyramidal**)
   - $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (2 bonding, 2 lone): **$\approx 104{,}5^\circ$** (**bent / angular**)

Notice that all three ($\text{CH}_4$, $\text{NH}_3$, $\text{H}_2\text{O}$) have **4 electron pairs** around the central atom! Why don't we call $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ tetrahedral? Because **molecular shape describes ONLY the positions of the ATOMS** (lone pairs stay invisible in the name, even though they shove the atoms into a bent shape!).

## The complete Grade 11 VSEPR shape table

First, always determine the number of **electron domains around the central atom** (bonding directions + lone pairs):

| Electron domains | Bonding atoms + Lone pairs | Molecular shape name | Ideal bond angle | Key CAPS examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **2** | 2 bonded, **0 lone** | **Linear** | $180^\circ$ | $\text{CO}_2$, $\text{BeCl}_2$, $\text{HCN}$, $\text{H}_2$, $\text{HCl}$ |
| **3** | 3 bonded, **0 lone** | **Trigonal planar** | $120^\circ$ | $\text{BF}_3$, $\text{BCl}_3$, $\text{SO}_3$ |
| **4** | 4 bonded, **0 lone** | **Tetrahedral** | $109{,}5^\circ$ | $\text{CH}_4$, $\text{CCl}_4$, $\text{NH}_4^+$ |
| **4** | 3 bonded, **1 lone** | **Trigonal pyramidal** | $\approx 107^\circ$ | $\text{NH}_3$, $\text{PCl}_3$, $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$ |
| **4** | 2 bonded, **2 lone** | **Bent / Angular** | $\approx 104{,}5^\circ$ | $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{H}_2\text{S}$, $\text{OF}_2$ |
| **5** | 5 bonded, **0 lone** | **Trigonal bipyramidal** | $90^\circ$ & $120^\circ$ | $\text{PCl}_5$ |
| **6** | 6 bonded, **0 lone** | **Octahedral** | $90^\circ$ | $\text{SF}_6$ |

> **Important special case ($\text{SO}_2$):** Sulfur dioxide has 1 double bond, 1 single/dative bond, and **1 lone pair** on sulfur (3 domains total, 2 bonded + 1 lone) $\implies$ **bent / angular** ($\approx 119^\circ$).

## Worked examples

### Example 1 (step-by-step prediction): $\text{PCl}_3$ vs $\text{BF}_3$

Predict the molecular shape of (a) phosphorus trichloride ($\text{PCl}_3$) and (b) boron trifluoride ($\text{BF}_3$), and explain why they are NOT the same shape even though both have formula $\text{XY}_3$.

```steps
title: Comparing XY₃ shapes via lone pairs
step: **For $\text{BF}_3$:** Boron (Group 13) has **3** valence electrons and forms 3 single bonds to 3 F atoms $\implies$ **3 bonding pairs, 0 lone pairs**. Three equal domains spread flat $\implies$ **trigonal planar ($120^\circ$)**.
step: **For $\text{PCl}_3$:** Phosphorus (Group 15) has **5** valence electrons. Three go into P–Cl single bonds, leaving **2 electrons (1 lone pair)** on P $\implies$ **3 bonding pairs + 1 lone pair = 4 domains**.
step: Those 4 domains arrange tetrahedrally, and the lone pair pushes the three P–Cl bonds downward $\implies$ **trigonal pyramidal ($\approx 107^\circ$)**!
```

### Example 2 (exam explanation): why $\text{NH}_4^+$ is tetrahedral while $\text{NH}_3$ is trigonal pyramidal

- In $\text{NH}_3$, nitrogen has **3 bonding pairs and 1 lone pair**. The lone pair repels the bonding pairs downward, giving a **trigonal pyramidal** shape ($\approx 107^\circ$).
- When $\text{NH}_3$ bonds dative-covalently with $\text{H}^+$ to form $\text{NH}_4^+$, that lone pair becomes a fourth bonding pair! Now all **4 electron pairs are identical bonding pairs with 0 lone pairs** $\implies$ the shape opens up to symmetrical **tetrahedral ($109{,}5^\circ$)**!

```tryit
title: Quick shape predictor
prompt: Predict the molecular shape of (a) $\text{CCl}_4$, (b) $\text{H}_2\text{S}$, and (c) $\text{SF}_6$.
hint: Count valence electrons on the central atom (C = 4, S = 6) and check how many lone pairs remain after bonding!
answer: - (a) **$\text{CCl}_4$:** C has 4 bonds, 0 lone pairs $\implies$ **tetrahedral**.
- (b) **$\text{H}_2\text{S}$:** S (Group 16, same as O!) has 2 bonds, 2 lone pairs $\implies$ **bent / angular**.
- (c) **$\text{SF}_6$:** S shares 6 pairs with 6 F atoms, 0 lone pairs $\implies$ **octahedral**.
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Guessing molecular shape just from the formula (thinking every $\text{AB}_2$ is linear and every $\text{AB}_3$ is trigonal planar!). **Always check for lone pairs on the central atom first.**
> - Calling $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ or $\text{NH}_3$ "tetrahedral". Four electron domains give a tetrahedral *electron* arrangement, but the **molecular shape** (atom positions only) is **bent/angular** ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$) and **trigonal pyramidal** ($\text{NH}_3$).
> - Counting a double bond as two separate VSEPR directions. In $\text{CO}_2$, carbon has only **2** VSEPR directions, not 4!

## Practice

```questions
Q: State the VSEPR molecular shape of each of the following: (a) $\text{BeCl}_2$, (b) $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$, (c) $\text{PCl}_5$, (d) $\text{CO}_2$.
A: (a) $\text{BeCl}_2$: 2 bonding pairs, 0 lone pairs $\implies$ **linear** ($180^\circ$).
(b) $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$: 3 bonding pairs, 1 lone pair on O $\implies$ **trigonal pyramidal** ($\approx 107^\circ$).
(c) $\text{PCl}_5$: 5 bonding pairs, 0 lone pairs $\implies$ **trigonal bipyramidal**.
(d) $\text{CO}_2$: 2 double-bond domains, 0 lone pairs on C $\implies$ **linear** ($180^\circ$).
---
Q: Explain in terms of VSEPR theory why the bond angle in $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ ($104{,}5^\circ$) is smaller than in $\text{CH}_4$ ($109{,}5^\circ$), even though both central atoms are surrounded by four electron pairs.
A: $\text{CH}_4$ has four equal bonding pairs and zero lone pairs (symmetrical $109{,}5^\circ$). $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ has **two lone pairs** and two bonding pairs. Lone-pair–lone-pair and lone-pair–bonding-pair repulsions are stronger than bonding-pair–bonding-pair repulsions, pushing the two $\text{O}-\text{H}$ bonds closer together to $104{,}5^\circ$.
---
Q: Why do $\text{PCl}_5$ and $\text{SF}_6$ break the octet rule?
A: Phosphorus and sulfur are in **Period 3**, so their valence shell ($n = 3$) has available $3\text{d}$ orbitals allowing more than 8 electrons (**expanded octet**). Period 2 elements like N and O can never exceed 8!
```
