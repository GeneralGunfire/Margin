> **What you'll learn:** how to assign an **oxidation number** to any atom inside an element, compound, or polyatomic ion, how to tell immediately whether a reaction is **redox** (and who is oxidised vs reduced), and how to name the **oxidising agent** vs **reducing agent** without getting them backwards.
>
> **Prerequisites:** ions, valencies, and simple algebra.
>
> **Why this matters:** Term 4 Redox chemistry leads directly into Grade 12 Galvanic and Electrolytic Cells! Mastering oxidation numbers now makes every redox question effortless.

## The idea in plain language

In an acid–base reaction, a **proton ($\text{H}^+$)** moves from one species to another.
In a **redox reaction** (reduction–oxidation), **electrons ($e^-$)** move from one atom to another!

Two simple memory tricks guarantee you never mix up oxidation and reduction:
- **OIL RIG:** **O**xidation **I**s **L**oss of electrons; **R**eduction **I**s **G**ain of electrons.
- **Oxidation number check (even faster!):**
  - If an atom's oxidation number **INCREASES** (goes UP, e.g. $0 \to +2$, or $-1 \to 0$) $\implies$ **OXIDATION** (lost negative electrons!).
  - If an atom's oxidation number **DECREASES / IS REDUCED** (goes DOWN, e.g. $+7 \to +2$, or $0 \to -2$) $\implies$ **REDUCTION** (gained negative electrons!).

### Definitions to memorise

```definition
title: Oxidation and Reduction (in terms of electron transfer / oxidation numbers)
**Oxidation** is the **loss of electrons** (an **increase** in oxidation number).
**Reduction** is the **gain of electrons** (a **decrease** in oxidation number).
```

```definition
title: Reducing agent vs Oxidising agent
A **reducing agent** is a substance that is oxidised (it donates / loses electrons and causes another substance to be reduced).
An **oxidising agent** is a substance that is reduced (it accepts / gains electrons and causes another substance to be oxidised).
```

## Rules for assigning oxidation numbers (in strict priority order!)

Always assign the known rules from the top of this list first, then solve for the remaining unknown atom by algebra!

1. **Free uncombined elements:** oxidation number is **$0$** ($\text{Fe} = 0$, $\text{O}_2 = 0$, $\text{Cl}_2 = 0$, $\text{S}_8 = 0$).
2. **Simple one-atom (monatomic) ion:** equals its **charge** ($\text{Na}^+ = +1$, $\text{Mg}^{2+} = +2$, $\text{Cl}^- = -1$).
3. **Fluorine ($\text{F}$)** in compounds: always **$-1$**.
4. **Group 1 metals** in compounds: always **$+1$**. **Group 2 metals** in compounds: always **$+2$**.
5. **Hydrogen ($\text{H}$)** in compounds: **$+1$** (except **$-1$** in metal hydrides like $\text{NaH}$).
6. **Oxygen ($\text{O}$)** in compounds: **$-2$** (except **$-1$** in peroxides like $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$, or $+2$ in $\text{OF}_2$).
7. **Sum rule:**
   - For a **neutral molecule or formula unit**, the sum of all oxidation numbers $= \mathbf{0}$.
   - For a **polyatomic ion**, the sum of all oxidation numbers $= \mathbf{\text{the charge on the ion}}$.

> **Important format rule:** Always write the sign **BEFORE** the number for oxidation numbers (e.g. **$+6$**, **$-2$**), whereas ion charges are written number-then-sign ($2-$, $3+$).

## Worked examples

### Example 1 (calculating tricky oxidation numbers): dichromate and permanganate

Determine the oxidation number of:
(a) **$\text{Cr}$** in the dichromate ion, $\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-}$
(b) **$\text{Mn}$** in potassium permanganate, $\text{KMnO}_4$
(c) **$\text{S}$** in sulfuric acid, $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$

```worked
title: Example 1 (Oxidation number algebra)
Given: (a) $\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-}$, (b) $\text{KMnO}_4$, (c) $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$
Unknown: Oxidation numbers of $\text{Cr}$, $\text{Mn}$, and $\text{S}$
Equation: Sum of oxidation numbers = total charge on species
Substitution: (a) $2(\text{Cr}) + 7(-2) = -2 \implies 2(\text{Cr}) - 14 = -2 \implies 2(\text{Cr}) = +12 \implies \text{Cr} = +6$
Substitution: (b) $(+1) + \text{Mn} + 4(-2) = 0 \implies \text{Mn} - 7 = 0 \implies \text{Mn} = +7$
Substitution: (c) $2(+1) + \text{S} + 4(-2) = 0 \implies \text{S} - 6 = 0 \implies \text{S} = +6$
Answer: $\text{Cr} = +6$; $\text{Mn} = +7$; $\text{S} = +6$.
```

### Example 2 (analysing a full redox equation — spotting the agents!)

Consider the reaction:
$$\text{Cu}(s) + 4\,\text{HNO}_3(aq) \longrightarrow \text{Cu(NO}_3)_2(aq) + 2\,\text{NO}_2(g) + 2\,\text{H}_2\text{O}(\ell)$$
Assign oxidation numbers to identify: (a) the species oxidised, (b) the species reduced, (c) the **oxidising agent**, and (d) the **reducing agent**.

```steps
title: Tracking oxidation numbers across a full equation
step: Write oxidation numbers above each atom:
- $\text{Cu}(s) = \mathbf{0} \longrightarrow \text{Cu}^{2+}\text{ in Cu(NO}_3)_2 = \mathbf{+2}$ (**increased!**)
- $\text{H} = +1 \to +1$ (unchanged)
- $\text{O} = -2 \to -2$ (unchanged)
- $\text{N}$ in $\text{HNO}_3$: $(+1) + \text{N} + 3(-2) = 0 \implies \mathbf{+5}$.
- $\text{N}$ in $\text{NO}_3^-$ stays $+5$, **but** $\text{N}$ in $\text{NO}_2(g)$ is $\text{N} + 2(-2) = 0 \implies \mathbf{+4}$ (**decreased!**)
step: Match definitions:
- **$\text{Cu}$** goes from $0 \to +2$ (loses electrons) $\implies$ **$\text{Cu}$ is oxidised**, so **$\text{Cu}$ is the REDUCING AGENT**.
- **$\text{N}$ in $\text{HNO}_3$** goes from $+5 \to +4$ (gains electrons) $\implies$ **$\text{HNO}_3$ (or $\text{NO}_3^-$) is reduced**, so **$\text{HNO}_3$ is the OXIDISING AGENT**.
```

Notice the crucial rule: **Agents are ALWAYS reactants on the LEFT-hand side** — never pick a product on the right as the oxidising or reducing agent!

```tryit
title: Spot whether a reaction is REDOX or NOT redox!
prompt: Check oxidation numbers for both equations below. Which one is a **redox** reaction and which one is **not**?
(1) $\text{CaCO}_3(s) \longrightarrow \text{CaO}(s) + \text{CO}_2(g)$
(2) $\text{Cl}_2(g) + 2\,\text{Br}^-(aq) \longrightarrow 2\,\text{Cl}^-(aq) + \text{Br}_2(\ell)$
hint: A reaction is redox ONLY if at least two oxidation numbers CHANGE from left to right!
answer: - **(1) NOT redox:** $\text{Ca}$ stays $+2$, $\text{C}$ stays $+4$ (both in $\text{CO}_3^{2-}$ and $\text{CO}_2$), and $\text{O}$ stays $-2$. Zero electrons transferred!
- **(2) REDOX:** $\text{Cl}$ changes $0 \to -1$ (reduced; $\text{Cl}_2$ is the oxidising agent) and $\text{Br}$ changes $-1 \to 0$ (oxidised; $\text{Br}^-$ is the reducing agent).
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Swapping the agents: **the substance that is OXIDISED is the REDUCING agent** (because by giving away its electrons, it reduces the other chemical!).
> - Writing the *product* ($\text{Cu}^{2+}$ or $\text{NO}_2$) as the reducing/oxidising agent instead of the **reactant** ($\text{Cu}$ or $\text{HNO}_3$).
> - Thinking *every* reaction is redox! Neutralisation ($\text{HCl} + \text{NaOH}$) and precipitation ($\text{Ag}^+ + \text{Cl}^- \to \text{AgCl}$) involve **zero change** in oxidation numbers and are NOT redox!
> - Forgetting to divide by the subscript in $\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-}$ or $\text{S}_2\text{O}_3^{2-}$: oxidation number is always **per single atom**!

## Practice

```questions
Q: Find the oxidation number of the underlined/central atom in each species: (a) $\text{SO}_3^{2-}$, (b) $\text{NH}_4^+$, (c) $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$, (d) $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$.
A: (a) $\text{S} + 3(-2) = -2 \implies \text{S} = \mathbf{+4}$.
(b) $\text{N} + 4(+1) = +1 \implies \text{N} = \mathbf{-3}$.
(c) $2\text{Fe} + 3(-2) = 0 \implies \text{Fe} = \mathbf{+3}$.
(d) Peroxide rule! $\text{H} = +1 \implies 2(+1) + 2\text{O} = 0 \implies \text{O} = \mathbf{-1}$.
---
Q: In the thermite reaction $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3(s) + 2\,\text{Al}(s) \longrightarrow 2\,\text{Fe}(\ell) + \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3(s)$, identify (a) the substance oxidised, (b) the substance reduced, (c) the oxidising agent, and (d) the reducing agent.
A: $\text{Fe}$: $+3 \to 0$ (reduced); $\text{Al}$: $0 \to +3$ (oxidised).
(a) Substance oxidised: **$\text{Al}$**; (b) Substance reduced: **$\text{Fe}^{3+}$ / $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$**;
(c) Oxidising agent: **$\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$**; (d) Reducing agent: **$\text{Al}$**.
---
Q: State whether $\text{HCl}(aq) + \text{NaOH}(aq) \longrightarrow \text{NaCl}(aq) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(\ell)$ is a redox reaction. Give a reason using oxidation numbers.
A: **No**, it is an acid–base neutralisation, not redox, because **no element changes its oxidation number** ($\text{H} = +1$, $\text{Cl} = -1$, $\text{Na} = +1$, $\text{O} = -2$ on both sides).
```
