> **What you'll learn:** the strict format, tense, and register rules for an **Agenda & Minutes of a meeting**, an **Obituary**, and a **Formal Investigative Report** — three high-frequency DBE Paper 3 transactional texts where learners commonly drop format and tense marks.
>
> **Prerequisites:** formal register, concise summary skills, and active/passive sentence control.
>
> **Why this matters:** Unlike creative essays, official records (minutes, reports, obituaries) have **non-negotiable structural blocks**. Knowing the exact order of sections, past-tense reporting rules, and appropriate dignity/objectivity turns Section B into reliable marks.

## The idea in plain language

Every text in this lesson documents **real people, real decisions, or real facts** for an official record:
- **Agenda:** What a meeting *plans* to discuss (future-oriented checklist before the meeting).
- **Minutes:** What actually *happened and was decided* (objective past-tense record after the meeting).
- **Formal Report:** What an investigator *found out* and *recommends* (structured, evidence-based, impersonal).
- **Obituary:** A dignified public tribute recording a person's life, key contributions, and surviving family (factual + respectful, NOT an overly dramatic eulogy!).

## Format 1: Notice, Agenda & Minutes of a Meeting

Always check whether the prompt asks for **Agenda ONLY**, **Minutes ONLY**, or **Agenda + Minutes** (often an RCL, sports club, or community committee meeting).

```steps
title: Standard DBE sequence for Agenda & Minutes
step: **Heading:** Organisation name + purpose + date, time, and venue (e.g. `MINUTES OF THE GRADE 11 RCL COMMITTEE MEETING HELD IN ROOM 14 ON 18 AUGUST 2026 AT 14:15`).
step: **1. Welcome & Attendance / Apologies (Minutes):** List who opened the meeting, members present, and formal apologies noted (or `"As per attendance register"`).
step: **2. Minutes of Previous Meeting:** Confirmed as read and adopted (proposed by X, seconded by Y).
step: **3. Matters Arising:** Brief updates on action items from the previous meeting.
step: **4. New Business (numbered items):** Write **specific, substantive items** (`4.1 Recycling bins for Quad`, `4.2 Budget approval for Spring Market` — NEVER vague placeholders like `4.1 Stuff to discuss`). In **Minutes**, record: summary of discussion, **resolution/decision**, **person responsible**, and **deadline**!
step: **5. General & Date of Next Meeting + Closure:** State exact closing time (e.g. `The meeting adjourned at 15:05`) and leave space for **Chairperson** and **Secretary** signatures + date.
```

### Critical grammar rule for Minutes
- **Always use PAST TENSE** (`"The Treasurer reported..."`, `"It was resolved that..."`) and **reported speech** (never direct dialogue quotes like *"Sipho said, 'No way!'"*).
- Stay **strictly neutral and impersonal** — omit personal gossip or emotional adjectives.

## Format 2: Formal Investigative Report

Used when a principal, manager, or committee asks you to investigate a problem (e.g. cafeteria queues, vandalism in ablutions, cyberbullying survey) and propose solutions.

- **Title & Recipient block (`TO / FROM / DATE / SUBJECT`):** Crisp header identifying audience and investigator.
- **1. Terms of Reference (Introduction):** *Why* the report was commissioned, *by whom*, and *when* due (`"At the request of the Principal on 2 August 2026, an investigation was conducted into..."`).
- **2. Procedure / Methodology:** *How* evidence was gathered (numbered list: inspections, interviews, questionnaires).
- **3. Findings:** Objective, factual results — state what was observed **without leaping to advice yet**!
- **4. Conclusion:** Brief overarching cause/summary drawn directly from Findings.
- **5. Recommendations:** Concrete, practical actions phrased cleanly (`"It is recommended that the school install..."` or numbered action steps).
- **Sign-off:** Signature, full name, designation (`Head of RCL Safety Sub-committee`), date.

## Format 3: Obituary (vs Eulogy — do NOT confuse them!)

DBE exams regularly test whether you know the difference between a **written newspaper Obituary** and a **spoken funeral Eulogy**.

```compare
title: Obituary (formal notice/tribute) vs Eulogy (spoken funeral speech)
left: Obituary (Paper 3 standard)
- **Purpose:** Formal published record announcing a death, summarising life achievements, and noting survivors/funeral arrangements.
- **Tense & Person:** **Third person** (`he/she/they`) + **past tense** for life events, present tense for surviving family (*"She is survived by..."*).
- **Core sections:** Full name & age/dates; birthplace/education/career; character & community contribution; surviving family; **funeral/memorial details**.
- **Tone:** Dignified, warm, respectful, factual (no exaggerated clichés or conversational address to the corpse).
right: Eulogy (if explicitly requested as a speech)
- **Purpose:** Personal speech delivered directly to mourners at a memorial/funeral service.
- **Tense & Person:** Often **first person (`I`, `we`)** sharing shared anecdotes and personal grief.
- **Tone:** Intimate, emotional, conversational, addressing friends and family present in the room.
```

## Worked example: Turning messy meeting notes into formal Minutes

**Raw notes:** *Lindiwe says tuckshop queue is crazy at break. Everyone agrees. Thabo will make signs by Friday. Mpho says put Grade 11 prefects on duty. Voted yes.*

**Paper 3 Minutes entry:**
> **4.1 Tuckshop Queue Management**
> Ms L. Khoza reported severe congestion outside the tuckshop during first break, resulting in learners returning late to Period 4. Following discussion, **it was resolved** that two Grade 11 prefects would supervise dual queues from Monday, 24 August. **Action:** Mr T. Mokoena to design and laminate directional signage by Friday, 21 August.

```tryit
title: Fix a flawed Formal Report section
prompt: Read this snippet from a learner's Formal Report:
> *"3. Findings: I saw that the taps are totally broken and students waste loads of water. Therefore you should immediately hire a plumber today."*
Identify **2 register/structure errors** and rewrite it correctly!
hint: Check **first-person `"I"`**, informal slang, and **mixing Recommendations into Findings**!
answer: **2 errors:**
1. Uses informal first-person (`"I saw"`, `"totally broken"`, `"loads of"`, `"you should"`).
2. Mixes a **Recommendation** (`"hire a plumber"`) inside the **Findings** section!
**Fix:** *"3. Findings: Inspection of the East Block ablutions on 14 August revealed three leaking basin taps causing continuous water wastage."* *(Put `"It is recommended that a qualified plumber be contracted immediately"` under **5. Recommendations**.)*
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Writing an **Agenda** in past tense or adding outcomes before the meeting has happened! Agenda = numbered topics to be discussed; Minutes = past-tense record of what was discussed and resolved.
> - Writing `"He passed away peacefully"` five times in an **Obituary** without mentioning actual biographical facts (birthplace, career, hobbies, surviving family, service details).
> - Mixing **Recommendations** into **Findings** in a Formal Report, or using `"I"` instead of passive/impersonal reporting (`"Observations indicated..."`).

## Practice

```questions
Q: Identify the five core sections of a DBE Formal Investigative Report in strict order. (5)
A: **1. Terms of Reference (Introduction)** -> **2. Procedure** -> **3. Findings** -> **4. Conclusion** -> **5. Recommendations** (preceded by Title/To/From/Date header and followed by Signature/Designation).
---
Q: Why must **Minutes** always be written in the **past tense** and **reported speech**, even if heated debate occurred during the meeting? (2)
A: Minutes serve as an **official historical record** of organisational decisions made after the meeting ended; past tense and objective reported speech keep the record neutral, verifiable, and free of bias.
---
Q: What critical practical section belongs at the end of a newspaper **Obituary** so readers know how to pay respects? (1)
A: **Funeral / memorial service details** (date, time, venue — plus any family request regarding flowers or donations).
```
