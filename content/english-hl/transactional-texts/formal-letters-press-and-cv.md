> **What you'll learn:** exact DBE Paper 3 layout, register, and content rules for **formal letters** (complaint/request/application), **Letter to the Press / Editor**, and a **Curriculum Vitae (CV) with covering letter**, plus how the **25 marks** per transactional text are assessed (Content, Planning & Format **15** + Language, Style & Editing **10**).
>
> **Prerequisites:** register, tone, and formal correspondence conventions.
>
> **Why this matters:** Paper 3 Section B requires **two** transactional texts (`2 x 25 = 50 marks`, **180–200 words** body length each). Because **Format** sits directly inside the 15-mark Content criterion, missing a subject line, misplacing addresses, or using the wrong salutation/sign-off loses easy marks immediately!

## The idea in plain language

Transactional writing exists to **get something done in the real world**: fix a broken streetlight, challenge a misleading newspaper claim, or land a weekend job interview. Every transactional mark comes from matching three things cleanly:
1. **Audience:** Who reads this? (A municipal manager, a newspaper editor, a hiring manager — NEVER a casual mate!)
2. **Purpose:** What specific action or viewpoint are you communicating?
3. **Format:** The exact layout conventions (addresses, date, subject line, salutation, paragraphs, sign-off) required by DBE Paper 3.

### Word-count rule (crucial!)

The **body** of each Paper 3 transactional text must be **180–200 words**. Addresses, dates, salutations, and headings do **NOT** count toward the 180–200 words — only your main message does!

## Format 1: Standard Formal Letter (Request / Complaint / Application)

```steps
title: Exact layout of a DBE Formal Letter
step: **1. Sender's Address & Date (top right or left — align consistently!):** Street/PO Box, Suburb, Postal Code, plus full date written cleanly (`14 August 2026`, NOT `14/08/26`). Do NOT write your own name above your address.
step: **2. Blank line -> Recipient's Title & Address (left):** e.g. `The Municipal Manager`, `City of Tshwane`, `PO Box 440`, `Pretoria`, `0001`.
step: **3. Salutation:** `Dear Sir/Madam` (if unknown) OR `Dear Ms Ndlovu` (if named). **No punctuation after Dear...** or consistent modern open punctuation throughout.
step: **4. Subject Line (UPPERCASE or underlined):** Short, clear topic — e.g. `REQUEST FOR PEDESTRIAN CROSSING ON CHURCH STREET`.
step: **5. Body (180–200 words across 3–4 crisp paragraphs):** Para 1 states purpose immediately; Para 2 gives specific factual details/dates/evidence; Para 3 proposes constructive action and polite deadline/contact details.
step: **6. Sign-off (match the salutation!):** `Dear Sir/Madam` -> **`Yours faithfully`**; `Dear Ms Ndlovu` -> **`Yours sincerely`**. Follow with signature, printed full name, and title (`Mr`/`Ms` if helpful).
```

## Format 2: Letter to the Press / Editor

A Letter to the Press responds publicly to an **article or community issue**. Your real audience is both **the editor AND the general public reading the newspaper**.

- **Same two-address layout** as a formal letter, addressed to `The Editor`, name of publication (e.g. *The Daily Dispatch*), street/PO Box, city, code.
- **Salutation:** `Sir/Madam` or `Dear Editor`.
- **Subject line:** Clear stance/topic, e.g. `UNSAFE TAXI RANK INFRASTRUCTURE DEMANDS URGENT ACTION`.
- **Opening sentence:** Refer explicitly to the **article title and date** or ongoing public debate (*"Your report 'Commuters Left Stranded' (12 August) rightly highlighted..."*).
- **Tone:** Persuasive, informed, polite, crisp — **never** abusive, libellous, or emotional name-calling!
- **Sign-off:** `Yours faithfully`, signature, full name, and suburb (`Thabo Mokoena, Soweto`). *(Optional pseudonym only if requested by prompt — e.g. `Concerned Resident` — still include real name below for the editor's record.)*

## Format 3: Covering Letter + Curriculum Vitae (CV)

When applying for a part-time job, learnership, or bursary, the **Covering Letter** sells *why you fit* in 180–200 words, while the **CV** summarises factual credentials neatly so an employer can scan them in 15 seconds.

```compare
title: Covering Letter vs Curriculum Vitae (CV) — keep their roles separate!
left: Covering Letter (persuasive formal letter)
- **Format:** Full formal letter layout with Subject Line (`APPLICATION FOR WEEKEND LIBRARY ASSISTANT`).
- **Para 1:** Post applied for + where you saw the advert.
- **Para 2:** Highlight **2–3 relevant strengths/achievements** matching the job (never just repeat your entire CV line-by-line!).
- **Para 3:** Availability for interview + polite close (`Yours faithfully` / `Yours sincerely`).
right: Curriculum Vitae (structured factual summary)
- **Personal Details:** Full name, DOB/ID placeholder (or Grade/DOB), contact number, email, residential area.
- **Education:** Current school, Grade 11, key subjects/academic honours (most recent first).
- **Skills & Experience:** Volunteering, leadership/prefect roles, computer literacy, languages spoken.
- **Referees:** Two named referees (e.g. Teacher/Coach — NOT family!) with role and contact number.
```

> **Exam tip for CV prompts:** Read carefully! If the question says *"Write a **covering letter** to accompany your CV"*, write ONLY the covering letter (180–200 words). If it says *"Write a **CV and covering letter**"*, divide your effort cleanly so the combined factual/persuasive writing stays focused and legible without sprawling past two pages.

```tryit
title: Spot three format & tone mistakes
prompt: Look at this opening of a Letter to the Editor:
> *12/08/26 — Dear Editor, I am writing because your useless journalist wrote total nonsense last Tuesday about youth sport. Everybody in town knows you guys don't care about local clubs!*
> *Yours sincerely, Sipho.*
Identify **3 specific errors** and fix the salutation + opening sentence!
hint: Check date format, tone/register, specific article reference, and sign-off pairing!
answer: **3 errors:**
1. Abbreviated numeric date (`12/08/26`) and missing sender/recipient addresses.
2. Abusive, informal tone (`"useless journalist"`, `"total nonsense"`, `"you guys"`).
3. `Yours sincerely` mismatched with `Dear Editor` (should be `Yours faithfully`), plus missing surname/suburb.
**Fix:** *Dear Editor / `LACK OF INVESTMENT IN YOUTH SPORT` / "With reference to your article 'Declining Club Participation' (10 August), I must respectfully challenge the claim that young people have lost interest in local sport."* -> Sign off: *Yours faithfully, Sipho Dlamini (Mamelodi).*
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Mixing up **`Yours faithfully`** (used when you do NOT know the person's surname: `Dear Sir/Madam` / `Dear Editor`) and **`Yours sincerely`** (used ONLY when you wrote `Dear Mr/Ms Surname`).
> - Writing `"I am writing this letter to tell you..."` — redundant! Go straight to purpose: `"I wish to lodge a formal complaint regarding..."`.
> - Putting contractions (`can't`, `won't`, `I'm`) inside formal letters. Always write full forms (`cannot`, `will not`, `I am`).

## Practice

```questions
Q: Which sign-off MUST accompany the salutation `Dear Sir/Madam`, and which sign-off accompanies `Dear Mrs Naidoo`? (2)
A: `Dear Sir/Madam` -> **`Yours faithfully`**; `Dear Mrs Naidoo` -> **`Yours sincerely`**.
---
Q: In Paper 3 Section B, do the sender address, recipient address, date, and subject line count toward the 180–200 word limit? (1)
A: **No** — the 180–200 word range applies strictly to the **body** of the transactional text.
---
Q: Why should a covering letter NOT simply copy every line from the applicant's CV? (2)
A: A CV already lists dates, subjects, and duties in bulleted summary form; the covering letter's purpose is to connect **2–3 specific strengths directly to the requirements of that particular post** in polite, persuasive prose.
```
