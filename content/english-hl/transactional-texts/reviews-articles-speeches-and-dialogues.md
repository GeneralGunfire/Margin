> **What you'll learn:** the distinct conventions, tone, and structural blueprints for **Book/Film Reviews**, **Newspaper & Magazine Articles**, **Speeches**, and **Written Dialogues & Interviews** (all high-frequency Paper 3 Section B formats).
>
> **Prerequisites:** register, audience awareness, persuasive devices, and punctuation of spoken script formats.
>
> **Why this matters:** Each of these formats looks straightforward, yet learners lose marks by turning a **Review** into pure plot spoiler, a **Magazine Article** into an unformatted essay, or a **Dialogue** into comic-book speech bubbles or quotation-mark prose!

## The idea in plain language

Unlike official records (minutes, formal letters), media and spoken-word transactional texts have **personality and voice** — you are engaging readers or listeners directly:
- **Review:** Judges **craft and merit** (acting, pacing, themes, target audience) WITHOUT spoiling the ending!
- **Magazine / Newspaper Article:** Informs or entertains a specific readership using a catchy **headline, strapline/byline, lead paragraph, and subheadings** (and strictly factual inverted-pyramid order for hard news!).
- **Speech:** Built to be **heard aloud**, not read silently — uses direct audience address (`"Ladies and gentlemen..."`), short punchy clauses, rhetorical questions, and tripling (*rule of three*).
- **Dialogue / Interview:** Written as a **playscript** (`NAME:` without speech marks!) with **stage directions in brackets** showing tone and action.

## Format 1: Book / Film / Restaurant Review

```steps
title: Five-part blueprint for a sharp Review (180–200 words)
step: **1. Header Metadata:** Title of work + author/director + genre + optional star/age rating (keeps background crisp!).
step: **2. Engaging Hook & Context:** Introduce the premise and central conflict in 2–3 sentences without giving away the ending.
step: **3. Technical Evaluation (the core marks!):** Analyse **craft**: for a **film** — cinematography, soundtrack, pacing, lead acting; for a **book** — characterisation, prose style, tension; for a **restaurant** — menu variety, plating, service speed, value for money.
step: **4. Constructive Reservation:** One balanced critique (e.g. *While the sluggish middle act strains patience...*) proves mature judgement rather than blind hype.
step: **5. Verdict & Target Audience:** Recommend clearly who will enjoy it and why (*"Essential viewing for fans of..."*).
```

## Format 2: Newspaper Report vs Magazine Feature Article

```compare
title: Hard News Report vs Magazine Feature Article
left: Newspaper Report (objective & urgent)
- **Tone:** Strictly **neutral, third-person**, factual (NO personal `"I"` opinions!).
- **Structure (Inverted Pyramid):** Most vital facts (**Who, What, Where, When, Why/How**) right in **Paragraph 1 (Lead)**; minor quotes/background later.
- **Layout:** Bold factual **Headline**, **Byline** (`By Staff Reporter`), short 2–3 sentence paragraphs, brief eyewitness quotes.
right: Magazine Feature Article (engaging & reflective)
- **Tone:** Semi-formal, lively, witty, tailored to target magazine (e.g. youth lifestyle, tech, school magazine).
- **Structure:** Catchy **Headline + Strapline (sub-deck)**, **Byline**, anecdotal or provocative hook, thematic **Subheadings**, memorable wrap-up.
- **Style:** Rhetorical questions, vivid examples, second-person connection (`"you"`).
```

## Format 3: Speech vs Written Dialogue / Interview

### Written Speech (180–200 words)
- **Title + Salutation:** Acknowledge audience cleanly in order of protocol (`"Principal Nkosi, educators, and fellow Grade 11 learners..."` — NEVER start with `"Hi guys"` for a formal speech!).
- **Oral rhythm:** Vary short punchy sentences (*"Silence solves nothing."*) with rhythmic **triads** (*"It demands honesty, patience, and courage."*).
- **Direct engagement:** Inclusive pronouns (`we`, `our`, `us`) + 1–2 strategic rhetorical questions.
- **No stage-direction clutter:** Do not write `[pauses dramatically]` every second line — let your punctuation and diction carry delivery!
- **Closing:** Memorable call to reflection/action + courteous `"Thank you."`

### Written Dialogue / Interview (script format!)
1. **Brief 2-line setting note** above the exchange establishing relationship, place, and conflict/topic.
2. **Speaker names in UPPERCASE or bold on the left, followed by a colon (`:`):**
   - `THABO: Have you checked the choir rehearsal schedule yet?`
   - **NEVER use quotation marks (`"..."`)** around spoken lines in a script dialogue!
3. **Stage directions / tone cues in brackets before or inside the line:**
   - `LERATO: (lowering her binder, quietly) Coach moved it to Thursday.`
4. **Difference between Dialogue & Interview:** In an **Interview**, the interviewer asks **focused, open-ended follow-up questions** (NOT `"Yes/No"` questions!) so the interviewee shares insight smoothly; in a **Dialogue**, both speakers react emotionally and advance a situation or disagreement.

## Worked example: Fixing Dialogue punctuation

**Faulty learner script:**
> *"Sipho:" "Hey, why didn't you pitch for practice yesterday?"*
> *"Bongani:" Because I had to look after my little brother.*

**Corrected Paper 3 format:**
> *(After school outside the sports storeroom. Sipho approaches Bongani, holding a clipboard.)*
> **SIPHO:** *(frowning)* Why did you miss yesterday's practice without letting Coach know?
> **BONGANI:** *(sighing, looking down)* My aunt's clinic shift ran late, and there was nobody else to fetch my little brother from creche.

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Putting quotation marks (`"..."`) inside a **playscript dialogue** — script colons replace speech marks!
> - Spoiling the ending inside a **Book/Film Review**, or writing 150 words of pure plot summary with zero critique of acting, style, or themes.
> - Writing a **Newspaper Article** with `"I think this event was terrible"` — hard news MUST remain strictly third-person objective!

## Practice

```questions
Q: Why must a **Hard News Report** follow the **inverted pyramid** structure rather than saving the conclusion for the final paragraph? (2)
A: Busy readers (and sub-editors trimming stories for print space) need the **5 Ws + H (Who, What, Where, When, Why, How)** immediately in the opening lead paragraph, while background details follow in decreasing order of urgency.
---
Q: List three formatting rules specific to a **Dialogue / Interview script** in Paper 3. (3)
A: 1. Write speaker names on the left followed by a **colon** (`INTERVIEWER:`), leaving a neat blank line or clear alignment between turns.
2. **Omit quotation marks** around spoken words.
3. Place brief **actions/tone cues in brackets** before or alongside speech, introduced by a short 1–2 line setting note.
---
Q: When writing a **Formal Speech** to your school assembly, what makes spoken rhetoric differ from an essay? (2)
A: A speech opens with an **audience-appropriate greeting**, speaks directly to listeners (`we`, `you`, rhetorical questions), and favours **rhythmic, memorable spoken sentences** (short impact statements, repetition/triads) easy for an ear to follow live.
```
