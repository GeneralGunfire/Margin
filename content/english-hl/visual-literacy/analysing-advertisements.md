> **Why this matters:** Advertisements account for **10 out of 70 marks** in Paper 1 (Section C, Question 3). They are quick marks *if* you link visual details and word choices directly to the advertiser's goal!

## The idea in plain language

An advertisement is never neutral art — every font size, colour contrast, facial expression, and slogan has one mission: **to persuade a specific group of people to buy a product, donate money, or change behaviour**.

Think of an advert as a four-step funnel called **AIDA**:
1. **A — Attention:** stops the reader's eye in half a second (bold headline, shocking image, bright contrast, rhetorical question, pun).
2. **I — Interest:** keeps the reader looking by showing a problem they relate to or a striking visual metaphor.
3. **D — Desire:** makes the reader *want* the product/action (claims of speed/savings/status, expert endorsement, emotive words, inclusivity).
4. **A — Action:** gives a clear, easy next step (*"Scan QR code"*, *"Visit..."*, *"Call today while stocks last"*).

### Definitions to memorise

```definition
title: Target audience (target market)
The specific group of people (defined by age, income, lifestyle, interests, or needs) whom an advertisement is specifically designed to persuade.
```

```definition
title: Logo vs Slogan
A **logo** is the recognisable graphic symbol or emblem representing a brand or organisation.
A **slogan** is a short, memorable catchphrase associated with a brand or campaign (e.g. *"Just Do It"*).
```

## Five persuasive techniques examiners ask about constantly

1. **Manipulative / emotive language & pronouns:** using `"you"`, `"your"`, `"we"`, and `"our"` makes the reader feel personally addressed and part of an exclusive group; emotive adjectives (*"effortless"*, *"devastating"*, *"pure"*) trigger guilt, pride, or fear.
2. **Typography and layout:** ALL CAPS, exclamation marks, bold/oversized fonts draw the eye first; tiny fine print at the bottom (`*Ts & Cs apply`) hides limitations!
3. **Puns, alliteration, and ambiguity:** clever double meanings stick in memory (e.g. an airline advert saying *"We take your comfort to new heights"*).
4. **Expert authority & pseudo-science:** phrases like *"clinically proven"*, *"9 out of 10 dentists"*, or scientific-sounding words build trust — even when the sample size is never given!
5. **Visual symbolism:** linking the product to something admirable (showing an SUV parked on a snowy mountain peak suggests freedom, toughness, and status — not just transport).

### The golden rule of visual questions: NEVER just describe!

If a 2-mark question asks: *"Comment on the effectiveness of the visual in this advertisement"*:
- **0–1 mark (pure description):** *"The picture shows a plastic bottle next to a turtle in the sea."*
- **2 marks (visual + effect):** *"Placing a discarded plastic bottle right beside a fragile sea turtle creates a stark **visual contrast/threat** (1), triggering guilt/urgency in the reader so they feel compelled to support the anti-plastic campaign (1)."*

## Worked example

Imagine an advertisement for a solar power bank showing a smiling hiker on a mountain ridge holding a sleek black charger under the bold headline: **"NEVER LOSE SIGNAL IN THE WILD."** At the bottom sits small text: *"Waterproof. 10-day battery. Free delivery this weekend only — order at SunPeak.co.za"* with a mountain-peak logo.

**Exam questions & full-mark answers:**

1. **Who is the likely target market of this advertisement? Substantiate your answer. (2 marks)**
   - **Answer:** Outdoor enthusiasts / hikers / campers / adventure travellers (1), evidenced by the image of a hiker on a mountain ridge and references to `"in the wild"` and `"Waterproof"` (1). *(Never write `"Everyone"` or `"People with phones"` — that is too broad and scores 0!)*
2. **How does the advertiser use the AIDA principle through the headline and closing line? (2 marks)**
   - **Answer:** The bold, capitalised headline grabs **Attention** (`"NEVER LOSE SIGNAL"`) by addressing a hiker's fear of being cut off (1); the closing line drives **Action** by offering a clear website and urgency (`"this weekend only"`) (1).
3. **Identify ONE language/persuasive device in `"Waterproof. 10-day battery."` and explain its impact. (2 marks)**
   - **Answer:** Short, punchy **fragment sentences** (staccato style) with concrete specifications (1) make the benefits quick to scan and convey toughness, reliability, and efficiency (1).

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Answering *"Who is the target market?"* with *"Everybody"* or *"The general public"*. Almost every advert targets a specific age, role, interest, or income group — name it sharply and prove it from the text/image!
> - Describing what the picture shows without explaining **what message or emotion it creates**.
> - Calling a **slogan** a **logo** (logo = visual picture/icon; slogan = catchphrase in words).
> - Ignoring **fine print** at the bottom when asked to comment on reliability or bias (`*Only valid on selected models`).

## Practice

```questions
Q: A skincare advert claims: *"Dermatologist-approved HydraGlow locks in moisture for 48 hours of radiant confidence."* Identify TWO persuasive techniques used and explain their effect. (3)
A: 1. **Appeal to authority** (`"Dermatologist-approved"`) builds scientific trust and credibility (1).
2. **Emotive / aspirational language** (`"radiant confidence"`) promises emotional self-esteem rather than just skin cream, creating desire (1–2).
---
Q: Why do many charity adverts use a close-up photograph of a single child looking straight at the camera rather than a wide crowd shot? (2)
A: Eye contact and an individual face create an immediate **personal, emotional connection (pathos)** with the viewer, making suffering feel urgent and solvable rather than overwhelming.
---
Q: An advert for noise-cancelling headphones features an oversized graphic of a roaring jackhammer fading into a tiny silent musical note, with the caption: *"Turn chaos into calm."* Explain how the visual and words work together. (2)
A: The shrinking scale from a loud jackhammer to a gentle musical note visually mirrors the transformation from `"chaos"` to `"calm"`, emphasising how powerfully the headphones block harsh background noise.
```
