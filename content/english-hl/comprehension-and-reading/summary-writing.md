> **What you'll learn:** how DBE Paper 1 Section B (**Summary — 10 marks**) is marked, how to separate **7 core main ideas** from examples and fluff, how to paraphrase concisely in **one single fluent paragraph** (Home Language rule!), and how to avoid automatic word-count and lifting penalties.
>
> **Prerequisites:** identifying topic sentences and paraphrasing in your own words.
>
> **Why this matters:** Summary is the easiest **10/10** in Paper 1 once you follow the exact format rules — yet learners throw away 3–4 marks every exam simply by writing bullet points instead of a **fluent paragraph**, quoting verbatim, or going over 90 words!

## The idea in plain language

Imagine a friend asks what a 350-word article on *"How to sleep better"* actually advises. You don't recite statistics, brand names, or jokes — you condense just the **core instructions** into three crisp sentences in your own everyday English.

That is Paper 1 Section B:
- **7 marks** for **7 distinct main facts/points** extracted from the passage.
- **3 marks** for **language and style** (paraphrasing in your own words, grammar, clarity, and keeping inside the **90-word limit**).

### Crucial format difference: Home Language vs First Additional Language!

| Paper 1 Rule | English Home Language (HL) |
| --- | --- |
| **Format** | **ONE SINGLE FLUENT PARAGRAPH** (unless the question *explicitly* instructs numbered points — read the rubric instruction EVERY exam, though modern DBE HL papers require a **single paragraph of max 90 words**!). |
| **Word limit** | **Maximum 90 words** (write the exact word count in brackets at the bottom, e.g. `[84 words]` — never lie about your word count; markers count every word and **stop marking after word 90**!). |
| **Title** | **Do NOT include a title** unless specifically instructed — if omitted when not asked, you waste zero words! |
| **Language marks penalty** | If you **lift/quote sentences directly**, you lose language marks (1–3 marks docked depending on how many sentences were lifted!). |

## What to KEEP vs what to CUT

When highlighting points in a 300–400 word source text:

```compare
title: Keep the skeleton, cut the decoration
left: KEEP (Core main ideas)
- Topic sentences and main claims/reasons/solutions matching the **specific prompt focus** (e.g. *"Summarise seven ways teens can manage screen fatigue"* — ignore facts about *why smartphones were invented*!).
- Actionable steps, causes, consequences, or key arguments.
right: CUT (Fluff that wastes your 90 words)
- **Examples** (*"such as Instagram, TikTok and YouTube"* $\to$ cut or replace with *"social media"*).
- **Statistics, dates, names, and anecdotes** (*"In 2023, Dr Mokoena found 68%..."* $\to$ cut!).
- **Repetitions** saying the same point twice in different words.
- **Rhetorical questions & direct speech** (convert to concise statements).
- **Your own opinions** — NEVER invent points that aren't in the text!
```

## The 5-step summary workflow

```steps
title: Earning 10/10 on Paper 1 Summary in 15 minutes
step: **Read the prompt focus carefully:** underline *what* the 7 points must be about (e.g. *benefits*, *causes*, *solutions*, or *challenges*). Points outside that focus earn **0**!
step: **Hunt for 7–9 candidate points** across the passage, crossing out examples, stats, and quotes as you go.
step: **Draft each point in your own words** (strip adjectives/adverbs, merge pairs of synonyms, change active $\leftrightarrow$ passive or swap nouns/verbs to avoid lifting).
step: **Join the 7 points smoothly into ONE paragraph** using short connectors (*First, Furthermore, Additionally, Also, Finally*). Avoid repetitive fillers like *"The author also goes on to explain that..."* (that wastes 8 precious words per sentence!).
step: **Count every word carefully** (hyphenated words like `well-being` count as 1 word; contractions/articles `a`, `the` count as 1 word). Trim excess phrasing until you sit at **80–89 words**, write a neat final copy, strike a single neat line through your rough draft, and record `[86 words]` underneath!
```

## Worked example

**Prompt:** *Summarise, in ONE paragraph of NO MORE THAN 90 words, **SEVEN practical ways learners can improve memory before exams**.*

**Source extract (abridged 175 words):**
> *"Many Grade 11 learners stare at open textbooks for four hours the night before a test, yet brain scans show marathon cramming barely sticks. Instead, spreading revision into shorter twenty-five-minute bursts across several weeks strengthens neural pathways far better. Don't simply re-read notes passively either: close the book and force yourself to write down everything you recall from memory. Teaching a tricky concept aloud to a friend or even to an empty room exposes gaps immediately. Physical habits count too. Moderate aerobic exercise such as a brisk walk boosts blood flow to the hippocampus, while getting seven to nine hours of unbroken sleep allows the brain to file new facts overnight. Studying in a quiet space with your phone left in another room prevents costly attention switching. Finally, drinking enough water throughout the day keeps concentration sharp, because even mild dehydration slows mental processing."*

**Selecting & paraphrasing the 7 points:**
1. Space study into brief sessions over weeks (*not marathon cramming*).
2. Practise active recall by writing information down without looking at notes.
3. Explain difficult ideas aloud to someone else to spot weak areas.
4. Do regular light exercise like brisk walking to support brain function.
5. Sleep seven to nine hours nightly so memories consolidate.
6. Remove distractions by keeping mobile phones in a separate room.
7. Stay well hydrated to maintain focus.

**Final single-paragraph summary:**
> Learners boost exam recall by spacing brief study sessions across several weeks instead of cramming overnight. Practising active recall by writing facts from memory, and explaining difficult work aloud, quickly reveals knowledge gaps. Regular physical exercise stimulates brain function, while getting sufficient nightly sleep helps consolidate new information. Additionally, keeping mobile phones in another room eliminates distracting interruptions, and drinking adequate water throughout the day sustains sharp concentration. `[71 words]`

Check: all **7 distinct points** are present, completely paraphrased, written as **one fluent paragraph**, zero filler, **71 words** ($\le 90$)!

```tryit
title: Spot why this summary loses marks
prompt: A learner writes: *"First, the writer of the passage clearly believes that spreading revision into shorter twenty-five-minute bursts across several weeks strengthens neural pathways far better. Secondly, you should not simply re-read notes passively either..."*
Name **TWO** errors costing the learner marks here.
hint: Look at wordiness ("First, the writer of the passage clearly believes that...") and originality of wording.
answer: 1. **Lifting verbatim phrasing** (*"spreading revision into shorter twenty-five-minute bursts across several weeks strengthens neural pathways far better"* and *"simply re-read notes passively"* are copied directly from the passage, losing language marks).
2. **Wordy meta-commentary** (*"the writer of the passage clearly believes that..."*) wastes words and causes them to blow past the 90-word ceiling before reaching point 7!
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Writing **numbered bullet points** when the HL paper specifies **a single fluent paragraph** (automatic format/language deduction!).
> - Putting a fake lower word count like `[88 words]` when the paragraph is actually 108 words — markers recount, cross out everything after word 90, and any points sitting at word 91–108 score **0**!
> - Including meta-phrases (*"The text says..."*, *"According to the author..."*) that waste 15–20 words across the paragraph. Start straight with the subject (*"Learners can..."* or *"Cities reduce waste by..."*).
> - Writing 5 long points instead of **7 distinct points**. Always number your rough-draft points 1 to 7 before weaving them into your paragraph!

## Practice

```questions
Q: Read these 4 sentences and trim them to under 18 words in your own words: *"In my opinion, municipalities really ought to repair leaking water pipes promptly because millions of litres vanish daily into underground soil before ever reaching households."*
A: *Municipalities must fix leaking pipes quickly to stop massive daily water losses before supply reaches homes.* (16 words — cuts `"In my opinion"`, `"really ought to"`, `"millions of litres"`, `"into underground soil"`).
---
Q: If a question says: *"Summarise SEVEN **dangers** of fast fashion"*, and paragraph 2 lists **three economic benefits** of fast fashion, should you include one of those benefits to reach 7 points?
A: **No!** Points that do not directly answer the specific prompt focus (*dangers*) receive **0 marks** and waste precious words. Look elsewhere in the passage for the 7th danger.
---
Q: How many words do `"e-mail"`, `"2027"`, and `"isn't"` count as in a DBE English summary word count?
A: `"e-mail"` (hyphenated word) = **1 word**; `"2027"` (number) = **1 word**; `"isn't"` (avoid contractions in formal summaries, though counted as **1 word**; writing `"is not"` is **2 words**).
```
