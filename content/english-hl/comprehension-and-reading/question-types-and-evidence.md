> **What you'll learn:** how Paper 1 comprehension questions are split across cognitive levels (literal, reorganisation, inference, evaluation), what every DBE command verb (*quote*, *explain in your own words*, *account for*, *critically discuss*) demands, and how to use mark allocations so you never drop half a mark.
>
> **Prerequisites:** Grade 10 reading comprehension and paragraph scanning.
>
> **Why this matters:** Comprehension is worth **30 out of 70 marks** in Paper 1. Most lost marks happen not because learners didn't understand the passage, but because they quoted when asked to explain in their own words, or gave one point for a 3-mark question.

## The idea in plain language

Reading a comprehension passage like a novel (slowly from line 1 to line 60 and only then looking at the questions) wastes time and blurs details. Instead, treat Paper 1 Section A like detective work:

1. **Read the title, subheading, and source credit first:** they instantly tell you the topic, audience, and attitude of the writer.
2. **Skim the passage paragraph by paragraph**, jotting a 2-word topic label beside each paragraph so you know where every idea lives.
3. **Read the question carefully and circle TWO things:** the **command verb** (what to do) and the **mark allocation** (how many distinct points to write).

### The three question families you will meet

| Level | Share in Paper 1 | What it asks you to do | Example command verbs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1–2. Literal & Reorganisation** | ~40% | Locate explicit facts directly in the text or combine two facts from different paragraphs. | *State*, *Identify*, *List*, *Quote*, *Summarise* |
| **3. Inference** | ~40% | Read "between the lines" to explain *why*, *how*, or what is *implied* using clues in the text. | *Explain*, *Account for*, *Why does the writer...*, *Suggest* |
| **4–5. Evaluation & Appreciation** | ~20% | Judge effectiveness, tone, bias, writer's intention, or whether you agree — **always backed by evidence**. | *Comment on*, *Critically discuss*, *Evaluate*, *Is the title effective?* |

## Decoding Paper 1 command verbs

Every command verb carries strict marking rules in DBE English Home Language memos:

- **"Quote..." / "Provide evidence from paragraph 2...":** Copy the exact words inside quotation marks `"..."` with exact spelling and punctuation — **never** alter a word inside quotation marks. If it asks for *two consecutive words* or *a single phrase*, stop immediately after those words; quoting a whole sentence forfeits the mark!
- **"Explain in your own words..." / "Using your own words, state...":** Translate the writer's idea into fresh vocabulary (**paraphrase**). Lifting whole phrases directly from the text scores **0**, even if the idea is right!
- **"Account for..." / "Why does the author suggest...":** Give the **cause or reason** implied by the passage (always anchor it in what the passage actually states, never general outside knowledge).
- **"Comment on..." / "Discuss the effectiveness of...":** Needs TWO parts for full marks: (1) **identify the feature or point** (e.g. a contrast, metaphor, or statistic) AND (2) **explain its specific impact or effect** in this context!

```steps
title: The PE-Check routine for 2- and 3-mark comprehension answers
step: **Check the paragraph reference** in the question (e.g. *"Refer to paragraph 3"* — markers will ignore points taken from paragraph 5 if the question restricts you!).
step: **Match points to marks:**
- 1 mark = 1 crisp fact or exact quote.
- 2 marks = 1 clear point + 1 supporting reason/explanation, OR 2 distinct points.
- 3 marks = 2 points + concrete textual backing/comment, OR 3 clear ideas.
step: **Test the "quote vs own words" rule:** did the question ask you to *quote*, or *explain in your own words*? Never confuse the two!
step: **Check the comparative/linking word:** if a question compares Text A and Text B (*"Does Text B support paragraph 4 of Text A?"*), state *"Yes/No"* explicitly first, then mention specific details from **BOTH** texts!
```

## Worked example

Read this short extract from an article on urban noise:

> *"By midnight, city streets used to settle into a gentle hush, broken only by distant wind. Today, generator hums, delivery scooters and glowing billboards keep neighbourhoods wide awake long after sunset. Medical researchers warn that this relentless 'sensorystatic' quietly erodes deep sleep — even in people who believe they have grown used to the racket."*

**Sample questions & model full-mark answers:**

1. **Quote TWO consecutive words from the extract that describe city streets at midnight in the past. (1 mark)**
   - **Answer:** `"gentle hush"`. *(Quoting `"settle into a gentle hush"` or `"a gentle hush"` breaks the "two consecutive words" rule and loses the mark!)*
2. **In your own words, explain the contrast the writer creates between the past and the present. (2 marks)**
   - **Answer:** Previously, cities became peaceful and quiet late at night, whereas now constant mechanical sounds and artificial lights keep urban areas noisy and bright well into the night. *(Notice: "peaceful/quiet" paraphrases "gentle hush"; "constant mechanical sounds and artificial lights" paraphrases generators, scooters and billboards.)*
3. **Why does the writer place `'sensorystatic'` in single inverted commas and follow it with `"even in people who believe they have grown used to the racket"`? (2 marks)**
   - **Answer:** The inverted commas highlight a coined/unusual term combining noise and light overload; adding that people who think they ignore the noise are still harmed emphasises how inescapable and damaging modern urban noise is to health.

```compare
title: Two common Paper 1 answer styles (0 marks vs full marks)
left: Vague / lifted answer (loses marks)
- *"Because today generator hums, delivery scooters and glowing billboards keep neighbourhoods wide awake."* (Lifted word-for-word when asked for own words!)
- *"It makes the reader feel something and emphasises the point."* (Empty filler — doesn't say *what* it emphasises!)
right: Precise / mark-earning answer
- Paraphrases every key idea freshly without copying sentences.
- Names the specific point AND explains its exact effect on meaning or the reader.
- Matches the number of distinct ideas directly to the mark allocation.
```

```tryit
title: Spot why this 2-mark answer got only 1 mark
prompt: A passage says: *"Students often highlight textbooks in neon yellow because it feels productive, yet passive re-reading locks very little into long-term memory."*
Question: **In your own words, why is highlighting ineffective for studying? (2 marks)**
Student wrote: *"Because passive re-reading locks very little into long-term memory."*
Why did the marker award 0 or 1 mark instead of 2?
hint: Check both the wording (lifted vs paraphrased) and whether the student explained the link between highlighting and lack of active thinking.
answer: The student **copied the exact sentence** word-for-word instead of using their own words! A full-mark own-words answer would say: *Highlighting creates a false sense of working hard (1) without forcing the brain to engage actively, so very little information is actually retained (1).*
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Exceeding the word count on "quote two consecutive words" or "quote a single word" questions. Count the words carefully and keep quotation marks tight!
> - Answering a "Do you agree?" or "Is the writer justified?" evaluation question with *"Yes, because everyone knows..."* without citing proof from the passage itself.
> - Leaving out one half of a Text A vs Text B comparison question — always explicitly mention **BOTH** Text A and Text B!

## Practice

```questions
Q: Read this sentence: *"Social media algorithms do not merely reflect our curiosity; they quietly herd our attention toward outrage because anger keeps fingers scrolling."*
(a) Quote a single word that shows algorithms act subtly and without us noticing. (1)
(b) In your own words, explain the metaphor `"herd our attention"` and why the writer uses it. (2)
A: (a) `"quietly"` (1 mark — exactly one word inside quotation marks).
(b) It compares social media users' minds to passive farm animals/cattle being guided without free choice (1), emphasising that platforms deliberately manipulate and steer what people look at for profit/engagement (1).
---
Q: A question asks: *"Refer to lines 14–18. Critically discuss the writer's claim that electric scooters solve traffic congestion. (3 marks)"* What three elements must your answer contain?
A: 1. Clear stance or identification of what the writer claims in lines 14–18 (1 mark).
2. Specific evidence or counter-evidence/flaw mentioned in the passage (1 mark).
3. Balanced judgement evaluating whether the claim holds up or is overstated (1 mark).
---
Q: What is the difference between **skimming** and **scanning** when tackling Paper 1 comprehension?
A: **Skimming** is reading quickly across topic sentences and headings to grasp the overall gist and tone; **scanning** is running your eyes down the page to hunt for one specific word, date, name, or line reference.
```
