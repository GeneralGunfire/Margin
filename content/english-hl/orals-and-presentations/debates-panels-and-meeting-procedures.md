> **What you'll learn:** formal **debating roles** (Proposition vs Opposition, 1st/2nd/3rd speakers, rebuttal, Floor & Reply), **panel discussion** dynamics, and **formal meeting procedure** roles (Chairperson, Secretary, motions, quorums, points of order) assessed in Grade 11 English HL orals.
>
> **Prerequisites:** argumentative structure, active listening, and formal register.
>
> **Why this matters:** Group oral tasks (debates, panels, forum discussions, mock meetings) test **interactive listening** — NOT five learners reciting memorised mini-speeches with their backs to each other! Knowing your exact procedural role earns effortless marks for teamwork and responsiveness.

## The idea in plain language

In solo speeches, you control the stage alone. In **debates, panel discussions, and formal meetings**, marks go to speakers who **listen sharply to what was just said** and respond directly:
- **Debate:** Two competing teams clash constructively over a formal **motion** (*"This House believes that..."*) under strict rules of etiquette and rebuttal.
- **Panel Discussion:** A **chairperson/moderator** guides 3–4 experts or stakeholders holding *different perspectives* on an issue (e.g. a parent, principal, learner, and tech specialist discussing school cellphone policies) before opening to audience Q&A.
- **Formal Meeting:** A chairperson runs a structured **agenda**, ensures a **quorum** is present, invites motions/seconders, manages voting, and keeps speakers strictly on procedure.

## Part 1: Formal Debating Rules & Speaker Roles

Every formal school debate centres on a **Motion** (e.g. *"This House would make community service compulsory for matriculants"*):
- **Proposition (Government / Affirmative):** Argues **FOR** the motion. Defines key terms fairly right at the start!
- **Opposition (Negative):** Argues **AGAINST** the motion. Directly **rebuts** the Proposition's case AND presents its own counter-case.

```steps
title: Who does what in a 3-a-side debate
step: **1st Proposition:** Welcomes Chairperson/adjudicators, **defines key terms of the motion**, outlines the team's split (`"Team line"`), and presents the first 1–2 core arguments.
step: **1st Opposition:** Accepts or reasonably challenges weak definitions, **rebuts 1st Proposition**, states Opposition's counter-line, and delivers first counter-arguments.
step: **2nd Proposition & 2nd Opposition:** Rebut the preceding speaker's main flaws **FIRST** (never skip rebuttal!), then deepen new constructive arguments with proof.
step: **3rd Speakers (Whip / Summary):** Bring **NO major brand-new case points** — instead, expose contradictions across the whole debate, rebuild damaged team pillars, and crystallise why their side won the clash!
step: **Points of Information (POIs) & Reply Speeches (where used):** Brief 15-second interjections offered politely (`"On a point of information, Madam Chair?"` — speaker may accept or decline cleanly).
```

### How to deliver sharp live rebuttal (`4-Step Rebuttal Formula`)
Never shout `"You are wrong!"` — dismantle the idea systematically:
1. **Quote/Pinpoint:** *"The First Opposition claimed that..."*
2. **Counter:** *"However, that overlooks..."*
3. **Prove:** Give one logical reason or real example showing why the claim fails.
4. **Impact:** *"Therefore, their central economic objection collapses."*

## Part 2: Panel Discussions & Forums

```compare
title: Formal Debate vs Panel Discussion
left: Formal Debate (adversarial binary clash)
- **Goal:** Defeat the opposing team's case on a **Yes/No motion**.
- **Structure:** Fixed alternating turns; strict timing; formal address (`"Madam Chairperson, ladies and gentlemen..."`).
- **Interaction:** Direct **rebuttal** of rivals + Points of Information.
right: Panel Discussion (collaborative multi-angle exploration)
- **Goal:** Explore a complex topic from **3–4 distinct roles/perspectives** for an audience.
- **Structure:** Moderator introduces panel -> brief opening views -> **moderated back-and-forth questions** -> audience Q&A -> moderator summary.
- **Interaction:** Respectful building and challenging (`"I appreciate Dr Pillay's concern; yet from a learner's perspective..."`).
```

### What makes a Panel Moderator score 90%+?
- Keeps introductions crisp and sets ground rules.
- Never hogs the microphone — asks **provocative, follow-up questions** bridging two panellists (*"Mrs Jacobs wants stricter curfews; Mr Dlamini, how would shift-working parents manage that?"*).
- Intervenes courteously if one panellist interrupts or drifts off topic, then summarises shared insights cleanly.

## Part 3: Formal Meeting Procedure Vocabulary

Whether tested in a group oral simulation or Paper 3/4 comprehension context, know these core terms:

| Term | Exact Meaning in Meeting Procedure |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Quorum** | Minimum number of members who MUST be present for decisions/votes to be legally valid. |
| **Motion** | A formal proposal put forward for discussion and vote (`"I propose that..."`). |
| **Seconder** | A second member who formally supports discussing/voting on a motion (`"I second the motion"`). |
| **Point of Order** | Interruption raising a breach of meeting rules or procedure (decided immediately by Chairperson). |
| **Casting Vote** | Tie-breaking extra vote exercised by the Chairperson when a vote splits 50/50. |
| **Adjourn** | Formally close the meeting until a specified future time/date. |

## Worked example: Live debate rebuttal vs empty contradiction

**Opponent's claim:** *"Compulsory community service would waste matriculants' study time and lower university pass rates."*

- **Weak response:** *"That's completely untrue! Matriculants aren't that busy and volunteering is nice."* (No logic, vague!)
- **Strong 4-step rebuttal:** *"The Second Opposition argues that thirty hours of service will ruin matric results. **However**, thirty hours across nine months averages less than **forty-five minutes per week** — less than a single social-media scroll session. **More importantly**, structured community leadership strengthens university bursary applications and focus. **Thus**, far from harming academic futures, brief service enriches them."*

```tryit
title: Act as Panel Moderator bridging two clashing views
prompt: Panellist A (a local shop owner) says: *"Banning plastic bags outright will bankrupt small spaza shops."*
Panellist B (an environmental scientist) says: *"Every thin bag clogs storm drains for decades."*
Write **2 moderator sentences** that acknowledge both and ask a sharp bridging question to move the discussion forward!
hint: Avoid taking sides yourself — invite practical solutions!
answer: **Example moderator bridge:** *"Thank you both — we clearly face a tough tension between immediate small-business costs and long-term municipal flooding. Turning to both of you, what affordable phase-in or subsidised reusable alternative could protect spaza margins while cutting drain blockages?"*
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - **Zero eye contact / ignoring teammates and opponents:** Reciting a pre-written speech in a debate without addressing **at least one point** made by the previous speaker drops responsiveness marks drastically.
> - **Attacking people instead of arguments:** Address comments strictly through the **Chairperson** (`"Madam Speaker, the Opposition forgets..."`), never directly (`"You guys are lying..."`).
> - **Confusing a Point of Order with a Point of Information:** A **Point of Information** challenges the *content/facts* of a debater's argument; a **Point of Order** alerts the Chair to a *rule/procedural violation* (such as exceeding time or insulting language).

## Practice

```questions
Q: Why must the **First Proposition** speaker define key terms of the motion clearly at the very start of a debate? (2)
A: Clear definitions establish **common ground and fair boundaries** so both teams clash over the exact same meaning rather than talking past each other on technicalities.
---
Q: Distinguish between a **quorum**, a **seconder**, and a **casting vote** in formal meeting procedure. (3)
A: A **quorum** is the minimum attendance required for valid decisions; a **seconder** is the member who backs a proposed motion so it can be debated/voted on; a **casting vote** is the Chairperson's tie-breaking vote if a ballot ends even.
---
Q: What critical error happens when a **3rd Speaker** in a debate spends three minutes introducing brand-new arguments instead of rebutting and summarising? (2)
A: The opposing side gets no fair chance to answer brand-new points late in the debate (breaking debate fairness rules), and the 3rd speaker fails their core duty to **clash, rebuild, and summarise** why their team carried the main themes.
```
