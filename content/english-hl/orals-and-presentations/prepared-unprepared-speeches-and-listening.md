> **What you'll learn:** how Paper 4's **50 oral marks** work across CAPS/ATP SBA tasks (**Listening Comprehension**, **Prepared Speech**, and **Unprepared Speech**), how to research and deliver a persuasive speech using **cue cards** instead of reading off A4 sheets, and how to think fast during **2–3 minute unprepared speaking**.
>
> **Prerequisites:** rhetorical structure, register awareness, and active note-taking.
>
> **Why this matters:** Paper 4 contributes **50 marks** directly to your end-of-year mark — equal to an entire essay or two transactional texts! Strong preparation and smart cue-card technique turn stage nerves into confident marks.

## The idea in plain language

Oral assessment does NOT grade whether you sound like a radio actor — examiners assess **three practical communication skills**:
1. **Research & Structure:** Did you gather credible facts/examples and organise a logical beginning, middle, and conclusion?
2. **Audience Connection:** Eye contact, clear vocal projection, varied pace/pauses, and natural posture — NOT staring down at a full printed page!
3. **Active Listening:** Extracting main ideas, facts vs opinions, speaker bias, and tone accurately while listening twice to an unseen audio/read passage.

## Task 1: Listening Comprehension (15 marks SBA)

In Listening Comprehension, your teacher reads (or plays) a passage **twice**:
- **First listening (DO NOT write frantically!):** Listen for **overall purpose, tone, speaker's attitude, and main structure** — jot only 3–4 broad topic anchors.
- **Second listening (targeted note-taking):** Now you already have the questions (or key headings) in front of you! Use shorthand symbols (`+`, `->`, `!=`, `eg.`) to capture **numbers, reasons, contrasts, and exact tone cues**.

```steps
title: Golden rules for Listening Comprehension answers
step: **Distinguish Fact vs Opinion:** Watch for hedging/emotive words (*"believes"`, `"recklessly"`, `"unfortunately"`) vs verifiable numbers/dates.
step: **Read the tone of delivery:** A speaker pausing heavily, stressing `"allegedly"`, or using sarcasm signals attitude marks!
step: **Answer in full, precise English after reading finishes:** Convert rushed shorthand notes cleanly before handing in.
```

## Task 2: Prepared Speech (research, cue cards & delivery)

A Grade 11 Prepared Speech runs **3–5 minutes** and usually requires a **research portfolio/notes** alongside delivery.

```compare
title: Reading a full script vs Using Cue Cards
left: Reading off A4 sheets (caps delivery marks!)
- Head drops toward paper -> **zero eye contact**.
- Voice turns flat and robotic because your eyes chase long lines.
- One lost place creates awkward panic.
right: Palm-sized Cue Cards (top delivery band!)
- Numbered cards (`1/5`, `2/5`...) with **keywords, one stat, and transition prompts ONLY** (never full paragraphs!).
- Frees eyes to sweep three zones of the room (`left -> centre -> right`).
- Sounds natural, conversational, and authoritative even if exact phrasing varies slightly.
```

### The 3-part speech architecture that holds attention
1. **Hook + Credibility + Core Message (30 sec):** Start with a vivid real-world scenario, sharp question, or startling fact — NEVER `"Good morning, today my topic is..."`! Greet briefly, then state your core thesis cleanly.
2. **2–3 Signposted Pillars (2.5–3 min):** Use verbal signposts (`"First, consider the hidden cost..."`, `"Yet data shows an unexpected twist..."`) and blend **1 real statistic/source** with **1 human example** per point.
3. **Call-back Conclusion (30 sec):** Echo your opening image or question, deliver one decisive takeaway sentence, pause for one full beat, and end clearly (`"Thank you."`).

## Task 3: Unprepared Speech (impromptu thinking in 2–5 minutes prep)

In an Unprepared Speech, you draw a prompt (e.g. a quote, image, or topical statement) and get **short prep time** before speaking for **2–3 minutes**. When nerves hit, use the **PREP + Past/Present/Future** rescue scaffold:

```steps
title: Fast 60-second PREP framework for Unprepared Speaking
step: **P — Point:** State your immediate, clear take on the prompt right away (*"True leadership starts when nobody is watching."*).
step: **R — Reason:** Give 1–2 logical reasons *why* that holds true (`"First, unseen habits shape public crisis choices..."`).
step: **E — Example:** Anchor it in **one concrete personal, school, or South African example** (a sports captain cleaning up after teammates, a community volunteer).
step: **P — Point (Restate + Expand):** Zoom out slightly (*"Whether on a netball court or in Parliament..."*) and finish cleanly with one strong closing line.
```

## Worked example: Designing a Cue Card (not a mini-essay!)

**Weak cue card:** *"Good morning ma'am and classmates. Today I am going to talk about why load-shedding hurts small township bakeries and salons. According to a 2024 report, generators cost..."* (Too dense — forces reading!)

**Strong Cue Card #1 (Hook & Thesis):**
> **[CARD 1 — HOOK & THESIS]**
> - *05:30 — corner bakery dark; dough spoiling*
> - *Greeting (brief)*
> - **Thesis:** Energy instability hits micro-businesses hardest -> **3 realities**: cost, spoilage, lost jobs.
> - `[PAUSE — look centre]`

```tryit
title: Build an Unprepared Speech PREP outline in 60 seconds
prompt: You draw this unprepared prompt: **"Social media connects phones, not people."**
Write a 4-bullet **PREP outline** (`Point -> Reason -> Example -> Point`) that you could speak from for 2 minutes!
hint: Keep each bullet to **under 12 words** — quick spoken anchors, not written essays!
answer: **Example PREP outline:**
- **Point:** Digital reach multiplies messages, yet often weakens genuine human presence.
- **Reason:** Algorithmic feeds reward quick outrage and filtered perfection over patient listening.
- **Example:** Four friends at one lunch table scrolling separate feeds instead of talking through a tough week.
- **Point:** Real connection begins the moment we put the screen face-down and actually listen.
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Exceeding or falling far short of the **time limit** (practise aloud with a stopwatch twice beforehand!).
> - Repeating `"um"`, `"like"`, `"you know"`, or rocking back and forth nervously — plant both feet shoulder-width apart and **embrace a 1-second silent pause** instead of filler sounds!
> - Submitting research with zero credible references or copying Wikipedia paragraphs verbatim onto cue cards.

## Practice

```questions
Q: During a **Listening Comprehension** test, why should you avoid writing nonstop sentences during the **first** reading? (2)
A: Writing continuously during the first reading distracts you from grasping the **overall argument, tone, and structure**; noting only broad anchor words first lets you target specific answers accurately on the second reading.
---
Q: Why do palm-sized **keyword cue cards** score higher in Prepared Speeches than reading off A4 sheets? (2)
A: Keyword prompts prevent eye-drop and monotone reading, letting the speaker maintain **natural eye contact, responsive vocal variety, and confident body language**.
---
Q: Name the four steps of the **PREP** scaffold for an Unprepared Speech. (4)
A: **P**oint -> **R**eason -> **E**xample -> **P**oint (restated with broader closing insight).
```
