> **What you'll learn:** the **exact exam function** of every punctuation mark (dash, hyphen, parenthesis, colon, semicolon, apostrophe, ellipsis, inverted commas), how to beat common South African spelling & homophone traps, and how **register** shapes word choice and sentence style.
>
> **Prerequisites:** basic clauses and word classes.
>
> **Why this matters:** Paper 1 NEVER just asks *"What is this punctuation mark?"* — it asks **"Explain the function of the dash / italics / inverted commas in line 12."** Knowing exact functional phrasing guarantees easy marks!

## The idea in plain language

Punctuation marks are **traffic signals for readers**: they tell the voice where to pause, where a surprising twist drops, which words team together as one adjective, and whether the writer actually trusts a quoted phrase!

### The Paper 1 Punctuation Functions Table

Memorise the **exact grammatical function** for each mark — especially pairs learners constantly mix up (**hyphen vs dash**, **colon vs semicolon**, **parenthesis**):

| Mark | Visual | Key Paper 1 Exam Function | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Hyphen** | `-` (short, no spaces) | Joins two words into a **compound modifier/noun** before a noun, OR adds a prefix to avoid double vowels. | *a **well-known** poet*, *co-operate*, *twenty-one* |
| **Dash** | `—` (longer, spaced) | **Single dash:** signals an abrupt pause, dramatic twist, or sudden explanation. **Pair of dashes:** acts as **parenthesis** adding extra, non-essential info! | *She opened the door — empty.* / *The play — written in 1960 — still resonates.* |
| **Brackets / Parenthesis** | `( ... )` or `, ... ,` or `— ... —` | Encloses extra explanatory, aside, or background information without breaking the main sentence's grammar. | *Pretoria (South Africa's administrative capital) hosts...* |
| **Colon** | `:` | Introduces a **list**, an **explanation**, a **definition**, or a **direct quotation** (also separates speaker from dialogue in drama!). | *Bring three items: a pen, ruler and ID.* |
| **Semicolon** | `;` | Joins **two closely related independent clauses** without a conjunction (fixing a comma splice!), OR separates long phrases inside a complex list. | *Winter arrived early; heating bills soared.* |
| **Inverted commas** | `'...'` or `"..."` | Marks **direct speech**, **titles**, **foreign/slang words**, OR **scare quotes** showing **irony, doubt, or sarcasm**! | *Officials promised `"world-class"` service.* |
| **Ellipsis** | `...` | Shows **omitted words** inside a quote, a **trailing off** thought, hesitation, or **suspense** leaving the reader to imagine the rest. | *If only she had checked the lock...* |
| **Italics** | *abc* | Shows **emphasis**, **titles** of books/films/plays, **foreign words**, or a character's **inner thoughts**. | *The Grapes of Wrath*, *ubuntu*, *That* was the real reason. |

## Apostrophes, homophones and spelling traps

Two strict jobs ONLY for the **apostrophe (`'`)**:
1. **Contraction / omission:** shows missing letters (*`don't`* = do not, *`they're`* = they are, *`you're`* = you are). **Never use contractions in formal Paper 3 letters or Paper 2 literary essays!**
2. **Possession (ownership):**
   - Singular owner $\to$ add **`'s`** (*the girl's book*, *Chris's bag* or *James's hat*).
   - Plural ending in `-s` $\to$ add **`'`** AFTER the `s` (*the learners' desks*, *the ladies' room*).
   - Irregular plural NOT ending in `-s` $\to$ add **`'s`** (*the children's toys*, *men's shoes*).
   - **ZERO apostrophes on plural nouns** (*"2000's"* or *"CD's"* or *"apple's for sale"* $\to$ WRONG! Write `apples`, `2000s`, `CDs`)!

### High-frequency homophone & confusable pairs

- **`affect` (verb)** = influence (*Stress affects sleep.*) vs **`effect` (noun)** = result (*The effect was positive.*)
- **`practice` (noun)** vs **`practise` (verb)** (*More **practice** helps; **practise** daily.*) — same for **`advice` (noun) / `advise` (verb)** and **`licence` (noun) / `license` (verb)**!
- **`stationary`** = standing still (car) vs **`stationery`** = pens and paper (`e` for envelopes!).
- **`lose`** = misplace/fail vs **`loose`** = not tight.
- **`complement`** = complete nicely vs **`compliment`** = praise.
- **`principal`** = school head / main (`pal` = person!) vs **`principle`** = moral rule (`rule` ends in `-le`!).

## Register and style

**Register** is the level of formality suited to your **audience, purpose, and context**:
- **Formal:** objective, third-person, full sentences, zero slang or contractions, precise vocabulary (used in academic essays, business letters, news reports).
- **Informal / Colloquial:** conversational, contractions (`can't`, `won't`), idioms, first/second person (used in friendly letters, diary entries, spoken dialogue).
- **Slang / Jargon:** slang belongs to a specific subculture (`"vibe"`, `"lit"` — avoid in formal writing!); **jargon** is technical vocabulary of a specific profession (`"triage"` in medicine, `"litigation"` in law).

## Worked examples

### Example 1 (explaining punctuation in context)

Read: *"Our recycling programme — launched with great fanfare in 2022 — collected barely 4% of plastic bottles: the bins were locked after hours."*

1. **State the function of the pair of dashes around `"launched with great fanfare in 2022"`. (1 mark)**
   - **Answer:** They act as **parenthesis**, enclosing additional background/ironic information about how loudly the programme was originally promoted without interrupting the main clause.
2. **State the function of the colon (`:`) after `"plastic bottles"`. (1 mark)**
   - **Answer:** It introduces an **explanation / reason** directly clarifying why the collection rate was only 4%.

prompt: Explain the punctuation error in: *"He is a **well known** scientist **–** respected worldwide — who works quietly."*
hint: Check how compound adjectives before a noun are punctuated, and check the pair of dashes!
answer: 1. **`well-known`** needs a **hyphen** because the two words combine into a single compound adjective before `"scientist"`.

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Saying *"The hyphen adds extra information"* when it is actually a **dash**! Remember: **hyphen** joins **words** (`up-to-date`); **dash** separates **clauses/phrases** (`She ran — too late!`).
> - Saying *"The apostrophe shows plural"* — NEVER! Apostrophes show **omission** or **possession**, never ordinary plurals!
> - Writing `"Because it looks nice"` or `"To pause"` for a semicolon or colon — name the exact relationship (**links two related main clauses**, **introduces an explanation/list**).

## Practice

```questions
Q: Correct the **THREE** punctuation/word-choice errors in: *"Its essential that schools' advise learners to check they're sources before submitting."* (3)
A: 1. `Its` $\to$ **`It's`** (contraction of *It is*).
2. `schools'` $\to$ **`schools`** (ordinary plural subject — no possessive!).
3. `they're` $\to$ **`their`** (possessive determiner, not *they are*). *(Note: `advise` is the verb here, so `advise` is correct!)*
---
Q: Give the exact grammatical/stylistic function of the **inverted commas** in: *"Several influencers promote 'miracle' detox teas to teenagers."* (2)
A: They function as **scare quotes / ironic quotation marks** (1), showing the writer's **doubt, sarcasm, or disbelief** that the teas genuinely perform miracles (1).
---
Q: Choose the correct word in brackets:
(a) Heavy traffic had an immediate [affect / effect] on deliveries.
(b) The school [principle / principal] emphasised honesty as a core [principle / principal].
A: (a) **effect** (noun after `"an"`).
(b) **principal** (person), **principle** (moral rule).
```
