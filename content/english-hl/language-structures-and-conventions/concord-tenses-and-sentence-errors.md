> **What you'll learn:** how to solve every tricky **concord (subject–verb agreement)** trap tested in Paper 1 Question 5, how to keep **verb tenses** consistent, and how to spot and fix **comma splices, sentence fragments, dangling/misplaced modifiers, and split infinitives**.
>
> **Prerequisites:** identifying the subject, finite verb, and clause boundaries in a sentence.
>
> **Why this matters:** Paper 1 Section C Question 5 (Language in a prose text — 10 marks) almost always includes an extract packed with deliberate concord traps, comma splices, and misplaced modifiers waiting to be corrected!

## The idea in plain language

Grammar errors happen when different parts of a sentence stop agreeing with each other:
- **Concord (Subject–Verb Agreement):** a **singular subject** takes a **singular verb** (`The girl runs` / `is` / `has`); a **plural subject** takes a **plural verb** (`The girls run` / `are` / `have`). Simple! Where learners trip is when extra prepositional phrases or tricky pronouns wedge themselves between the subject and the verb!
- **Clause boundaries:** every full sentence needs at least one **main clause** (subject + finite verb expressing a complete thought). Joining two full sentences with only a comma creates a **comma splice**; missing a finite verb creates a **fragment**.

## The 6 classic Paper 1 concord traps

1. **Intervening prepositional phrases (`of...`, `with...`, `in...`):**
   - Cross out the phrase! *"A **box** [of heavy textbooks] **is** on the desk."* (`box` = singular $\implies$ `is`, NOT `are`!)
2. **Paired distractions (`as well as`, `along with`, `together with`, `including`):**
   - These do **NOT** create a plural subject! *"The **captain**, along with her teammates, **was** cheered."* (`captain` = singular $\implies$ `was`!)
3. **Indefinite pronouns (`each, every, everyone, everybody, someone, nobody, neither, either`):**
   - **ALWAYS SINGULAR!** *"Everyone in the two classes **has** submitted."* (*"Each of the boys **plays** well."*)
4. **`Either ... or` and `Neither ... nor` (Proximity Rule):**
   - The verb agrees with the subject **CLOSER** to the verb!
   - *"Neither the players nor the **coach was** ready."* (`coach` closer = singular)
   - *"Neither the coach nor the **players were** ready."* (`players` closer = plural)
5. **Collective nouns (`team, class, crowd, committee, herd`):**
   - Acting as **one single unit** $\implies$ **singular** (*"The committee **has** reached a decision."*).
   - Acting as **split individuals** $\implies$ **plural** (*"The jury **are** divided in their opinions."*).
6. **Amounts, time, distance, and titles:**
   - Treated as **one single sum/unit** $\implies$ **singular**! (*"Fifty rand **is** too much."* / *"Two hours **seems** long."* / *"The Chronicles of Narnia **is** popular."*)

## Fixing the top 4 sentence structure errors

```compare
title: Faulty sentence vs Clean Paper 1 correction
left: Faulty constructions
- **Comma splice:** *"We arrived early, the gates were still locked."* (Two main clauses glued by a mere comma!)
- **Fragment:** *"Running frantically toward the bus stop in the rain."* (No finite verb — `-ing` participle alone is not finite!)
- **Misplaced modifier:** *"Walking down the corridor, the broken locker surprised Thabo."* (Suggests the *broken locker* was walking down the corridor!)
- **Incorrect `who` / `whom` or `fewer` / `less`:** *"Less people came."* (Countable people take `fewer`!)
right: Corrected sentences
- Fix with **semicolon**, **full stop**, or **conjunction**: *"We arrived early**;** the gates were..."* OR *"...early**, but** the gates were..."*
- Supply a subject + finite verb: *"She **was** running frantically..."*
- Place the doer right after the comma: *"Walking down the corridor, **Thabo** was surprised by the broken locker."*
- **Fewer** = countable (`fewer learners`); **less** = uncountable (`less water`, `less time`).
```

### Bonus Paper 1 grammar rule: `its` vs `it's` and tense shifts

- **`it's`** = `it is` or `it has`. **`its`** = belonging to it (NO apostrophe — just like `his`, `hers`, `theirs`!).
- **Consistent tense:** do not drift arbitrarily from past tense to present tense inside one narrative sentence unless expressing a universal timeless truth.

## Worked examples

### Example 1 (editing a Question 5 style paragraph)

Correct the **FOUR** grammatical errors in this short paragraph:

> *"Every single one of the finalists are nervous before the debate, however the adjudicators always remain calm. Having prepared for months, the trophy was finally lifted by our school team. Less mistakes were made this year than last season."*

```steps
title: Hunting and fixing all four errors systematically
step: **`"Every single one ... are"` (Concord):** the subject is `"Every single one"` (singular indefinite pronoun, ignore `"of the finalists"`). Change **`are` $\to$ `is`**.
step: **`"...debate, however the..."` (Comma splice):** `"however"` is a conjunctive adverb, NOT a FANBOYS conjunction (`for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so`), so a comma before `"however"` creates a comma splice! Replace the comma before `"however"` with a **semicolon (`; however,`)**.
step: **`"Having prepared for months, the trophy..."` (Dangling/misplaced modifier):** a trophy cannot prepare for months! Fix the clause after the comma to name the real doer: **"...months, our school team finally lifted the trophy."**
step: **`"Less mistakes"` (Countable vs uncountable):** `"mistakes"` can be counted (`1, 2, 3 mistakes`), so change **`Less` $\to$ `Fewer`**.
```

```tryit
title: Quick concord + pronoun test
prompt: Choose the correct word in brackets:
(a) A bouquet of red roses [was / were] placed on the lectern.
(b) Neither the parents nor the principal [supports / support] the curfew change.
(c) Between you and [I / me], that film was overrated.
hint: (a) Cross out *"of red roses"*; (b) match the subject closest to the verb; (c) *"between"* is a preposition taking the **object** pronoun!
answer: - (a) **was** (`bouquet` is singular).
- (b) **supports** (`principal` is closer and singular).
- (c) **me** (`between` takes object pronouns: *between us*, *between you and me* — *"between you and I"* is a famous hypercorrection error!).
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Joining two independent clauses with `, however,` instead of `; however,` — that is one of the sneakiest comma splices tested in Paper 1!
> - Making the verb agree with the *nearest noun* inside a prepositional phrase (*"The **causes** of this crisis **is** complex"* $\to$ wrong! Subject is plural **`causes`**, so verb must be **`are`**!).
> - Using `"could of"` or `"should of"` instead of **`could have`** / **`should have`**.

## Practice

```questions
Q: Identify and correct the error in each sentence:
(a) The sound of barking dogs keep the entire street awake.
(b) Covered in mud, the owner washed the puppy happily.
(c) Ten kilometres are too far to walk before sunrise.
A: (a) Concord error: subject is `"sound"` (singular) $\to$ change `keep` to **`keeps`**.
(b) Misplaced modifier (suggests the *owner* was covered in mud!) $\to$ **`The owner happily washed the puppy, which was covered in mud.`** (or `Covered in mud, the puppy was happily washed...`).
(c) Distance treated as one unit $\to$ change `are` to **`is`**.
---
Q: Correct the punctuation error without turning it into two separate sentences: *"The storm knocked out the tower, therefore nobody received the alert."*
A: Replace the comma before `"therefore"` with a **semicolon**: *"The storm knocked out the tower**;** therefore**,** nobody received the alert."*
---
Q: Rewrite so the split infinitive is avoided if instructed formally: *"Learners must aim to carefully verify every source."*
A: *"Learners must aim **to verify** every source **carefully**."*
```
