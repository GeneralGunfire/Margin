> **What you'll learn:** how to spot and eliminate **redundancy (tautology)**, **ambiguity**, **malapropisms**, **spoonerisms**, **euphemism**, and **portmanteau/neologisms**, and how to complete a full **Paper 1 Question 5 editing passage** accurately.
>
> **Prerequisites:** concord, punctuation, active/passive voice, and diction.
>
> **Why this matters:** Question 5 of Paper 1 (10 marks) packs a wide variety of editing and Critical Language Awareness (CLA) traps into 12 short lines — knowing exact terminology like *tautology*, *malapropism*, and *euphemism* turns tricky 1-mark questions into easy points!

## The idea in plain language

Good editing strips away three enemies of clear English:
1. **Dead weight (`tautology / redundancy`):** saying the exact same thing twice (*"revert back"*, *"true facts"*, *"10 a.m. in the morning"*).
2. **Accidental confusion (`ambiguity` and `malapropism`):** phrasing that can mean two things at once, or accidentally grabbing a similar-sounding wrong word (*"He is the **pinnacle** of virtue"* when you meant *"paragon"*, or *"electric **circus**"* for *"electric circuit"*).
3. **Manipulative sugar-coating or prejudice (`euphemism`, `jargon`, `stereotype`, `loaded diction`):** Critical Language Awareness (CLA) asks you to notice how language hides harsh truths or nudges opinion!

### Key Paper 1 editing & CLA terms to memorise

```definition
title: Redundancy / Tautology
Unnecessary repetition of the same idea using different words in the same phrase (e.g. *"free gift"*, *"enter into the room"*, *"return back home"*, *"past history"*).
```

```definition
title: Ambiguity
A word, phrase, or sentence that can be interpreted in **more than one way**, usually due to vague pronoun reference or misplaced words (e.g. *"Visiting relatives can be exhausting"*).
```

```definition
title: Malapropism vs Spoonerism
A **malapropism** is the accidental misuse of a real word that sounds similar to the intended word (e.g. *"The witness gave an **illiterate** account"* instead of *"inaccurate"* or *"literal"*).
A **spoonerism** swaps the initial consonant sounds of two nearby words, often humorously (e.g. *"blushing crow"* for *"crushing blow"*).
```

```definition
title: Euphemism
Replacing a harsh, blunt, or unpleasant expression with a milder, more polite, or indirect one (e.g. *"downsizing / letting staff go"* instead of *"firing workers"*; *"passed away"* instead of *"died"*).
```

### Word formation terms commonly tested in Question 5

- **Portmanteau (blend):** fusing parts of two words to make a new one (*`brunch`* = breakfast + lunch; *`smog`* = smoke + fog; *`podcast`* = iPod + broadcast).
- **Acronym vs Initialism:** an **acronym** is pronounced as one word (**`AIDS`**, **`NASA`**, **`SANParks`**); an **initialism** is read letter by letter (**`SABC`**, **`DBE`**, **`ATM`**).
- **Neologism:** a newly coined word or phrase entering language (*`doomscrolling`*, *`selfie`*).
- **Literal vs Figurative meaning:** *"He hit the roof"* literally means punching the ceiling; figuratively it means *he exploded in anger*.

## Worked example: Full Question 5 Paper 1 Editing Walkthrough

Read this 5-line passage containing **SIX deliberate Paper 1 errors / language items**:

> *"1. Yesterday, our school's eco-club decided to **collaborate together** on a brand new recycling drive. 2. **'This initiative will bring genuine change,'** the chairperson stated enthusiastically. 3. Every learner and teacher **were** asked to separate plastic from cardboard. 4. Unfortunately, some posters were **illegible** because **of the heavy rain that fell during break, ruining the ink on the boards near the hall**. 5. Wait — why did the caretaker throw the cardboard in the wet **bin?**"*

Let's answer typical Paper 1 editing questions line by line:

```steps
title: Editing Question 5 like a marker
step: **Remove the tautology in sentence 1:** `"collaborate"` already means *work together*! Delete **`together`** (leave `"collaborate"`).
step: **Correct the punctuation of `"brand new"` in sentence 1:** when used directly before a noun as a compound adjective, hyphenate **`brand-new`**.
step: **Correct the concord error in sentence 3:** `"Every"` makes a compound subject singular! Change **`were` $\to$ `was`**.
step: **Convert sentence 2 into reported speech:** *The chairperson stated enthusiastically that **that** initiative **would** bring genuine change.* (Notice `this` $\to$ `that`, `will` $\to$ `would`!)
step: **Identify the root prefix & meaning of `"illegible"` in sentence 4:** prefix **`il-`** (not) + root *legible* (readable) = **cannot be read** (compare *unreadable*).
step: **Comment on the effect of `"Wait —"` (line 5):** conversational interjection + dash interrupts the formal flow to create sudden **surprise / questioning tone**.
```

```tryit
title: Spot the ambiguity and the malapropism
prompt: Fix BOTH sentences below:
(a) **Ambiguity:** *"I saw the astronomer with the telescope."*
(b) **Malapropism:** *"The mayor promised to **eradicate** the ribbon at the new clinic opening."*
hint: (a) Who was holding the telescope — *I* or *the astronomer*? (b) What verb means *"cut/sever"* or *"open officially"*, not *"destroy completely"*?
answer: - (a) Rewrite cleanly depending on intended meaning: **"Using the telescope, I saw the astronomer."** OR **"I saw the astronomer who was holding the telescope."**
- (b) Change **`eradicate`** to **`cut`** (or **`inaugurate`** / **`sever`**)! (`Eradicate` means wipe out completely — a classic malapropism!)
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Replacing *both* words of a tautology poorly instead of deleting the redundant twin (for *"reverse backwards"*, simply write **`reverse`** or **`go backwards`**).
> - Rewriting an entire sentence when the paper asks *"Correct the single error in line 3"* — follow instructions cleanly by writing the corrected word/phrase clearly (or underlining it if writing the whole sentence).
> - Confusing **acronym** (`NATO`, spoken as a word) with **initialism / abbreviation** (`BBC`, `e.g.`).

## Practice

```questions
Q: Remove the tautology / redundancy in each phrase:
(a) *free complimentary ticket*
(b) *end result*
(c) *return back again*
A: (a) **complimentary ticket** (or **free ticket**)
(b) **result** (or **outcome**)
(c) **return**
---
Q: Explain the **ambiguity** in: *"The teacher told the student that she had made a mistake."* (2)
A: The pronoun **`she`** is ambiguous (1): it is unclear whether **the teacher** made the mistake or **the student** made the mistake (1).
---
Q: Identify the **malapropism** and give the correct word: *"Our captain is always **puncture** for team practice."* (2)
A: Malapropism: **`puncture`** (1). Intended word: **`punctual`** (1).
```
