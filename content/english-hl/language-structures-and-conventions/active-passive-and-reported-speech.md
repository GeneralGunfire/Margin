> **What you'll learn:** how to convert any sentence smoothly between **active and passive voice** without losing its exact tense or concord, when writers deliberately use the passive voice, and how to transform **direct speech** into **reported (indirect) speech** with accurate tense backshifting, pronouns, and time/place adverbs.
>
> **Prerequisites:** finite verbs, tense identification (simple, continuous, perfect), and pronouns.
>
> **Why this matters:** Active/passive and direct/indirect speech transformations are staple Paper 1 Question 5 items — and knowing *why* writers choose passive voice (e.g. hiding blame or sounding scientific) also earns marks in Comprehension and Editorial analysis!

## The idea in plain language

### 1. Active vs Passive Voice: spotlighting the doer vs the receiver

- **Active voice:** puts the **doer (agent)** in the spotlight as the grammatical subject:
  > *"The principal **signed** the certificate."* (Direct, lively, clear responsibility.)
- **Passive voice:** flips the **receiver (object)** into the subject spot, changes the verb to **`form of BE + past participle`**, and tucks the doer behind **`by ...`** (or drops the doer completely!):
  > *"The certificate **was signed** (by the principal)."*

**Why use passive voice?**
1. **Scientific / formal reports:** when *what happened* matters more than *who did it* (*"Twenty grams of salt **were dissolved**..."*).
2. **Hiding responsibility / diplomacy:** saying *"Mistakes **were made**"* avoids naming who made them!

### 2. Direct vs Reported (Indirect) Speech: reporting later in another place

When someone speaks in direct quotation marks right now (`"I am leaving tomorrow"`), and you report what they said **afterwards** using a past-tense reporting verb (`She said that...`), four things must shift together so the report stays true:
1. **Pronouns & possessives:** `I` $\to$ `she/he`, `my` $\to$ `her/his`, `we` $\to$ `they`.
2. **Verb tenses ("one step back into the past"):** see table below!
3. **Time & place words:** `now` $\to$ `then`, `here` $\to$ `there`, `this` $\to$ `that`, `today` $\to$ `that day`, `tomorrow` $\to$ `the next/following day`, `yesterday` $\to$ `the previous day / the day before`.
4. **Punctuation:** remove quotation marks, and turn questions (`?`) or commands (`!`) into statements!

## Transformation tables you can rely on

### Active $\to$ Passive verb tense map (NEVER change the tense!)

| Tense | Active example | Passive structure (`BE + V3`) | Passive example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Simple present | *writes / write* | `am / is / are + V3` | *is written / are written* |
| Present continuous | *is writing* | `am / is / are + being + V3` | *is being written* |
| Simple past | *wrote* | `was / were + V3` | *was written* |
| Past continuous | *was writing* | `was / were + being + V3` | *was being written* |
| Present perfect | *has / have written* | `has / have + been + V3` | *has been written* |
| Future (`will`) / Modals | *will / must write* | `will / must + be + V3` | *will be written* |

> **Golden rule for passive voice:** check whether the **NEW subject** is singular or plural! *"The chef **has cooked** the meals"* $\to$ *"The meals **have been cooked** by the chef"* (`has` flips to `have` to match plural `meals`!).

### Direct $\to$ Reported speech tense backshift (when reporting verb is past, e.g. `said` / `asked`)

| Direct speech tense | Reported speech tense | Example (`He said that...`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simple present (`work`) | Simple past (`worked`) | *"I work hard."* $\to$ *he worked hard.* |
| Present continuous (`is working`) | Past continuous (`was working`) | *"She is working."* $\to$ *she was working.* |
| Simple past (`worked`) | **Past perfect (`had worked`)** | *"I worked late."* $\to$ *he **had worked** late.* |
| Present perfect (`has worked`) | **Past perfect (`had worked`)** | *"I have finished."* $\to$ *he **had finished**.* |
| Modals (`will, can, may`) | `would, could, might` | *"I can help."* $\to$ *he **could** help.* |
| Commands / imperatives | `told/ordered + (object) + to + verb` | *"Sit down!"* $\to$ *She told us **to sit down**.* |
| Yes/No questions | `asked + if / whether + statement order` | *"Are you ready?"* $\to$ *He asked **if I was** ready.* |

*(Exception: If reporting a **timeless scientific fact** — e.g. *"The teacher said, 'Water boils at 100 °C'"* — keep present tense: *"...that water **boils** at 100 °C"*! And if the reporting verb is present tense — e.g. `She says that...` — do NOT backshift the verb tense!)*

## Worked examples

### Example 1 (Passive $\to$ Active when the doer is missing)

Rewrite in the **active voice**: *"The broken streetlights have not been repaired yet."*

Wait — where is `by ...`? There is no agent mentioned! When converting an **agentless passive** back to active voice, supply a sensible neutral subject like **`someone`**, **`they`**, or **`the technicians / workers`**:
> **Active:** *"They / Technicians **have not repaired** the broken streetlights yet."* (Present perfect stays present perfect!)

### Example 2 (Reported speech with a question + time shift)

Rewrite in **reported speech**:
`The journalist asked the mayor, "Did you read this audit report yesterday?"`

```steps
title: Transforming a direct question cleanly
step: **Reporting verb & connector:** it is a Yes/No question (`Did...?`), so remove the question mark and use **`if`** or **`whether`** (never `"that if"`!).
step: **Word order:** turn question order (`did you read`) into **statement order** (`subject + verb`).
step: **Backshift tense:** `"Did ... read"` is **simple past** $\to$ shifts back to **past perfect (`had read`)**!
step: **Shift pronouns & demonstratives/time:** `"you"` (the mayor) $\to$ `he/she`; `"this"` $\to$ `that`; `"yesterday"` $\to$ `the previous day / the day before`.
step: **Final reported sentence:** *The journalist asked the mayor **whether he/she had read that audit report the previous day**.* (Ends with a **full stop**, NOT a question mark!)
```

```tryit
title: Spot two subtle errors in one reported-speech sentence
prompt: Direct: *Lindiwe asked her brother, "Where are you putting my keys now?"*
Student wrote: *Lindiwe asked her brother where was he putting her keys now.*
Find **BOTH** errors in the student's answer!
hint: Check (1) statement vs question word order after `where`, and (2) the time adverb `now`.
answer: 1. **Question word order error:** in reported questions, do NOT keep `"was he"` — use normal statement order **`he was`**!
2. **Unshifted time word:** **`now`** must change to **`then`**!
Correct: *Lindiwe asked her brother where **he was** putting her keys **then**.*
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Keeping a **question mark `?`** at the end of a reported question (`He asked where I lived?` $\to$ wrong! Reported questions are **statements** ending with a **full stop**).
> - Leaving **`being`** or **`been`** out of passive transformations (*"The song is sung"* instead of *"The song is **being** sung"* for continuous tense!).
> - Forgetting to backshift **simple past** into **past perfect (`had + V3`)** when the reporting verb is past (`"I saw him"` $\to$ `She said she **had seen** him`).

## Practice

```questions
Q: Rewrite in the **passive voice**: *"Heavy storms are damaging coastal bridges across the province."* (2)
A: *"Coastal bridges across the province **are being damaged** by heavy storms."* (Present continuous `are damaging` $\to$ `are being damaged`; plural concord matches `bridges`!)
---
Q: Rewrite in **direct speech**: *Sipho promised his coach that he would attend training the following morning.* (3)
A: *Sipho said to/told his coach, **"I will attend training tomorrow morning."*** (or *"I promise I will..."* — restore quotation marks, `would` $\to$ `will`, `he` $\to$ `I`, `the following morning` $\to$ `tomorrow morning`.)
---
Q: Why might a newspaper headline write `"Three suspects arrested in dawn raid"` (passive) rather than naming who arrested them? (1)
A: Because the **action and recipients** (`Three suspects arrested`) are the main news focus while the agent (police) is obvious and unnecessary in a concise headline.
```
