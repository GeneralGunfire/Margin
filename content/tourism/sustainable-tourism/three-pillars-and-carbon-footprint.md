> **What you'll learn:** the meaning of **sustainable and responsible tourism**, the **three interdependent pillars (`Planet, People, Profit` — Environmental, Social, Economic)**, **positive and negative tourism impacts** on each pillar, **responsible tourist behaviour** & **good industry environmental practices**, and **global warming and tourism's carbon footprint** (*causes, consequences, and practical ways hotels, airlines, and tourists reduce emissions*)!
>
> **Prerequisites:** Grade 10 environmental awareness and Grade 11 transport/attractions.
>
> **Why this matters:** Sustainability sits at the heart of **Section D (`50 marks`)** and **Term 4 ATP recovery** — exams always test balancing **all three pillars (`not only nature!`)**, giving **specific good business practices** (`greywater, LED/solar, local procurement`), and explaining **carbon footprint reduction**!

## The idea in plain language

Suppose a coastal resort builds 10 luxury bungalows right over rare turtle-nesting dunes, drains local borehole water so village taps run dry, and sends 90% of profits offshore. Within five years, **the dunes erode, turtles vanish, locals protest, and tourists stop coming!**

**Sustainable tourism** means developing and managing tourism **today without destroying the natural environment, local cultures, or community livelihoods needed by future generations**. **Responsible tourism** is the active daily choices made by tourists, guides, and businesses to *create better places to live in and better places to visit*.

## Part 1: The Three Pillars (`Triple Bottom Line`: Planet, People, Profit)

Never define sustainability as *"only saving rhinos and trees"* — true sustainability balances **all three pillars simultaneously**:

| Pillar | Positive Tourism Impacts (`When Managed Well`) | Negative Impacts (`When Mismanaged`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Planet (`Environmental`)** | Funds national parks (`SANParks`) & anti-poaching; rehabilitates ecosystems; educates visitors on conservation. | Litter, plastic waste, coral/dune trampling, excessive water/energy consumption, wildlife harassment, **greenhouse gas emissions (`flights & diesel buses`)**. |
| **2. People (`Social / Cultural`)** | Builds cross-cultural pride; revitalises traditional crafts/music/languages; improves clinics, roads, and clean water shared by residents. | Overcrowding/loss of local privacy; disrespecting sacred sites/dress; **cultural commodification** (*"staged stereotypes"*); rising crime or local inflation pricing residents out. |
| **3. Profit (`Economic`)** | Creates direct/indirect jobs (`guides, chefs, farmers, builders`); earns **foreign exchange**; stimulates small SMMEs via the **multiplier effect**. | **Seasonal unemployment** (*winter lay-offs*); **economic leakage** (*profits/food imported offshore*); over-dependence on tourism when crises hit. |

## Part 2: Responsible Tourist Behaviour & Good Environmental Practices

```compare
title: What Tourists Should Do vs What Tourism Businesses Should Do
left: Responsible Tourist Behaviour
- **Respect nature & wildlife:** Stay on marked trails, **leave no litter**, keep quiet safe distance from animals (*never feed baboons/wildlife*), refuse banned ivory/coral souvenirs.
- **Respect local people & culture:** Ask permission **before photographing residents/children**; dress respectfully near sacred sites; learn basic local greetings.
- **Buy local (`Fair Trade`):** Support local crafts, family eateries, and registered community guides rather than imported mass trinkets.
- **Save water & energy:** Reuse hotel towels (`skip daily sheet washes`), take short showers, switch off AC/lights when leaving the room!
right: Good Business Practices (`Hotels, Lodges & Operators`)
- **Water management:** Low-flow showerheads, dual-flush toilets, rainwater harvesting, **greywater irrigation** for indigenous water-wise gardens!
- **Energy efficiency:** **Solar water geysers & solar PV panels**, LED bulbs, motion-sensor corridor lighting, key-card power cut-offs.
- **Waste reduction (`3 Rs`):** **Reduce, Reuse, Recycle** — replace mini plastic shampoo bottles with refillable dispensers, compost kitchen organics, donate usable linen locally.
- **Fair social & economic practice:** Hire and train **local community staff**, pay fair wages, buy **fresh local produce** (`cutting "food miles"`!).
```

## Part 3: Global Warming & Tourism's Carbon Footprint

What is a **carbon footprint**? It is the **total amount of greenhouse gases (`especially carbon dioxide, CO2`) released into the atmosphere** directly or indirectly by a person, flight, hotel stay, or tour!

```steps
title: Causes, climate consequences & how the tourism industry cuts emissions
step: **1. Main Tourism Carbon Sources:** **Long-haul aviation** (`jet fuel burns huge CO2 at high altitude`), cruise ships burning heavy fuel oil, road coaches/4x4s, and coal-generated hotel electricity (`air-conditioning, laundry heating, heated pools`).
step: **2. Consequences Threatening Tourism Itself:** **Rising sea levels** eroding beachfront resorts & small islands (`Mauritius, Seychelles, Durban`); **coral bleaching** warming oceans; **extreme droughts (`Cape Town water crisis`), wildfires & floods (`KZN`)** closing parks; heatwaves shrinking snow/alpine seasons (`Lesotho`).
step: **3. How Tourism Reduces Its Carbon Footprint:**
- **Transport choices:** Promote domestic rail (**Gautrain**), fuel-efficient hybrid shuttles, direct flights (`fewer takeoffs/landings`), slow-travel itineraries (`fewer flights, longer single stays!`).
- **Clean energy:** Transition lodges/airports to **solar & renewable power** (`green building design`).
- **Carbon offsetting & green certification:** Calculate trip emissions and fund verified reforestation/clean-cookstove projects (`e.g. Fair Trade Tourism / Green Leaf Eco Standard`).
```

## Worked example: Fixing Unsustainable Resort Habits (`Three-Pillar Analysis`)

**Scenario:** *A coastal hotel waters exotic non-native grass lawns at noon with municipal drinking water, imports bottled European water while rejecting local spring suppliers, and fires 60% of cleaning staff every May.*

1. *Identify **ONE impact per pillar** (`Planet, People, Profit`) in this scenario (`6`).*
   - **Planet (`Environmental`):** Wasting scarce municipal drinking water on non-native lawns at hot midday (`high evaporation`) and creating unnecessary **transport carbon emissions** by importing foreign bottled water!
   - **People (`Social`):** Seasonal mass layoffs in May hurt local families' stability and dignity, breeding community resentment toward the resort.
   - **Profit (`Economic`):** Importing European bottled water causes **economic leakage** instead of boosting local suppliers via the multiplier effect!
2. *Give TWO practical fixes (`2`).* -> Replace exotic lawns with **indigenous drought-tolerant plants watered via greywater at dusk**, and **source local filtered/spring water in reusable glass bottles** alongside off-peak domestic deals (`DTGS!`) to retain staff year-round!

```tryit
title: Three Pillars & Carbon Footprint Check
prompt: Classify each action under **Planet**, **People**, or **Profit**, and say why it helps sustainability:
1. A safari lodge stops buying disposable plastic water bottles and gives every guest a reusable stainless flask (`refill stations on site`).
2. A tour operator signs a contract buying fresh vegetables and handmade baskets strictly from women's co-operatives within 20 km of the lodge.
hint: Plastic waste + emission cuts vs local wages/entrepreneurship!
answer: 1. **Planet** (cuts plastic landfill waste and bottling/transport carbon emissions). 2. **Profit & People** (stops economic leakage, grows local livelihoods, and builds dignified community partnership).
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Writing vague answers like *"be eco-friendly"* or *"save the planet"* instead of naming **concrete measurable actions** (`greywater recycling, key-card electricity switches, LED lighting, indigenous landscaping, local Fair Trade procurement, solar geysers`).
> - Confusing **People (`social/cultural welfare`)** with **Profit (`economic revenue, jobs, multiplier effect`)** — clearly separate community culture/dignity/health from wages, revenue, and local sourcing!
> - Thinking **airlines** are the *only* part of a holiday's carbon footprint — **hotel coal electricity, diesel transfers, laundry, and imported food miles** all add up too!

## Practice

```questions
Q: Name the **three pillars of sustainable tourism** and give ONE concrete example of a positive impact for each. (6)
A: 1. **Planet (`Environmental`):** Entry fees fund anti-poaching and habitat restoration (`e.g. SANParks`). 2. **People (`Social`):** Respectful heritage tours preserve living crafts and foster cross-cultural pride. 3. **Profit (`Economic`):** Local jobs and local food/craft purchasing circulate tourist spend within the community (`multiplier effect`).
---
Q: Define **carbon footprint** and state TWO practical ways a 3-star hotel in South Africa can lower its carbon footprint. (4)
A: A **carbon footprint** is the total greenhouse gas (`mainly CO2`) emitted directly or indirectly by an activity or business. A hotel can **(1)** install **solar water geysers / solar panels with key-card room switches and LED bulbs**, and **(2)** source **fresh seasonal food from nearby local farms** to cut food-transport emissions (`and use greywater/reusable dispensers`).
---
Q: Give THREE practical rules a **responsible tourist** should follow when visiting a rural cultural community or national park. (3)
A: 1. **Ask permission before photographing** community members (`especially children`). 2. **Stay on marked trails, take all litter out, and never feed wildlife**. 3. **Buy authentic locally made crafts and meals** directly from community entrepreneurs rather than bargaining aggressively down below fair value.
```
