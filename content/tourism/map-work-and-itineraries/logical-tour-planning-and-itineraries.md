## The idea in plain language

An **itinerary** is the master timetable and route map of a holiday — showing **where a tourist is going, how they get there, what they see, where they sleep, and at what time**.

**Logical tour planning** simply means building that timetable so it works smoothly in **real life** rather than only looking neat on paper! You never put a morning hike in Cape Town and an afternoon game drive in Kruger *by car* (`impossible 1 800 km drive!` — unless flying!) — nor do you cram 6 museums into one afternoon without lunch or rest!

## Part 1: Types of Itineraries & Core Concepts

- **Itinerary:** A detailed day-by-day written plan of a tour's route, transport, accommodation, attractions, activities, and timing.
- **Scheduled Tour (`Package / Group Tour`):** Fixed dates, pre-set route, published departure times, and standardised inclusions sold by tour operators (`departs whether 10 or 30 seats fill`).
- **Customised / Tailor-Made Itinerary (`FIT`):** Designed specifically around **one client's/family's** dates, special interests (`birdwatching, wine, accessibility`), and personal budget.
- **Logical Tour Planning:** Sequencing stops geographically in one sensible direction (**no zig-zagging backwards!**) with realistic travel times, activity balance, and rest breaks.

## Part 2: Six Factors Controlling a Logical Itinerary

```steps
title: Six checks every tour planner runs before writing Day 1
step: **1. Geographic sequence & realistic distances:** Check a distance table/map scale (`average comfortable touring speed ~80–100 km/h on highways, slower on mountain passes/gravel roads`). Never schedule more than ~4–5 hours of driving on a sightseeing day!
step: **2. Opening hours & check-in times:** Check museum/park gate hours (`e.g. Kruger gates close at sunset; Robben Island ferries have fixed sailings!`) and hotel **check-in (`~14:00`) / check-out (`~10:00`)** times.
step: **3. Tourist profile, interests & fitness:** Match pace to age/mobility (`young adventure travellers enjoy canopy tours & hikes; retirees prefer scenic stops, shorter walks, and midday breaks`).
step: **4. Balance & pacing (`Avoid "tour burnout"`):** Mix high-energy activities (`hike/safari`) with relaxed meals, scenic drives, or cultural experiences — never schedule 4 heavy activities back-to-back!
step: **5. Budget & accommodation match:** Stick within client spending limits (`e.g. 3-star guesthouses vs 5-star safari lodges; domestic flight vs coach`).
step: **6. Climate & seasonality:** Account for **Cape winter rain (`Jun–Aug`)**, **Kruger dry winter game-viewing (`May–Sep`)**, or **summer afternoon thunderstorms** on outdoor mountain plans.
```

## Part 3: Standard Day-by-Day Itinerary Structure (`Exam & PAT Format`)

Always include **all 7 core columns/items** (`never leave tourists wondering where they sleep at night!`):
1. **Day & Date**
2. **Time** (`realistic 24h blocks`)
3. **Transport** (`flight number, rental car, tour coach, walking transfer`)
4. **Attraction & Activities** (`specific named places + what the tourist does there`)
5. **Meals** (`B = Breakfast, L = Lunch, D = Dinner — clarify included vs own account`)
6. **Overnight Accommodation** (`specific town + establishment name/grade`)
7. **Estimated Budget / Cost Notes** (`when requested by brief`)

## Worked example: Two-Day Garden Route / Cape Peninsula Sample (`Model Exam Layout`)

**Client Brief:** *A young German couple arrives in Cape Town for a 2-day self-drive coastal & heritage nature loop (`Mid-range 3-star budget; loves scenery, penguins, and local food`).*

| Day | Time | Transport | Attractions, Activities & Meals | Accommodation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Day 1** | `08:30` | Collect Group B **rental car** (`unlimited km`) | Depart Cape Town CBD via **Chapman's Peak Drive** (`scenic photo stops`). | — |
| | `10:00` | Self-drive | Explore **Cape Point / Table Mountain National Park** (`lighthouse funicular & coastal walk`). Light lunch at Two Oceans Restaurant (`L`). | — |
| | `14:00` | Self-drive (`20 min`) | Visit **Boulders Beach Penguin Colony** (`Simon's Town boardwalk viewing`). | — |
| | `16:30` | Self-drive (`25 min`) | Check in at **Kalk Bay Harbour Guesthouse (3-star)**; harbour stroll and fish-and-chips supper (`D`). | **Kalk Bay Harbour Guesthouse (`3-star`)** |
| **Day 2** | `08:00` | — | Breakfast (`B`) and check out by `09:00`. | — |
| | `09:30` | Self-drive (`35 min`) | Morning botanical walk at **Kirstenbosch National Botanical Garden** (`Tree Canopy Walkway`). | — |
| | `12:30` | Self-drive (`25 min`) | Lunch (`L`) & Cape Malay cooking/heritage stroll in **Bo-Kaap** (`cultural uniqueness!`). | — |
| | `16:00` | Self-drive (`25 min`) | Return rental car at **Cape Town International Airport** ahead of `19:00` outbound flight. | *(Departing flight)* |

Notice why this itinerary scores full marks:
- **Zero zig-zagging:** City -> Chapman's Peak -> Cape Point -> Simon's Town -> Kalk Bay (`Day 1`) -> Kirstenbosch -> Bo-Kaap -> CPT Airport (`Day 2`) forms one clean geographic loop!
- **Realistic driving times:** Short `20–35 min` legs leave generous time to enjoy each stop!
- **Complete checklist:** Every day specifies times, transport, meals (`B/L/D`), attractions, and overnight accommodation (`except departure evening`).

```tryit
title: Spot the Three Logical Flaws in This Draft Itinerary!
prompt: A learner drafted **Day 1** for a pensioner couple:
*"07:00 Drive 490 km from Johannesburg to Kruger Park. 10:00 Do a 4-hour strenuous mountain hike. 13:00 Drive back 300 km to Pretoria for a wine tasting, then return 300 km to Kruger to sleep."*
Find **three logical errors** that break tour-planning rules!
hint: Check **driving speed/time**, **geographic routing**, and **tourist profile/fatigue**.
answer: 1. **Impossible driving time:** 490 km cannot be driven between `07:00` and `10:00` (`~163 km/h` average!). 2. **Irrational zig-zag routing:** Driving 300 km back to Pretoria for lunch and 300 km back to Kruger wastes 6+ hours on the road. 3. **Poor client fit:** A 4-hour *strenuous* mountain hike right after a 6-hour drive does not suit a pensioner couple!
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Writing vague bullets like *"Day 2: See tourist sights in Durban"* instead of **specific attraction names, logical order, transport mode, meals, and accommodation**!
> - Scheduling **too many kilometres** in one day (`driving 700 km AND visiting three parks on the same day`).
> - Forgetting to state **where the tourists sleep (`Overnight Accommodation`)** at the end of each touring day!

## Practice

```questions
Q: Distinguish between a **scheduled tour** and a **customised itinerary**. (4)
A: A **scheduled tour** follows fixed pre-planned dates, routes, and inclusions set by a tour operator for group bookings. A **customised itinerary** is tailored specifically to an individual client's or private group's chosen dates, pace, interests, and budget.
---
Q: Explain TWO reasons why **opening times and seasonal weather** must be checked before finalising an itinerary. (4)
A: 1. **Opening times:** Museums, ferries, and national park gates close at strict times — arriving late wastes travel time and disappoints clients. 2. **Seasonal weather:** Rainy winter seasons or summer afternoon storms affect safety and enjoyment of outdoor hikes, beaches, or open game drives.
---
Q: List FIVE essential elements that must appear in every **day-by-day tour itinerary**. (5)
A: Any five of: **Day & Date**, **Time schedule**, **Transport mode/details**, **Attractions & planned activities**, **Meals (`B/L/D`)**, **Overnight accommodation**, and **Budget/cost details**.
```
