## The idea in plain language

Think of an international airport like a carefully controlled border city divided into two zones:
- **Landside:** anyone dropping you off, greeting arrivals, checking bags, or visiting public airport shops can enter here without a boarding pass.
- **Airside:** once you pass **security screening** (and **passport control/immigration** for international flights), you enter airside — where only ticketed passengers, crew, and vetted staff reach departure gates, duty-free stores, and aircraft!

## Part 1: Essential Air-Travel Terminology (`CAPS Core`)

| Term | Plain Meaning & Example |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Gateway** | A major airport, seaport, or border post acting as the **main entry and exit point** for a country or province (`e.g. OR Tambo International is South Africa's primary air gateway`). |
| **Inbound flight** | A flight **arriving INTO** South Africa from abroad (`e.g. London -> Johannesburg`), bringing international tourists in. |
| **Outbound flight** | A flight **departing OUT OF** South Africa to another country (`e.g. Johannesburg -> Paris`). |
| **Domestic flight** | A flight taking off and landing **within South Africa's borders** (`e.g. Cape Town -> Durban`). |
| **Regional flight** | A flight connecting South Africa to **neighbouring Southern African / SADC countries** (`e.g. Johannesburg -> Gaborone, Windhoek, Maputo, or Harare`). |
| **Intercontinental / Transcontinental flight** | A flight crossing oceans/continents (`e.g. Johannesburg -> London, Dubai, or New York`). |
| **Short-haul / Medium-haul / Long-haul** | **Short-haul:** typically under ~3 hours (`domestic/nearby regional`); **Medium-haul:** ~3–6 hours (`Nairobi, Mauritius`); **Long-haul:** over ~6 hours (`Europe, Asia, Americas`). |
| **Scheduled vs Chartered flight** | **Scheduled:** operates on a **published timetable open to any ticket buyer** (`fly whether half-full or full`). **Chartered:** hired privately by a tour operator, sports team, or VIP group on **custom dates/routes** (`not sold individually on regular schedules`). |

## Part 2: South Africa's Airports (`ACSA & Key Non-ACSA Gateways`)

**ACSA (`Airports Company South Africa`)** manages South Africa's nine main public commercial airports:

```compare
title: ACSA International Hubs vs National/Domestic & Non-ACSA Gateways
left: Main ACSA International & Regional Airports
- **OR Tambo International (`JNB` — Johannesburg, Gauteng):** Africa's busiest hub and primary intercontinental gateway.
- **Cape Town International (`CPT` — Western Cape):** Second busiest gateway serving Western Cape leisure/conference routes.
- **King Shaka International (`DUR` — La Mercy, KwaZulu-Natal):** Gateway to Durban, beaches, and Drakensberg/Zulu heritage routes.
- *(Additional ACSA regional/national hubs: Chief Dawid Stuurman Intl in Gqeberha, Bram Fischer Intl in Bloemfontein, East London, George, Kimberley, Upington — several also handle seasonal/charter regional traffic.)*
right: Key Non-ACSA Commercial Gateways
- **Lanseria International (`HLA` — northwest of Johannesburg):** Popular privately owned hub for low-cost domestic routes and regional charters.
- **Kruger Mpumalanga International (`MQP` — near Mbombela):** Major safari gateway for Kruger National Park tourists.
- **Polokwane, Hoedspruit (`Eastgate`), Pietermaritzburg & Skukuza:** Provincial/private airports serving niche business and wildlife lodges.
```

## Part 3: Passenger Journey, Airport Zones & Technology (`iTravel`)

```steps
title: Passenger flow from arrival at the terminal to boarding
step: **1. Landside Check-in & Baggage Drop:** Arrive early (**domestic: ~1.5–2 hours prior; international: ~3 hours prior**). Use **self-service kiosks**, **mobile app check-in**, or airline desks to verify ID/passport, weigh checked luggage (**commonly 20–23 kg economy allowance**; excess fees apply above limit), and print a **boarding pass** + bag tag.
step: **2. Security Screening (`Transition to Airside`):** Present boarding pass; scan **carry-on bags** (international rules cap **liquids/gels/aerosols at 100 ml per container** inside a clear 1-litre bag) and walk through metal/body scanners.
step: **3. Immigration / Passport Control (`International departures only`):** Passport stamped or scanned at **biometric e-gates** before entering international duty-free and gates.
step: **4. Departure Lounge & Boarding:** Wait at assigned **gate** (`check flight information screens for gate changes`). Board in groups (`priority/business first, then seat rows`). Aircraft cabin seating includes **window, middle, aisle**, **emergency exit rows** (extra legroom, passengers must be able/willing to assist), **overhead bins**, and **galleys** (crew food-prep zones).
step: **5. Arrival, Customs & Baggage Claim:** Reverse order: passport control (`inbound`) -> **baggage carousel** -> **Customs channels** (**Green Channel:** *"Nothing to Declare"*; **Red Channel:** *"Goods to Declare"* such as taxable imports, restricted food/plants, or large cash amounts).
```

- **Modern Airport Technology (`iTravel`):** Digital e-tickets on smartphones, QR mobile boarding passes, automated bag-drop kiosks, **RFID baggage tracking** alerts, biometric facial boarding, and airport Wi-Fi apps cut queues and reduce lost luggage.

## Worked example: Reading a 24-Hour Airline Timetable

Suppose a tourism timetable lists flight **SA 323** (`Johannesburg JNB -> Cape Town CPT`) and **SA 408** (`Cape Town CPT -> Gqeberha PLZ`):

| Flight | Route | Days (`1=Mon ... 7=Sun`) | Departs | Arrives | Aircraft |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **SA 323** | JNB -> CPT | `1 2 3 4 5 - 7` | `08:15` | `10:25` | B737-800 |
| **SA 408** | CPT -> PLZ | `1 2 3 4 5 6 7` | `12:00` | `13:15` | A320 |

1. *Does SA 323 operate on Saturdays?* -> Check the `Days` column: `6` is replaced by `-`, so **NO**, SA 323 does **not** fly on Saturdays (`day 6`).
2. *How long is the flight from JNB to CPT?* -> `10:25 - 08:15` = **2 hours 10 minutes**.
3. *How much layover/connecting time does a Monday passenger have in Cape Town?* -> Arrives `10:25`, next flight departs `12:00`: **1 hour 35 minutes** (`95 minutes` — enough for a domestic connection on one ticket!).
4. *When should an economy international passenger arrive at JNB for a `19:40` flight to London?* -> **3 hours early = `16:40`**.

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Mixing up **landside** (public side before security) and **airside** (secure side after security/passport control).
> - Confusing **inbound** (`flying INTO South Africa`) with **outbound** (`flying OUT OF South Africa`), or calling a flight from Johannesburg to Windhoek **"domestic"** — crossing an international border inside SADC makes it a **regional flight**!
> - Reading `16:30` minus `14:45` as `1.85` hours instead of **1 hour 45 minutes** (`remember 60 minutes per hour!`).

## Practice

```questions
Q: Distinguish between a **scheduled flight** and a **chartered flight**, giving one tourism scenario suited to a charter. (4)
A: A **scheduled flight** operates on fixed published routes and times with seats sold to the general public (`whether full or not`). A **chartered flight** is privately hired for a specific group, route, and time (e.g. flying a corporate incentive group or sports team directly to a remote safari lodge airstrip).
---
Q: Name South Africa's **three busiest ACSA international airports** and explain the term **gateway**. (4)
A: **OR Tambo International (Johannesburg)**, **Cape Town International**, and **King Shaka International (Durban)**. A **gateway** is the primary airport, harbour, or border post through which tourists enter or exit a country or tourism region.
---
Q: State TWO functions of **airport technology (`iTravel`)** that speed up passenger processing before boarding. (2)
A: 1. **Online / mobile app check-in and QR boarding passes** let travellers skip desk queues. 2. **Self-service bag-drop kiosks and biometric e-gates** verify identity and print luggage tags rapidly.
```
