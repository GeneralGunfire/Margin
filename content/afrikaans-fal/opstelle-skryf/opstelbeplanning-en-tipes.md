> **What you'll learn:** the **DBE Grade 11 Afrikaans FAL Essay (`250–300 woorde`, `50 marks`)** structure, how **Inhoud en Beplanning (30)**, **Taal, Styl en Redigering (15)**, and **Struktuur (5)** are marked, and how to plan & write **Verhalende** (Narrative), **Beskrywende** (Descriptive), **Beredeneerde** (Argumentative/Discursive), and **Bespiegelende** (Reflective) essays using clean **STOMPI** Afrikaans!
>
> **Prerequisites:** STOMPI word order, past/present tense consistency, and Groep 1/2/3 conjunctions.
>
> **Why this matters:** In Afrikaans FAL Vraestel 3, the essay alone is worth **50 out of 100 marks** — and the word count is ONLY **250–300 words**! Short, clean, well-planned Afrikaans sentences beat long translated English sentences every single time.

## The idea in plain language

The biggest mistake FAL learners make in an Afrikaans essay is **thinking up a complicated English sentence first and translating it word-for-word** (*"Since the dawn of my teenage existence, I have always pondered..."*). That produces broken word order and heavy grammar penalties!

Instead, think **simple, vivid, idiomatic Afrikaans**:
- **250–300 words** is about **4 to 5 well-built paragraphs** (~55–65 words per paragraph).
- Keep sentences short-to-medium (`10–16 words`), mix in **2–3 clean Groep 2/3 conjunctions** (`daarom`, `omdat`, `alhoewel`, `toe`) and **one or two natural intensiewe vorme / idiome**, and **stick strictly to ONE tense**!

### How the 50 marks are awarded (KABV EAT/FAL Rubric)
- **Inhoud en Beplanning (30 marks):** Staying strictly on the prompt, original ideas, developed plot/detail, and a clear **beplanningsdiagram (kopkaart / raamwerk)**!
- **Taal, Styl en Redigering (15 marks):** Accurate STOMPI word order, correct tense (`het ... ge-` vs present), proper spelling/skryftekens, rich natural vocabulary.
- **Struktuur (5 marks):** Catchy introduction (`inleiding`), logical paragraph links (`paragrafering`), satisfying conclusion (`slot`).

## Step-by-step Essay Planning (10 minutes before drafting!)

```steps
title: Die 5-stap Skryfproses vir 'n 250–300-woord Opstel
step: **1. Kies jou onderwerp slim:** Read ALL prompts (including **visuele prikkels / foto's**). Choose the topic where you ALREADY know the core Afrikaans vocabulary (`skool, familie, sport, natuur, vriendskap`) — NEVER pick a topic whose nouns you don't know!
step: **2. Teken 'n vinnige Beplanning (Kopkaart / Raamwerk):** Label it **`Beplanning`** neatly on top, plan 4–5 paragraph bullets (Inleiding -> Liggaam 1 -> Liggaam 2 -> Liggaam 3 -> Slot), and cross it neatly with one line after drafting.
step: **3. Skryf die Konsep (`250–300 woorde`):** Focus on **one small scene or clear opinion**, check **`v1` in Posisie 2** on every line, and avoid switching tenses halfway!
step: **4. Redigeer STOMPI, Tye & Skryftekens (5–7 min):** Hunt specifically for: (a) missing **`het`** or **`ge-`** in past tense, (b) `'n` wrongly capitalised (`'N`), (c) English words (`so`, `because`, `like`) accidentally slipped in, (d) missing **`nie`** at the end of negatives!
step: **5. Tel jou woorde:** Stay strictly within **250–300 words** (write exact count below if required).
```

## Comparing the 4 Main Afrikaans FAL Essay Types

| Opstelsoort | Goal & Focus | Best Tense & Key Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Verhalend** (*Narrative*) | Tell one tight, believable story with a turning point. | Usually **Verlede tyd** (`het ... ge-`). Max 2–3 characters, small timeframe, strong hook (`In medias res`), short realistic dialogue lines (`"..."`). |
| **2. Beskrywend** (*Descriptive*) | Paint a vivid atmosphere (a storm, market, quiet morning) using **5 senses**. | Often **Teenwoordige tyd**! Use strong adjectives, **intensiewe vorme** (*pikswart, yskoud*), and **beeldspraak** (*soos / personifikasie*). |
| **3. Beredeneerd** (*Argumentative / Discursive*) | Argue clearly **for/against** a view (`Selfone moet verbied word`) or weigh both sides fairly. | **Teenwoordige tyd**. Use crisp signposts: *Eerstens, Daarenteen, Gevolglik, Ten slotte*. Avoid slang or insults! |
| **4. Bespiegelend** (*Reflective*) | Share honest personal growth/memory around a theme (`'n Besluit wat my verander het`). | Blend **Past memory** -> **Present reflection** cleanly in separate paragraphs (never jump randomly!). |

```compare
title: Weak "English-translated" opening vs Clean Idiomatic Afrikaans
left: Literal translation trap (breaks STOMPI!)
- *"In my opinion I think technology is making teenagers to be very lazy because they are always sitting on their phones."*
- Clumsy literal English (`making ... to be`, `sitting on their phones`).
right: Clean Grade 11 FAL Afrikaans (natural & high-scoring!)
- *"Tegnologie bied talle voordele, maar oormatige skermtyd steel dikwels ons konsentrasie. Jongmense spandeer ure op sosiale media en vergeet hoe waardevol persoonlike gesprekke is."*
- Short, natural STOMPI sentences, vivid verb (`steel`), clear contrast (`maar`).
```

## Worked example: Turning a Visual Prompt into a 4-Paragraph Plan

**Visual Prompt:** *A photograph shows an old pair of dusty running shoes hanging over a wooden chair next to a bright trophy.*

- **Trap:** Writing *"In hierdie prent sien ek ou skoene..."* -> **0 creativity, loses Content marks!**
- **Strong Plan (Verhalend/Bespiegelend, Verlede tyd, ~275 woorde):**
  - **Paragraaf 1 (Inleiding — Hook):** Describe the quiet bedroom and the contrast between the muddy, torn takkies and the shiny trophy on the shelf.
  - **Paragraaf 2 (Agtergrond):** Briefly tell how you nearly quit athletics after coming last in Grade 9 (`het gedink ek is te stadig`).
  - **Paragraaf 3 (Klimaks):** One rainy Friday morning (`Gister? Nee: Daardie reënerige Vrydagoggend`), lacing up those worn shoes anyway and finishing the 1500 m race despite cramps.
  - **Paragraaf 4 (Slot — Echo):** Returning to the worn shoes — explaining why you refuse to throw them away, because every scratch taught you perseverance (`deursettingsvermoë`).

```tryit
title: Spot & fix 4 typical FAL essay mistakes in one short paragraph
prompt: Read this learner paragraph (Past tense!) and correct **4 errors**:
> `'N Ou man het stadig in die straat gestap. Hy was baie moeg, omdat hy het die hele dag gewerk. Hy sien skielik 'n verlore beursie en hy tel dit op. Hy het besluit om dit nie teruggee nie? Nee: hy het dit eerlik polisie toe gebring.`
*(Fix the 4 marked bugs: `'N`, `omdat hy het ... gewerk`, tense shift `sien / tel`, and `teruggee` with `om ... te` if used!)*
hint: Check `'n` capitalisation, Groep 3 `omdat` verb order, past tense consistency (`het gesien / het opgetel`), and separable verb `terug + gee`!
answer: **`'n Ou man`** het stadig in die straat gestap. Hy was baie moeg, omdat hy die hele dag **`gewerk het`**. Hy **`het`** skielik 'n verlore beursie **`gesien`** en dit **`opgetel`**. *(En as `om ... te` gebruik word: om dit **terug te gee**!)*
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - **Switching tenses accidentally** from *het gestap* (Past) to *hy kyk om hom rond* (Present) inside the same narrative scene — pick ONE main tense and proofread every verb!
> - **Preaching in the last line** (*"So ons almal moet leer om hard te werk in die lewe"*) — end with a quiet personal image or decisive concluding statement instead.
> - Skipping **Beplanning** — even a neat 5-bullet outline shows planning and keeps you strictly within **250–300 words**!

Q: Hoeveel punte uit 50 word aan **Inhoud en Beplanning** toegeken in Vraestel 3 Afdeling A, en hoekom is 'n goeie onderwerpkeuse so belangrik? (2)
A: **30 uit 50 punte**! As jy 'n onderwerp kies waarvoor jy die kern Afrikaanse woordeskat ken, bly jou idees oorspronklik, ryk en direk op die onderwerp gerig sonder lomp Engelse vertalings.
