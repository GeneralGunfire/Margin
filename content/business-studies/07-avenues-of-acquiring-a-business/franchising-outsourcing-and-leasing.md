> **What you'll learn:** the three main **CAPS avenues of acquiring or operating business rights without starting from scratch** (**Franchising, Outsourcing, and Leasing**), the **advantages and disadvantages** of each for both parties, and **contractual implications** (*initial franchise fees, ongoing royalties, service-level agreements, lease maintenance & ownership rules*) (*CAPS Business Ventures · Paper 2*).
>
> **Prerequisites:** Chapter 6 (`forms of ownership and start-up risks`).
>
> **Why this matters:** Paper 2 Section B & C love comparing **Franchising vs Outsourcing vs Leasing** — especially explaining why **royalties** can eat franchise profits, why **outsourcing non-core functions** (`cleaning, IT, payroll`) saves management focus, and why **leasing equipment/vehicles** frees working capital!

## The idea in plain language

Building an unknown brand, buying R3 million of bakery ovens, and hiring IT engineers from scratch takes years and huge cash!
Smart entrepreneurs accelerate growth using **three proven acquisition/access avenues**:
1. **Franchising:** Buying a tested **"business-in-a-box"** brand (`e.g. Nando's, Mugg & Bean, Sorbet`) from a **Franchisor**.
2. **Outsourcing:** Contracting an **outside specialist company** to perform a non-core task (`security, payroll, delivery couriers, IT support`).
3. **Leasing:** Paying monthly rentals to use **expensive premises, vehicles, or machinery** (`lessor -> lessee`) without buying them upfront!

## Part 1: Franchising (`Franchisor vs Franchisee`)

- **Franchisor:** The owner who developed the original successful brand, recipes, and operating manual (`receives fees + expands fast`).
- **Franchisee:** The entrepreneur who buys a licence to open a branch under the franchisor's brand and system.

```compare
title: Franchising — Advantages vs Disadvantages (Franchisee Focus)
left: Advantages for the Franchisee
- **Immediate brand recognition:** Customers already trust the name, logo, and menu from Day 1 (`far lower failure risk than an unknown start-up!`).
- **Proven systems, training & ongoing support:** Staff training manuals, national advertising (`ATL`), and bulk-purchasing discounts with vetted suppliers.
- **Easier bank finance:** Commercial banks lend more readily to reputable franchises than unverified start-ups.
right: Disadvantages & Contractual Implications
- **High initial franchise fee + ongoing royalties:** Pay a steep upfront fee AND a monthly percentage of **gross turnover/sales (`royalties` + marketing levy)** even in slow months!
- **Strict lack of independence:** Cannot alter prices, décor, menu recipes, or suppliers without **written franchisor permission**!
- **Reputational ripple risk:** If one badly run branch overseas gets bad press, your branch loses customers too (`plus strict renewal/exit penalties in contract`).
```

## Part 2: Outsourcing (`Contracting Non-Core Functions`)

Why does a Cape Town law firm hire an external cleaning/IT company instead of employing full-time plumbers, cleaners, and network technicians?

| Aspect | Explanation (`CAPS Core`) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Core Purpose** | Handing over non-core activities (`cleaning, security, payroll, logistics, call centres`) to specialist external contractors so staff focus 100% on the business's main product! |
| **Advantages** | Cuts permanent HR/training admin & equipment costs; gives immediate access to **expert technology and skills** (`e.g. cyber-security specialists`); converts fixed salaries into flexible contracts. |
| **Disadvantages & Risks** | **Loss of direct quality control** over external workers; risk of **leaking confidential company data/IP**; contractor strikes or sudden contract price hikes disrupt your operations! |
| **Contractual Implications** | Requires a strict **Service-Level Agreement (`SLA`)** specifying quality targets, confidentiality clauses, termination notice, and clear compliance with labour laws (`no disguising exploitation!`). |

## Part 3: Leasing (`Lessor -> Lessee`)

- **Operating Lease vs Financial Lease:** Paying agreed instalments to use **premises (`retail shop`), delivery vans, printers, or factory machinery** (`Lessor = owner; Lessee = business using the asset`).

```steps
title: Leasing advantages, disadvantages & contractual implications
step: **1. Preserves cash & working capital (`Advantage`):** Avoids locking huge lump sums into depreciating machinery or property deposits — keeps cash free for stock and wages!
step: **2. Upgrades & tax deductibility (`Advantage`):** Allows swapping outdated laptops/vehicles when leases expire; lease payments are generally **tax-deductible operating expenses** (`lessors often handle major maintenance`).
step: **3. Higher long-term cost & zero ownership (`Disadvantage`):** Total rent paid over 5 years often exceeds cash purchase price, and **the lessee owns nothing at the end** (`unless an option-to-buy exists`)!
step: **4. Contractual rules:** Contracts enforce **strict monthly payments** (`even if business slows`), mileage/usage caps, insurance responsibilities, and **heavy early-termination penalties** if you exit early.
```

## Worked example: Choosing Between Franchising, Outsourcing, and Leasing

**Scenario:** *Lerato wants to open a **coffee & gourmet-toast cafe** inside a busy Durban mall (`startup budget: R450 000`). She has zero hospitality branding experience, cannot afford to buy the R4 million shop building or R120 000 commercial espresso systems cash, and worries about managing complicated monthly SARS/payroll accounting.*

Recommend **which avenue fits EACH of her three needs**, giving one contractual implication per choice (`6 marks`):
1. **Branding & Café Blueprint -> Franchising:** Buy an established café franchise (`reduces failure risk via training & brand trust`). *Contractual implication:* She must pay **monthly royalties (% of turnover)** and follow strict menu/supplier rules!
2. **Mall Shop Space & Espresso Machines -> Leasing:** Rent the retail unit and coffee machines (`frees her R450 000 for stock & staff salaries`). *Contractual implication:* Fixed monthly rent obligations and **zero asset ownership** at lease end unless buyout is agreed.
3. **Monthly Payroll & SARS Accounting -> Outsourcing:** Contract a local bookkeeping firm (`ensures accurate tax/PAYE compliance`). *Contractual implication:* Signed **SLA & confidentiality agreement** protecting employee salary records!

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Confusing **Franchising** (`licensing a complete branded business model with royalties`) with **Outsourcing** (`hiring an external contractor to handle one specific task like security/IT`).
> - Saying a lessee **always owns the leased asset automatically** after the contract — **ownership remains with the lessor** unless a separate buyout option is exercised!
> - Ignoring **contractual implications** (`royalties, supplier tie-ins, SLAs, non-compete clauses, early-exit penalties`) when questions ask to *"advise or evaluate"*.

## Practice

```questions
Q: Explain TWO advantages and TWO disadvantages of buying a **franchise** compared to starting an independent business. (8)
A: **Advantages:** (1) Immediate **brand recognition and customer trust** reduces start-up risk; (2) Proven training, operating manuals, and bulk supplier discounts save costly trial-and-error. **Disadvantages:** (1) Steep upfront franchise fees and ongoing **monthly royalties (% of turnover)** reduce profit; (2) **Strict contractual restrictions** limit creativity on pricing, menu/products, and suppliers.
---
Q: Define **outsourcing** and state TWO risks a firm must manage via a **Service-Level Agreement (`SLA`)**. (4)
A: **Outsourcing** means contracting an external specialist business to perform a non-core function (`e.g. IT, security, cleaning, logistics`). Risks managed via an SLA include **(1) inconsistent service quality / missed deadlines** and **(2) breach of confidential client/business data**.
---
Q: Give TWO financial reasons why a fast-growing logistics company prefers **leasing** delivery vans instead of paying cash upfront. (4)
A: 1. **Preserves working capital (`cash flow`)** for fuel, drivers, and expansion instead of tying cash up in vehicles. 2. **Monthly lease payments are predictable tax-deductible operating expenses**, and fleets can be upgraded regularly without resale headaches.
```
