> **What you'll learn:** quick recap of **Grade 10 forms of ownership** (*sole trader, partnership, close corporation, co-operative, private company `(Pty) Ltd`, public company `Ltd`*), the **benefits and challenges of forming a company** over sole traders/partnerships/CCs/co-operatives (**including limited liability, continuity, capital access, and company taxation**), and when each form suits a growing venture (*CAPS Business Ventures · Paper 2*).
>
> **Prerequisites:** Grade 10 forms of ownership basics (`liability, continuity, profit sharing`).
>
> **Why this matters:** Chapter 6 (`601 Ownership Comparison + 602 Forming a Company`) is one of the highest-mark Paper 2 chapters! Exams always ask why a growing partnership or sole trader should convert into a **Private Company `(Pty) Ltd`** (`or Public Company Ltd`), especially highlighting **separate legal personality, limited liability, and corporate tax vs personal tax**!

## The idea in plain language

When you cut grass on weekends, being a **Sole Trader** is cheap and easy.
But the moment you sign R2 million supply contracts, hire 25 staff, and risk lawsuits if a machine hurts someone, **Sole Trader & Partnership unlimited liability becomes terrifying**: if the business fails, creditors can seize **your family house, car, and personal savings**!

Converting into a **Company (`(Pty) Ltd` or `Ltd`)** creates a **brand-new separate legal person** at **CIPC (`Companies Act 71 of 2008`)**: the company owns its own assets, owes its own debts, pays its own tax, and survives even if owners retire or pass away!

## Part 1: Quick Recap of Forms of Ownership (`SA Context`)

| Form of Ownership | Owners & Name Ending | Liability (`Debt Risk!`) | Continuity (`Lifespan`) | Key Note (`SA Law`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sole Trader** | 1 owner (`trading as...`) | **Unlimited** (*owner's personal home/savings at risk!*) | **None** (`ends if owner dies/retires`) | Easy/cheap to start; taxed in owner's personal income bracket. |
| **Partnership** | 2+ partners (`no upper cap under 2008 Act`) | **Unlimited (`jointly & severally`)** | **None** (`dissolves if 1 partner leaves/dies`) | Combined skills/capital, yet **1 partner's bad debt risks EVERY partner's house!** |
| **Close Corporation (`CC`)** | 1–10 members (`CC`) | **Limited** (*separate legal entity*) | **Yes (`perpetual`)** | **No NEW CCs can be registered** since Companies Act 71 of 2008 took effect (`existing CCs continue or convert to (Pty) Ltd`). |
| **Co-operative** | 5+ primary members (`Co-op Ltd`) | **Limited** | **Yes** | Democratic **1 member = 1 vote** (`regardless of shares!`); serves members' shared needs (`farm, sewing, credit co-ops`). |
| **Private Company** | 1+ shareholders (`(Pty) Ltd`) | **Limited** (*shareholders lose only share capital invested!*) | **Yes (`perpetual`)** | **Cannot sell shares to the public** (`share transfers restricted`); min 1 director; NOT forced to publish public financials unless required by MOI/audit rules. |
| **Public Company** | 1+ shareholders (`Ltd`) | **Limited** | **Yes (`perpetual`)** | **May sell shares to the public on the JSE**, raising massive capital; min 3 directors; strict annual **audited financial statements (`AFS`)** & AGM rules! |

## Part 2: Benefits & Challenges of a Company vs Other Forms (`CAPS Core`)

```compare
title: Why Choose a Company ((Pty) Ltd / Ltd) — Benefits vs Challenges
left: Major Benefits of a Company
- **Separate Legal Personality (`Juristic Person`):** The company can sue, be sued, and sign property/bank contracts in its **own name**.
- **Limited Liability:** If the company goes insolvent, shareholders lose only the money paid for shares — **personal homes and cars stay safe** (`unlike Sole Traders & Partnerships!`).
- **Continuity (`Perpetual Existence`):** Shares simply pass to heirs or new buyers; operations continue smoothly without dissolving!
- **Superior Capital Raising:** Easily brings in investors via **selling shares** (`huge scale on JSE for Public Ltd`) and wins larger commercial bank loans.
- **Taxation Benefits (`Important!`):** Companies pay **flat Corporate Income Tax (`currently 27% in SA` — or lower progressive rates if qualifying as a Small Business Corporation `SBC`!)**, whereas high-earning Sole Traders/Partners can hit **45% personal marginal tax**! *(Note: dividends paid out face Dividend Tax too, so check accountant advice!)*
right: Challenges of Running a Company
- **High Formation & Admin Costs:** Registration at CIPC, drafting a tailored **MOI**, accounting/audit fees, and annual CIPC returns cost more than starting a sole trader.
- **Strict Legal Compliance:** Must obey the **Companies Act 71 of 2008**, **SARS corporate filings**, and (for Public `Ltd`) **JSE/King Code transparency** and **audited financial statements**!
- **Less Privacy (`Public Company`):** Public companies must publish financial reports openly so competitors can inspect profit margins.
- **Possible Separation of Ownership & Control:** Shareholders own the firm while directors manage it — **agency conflict** arises if directors prioritise short-term bonuses over long-term value!
- **Double Taxation Risk:** Company profit is taxed (`Corporate Tax`) AND distributed dividends face **Dividends Withholding Tax** (`whereas sole traders pay tax once in personal returns`).
```

## Worked example: Advising a Growing Partnership on Converting to a Company

**Scenario:** *Brothers Sipho and Kabelo run **TechFix Repairs** as a **Partnership** with R1.8 million annual turnover. They want to buy expensive diagnostics gear, attract two angel investors for R600 000 equity, and protect Kabelo's family house from business debt.*

1. *Identify TWO risks of staying a **Partnership** (`4`).* ->
   - **Unlimited liability (`joint & several`):** If TechFix fails to pay a R900 000 debt, creditors can seize **Kabelo's personal family home**!
   - **Lack of continuity:** If either brother resigns or passes away, the partnership legally dissolves immediately.
2. *Recommend the best **new form of ownership** (`1`) and justify with THREE reasons (`6`).* -> **Private Company (`TechFix (Pty) Ltd`)** because:
   1. **Limited liability** shields Kabelo's and Sipho's personal assets from business insolvency.
   2. **Issuing shares privately** lets the two angel investors buy equity smoothly **without exposing company secrets on the JSE** (`unlike a Public Ltd`).
   3. **Perpetual continuity + Corporate/SBC tax efficiency** ensures long-term survival and potentially lower tax rates than high personal marginal tax brackets!

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Writing that *"companies pay NO tax"* or *"directors are always personally liable for company debts"* — companies have **limited liability** (`shareholders protected unless fraud/reckless trading occurs`) and pay **corporate income tax (`27%` / SBC scales)** plus **dividends tax** when profits are distributed!
> - Confusing **Public Company (`Ltd`, public shares, min 3 directors, listed/audited)** with **Private Company (`(Pty) Ltd`, private share transfers, min 1 director)**.

## Practice

```questions
Q: Explain THREE major advantages of forming a **Private Company (`(Pty) Ltd`)** compared to remaining a **Sole Trader**. (6)
A: 1. **Limited liability:** Shareholders' personal homes and savings cannot be seized for ordinary company debts. 2. **Perpetual continuity:** The company continues operating seamlessly even if shareholders retire, sell shares, or die. 3. **Greater access to capital & corporate tax:** Can raise equity from private investors by issuing shares and pays flat corporate/SBC tax rates rather than personal marginal rates up to 45%.
---
Q: Contrast a **Private Company (`(Pty) Ltd`)** and a **Public Company (`Ltd`)** across **(a) public share sales**, **(b) minimum directors**, and **(c) financial disclosure**. (6)
A: *(a)* **Private:** Prohibited from offering shares to the general public (`restricted transfer`). **Public:** Allowed to invite the public to subscribe (`often JSE-listed`). *(b)* **Private:** Minimum **1 director**. **Public:** Minimum **3 directors**. *(c)* **Private:** Financial statements remain private (`audit only if required by public interest score/MOI`). **Public:** Mandatory annual **audited financial statements (`AFS`)** open to public scrutiny.
---
Q: State TWO challenges or disadvantages a business faces after registering as a **Company**. (4)
A: 1. **Higher start-up and ongoing compliance costs** (`MOI drafting, CIPC annual returns, accounting/audit fees`). 2. **Potential double taxation** (`corporate tax on profits plus dividend withholding tax when profits are distributed to shareholders`) and complex governance rules under the Companies Act.
```
