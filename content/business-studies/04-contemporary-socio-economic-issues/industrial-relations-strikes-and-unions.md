## The idea in plain language

When wages fall behind inflation or safety rules break down, conflict erupts between **employers (`management`)** and **employees (`workers`)**.
- In the past (`apartheid era`), black workers lacked legal union recognition and disputes turned violent.
- Today, the **Labour Relations Act (`LRA 66 of 1995`)** gives both workers and bosses clear, democratic **rules of the game**: talk first, mediate via **CCMA / Bargaining Councils**, and follow legal steps before either side uses industrial action!

## Part 1: Strikes vs Go-Slows vs Lockouts (`Do NOT Swap Who Starts It!`)

| Form of Industrial Action | Initiated By | What Actually Happens | Protected vs Unprotected (`LRA`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Strike** | **Employees / Trade union** | Workers **collectively refuse to work** (`down tools completely`) to force employer concessions on wages or conditions. | **Protected:** Follows LRA steps (*deadlock declared -> CCMA conciliation failed -> 48 hours written notice*). Workers **cannot be fairly dismissed** merely for striking (`though "no work, no pay" applies!`). **Unprotected ("wildcat"):** Skips LRA rules -> workers face warnings/dismissal! |
| **Go-Slow (`Work-to-Rule`)** | **Employees** | Workers stay on the clock yet **deliberately work slowly** or follow every minor manual step strictly to delay output (`harder to prove, yet slashes productivity!`). | Usually treated as industrial action / breach of normal performance duties if unprocedural. |
| **Lockout** | **Employer (`Management`)** | The **employer shuts the factory gates and refuses workers entry** (`stopping pay`) until employees accept management's final offer or end a deadlock! | Must also follow LRA conciliation + 48 hours notice to be protected. |

## Part 2: Purpose of the Labour Relations Act (`LRA 66 of 1995`)

```steps
title: Core purposes of South Africa's Labour Relations Act (LRA)
step: **1. Promote economic development, social justice & workplace peace:** Gives effect to Section 23 of the Constitution (`fair labour practices`).
step: **2. Protect freedom of association:** Every worker has the right to form/join a **trade union**; every employer may join an **employers' organisation** without victimisation.
step: **3. Provide a clear framework for collective bargaining:** Establishes **Bargaining Councils** (`sector-wide deals`) and **Workplace Forums** (`joint problem-solving on retrenchments/safety`).
step: **4. Establish fair dispute resolution (`CCMA & Labour Courts`):** Requires conciliation/arbitration before strikes or lockouts, preventing unfair dismissals (`substantive & procedural fairness`)!
```

## Part 3: Trade Unions — Brief History, Roles & Functions

```compare
title: Trade Unions in South Africa — Roots & Workplace Functions
left: Brief History (`SA Context`)
- **Pre-1979:** Early industrial unions excluded African workers from official bargaining until **1979 Wiehahn Commission** recommended African union recognition after the **1973 Durban strikes** and **1976 unrest**!
- **1985 (`COSATU`) & anti-apartheid struggle:** Unions (`NUM, NUMSA, SACCAWU`) fought both workplace exploitation and political apartheid (`calling stay-aways and living-wage campaigns`), shaping the post-1994 **LRA** and **NEDLAC**.
right: Modern Roles & Functions of Trade Unions
- **Collective bargaining:** Negotiate better wages, hours, overtime, bonuses, and safe working conditions on behalf of members (`stronger together than one lonely worker!`).
- **Protect against unfair treatment:** Represent members at **disciplinary hearings & CCMA** against unfair dismissal or discrimination (`EEA`).
- **Educate & upskill members:** Inform workers of labour rights (`BCEA, COIDA, OHS`), health/HIV support, and financial literacy.
- **Advise management & represent labour nationally:** Serve on Workplace Forums and **NEDLAC** (`shaping national economic/labour policy`).
```

## Worked example: Protected vs Unprotected Strike & Lockout Analysis (`Case Study`)

**Scenario:** *Workers at **Kalahari Packaging** discover management cut overtime rates without consulting anyone. Angry assembly-line operators immediately walk off the machines at `10:00` without talking to their union or reporting to the CCMA (`Action A`). Management responds the next morning by locking the main gate until staff sign a pay freeze (`Action B`).*

1. *Was **Action A** a **protected** or **unprotected** strike? Give TWO reasons (`3`).* -> **Unprotected (`wildcat`) strike** — workers did **not** refer the dispute to the **CCMA/Bargaining Council** for conciliation first, and did **not** give **48 hours' written notice**!
2. *Name **Action B** (`1`) and explain why it is ALSO procedural trouble if notice wasn't served (`2`).* -> **Lockout** (initiated by employer). Like a strike, a legal lockout requires prior CCMA referral and 48 hours' notice under the LRA!
3. *Advise workers how a **trade union** should help properly (`2`).* -> Elected shop stewards should lodge an official dispute with the **CCMA** for conciliation/arbitration and negotiate respectfully with management first!

```tryit
title: Industrial Relations Quick Check
prompt: Decide whether each statement is **True** or **False** (`correct false ones!`):
1. Under the **no work, no pay** rule, employers must still pay full daily wages during a protected strike.
2. A **lockout** happens when angry trade union members padlock the manager's office from outside.
3. A **go-slow** keeps workers inside the building while output drops intentionally.
hint: Who locks the gate in a lockout? Does "no work, no pay" pay strikers?
answer: 1. **False** — even during a legal **protected strike**, employers do NOT have to pay wages for days not worked (`though they cannot fire workers merely for striking!`). 2. **False** — a **lockout** is initiated by the **employer** shutting workers out during a dispute. 3. **True**.
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Describing a **lockout** as *"workers locking the boss out"* — a lockout is **ALWAYS initiated by the employer**!
> - Saying *"protected strikes mean workers get paid while striking"* — protected means **protection from unfair dismissal**, NOT exemption from **"no work, no pay"**!
> - Listing **only strikes** when asked about industrial action — remember **go-slows, work-to-rule, picketing, and lockouts**.

## Practice

```questions
Q: Distinguish between a **strike**, a **go-slow**, and a **lockout**. (6)
A: A **strike** is a collective refusal to work initiated by **employees**. A **go-slow** is employees remaining at work while **deliberately working much slower** to reduce productivity. A **lockout** is initiated by the **employer**, who closes the workplace and bars employees from working until a dispute settles.
---
Q: State THREE functions of a **trade union** inside a modern South African company. (6)
A: 1. **Collective bargaining** for fair wages, working hours, and safe working conditions. 2. **Representing workers** in grievance/disciplinary hearings and CCMA disputes against unfair dismissal. 3. **Educating members** about their legal rights (`LRA, BCEA, OHS Act`) and consulting management via Workplace Forums.
---
Q: List the THREE steps required before a **strike** becomes legally **protected** under the **LRA 66 of 1995**. (3)
A: 1. The issue in dispute must be referred to the **CCMA or a Bargaining Council** for conciliation. 2. A **certificate of non-resolution (`deadlock`)** is issued (or 30 days pass unresolved). 3. The union/employees serve **at least 48 hours' written notice** to the employer (`7 days if the state is the employer`).
```
