## The idea in plain language

Put five brilliant matriculants or engineers in one room on Monday morning, and by Wednesday afternoon **arguments usually erupt over who leads, who works hardest, and whose idea wins**!
That friction does NOT automatically mean the team failed — psychologists (**Bruce Tuckman**) proved **almost every healthy team passes through five predictable stages**, and **managed conflict actually makes ideas sharper (`functional conflict`)** when handled fairly!

## Part 1: Five Stages of Team Development (`Tuckman Model`)

```steps
title: Five sequential stages every team passes through
step: **1. Forming (`Polite Orientation`):** Members first meet, act cautiously and politely, explore goals/roles, and look strongly to the leader for clear rules (`excitement + uncertainty`).
step: **2. Storming (`Power Clashes & Friction`):** Real personalities emerge; members argue over **authority, workload fairness, and competing ideas**! *Leadership rule:* Do NOT panic — mediate rules fairly so the team does not get stuck here forever!
step: **3. Norming (`Agreement & Trust`):** Group establishes shared **ground rules (`norms`)**, respects each member's unique strengths, resolves differences calmly, and builds cohesion!
step: **4. Performing (`High-Output Flow`):** Mature, motivated team works smoothly with minimal supervision, shares credit, solves problems creatively, and hits peak goals!
step: **5. Adjourning / Mourning (`Wrap-Up`):** For temporary project teams (`e.g. a festival committee`), tasks conclude, achievements are celebrated, and members disperse (`mixed pride & sadness`).
```

## Part 2: Team Dynamics Theories (`Why Diverse Personalities Win`)

If everyone on a team is a loud big-picture visionary and nobody checks spreadsheets or deadlines, the project crashes! CAPS highlights **team dynamics theories** that assign balanced roles:
- **Belbin Role Theory (`Meredith Belbin`):** Identifies **9 complementary team roles** grouped into **Action-oriented (`Shaper, Implementer, Completer Finisher`)**, **People-oriented (`Coordinator, Teamworker, Resource Investigator`)**, and **Thinking-oriented (`Plant [creative ideas], Monitor Evaluator [objective critic], Specialist`)**. Every team needs *both* creative **"Plants"** and detail-checking **"Completer Finishers"**!
- **Margerison-McCann Team Management Profile:** Maps work preferences (`Advisor, Explorer, Organiser, Controller, Thruster, Upholder, Maintainer, Creator`) anchored by a **Linker** who keeps diverse members cooperating!
- **Jungian Theory (`Personality Preferences`):** Balances **Extraversion vs Introversion**, **Sensing (`facts`) vs Intuition (`big picture`)**, **Thinking (`logic`) vs Feeling (`empathy`)**, and **Judging (`planned`) vs Perceiving (`flexible`)**.
- **Group Consensus:** Reaching decisions everyone can genuinely **support and live with** (`not forced 51% bullying!`) through open listening and shared compromise.

## Part 3: Conflict Causes & Constructive Conflict Resolution

```compare
title: Functional vs Dysfunctional Conflict & Resolution Steps
left: Causes & Types of Conflict
- **Common causes:** Unclear job roles, unfair workload/pay, scarce budgets/resources, clashing personalities, poor communication, **different cultural values**, ignoring deadlines.
- **Functional (`Constructive`) conflict:** Respectful debate over *ideas and methods* that sparks better solutions and exposes hidden flaws!
- **Dysfunctional (`Destructive`) conflict:** Personal insults, sabotage, shouting, silent sulking, and clique gossip that wreck morale and profits.
right: Steps to Manage Conflict Professionally
- **1. Acknowledge conflict early** (`don't pretend it will vanish!`).
- **2. Meet privately in a neutral space** with both parties (`stay calm, separate people from the problem`).
- **3. Let each side speak without interruption** (`active listening`).
- **4. Identify the real root cause** & brainstorm **win–win compromises** (`or clarify roles/workload`).
- **5. Agree on concrete actions + follow-up check** (`if unresolved internally, follow formal company **Grievance Procedure** or external **CCMA conciliation/arbitration**!`).
```

## Worked example: Diagnosing a Team Stage & Resolving Conflict (`Paper 2 Case Study`)

**Scenario:** *Five IT developers at **CloudSA** were grouped last week to build a new banking app. On Day 1 everyone smiled politely (`Phase A`). By Day 5, Thabo and Priya argued loudly because Thabo loves rushing creative prototypes without testing, whereas Priya insists on checking security bugs line-by-line (`Phase B`).*

1. *Name **Phase A** and **Phase B** using Tuckman's stages (`2`).* -> **Phase A = Forming**; **Phase B = Storming**.
2. *Using **Belbin's Team Role theory**, explain why BOTH Thabo and Priya are actually essential to CloudSA (`4`).* -> Thabo acts like a creative **Plant / Resource Investigator** sparking rapid innovation, while Priya acts like a **Monitor Evaluator / Completer Finisher** catching critical security flaws before launch — **differences are complementary strengths, not defects!**
3. *Give THREE conflict-management steps the project manager should take (`3`).* -> Hold a calm private meeting letting both listen without interrupting, define **clear separated role boundaries** (`Thabo prototypes -> Priya runs security QA`), and agree on shared sprint deadlines to guide the team into **Norming**!

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Skipping **Storming** or treating Storming as *"proof the team must be dissolved immediately"* — conflict in **Storming** is **normal** and guides the team into **Norming** if led fairly!
> - Confusing **Grievance (`employee complaining formally about management/unfair treatment`)** with **Disciplinary action (`management addressing employee misconduct`)**.
> - Saying *"conflict is ALWAYS bad in business"* — healthy **functional conflict** prevents "groupthink" and catches terrible decisions early!

## Practice

```questions
Q: List the **five stages of team development (`Tuckman`)** in the correct chronological order and briefly describe **Storming** vs **Norming**. (7)
A: 1. **Forming**, 2. **Storming**, 3. **Norming**, 4. **Performing**, 5. **Adjourning (`Mourning`)**. During **Storming**, members clash over roles, leadership, and working styles. During **Norming**, differences settle into shared ground rules, mutual respect, and unified trust.
---
Q: Explain ONE benefit of applying **Belbin's team role theory** or **Jungian theory** when assembling a project team. (4)
A: Managers deliberately blend **complementary personalities and strengths** (`e.g. creative "Plants" paired with detail-focused "Completer Finishers"` or big-picture thinkers paired with practical organisers) so blind spots are covered and conflict becomes constructive rather than chaotic.
---
Q: Outline FOUR practical steps a manager should follow to resolve **dysfunctional conflict** between two employees. (4)
A: 1. Address the issue promptly in a **private, neutral venue**. 2. Allow each employee to state facts without interruption (`focus on behaviour, not personal insults`). 3. Identify the root cause together and agree on a **mutually acceptable solution / role boundary**. 4. Follow up later to ensure cooperation (`escalating to formal grievance/CCMA only if unresolved`).
```
