> **What you'll learn:** quick recap of the **primary, secondary, and tertiary business sectors**, how enterprises across all three sectors depend on each other via **supply chains (`backward & forward linkages`)**, and how disruption in one sector ripples through the entire chain (*CAPS Business Ventures · Paper 2*).
>
> **Prerequisites:** Grade 10 business sectors and formal vs informal businesses.
>
> **Why this matters:** Chapter 5 may be short in teaching hours (`~1 hour`), yet Paper 2 loves giving a **real South African product (`e.g. a loaf of bread, steel car door, or cotton T-shirt`)** and asking you to **trace its exact forward and backward links across Primary -> Secondary -> Tertiary enterprises**!

## The idea in plain language

Think about the **bottle of orange juice** in a Durban café fridge:
- It started on a **farm** (`Primary sector` extracting raw oranges from nature).
- Next, a **juicing & bottling factory** (`Secondary sector`) squeezed, pasteurised, bottled, and labelled it.
- Finally, **trucking couriers, wholesalers, banks, advertisers, and the café** (`Tertiary sector`) moved, financed, marketed, and sold it to you!

**Not one of those three sectors can survive without the other two!**

## Part 1: The Three Sectors & Their Interdependent Links

| Sector | Core Economic Activity | South African Examples | Links to Other Sectors |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Primary Sector** | Extracts **raw natural resources** directly from Earth, sea, or sky. | Farming (`citrus, maize, timber, dairy`), mining (`gold, coal, iron ore`), forestry, commercial fishing. | **Forward link -> Secondary:** Supplies raw timber, ore, wool, fruit to factories. **Backward reliance <- Secondary & Tertiary:** Needs tractors/fertiliser (`Secondary`) + transport, bank loans, insurance (`Tertiary`)! |
| **2. Secondary Sector** | **Processes & transforms raw materials** (`manufacturing, construction, energy`) into semi-finished or finished goods. | Bakeries, car assembly plants (`Toyota KZN / VW Kariega`), textile mills, paper factories, Eskom power stations, construction firms. | **Backward link <- Primary:** Buys raw inputs. **Forward link -> Tertiary:** Sells finished goods to wholesalers, retailers, exporters (`plus relies on Tertiary logistics & marketing!`). |
| **3. Tertiary Sector** | Provides **services and distribution** (`retail, wholesale, transport, banking, insurance, healthcare, IT, tourism`). | Checkers, Takealot, FNB, Transnet freight, law firms, hair salons, guesthouses. | **Supplies & sells to BOTH Primary & Secondary:** Transports raw ore and finished food, insures factories, provides business loans, advertises, and sells directly to final consumers! |

## Part 2: Forward vs Backward Linkages (`Exam Vocabulary`)

```steps
title: How a single crisis ripples across all three sectors
step: **1. Forward Linkage (`Downstream supply`):** When an earlier sector sends its output **forward** to the next stage (*e.g. a timber plantation supplying wood pulp to a paper mill -> paper mill supplying cardboard boxes to an apple exporter*).
step: **2. Backward Linkage (`Upstream demand`):** When a business buys inputs or services **back** from earlier or supporting sectors (*e.g. a car factory demanding steel from Primary mining, tyres from Secondary rubber plants, and auditing/transport from Tertiary firms*).
step: **3. Ripple Impact of Disruption:** If a drought hits maize farms (**Primary**), milling factories (**Secondary**) run below capacity and lay off shift staff, which forces supermarkets (**Tertiary**) to raise bread prices or import grain!
```

## Worked example: Tracing a Product Through All Three Sectors (`Paper 2 Question`)

**Prompt:** *Trace how **primary, secondary, and tertiary enterprises** work together to bring a **locally made wooden school desk** to learners (`6 marks`), AND show ONE **backward link** from the furniture factory (`2 marks`).*

1. **Primary enterprise:** A **forestry company** (`e.g. in Mpumalanga/KZN`) grows and harvests raw pine trees; meanwhile, iron-ore mines extract raw ore for steel legs.
2. **Secondary enterprise:** A **sawmill & furniture manufacturer** cuts raw logs into planks, welds steel frames, varnishes, and assembles finished desks (`manufacturing`).
3. **Tertiary enterprise:** **Freight trucking companies** deliver desks to school suppliers/warehouses, **banks** process school EFT payments, and **insurance/marketing firms** insure and promote the supplier!
4. **Backward link from the furniture factory (`Secondary -> Tertiary/Primary`):** The factory relies *backward* on **Primary timber growers** for wood AND on **Tertiary electricity/transport/IT service providers** to run its saws and deliver parts!

```tryit
title: Sector & Link Classification Check
prompt: Classify each enterprise as **Primary**, **Secondary**, or **Tertiary**, and explain how the third relies on the other two:
1. **Sishen Iron Ore Mine** (`Northern Cape`)
2. **ArcelorMittal Steel Mill** (`turning iron ore into steel sheets`)
3. **Bidvest Freight Logistics** (`trucking steel sheets to Durban exporters`)
hint: Extracting = Primary; Transforming/Manufacturing = Secondary; Moving/Service = Tertiary.
answer: 1. **Primary**; 2. **Secondary**; 3. **Tertiary** (`Bidvest Freight earns revenue ONLY because Primary mines extract ore and Secondary mills manufacture steel sheets needing delivery!`).
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Calling a **supermarket (`Pick n Pay`)** *Secondary* because it sells food — supermarkets do NOT manufacture goods, they resell and deliver services (**Tertiary**)!
> - Describing only **Primary -> Secondary -> Tertiary** as a one-way street — remember **Primary and Secondary firms ALSO buy Tertiary services** (`banking, legal advice, insurance, fibre internet, logistics`) every day!

## Practice

```questions
Q: Using **cotton clothing**, explain the link between **primary**, **secondary**, and **tertiary** sector enterprises. (6)
A: **Primary:** Cotton farmers grow and harvest raw cotton bolls. **Secondary:** Textile mills spin yarn and garment factories sew cotton into finished T-shirts. **Tertiary:** Freight couriers, wholesalers, clothing retailers, banks, and marketing agencies distribute, finance, advertise, and sell the shirts to consumers.
---
Q: Explain what happens to the **secondary** and **tertiary** sectors if a major **mining or agricultural strike (`Primary sector`)** lasts four weeks. (4)
A: **Secondary manufacturers** suffer raw-material shortages, halting production lines and losing export contracts. **Tertiary freight hauliers, ports, wholesalers, and retail stores** lose transport fees and inventory sales, hurting revenue and jobs across the economy.
---
Q: Give ONE example of how a **Primary farm** depends directly on a **Tertiary business**. (2)
A: A commercial fruit farm depends on a **commercial bank** for seasonal crop loans (`or an insurance firm for hail cover / a refrigerated logistics company to reach export ports`).
```
