## The idea in plain language

Suppose an accountant arrives strictly at `08:00`, wears a tailored suit, formats spreadsheets neatly, and meets every deadline — **yet secretly hides R400 000 of unpaid VAT so the boss saves tax!**
- Is that accountant acting **professionally** on the surface? **Partly yes** (competence, punctuality, polish).
- Is that accountant acting **ethically**? **Absolutely NO!**

**Professionalism** focuses on **how competently, respectfully, and reliably you perform your job standards** (`dress, punctuality, expertise, boundaries`), whereas **Ethics** focuses on **moral right vs wrong** (`honesty, fairness, never harming or cheating people — even when nobody is watching!`).

## Part 1: Professionalism vs Ethics Compared

```compare
title: Professionalism vs Ethics (Exam Distinction!)
left: Professionalism (`How You Work`)
- **Meaning:** Following the **expected standards, conduct, competence, and etiquette** of a profession or workplace.
- **Examples:** Punctuality, neat dress code, respectful email tone, keeping skills updated, protecting client confidentiality, honouring deadlines.
- **Driven by:** Employer policies & professional councils (`SAICA, HPCSA, SACE`).
right: Ethics (`Moral Right vs Wrong`)
- **Meaning:** Making decisions guided by **moral principles of honesty, integrity, fairness, and respect for human dignity & the law**.
- **Examples:** Refusing bribes/kickbacks, truthful advertising, fair hiring without nepotism, paying correct taxes, protecting environment/whistleblowers.
- **Driven by:** Conscience, **Code of Ethics**, law, and **King Code governance**.
```

## Part 2: Ethical Theories & Core Principles (`CAPS`)

| Ethical Principle / Theory | Plain Meaning in Business | Practical Workplace Test |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Utilitarianism / Consequence focus** | Choosing actions creating the **greatest net good / least harm** for all stakeholders (`customers, staff, community`), not just owners! | Recalling a faulty brake batch even at short-term loss to save drivers' lives. |
| **Duty / Principle Ethics (`Deontology`)** | Some acts (`lying, stealing, bribery`) are **intrinsically wrong regardless of profit** — obey honest universal rules. | Never falsifying safety test scores even if a big client demands speed. |
| **Integrity & Honesty** | Alignment between what leaders promise and actually do (`no secret price-fixing or fake invoices`). | Billing clients strictly for hours/parts actually delivered. |
| **Avoiding Conflict of Interest** | Never putting personal/family gain ahead of company duty (`disclose relationships upfront and step out of voting!`). | A procurement manager refusing to award a tender secretly to her brother's catering firm! |
| **Confidentiality & Privacy (`POPIA`)** | Never leaking or selling customer ID numbers, salaries, or trade secrets to outsiders. | Locking HR folders and encrypting digital customer databases. |

## Part 3: King Code of Corporate Governance (`King III 2009 / King IV`)

*(Note: CAPS names **King Code 2009 / King III**, while modern South African governance also applies updated **King IV (`2016`)** outcomes — both anchor ethical, effective board leadership!)*

```steps
title: Core pillars of King Code governance applied in business
step: **1. Transparency:** Disclose financial results, risks, bonuses, and decisions **honestly and clearly** to shareholders, workers, and regulators (`no hidden offshore accounts!`).
step: **2. Accountability:** Directors and managers **answer openly** for their choices, profits, AND mistakes — never scapegoating junior clerks!
step: **3. Responsibility:** Take **corrective action** immediately when operations harm employees, communities, or the environment (`Triple Bottom Line: People, Planet, Profit`).
step: **4. Fairness:** Respect the legitimate rights of **all stakeholders** (`minority shareholders, customers, workers, suppliers, communities`) without favouritism or exploitation.
step: **5. Ethical Leadership & Codes of Conduct:** Boards must actively model integrity, set an enforceable **Code of Ethics**, protect **whistleblowers**, and integrate sustainability into strategy!
```

## Worked example: Spotting Breaches & Applying King Code (`Paper 2 Case Study`)

**Scenario:** *At **MetroBuild Ltd**: **(1)** The procurement officer secretly awards a R2 million paint tender to his cousin's overpriced business without declaring their family link; **(2)** Two sales managers regularly arrive 40 minutes late and snap rudely at callers; **(3)** The CEO tells auditors to hide factory river-pollution fines from the annual report.*

1. *Classify `(1)`, `(2)`, and `(3)` as **unethical**, **unprofessional**, or **both**, giving reasons (`6`).*
   - **(1) Unethical (`and unprofessional`) — Conflict of interest / nepotism:** Uses company trust for private family enrichment at the firm's expense.
   - **(2) Unprofessional:** Lateness and rude customer tone violate workplace standards of courtesy and punctuality (`though not theft`).
   - **(3) Unethical (`violates King Code Transparency & Responsibility`):** Concealing environmental fines misleads shareholders and hides ecological harm!
2. *Explain how **King Code (`Accountability & Transparency`)** fixes `(3)` (`2`).* -> Directors must publish **honest integrated reports** (`Transparency`), answer for environmental damage (`Accountability`), and fund immediate filtration fixes (`Responsibility`).

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Defining **professionalism** and **ethics** as *"both mean behaving nicely"* — always distinguish **workplace competence/conduct (`professionalism`)** from **moral right/wrong (`ethics`)**!
> - Naming only *"honesty"* for the King Code instead of the **exact governance principles** (**Transparency, Accountability, Responsibility, Fairness**).
> - Recommending firing whistleblowers — ethical King Code governance **protects confidential whistleblowers** who report misconduct in good faith!

## Practice

```questions
Q: Distinguish clearly between **professionalism** and **ethics** in the workplace, giving ONE example of each. (4)
A: **Professionalism** refers to adhering to high standards of competence, punctuality, appearance, and respectful conduct in a job (e.g. answering client emails promptly and courteously). **Ethics** refers to moral principles of right and wrong guiding honest, fair behaviour (e.g. refusing a supplier's cash kickback and reporting fraud).
---
Q: Define **conflict of interest** in a business context and explain how a manager should handle one ethically. (4)
A: A **conflict of interest** arises when an employee's personal, family, or financial interests clash with their duty to act in the employer's best interests. Ethically, the manager must **declare the relationship openly in writing** and **recuse themselves (`step aside`)** from any tender or hiring decision involving that party.
---
Q: Explain THREE pillars of the **King Code of Governance** and state how each prevents corporate scandals. (6)
A: 1. **Transparency:** Forces honest, clear financial and operational reporting so fraud cannot hide. 2. **Accountability:** Requires directors to justify decisions and accept consequences for failures. 3. **Responsibility & Fairness:** Ensures boards balance profit with fair treatment of workers, suppliers, communities, and the environment.
```
