>
> **Why this matters:** Remember `past-papers-history-business-studies.md`! **Creative thinking and problem solving** is regularly examined as a **40-mark Paper 2 Section C essay** — requiring you to **compare problem-solving vs decision-making**, apply **Delphi vs Force-Field**, and explain **how managers break mental blocks**!

## The idea in plain language

When sales drop 15%:
- **Routine thinking** repeats yesterday's habit (*"Just put up the same 10% discount poster we always do"*). It is fast for predictable daily tasks, yet **useless when the market itself changes**!
- **Creative thinking** breaks habits to invent **fresh, valuable solutions** (*"Partner with nearby gyms for recovery-smoothie bundles or switch to subscription refills!"*).

Notice: **Decision-making** picks ONE option from known choices; **Problem-solving** investigates the *root cause*, invents multiple options (`creative thinking!`), picks the best fit, and checks if it actually worked!

## Part 1: The Business Problem-Solving Cycle (`7 Clear Steps`)

```steps
title: Systematic problem-solving process in business
step: **1. Identify the real problem:** Separate the *symptom* (`angry customer reviews`) from the *root cause* (`slow oven temperature or broken booking app`).
step: **2. Define the problem clearly:** State what needs fixing, by when, and with what budget.
step: **3. Formulate a strategy / gather information:** Collect facts and pick the right problem-solving team & technique.
step: **4. Generate alternative creative solutions:** Use **Brainstorming, Delphi, Nominal Group, or Mind Mapping** without judging wild ideas too early!
step: **5. Evaluate alternatives against reality (`Force-Field Analysis`):** Weigh **cost, profitability, legal rules, skills needed, and risks** for every option.
step: **6. Choose & implement the best solution:** Assign clear responsibilities, timelines, and budget (`action plan`).
step: **7. Monitor & review (`Feedback`):** Measure results after 30–90 days and adjust if the problem persists!
```

## Part 2: Routine vs Creative Thinking & Mental Blocks

```compare
title: Routine vs Creative Thinking & How to Break Mental Blocks
left: Routine vs Creative Thinking
- **Routine Thinking:** Follows established rules, past habits, and standard recipes (`great for payroll accuracy and safety checklists`; weak against new competitors!).
- **Creative Thinking:** Generates **original, unconventional, useful ideas** (`great for new product launches, cost cutting, and surviving market disruption!`).
right: Common Mental Blocks to Creativity
- **Fear of failure / ridicule:** Staff stay quiet because managers mock unusual suggestions.
- **Rigid habits ("We've always done it this way"):** Refusing to question old procedures.
- **Functional fixedness:** Seeing only one narrow use for a tool, shop space, or skill.
- **Time pressure & fatigue:** Overworked staff never get quiet space to experiment (`fix via brainstorming workshops & safe non-judgmental ground rules!`).
```

## Part 3: Core Problem-Solving Techniques (`Know Application & Advantage!`)

| Technique | How It Works (`CAPS Exam Accuracy`) | Best Used When |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Delphi Technique** | Anonymous **written questionnaires** are sent across several rounds to **independent experts** (`who never meet face-to-face`); summaries circulate between rounds until experts reach balanced consensus! | Complex long-term strategic risks (`new market entry, automation`) where loud bosses might intimidate junior experts in a room! |
| **Force-Field Analysis** | Draw a T-table listing **Driving Forces (`pros pushing FOR change`)** vs **Restraining Forces (`cons/risks pushing AGAINST change`)**, assign numeric weights (`1–5`), and strengthen drivers while weakening blockers! | Deciding **whether a major proposed change is realistic** (`e.g. opening a night shift or buying expensive new machinery`). |
| **Brainstorming** | Small group rapidly calls out **every spontaneous idea** without any criticism during Phase 1 (`quantity breeds quality!`), then groups and evaluates winners in Phase 2. | Quick marketing slogans, packaging ideas, or everyday operational fixes. |
| **Nominal Group Technique (`NGT`)** | Every member writes ideas **silently on paper first**, reads them out one by one (`no arguing yet`), then votes privately by ranking top choices! | Preventing **extroverts/managers from dominating quiet thinkers** inside a team meeting. |

## Part 4: Evaluating Creative Ideas Against Reality

Even a brilliant idea fails if it breaks the budget or law! Every creative idea MUST survive **four reality checks**:
1. **Financial feasibility & ROI:** Can we afford the setup cost without starving working capital?
2. **Operational capability:** Do our staff and machines possess the skills (`or realistic training`) to run it?
3. **Legal & ethical compliance:** Does it satisfy **CPA, OHS, LRA, and environmental rules**?
4. **Market demand:** Will real customers pay enough for it to cover costs and earn profit?

## Worked example: Applying Delphi vs Force-Field Analysis (`Paper 2 Essay Style`)

**Scenario:** *CleanTech Laundromat wants to convert 10 diesel dryers to rooftop **solar-thermal hybrids** (`cost: R650 000`). Senior directors disagree sharply during meetings, and younger technicians feel intimidated speaking up.*

1. *Recommend the best technique to gather **unbiased expert input without intimidation** (`3`).* -> **Delphi Technique** (or **Nominal Group Technique** for an in-person staff session!): **Delphi** collects **anonymous written questionnaires from external solar/finance experts** across multiple rounds so strong personalities cannot dominate or silence honest technical warnings!
2. *Show how CleanTech would test whether the R650 000 switch makes sense using **Force-Field Analysis** (`4`).* ->
   - **Driving Forces (`Pros`):** Saves R18 000/month diesel (`weight 5`), avoids load-shedding downtime (`weight 5`), attracts eco-conscious hotels (`weight 3`) -> **Total = 13**.
   - **Restraining Forces (`Cons`):** High R650 000 upfront loan cost (`weight 4`), cloudy winter weeks backup (`weight 3`) -> **Total = 7**.
   - *Conclusion:* Driving forces (`13 > 7`) outweigh restraining forces — proceed if CleanTech arranges affordable green financing!

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Saying **Delphi Technique** puts experts around a boardroom table arguing live — **Delphi specifically keeps experts SEPARATE and ANONYMOUS via questionnaires** so group pressure cannot distort answers!
> - Stopping **Brainstorming** after listing wild ideas — always add **evaluating the ideas against cost, risk, and business reality**.
> - Writing *"Decision-making and problem-solving mean the exact same thing"* — **decision-making** is choosing between alternatives (`Step 6`), whereas **problem-solving** is the full end-to-end process from root-cause diagnosis to post-implementation review.

## Practice

```questions
Q: Distinguish between **routine thinking** and **creative thinking**, explaining when a business needs each. (4)
A: **Routine thinking** repeats established rules and familiar solutions (`useful for consistent accounting, safety checks, and daily admin`). **Creative thinking** breaks old patterns to produce original, useful solutions (`essential when launching new products, beating competitors, or solving crisis disruptions`).
---
Q: Describe how a business applies the **Delphi technique** and state TWO advantages of using it. (6)
A: Management sends **anonymous written questionnaires** to independent experts who never meet directly, circulates summarised responses across multiple rounds until balanced consensus emerges. **Advantages:** (1) Eliminates peer pressure and dominant personalities; (2) Brings specialised expert knowledge from anywhere geographically without travel costs.
---
Q: Explain TWO ways a manager can break **mental blocks to creative thinking** among employees. (4)
A: 1. Create a **psychologically safe rule ("zero criticism during idea-generation")** so staff share bold ideas without fear of ridicule. 2. Use structured silent techniques like **Nominal Group Technique** and reward helpful innovations (`suggestion boxes / bonuses`).
```
