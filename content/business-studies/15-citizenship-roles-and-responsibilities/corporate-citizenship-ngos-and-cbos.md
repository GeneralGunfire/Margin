> **What you'll learn:** the **meaning of corporate & individual economic citizenship**, **how citizens and businesses contribute to social and economic community development**, and the **distinct roles of businesses, civil society, NGOs (`Non-Governmental Organisations`), and CBOs (`Community-Based Organisations`)** in building resilient South African communities (*CAPS Business Roles · Paper 2*).
>
> **Prerequisites:** Chapter 12 (`King Code social responsibility`) and Chapter 4 (`socio-economic challenges`).
>
> **Why this matters:** Paper 2 questions ask how a profitable business goes beyond just paying tax to act as a **responsible corporate citizen** (`CSR/CSI`) alongside **NGOs** (`national/regional reach`) and **CBOs** (`grassroots neighbourhood reach`)!

## The idea in plain language

No business thrives inside a collapsing neighbourhood! If schools lack textbooks, roads decay, and youth face zero hope, crime rises and skilled staff leave.

**Corporate Citizenship** means treating a business as a **participating citizen of its community and country**: obeying laws, paying taxes honestly, treating workers/nature respectfully (`CSR`), and teaming up with **civil society (`NGOs & CBOs`)** so both the business and the community grow stronger together!

## Part 1: Citizenship Roles — Business vs Civil Society vs NGOs vs CBOs

| Stakeholder | Who They Are (`Exact Distinction!`) | Key Role in Community & Economic Development |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Businesses (`Corporate Citizens`)** | Profit-making firms (`small shops to JSE giants`). | Create sustainable jobs, upskill workers (`learnerships/internships`), buy from local SMMEs, pay taxes honestly, cut pollution, and invest **CSI (`Corporate Social Investment`)** into clinics, bursaries, and sports fields. |
| **Civil Society (`Umbrella Term`)** | Voluntary **non-state, non-profit** associations outside government and private companies (`faith groups, unions, sports clubs, consumer watchdogs, NGOs, CBOs`). | Hold government and corporations **accountable** (`e.g. consumer campaigns, anti-corruption advocacy, environmental protection`) and mobilise volunteers. |
| **NGOs (`Non-Governmental Organisations`)** | Structured, professionally staffed non-profit organisations operating **nationally or internationally** (`often registered NPOs`, e.g. *Gift of the Givers, Childline, WWF-SA, Treatment Action Campaign*). | Deliver large-scale disaster relief, healthcare advocacy, wildlife conservation, legal aid, and structured training funded by **corporate CSI grants, public donors, and international aid**. |
| **CBOs (`Community-Based Organisations`)** | **Grassroots, locally run** neighbourhood groups formed by residents themselves (`e.g. street-level soup kitchens, local youth coding clubs, neighbourhood watch, township gardening co-ops`). | Spot **urgent hyper-local needs** quickly with deep resident trust, mobilise volunteers immediately, and ensure outside business donations actually reach vulnerable households! |

## Part 2: How Individual Citizens Contribute Through Business & Enterprise

```steps
title: Five ways citizens & businesses uplift communities ethically
step: **1. Founding ethical local enterprises (`Job creation`):** Starting honest businesses (`employing local youth, paying fair BCEA wages`) reduces poverty directly far more sustainably than one-off charity handouts.
step: **2. Supporting local SMMEs & fair trade:** Buying vegetables from neighbourhood growers or stationery from black/women-owned SMMEs spreads economic life (`enterprise development`).
step: **3. Employee volunteering & skills mentoring:** Giving accountants, engineers, or chefs paid volunteer hours to mentor school learners, audit CBO finances cleanly, or coach start-ups!
step: **4. Cause-related partnerships (`Business + NGO/CBO`):** Business supplies **funding, logistics, and management governance** while the **CBO/NGO supplies community access and grassroots expertise** (`far higher impact than either acting alone!`).
step: **5. Environmental & civic stewardship:** Organising river clean-ups, sponsoring public libraries/parks, and reporting municipal corruption/dumping ethically.
```

## Worked example: Choosing & Partnering with an NGO or CBO (`Case Study`)

**Scenario:** *Horizon Hardware (`East London`) earns R400 000 profit and wants to spend **R40 000 CSI** helping hungry school learners in the surrounding township (`who walk past the store daily`). Management wonders whether to simply throw loose food parcels out the shop gate once a year OR partner with **Khanya Neighbourhood Kitchen** (`a local parent-run feeding CBO`) and **EduFeed SA** (`a registered national school-nutrition NGO`).*

1. *Distinguish between **Khanya Neighbourhood Kitchen (`CBO`)** and **EduFeed SA (`NGO`)** (`4`).* -> **Khanya (`CBO`)** is a **grassroots, community-run organisation** inside that exact township knowing which families need daily meals most; **EduFeed SA (`NGO`)** is a larger structured **non-profit organisation** with formal logistics, nutritionists, and audit systems operating across regions.
2. *Advise Horizon Hardware why partnering is far smarter than handing out parcels at the gate once (`4`).* -> Random one-day handouts create stampedes and end tomorrow (**short-term charity**), whereas partnering supplies steady dry-food stock + kitchen shelving to **Khanya (`local trust`)** under **EduFeed's nutrition/financial accountability**, uplifting children **all year** and building loyal community goodwill toward Horizon Hardware!

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Thinking **NGOs and CBOs are identical** — remember **CBOs** are **hyper-local, grassroots resident organisations**, whereas **NGOs** are larger formal non-profit organisations operating regionally/nationally (`though both sit under Civil Society!`).
> - Treating **corporate citizenship** as *"giving leftover charity money once a year while polluting rivers and underpaying staff"* — genuine citizenship starts **inside core business practices** (`fair wages, honest taxes, safe products, low waste`) BEFORE CSI donations!

## Practice

```questions
Q: Define **corporate citizenship** and explain TWO ways a profitable supermarket benefits *itself* when investing in local community upliftment (`CSI`). (6)
A: **Corporate citizenship** means a business acts ethically and responsibly toward its employees, community, environment, and society beyond merely chasing short-term profit. **Benefits to the business:** (1) Builds strong **brand loyalty and community goodwill** (`more local shoppers, lower vandalism/crime`); (2) Improves **local education and health**, creating a stronger future workforce!
---
Q: Distinguish between a **Non-Governmental Organisation (`NGO`)** and a **Community-Based Organisation (`CBO`)**, giving one example of each. (4)
A: An **NGO** is a formal, professionally run non-profit organisation operating on a wider regional, national, or global scale (`e.g. Gift of the Givers / WWF-SA`), whereas a **CBO** is a small grassroots group created and run by residents within one local community (`e.g. a township garden co-op or local soup kitchen`).
---
Q: Suggest THREE ways **civil society organisations** hold businesses and government accountable to citizens. (3)
A: 1. Monitoring and exposing **pollution, unsafe products, or corruption**. 2. Organising peaceful **consumer boycotts, petitions, or advocacy campaigns**. 3. Submitting public feedback during parliamentary law-making and municipal budgeting.
```
