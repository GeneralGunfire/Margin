## The idea in plain language

Marketing does NOT look the same inside a Sandton corporate headquarters and at a thriving **Gqeberha taxi-rank fruit/vetkoek stall** — yet **BOTH** must understand customers, pricing, placement, and trust!

Meanwhile, technology and international exports let even small South African makers (`rooibos tea, biltong, software, crafts`) sell worldwide — **provided** Marketing and **Production (`Chapter 17`)** communicate cleanly on volume, labels, quality, and deadlines!

## Part 1: Marketing in the Formal vs Informal Sectors

```compare
title: Formal Sector vs Informal Sector Marketing (SA Context)
left: Formal Sector Marketing
- **Registered businesses (`CIPC, SARS, VAT`)** with dedicated marketing budgets and departments.
- Uses formal **ATL & BTL media** (`TV, Google/Instagram ads, glossy brochures, CRM loyalty apps`), registered trade marks, formal contracts, and credit/card facilities.
- Subject to strict **CPA, ASA (`advertising standards`), and trade-mark compliance**.
right: Informal Sector Marketing
- **Unregistered micro-enterprises** (`street vendors, spaza shops, home-based tailors, car-wash stalls`) operating largely on cash/mobile wallets with tiny budgets.
- Relies powerfully on **word-of-mouth referrals, prime foot-traffic location (`outside stations/schools`), colourful hand-painted boards, loud personal calling, free tasting bites, and flexible micro-pack sizes** (`single sweets, R5 spice sachets matching daily pocket cash!`).
- **Opportunities:** Zero red tape, instant price agility, close personal bond with regular neighbours!
```

## Part 2: Technology in Marketing (`E-Marketing, E-Commerce & Data`)

```steps
title: Five ways modern technology reshapes marketing (`Benefits & Risks`)
step: **1. Websites & E-commerce (`24/7 global store`):** Customers browse catalogues, compare reviews, and pay online anytime (`Takealot, Shopify stores`), slashing retail shop-rent overheads!
step: **2. Social Media & Influencer Marketing:** Targeted ads reach **exact age, city, and interest groups** (`cheap measurable BTL compared to TV`!) with two-way instant comments/WhatsApp Business chats.
step: **3. CRM & Email/SMS Databases:** Tracks buying history to send personalised birthday vouchers and reorder reminders (`higher customer retention`).
step: **4. Digital Analytics:** Click-through rates and conversion tracking prove **exactly which ad paid off**!
step: **5. Key Tech Risks to Manage:** **POPIA (`data privacy`) fines** if sending spam or leaking client emails, **cyber-fraud/fake payment proofs**, negative comments going viral in minutes, and excluding offline rural shoppers!
```

## Part 3: Imports, Exports & Aligning Foreign Marketing with Production

Why can't a South African jam factory simply ship identical local jars straight to Germany or Japan overnight?

| Factor | How Foreign Marketing Impacts **Production (`Alignment!`)** |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1. Language, Metric & Legal Labels** | Production packaging lines must print **foreign-language ingredient/allergen labels**, metric/imperial units, and import-country food-safety certifications (`e.g. EU phytosanitary or Halal/FDA rules`). |
| **2. Cultural & Climate Adaptation** | Recipes, plug voltages (`110V vs 230V`), clothing sizes, and moisture-proof export cartons must be modified by Production (`chapter 17`) to survive long ocean voyages and suit local cultural norms (`e.g. colour/number taboos!`). |
| **3. Strict Volume & Delivery Deadlines** | Shipping delays at harbours miss seasonal retail windows (`e.g. European winter citrus demand`), so **Production scheduling & quality control (`SABS/ISO`)** must guarantee **zero-defect bulk runs** on exact vessel cutoff dates! |
| **4. Currency & Tariff Costs (`Imports vs Exports`)** | Import tariffs, freight insurance, and Rand fluctuations (`cross-link Tourism Forex!`) force Finance, Marketing, and Production to manage unit costs tightly so export prices stay competitive. |

## Worked example: Aligning Export Marketing with Production (`Paper 1 Question`)

**Scenario:** *SunBurst Rooibos (`Western Cape`) signs an export deal to supply **50 000 boxes of organic rooibos tea** to Japanese supermarkets (`previous local output was 15 000 boxes/month`).*

1. *Identify THREE adjustments **Production (`and Packaging`)** must make to align with this **foreign marketing deal** (`6`).* ->
   - **Packaging & Language:** Redesign cartons with **Japanese-language ingredient, barcode, and organic certification labels** (`moisture-sealed for sea freight`).
   - **Quality & Safety Consistency:** Enforce strict batch inspection (**ISO/HACCP standards**) because Japanese retailers reject entire shipments over minor defects!
   - **Capacity & Scheduling:** Shift from small batch schedules toward higher-capacity shifts and reserve raw rooibos inventory early so sea-freight deadlines are never missed!
2. *Explain ONE way **informal South African tea vendors** could still promote rooibos cheaply using **micro-marketing** (`2`).* -> Sell **affordable 5-teabag trial packs** beside taxi ranks with free hot tasting cups (`word-of-mouth + convenient location + small-pack pricing`)!

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Describing **informal-sector marketing** as *"bad or non-existent"* — informal traders apply sharp **location (`foot traffic`), micro-packaging, word-of-mouth, and relationship pricing**!
> - Answering *"foreign marketing"* without explaining **how Production must change** (`packaging languages, safety certifications, higher batch capacity, moisture-proof export boxes, strict shipping schedules`)!
> - Ignoring **imports** alongside **exports**: cheap imports force local Marketing & Production to cut waste, lift quality, or highlight **"Proudly South African"** local jobs.

## Practice

```questions
Q: Compare **two marketing methods** commonly used in the **informal sector** with **two methods** used in the **formal sector**. (4)
A: **Informal sector:** Prime foot-traffic positioning (`taxi ranks/streets`), personal word-of-mouth, and flexible micro-pack sizes matching pocket cash. **Formal sector:** Paid mass advertising (`TV/social media ads`) and formal digital loyalty/CRM campaigns backed by registered trade marks.
---
Q: Explain TWO advantages and TWO risks of using **social media and e-commerce technology** in marketing. (8)
A: **Advantages:** (1) Reaches targeted customers 24/7 at much lower cost than national TV; (2) Provides live analytics (`clicks/sales`) and instant two-way customer engagement. **Risks:** (1) Negative complaints go viral publicly in minutes; (2) Cyber-security breaches or **POPIA** violations if customer data is mishandled.
---
Q: State THREE ways **foreign (`export`) marketing** requires close alignment with the **production function**. (6)
A: 1. Adapting **product specifications, voltages, or recipes** to foreign legal and cultural expectations. 2. Printing **translated/compliant export packaging labels** strong enough for long sea/air freight. 3. Expanding **production scheduling and strict ISO/SABS quality inspection** to meet large container deadlines without rejected batches.
```
