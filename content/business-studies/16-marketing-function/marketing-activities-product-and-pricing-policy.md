## The idea in plain language

Building the best trainers in Africa means nothing if **nobody finds them, understands them, trusts the brand, or can afford the price**!

Marketing is far bigger than Instagram ads — it connects **production (`Chapter 17`)** to **paying customers** through **marketing activities** and the famous **4Ps (`Product, Price, Place/Distribution, Promotion/Communication`)**.

## Part 1: Core Marketing Activities (`CAPS Functional Steps`)

1. **Locating the consumer & Market research:** Discovering *who* buys, *where* they shop, and *what* features they value.
2. **Standardisation & Grading:** Setting uniform size/weight/safety rules (**standardisation**, e.g. 500 g butter bricks) and sorting agricultural/raw quality tiers (**grading**, e.g. Grade A vs B fruit).
3. **Storage & Warehousing:** Holding seasonal/bulk stock safely (`cold chains`) until customers want to buy, preventing shortages or price crashes!
4. **Transport (`Logistics`):** Moving goods safely (`road, rail, sea, air`) from factory to market (`creating place utility`).
5. **Financing & Risk-Taking:** Funding credit sales/campaigns and insuring stock against theft, spoilage, fire, or shifting fashion!
6. **Buying & Selling:** Negotiating supplier purchases and transferring legal ownership to buyers cleanly.

## Part 2: Product Policy (`Goods, Development, Packaging & Trade Marks`)

### A. Consumer vs Industrial Goods (`Product Classification`)
- **Convenience goods:** Everyday low-cost items bought fast (`bread, toothpaste, airtime`).
- **Shopping goods:** Compared carefully on price/quality/style (`sneakers, fridges, phones`).
- **Speciality goods:** Unique prestige items buyers seek purposefully (`luxury cars, designer watches`).
- **Industrial goods:** Raw materials/machinery sold to **other businesses** to manufacture outputs (`steel sheets, commercial ovens`).

### B. Product Development Steps & Product Life Cycle (`PLC`)
- **Development cycle:** Idea generation -> Screening (`drop weak ideas`) -> Concept testing -> Business cost/profit analysis -> Prototype testing -> Market launch (`Introduction -> Growth -> Maturity -> Decline` on the **PLC**!).

### C. Packaging & Trade Marks (`CAPS Exam Favourites!`)
- **Why Packaging Matters:** Protects contents (`breakage/spoilage`), aids stacking/transport, **informs consumers (`ingredients, expiry, barcodes`)**, and attracts attention on crowded shelves!
- **CAPS Packaging Types:**
  - **Family packaging:** Consistent colour/logo across a whole product range (`e.g. Cadbury purple across slabs` — builds instant brand unity!).
  - **Specialty / Prestige packaging:** Distinct luxury/gift bottles or tins (`perfume bottles, boutique chocolates`).
  - **Re-usable (`Double-use`) packaging:** Container stays useful *after* contents finish (`ice-cream tubs, glass coffee jars` — strong appeal in budget/eco markets!).
  - **Multiple (`Bulk`) packaging:** Bundling related items together (`6-pack drinks, combo toiletries` — boosts basket size).
  - **Kaleidoscopic (`Changing`) packaging:** Regularly updates artwork for World Cups, holidays, or sports collectibles.
- **Branding & Trade Marks:** A registered **trade mark (`®`)** stops rivals copying your logo/name (`Chapter 4`), builds repeat trust, and lets successful brands charge stronger margins!

## Part 3: Pricing Policy (`How Businesses Set Selling Prices`)

| Pricing Strategy | How It Works (`Exact CAPS Rule`) | Best Used When |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cost-Plus (`Mark-up`) Pricing** | Calculate **total unit cost** (`Chapter 17`) + add fixed profit markup `%` (`e.g. R100 cost + 40% = R140`). | Retail/manufacturing guaranteeing every sale covers overheads (`if rivals match!`). |
| **Price Skimming** | Launch a brand-new innovation at a **very high initial price** to recover R&D quickly from eager early adopters, then lower later! | Cutting-edge smartphones, patented gadgets, luxury launches with little competition! |
| **Penetration Pricing** | Launch at a **deliberately LOW introductory price** to grab rapid market share and steal customers from entrenched rivals, raising price slowly later! | Entering crowded everyday markets (`new laundry detergent, fibre ISP, streaming app`). |
| **Psychological (`Odd/Even`) Pricing** | Set prices just below round numbers (`R99.95` instead of `R100`, or crisp `R5 000` for prestige luxury) to shape buyer perception! | Everyday retail (`odd`) vs luxury prestige (`even`). |
| **Bait / Loss-Leader Pricing** | Advertise one popular staple below normal margin (`e.g. R12 milk`) to lure shoppers into buying profitable groceries inside! | Supermarkets (`NEVER confuse with illegal fake "bait-and-switch" stock tricks banned by CPA!`). |

## Worked example: Product Packaging & Pricing Strategy (`Paper 1 Case Study`)

**Scenario:** *Karoo Crunch launches a new **high-protein roasted chickpea snack** packed in sturdy **plastic jars customers reuse for kitchen spices** (`Decision A`). To win shelf space against giant chip brands quickly, they price jars at **R19.95** — 30% cheaper than competitors for the first 3 months (`Decision B`).*

1. *Identify the **packaging type** in Decision A (`2`) and give ONE consumer benefit (`2`).* -> **Re-usable / Double-use packaging** — buyers reuse the sturdy jar for spices/storage afterwards, increasing perceived value and reducing immediate bin waste!
2. *Name **TWO pricing strategies** combined in Decision B (`4`).* -> **Penetration pricing** (low introductory price 30% below entrenched chip rivals to capture market share fast) AND **Psychological / odd pricing** (`R19.95` feels noticeably cheaper than `R20.00`!).

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Mixing up **Price Skimming** (`HIGH launch price -> drops later`) with **Penetration Pricing** (`LOW launch price -> rises later`)!
> - Confusing **Standardisation** (`setting uniform sizes/weights/safety specs across batches`) with **Grading** (`sorting finished/agricultural output into quality classes like Grade A/B`).
> - Placing packaging or trade marks under *Marketing Communication (`Promotion`)* when questions specifically ask for **Product Policy elements** (`design, quality, packaging, branding, trade marks`).

## Practice

```questions
Q: Distinguish between **standardisation** and **grading** as marketing activities, giving one example of each. (4)
A: **Standardisation** sets uniform physical specifications (`weight, dimensions, hygiene`) that products must meet (`e.g. every loaf weighing 700 g`). **Grading** sorts agricultural or natural products into distinct quality tiers (`e.g. classifying eggs or apples into Extra Large Grade 1 vs Grade 2`).
---
Q: Recommend a suitable **CAPS packaging type** for *(a)* luxury perfume aimed at Gift buyers, and *(b)* a range of 8 matching spices sold by one brand. Explain each choice. (4)
A: *(a)* **Specialty / prestige packaging** (`crafted glass bottle protecting fragrance and conveying luxury gift appeal`). *(b)* **Family packaging** (`uniform jars, colours, and logo across all 8 spices create instant shelf visibility and cross-selling trust`).
---
Q: Contrast **price skimming** and **penetration pricing**, stating ONE type of product suited to each strategy. (4)
A: **Price skimming** starts with a **high launch price** to recover R&D costs from early adopters before lowering later (`suited to patented electronics or innovative tech`). **Penetration pricing** launches with a **low entry price** to quickly steal market share in competitive markets (`suited to new everyday snacks, soaps, or mobile data deals`).
```
