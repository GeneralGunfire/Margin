>
> **Prerequisites:** Chapter 10 (`Gantt charts & action plans`) and Chapter 6 (`ownership & shares`).
>
> **Why this matters:** Paper 2 exams test **advising an entrepreneur on whether to raise Equity vs Debt capital**, weighing **interest repayments vs loss of ownership/control**, and checking practical launch readiness!

## The idea in plain language

You have a registered company (`Chapter 6`) and a neat Gantt chart (`Chapter 10`). Now comes the moment of truth: **paying the bills before your first paying customers walk in!**

Every entrepreneur faces the same fundamental funding fork:
- **Equity (`Own or Investor Share Capital`):** Cash put in by yourself, family, angel investors, or shareholders in exchange for **ownership shares** (`no monthly loan interest, YET you share control and future profits!`).
- **Debt (`Borrowed Capital`):** Cash borrowed from a bank, supplier, or micro-lender (`you keep 100% ownership, YET you MUST repay principal + interest every month even if sales flop!`).

## Part 1: Key Factors Before Initiating & Launching a Venture

```steps
title: Five readiness checks while starting a venture from your action plan
step: **1. Operations & location readiness:** Confirm municipal zoning/health certificates, reliable power/water backup, security, and supplier delivery reliability before launch day.
step: **2. Cash-flow buffer (`Working capital`):** Keep enough liquid cash for **3–6 months of rent, electricity, stock, and wages** — profitable firms still go bankrupt if customers pay late!
step: **3. Staff hiring & workplace culture:** Recruit the right skilled people (`not merely unemployed relatives without skills!`), induct them clearly, and build customer-first habits from Day 1.
step: **4. Pricing & break-even check:** Verify your selling price covers both variable costs and fixed overheads (`leaving healthy profit margin`).
step: **5. Legal, tax & risk protection:** Register with **CIPC, SARS, UIF/COIDA**, check Consumer Protection Act (`CPA`) warranties, and take adequate business insurance!
```

## Part 2: Equity vs Debt Capital & Other Sources of Funding

| Funding Type | Specific Examples (`SA Context`) | Advantages | Disadvantages / Risks |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Equity Capital (`Own / Investors`)** | **Owner's personal savings**, issuing shares to partners/friends, **Angel Investors**, **Venture Capital (`VC`) firms** | **Zero fixed monthly interest repayments**; no collateral seizure if a slow month happens; angels/VCs often bring **mentorship & industry contacts**! | **Dilutes ownership and control** (`investors want voting seats and a slice of profits forever`); personal savings risk family security if the firm fails. |
| **2. Debt Capital (`Borrowed Funds`)** | **Bank term loans**, **bank overdrafts** (`short-term cash gaps`), **mortgage bonds**, **supplier trade credit** (`buy now, pay in 30–60 days`), **instalment sale / leasing** | **Owner keeps 100% ownership & future profits** (`once repaid, lender leaves!`); interest paid on business loans is generally **tax-deductible**! | **Mandatory monthly repayments + high interest** (`hits cash flow hard during slow start-up months!`); requires **collateral (`house/assets`)** or strong credit score. |
| **3. Grants & Development Finance** | Government/agency **grants (`NYDA` for youth, `DTIC / SEFA`)**, business competitions, **crowdfunding** (`Pre-orders / BackaBuddy-style campaigns`) | **Non-repayable grants** (`free capital if strict criteria are met!`) or cheap development interest rates; crowdfunding also **tests real market demand early**! | Intense paperwork, strict compliance audits, long approval delays, and stiff competition (`never launch waiting passively on one grant!`). |

## Part 3: Choosing Between Equity & Debt (`Decision Rules`)

```compare
title: When Equity Wins vs When Debt Wins
left: Prefer Equity Capital When...
- The venture is a **brand-new high-risk innovation** (`e.g. software app or prototype`) with **unpredictable cash flow** in Year 1 (`cannot survive rigid monthly bank instalments!`).
- The founder lacks physical **collateral (`property`)** yet welcomes an experienced investor's network and guidance.
right: Prefer Debt Capital (`Loan / Trade Credit`) When...
- The venture has **steady, predictable monthly sales** easily covering interest (`e.g. expanding an already-profitable bakery or buying an extra delivery van`).
- The founder refuses to surrender **voting shares or long-term profit percentages** to outside partners!
```

## Worked example: Recommending Funding for a Start-Up Case Study

**Scenario:** *Nomsa (`23`) invented a biodegradable hair-care line from marula oil. She has **R25 000 savings**, zero property collateral, and needs **R280 000** to buy mixing tanks and retail packaging. An experienced cosmetics executive offers R280 000 in exchange for **30% shares**, while a bank offers a **R280 000 loan at 16% interest** ONLY if her parents mortgage their house.*

1. *Distinguish between the two offers using exact CAPS terms (`2`).* -> The cosmetics executive offers **Equity capital (`30% ownership share`)**, whereas the bank offers **Debt capital (`interest-bearing secured loan`)**.
2. *Advise Nomsa which option is safer and explain TWO reasons (`6`).* -> Recommend **Equity capital (`the cosmetics executive`)**, **while negotiating fair share protection**:
   - **Cash-flow protection:** Zero rigid 16% monthly loan repayments during Year 1 while supermarkets slowly test her brand.
   - **No loss of parents' home (`risk safety`):** Avoids risking family collateral (`mortgage`) AND gains an **experienced cosmetics mentor + retail buyer contacts**!
   - *(Also suggest checking **NYDA youth grants / SEFA micro-finance** to reduce the % equity she must give away!)*

```tryit
title: Equity vs Debt & Working Capital Quick Check
prompt: Answer quickly:
1. Classify **supplier trade credit (`30 days to pay for stock`)** and **issuing new private shares** as **Equity** or **Debt**.
2. Why is a **bank overdraft** suitable for paying wages 5 days before a big client invoice clears, rather than buying a R900 000 factory machine?
hint: Repayable liability vs ownership share; short-term vs long-term finance matching!
answer: 1. Trade credit = **Debt** (`short-term liability`); issuing shares = **Equity**. 2. **Matching principle:** An **overdraft** is expensive short-term finance meant for temporary cash-flow gaps, whereas long-term machinery belongs on a **multi-year term loan, lease, or equity**!
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Saying **Equity capital is "free money"** — equity is often the **most expensive** long-term finance because shareholders own a slice of your company's profits **forever** (`only **grants** are non-repayable!`).
> - Calling a **bank loan** *"own capital"* — borrowed money is **borrowed/debt capital** (`liabilities`).
> - Advising start-ups to use a **short-term credit card or overdraft** to buy long-term buildings or heavy machinery!

## Practice

```questions
Q: Distinguish between **equity capital** and **debt capital**, giving ONE advantage and ONE disadvantage of each. (8)
A: **Equity capital** is funds invested by owners or shareholders in exchange for ownership (`e.g. savings, angel investors, shares`). *Advantage:* No mandatory monthly interest repayments; *Disadvantage:* Dilutes ownership control and profit share. **Debt capital** is money borrowed from lenders (`e.g. bank loans, overdrafts, trade credit`) to be repaid with interest. *Advantage:* Founder retains 100% ownership; *Disadvantage:* Fixed monthly repayments strain cash flow and often require collateral.
---
Q: Explain TWO ways **supplier trade credit (`30–60 days`)** and **crowdfunding** help a cash-strapped entrepreneur launch a venture. (4)
A: **Trade credit** allows the business to receive and sell inventory *before* paying the supplier, easing cash flow. **Crowdfunding** collects pre-orders or small contributions online from future customers — raising upfront capital while proving genuine market demand!
---
Q: State THREE operational or legal aspects an entrepreneur must check BEFORE opening doors on launch day (`based on an action plan`). (3)
A: Any three of: **CIPC/SARS & UIF/COIDA legal registrations**, **sufficient 3–6 month working capital cash buffer**, **staff recruitment & OHS safety training**, and **verified supplier/power/insurance readiness**.
```
