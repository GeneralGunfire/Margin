## The idea in plain language

Imagine pitching an investor for R500 000 by reading aloud from a 40-page spreadsheet with your back turned to the room — **nobody will invest a cent!**

Great **business presentation** turns complex sales, budget, or market numbers into **crystal-clear stories**: pairing confident **verbal delivery**, strong **non-verbal presence**, clean **tables/charts/slides**, and composed handling of tough Q&A!

## Part 1: Verbal vs Non-Verbal Presentation

```compare
title: Verbal vs Non-Verbal Presentation Elements
left: Verbal Presentation (`Spoken & Written Words`)
- **Spoken delivery:** Clear voice projection, calm steady pace, **varying pitch/tone** (`no monotone robot voice!`), plain jargon-free explanations suited to the audience (`bankers vs factory staff`).
- **Structure:** Strong opening hook (`30-sec summary`), logical signposted middle (`"First... Second..."`), memorable closing recommendation.
- **Written support:** Executive summaries, **handouts**, cue cards (`NOT full-sentence scripts read word-for-word!`).
right: Non-Verbal Presentation (`Visual & Body Signals`)
- **Body language:** Upright open posture, genuine eye contact across the room, calm natural hand gestures (`no pacing nervously, hiding behind podium, or crossing arms defensively!`).
- **Appearance & props:** Neat professional attire, clean slide deck, crisp printed **posters/brochures**, interactive charts.
- **Space & time control:** Respecting the room layout (`never block the projector screen!`) and finishing **inside the time limit**.
```

## Part 2: Choosing the Right Visual Aid (`Tables, Graphs, Posters & Handouts`)

| Visual Tool | Best Used For (`Exam Match!`) | Golden Design Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Table** | Showing **exact figures side by side** (`branch sales, monthly expenses, cost vs revenue`). | Bold headers, align numbers neatly, keep rows concise (`no 100-row wall!`). |
| **Bar / Column Graph** | **Comparing discrete categories** (`e.g. Q1 vs Q2 vs Q3 profits across 4 branches`). | Label BOTH axes (`with units like Rands`), start y-axis at `0` so bars stay honest! |
| **Line Graph** | Showing **trends or changes continuously over time** (`12-month sales rise/drop`). | Clear time axis (`Jan–Dec`), distinct legend if comparing two lines. |
| **Pie Chart** | Showing **percentage share / proportions of ONE whole (`100%`)** (`e.g. budget split: 40% rent, 35% wages, 25% stock`). | Maximum ~5–6 slices with exact `%` labels (`too many tiny slices become unreadable!`). |
| **Flowchart / Diagram** | Explaining **process steps or company structure** (`e.g. organogram, complaint flow, production chain`). | Arrows flowing cleanly left-to-right or top-to-bottom (`no tangled spaghetti arrows!`). |
| **Slides (`PowerPoint`)** | Guiding live audiences through key points visually. | **"Rule of 6" (`max ~6 bullets × 6 words`)**, large readable font (`24pt+`), high contrast! |
| **Posters & Handouts** | **Posters:** Foyer/expo attraction summaries. **Handouts:** Detailed tables/take-home specs. | **Hand out detailed handouts AFTER (`not before!`)** a talk unless needed immediately, so listeners look at YOU instead of reading ahead! |

## Part 3: Responding Professionally to Questions & Feedback

```steps
title: Five steps to handle tough audience questions with confidence
step: **1. Listen fully without interrupting:** Let the investor or teacher finish the entire question while nodding calmly.
step: **2. Pause & acknowledge politely:** Say *"Thank you — that is an important question about our break-even timeline"* (`buys 2 seconds to think clearly and shows respect!`).
step: **3. Answer briefly, honestly & directly:** Give specific numbers/facts (`don't waffle or dodge!`). If you genuinely don't know one minor stat, **never lie or guess wildly** — say: *"I want to give you verified figures; may I confirm that exact supplier quote by email this afternoon?"*
step: **4. Stay composed under hostile/critical questions:** Never argue defensively, blame teammates, or mock a questioner — address the **issue calmly with evidence**.
step: **5. Check satisfaction & close cleanly:** Ask *"Does that answer your question?"* and log valid feedback to improve your report!
```

## Worked example: Choosing Visuals & Fixing Presentation Mistakes (`Case Study`)

**Scenario:** *Thabo must present his quarterly sales report to 12 directors (`20 min slot`): **(A)** showing how R1 000 000 marketing spend was split across TV 50%, Digital 30%, Print 15%, Events 5%; **(B)** showing monthly profit climbing from Jan to Dec; **(C)** currently his Slide 3 has 18 lines of 11-pt paragraph text.*

1. *Pick the best graph for `(A)` and `(B)` (`2`).* -> **(A) Pie chart** (`shares adding up to 100% of one budget`); **(B) Line graph** (`continuous trend over 12 months`).
2. *Give TWO practical fixes for Slide 3 (`4`).* -> 1. Cut 18 lines down to **4–5 short keyword bullets** (`large 24pt+ font`) while keeping full paragraphs on **speaker cue cards or post-talk handouts**. 2. Move complex exact Rand numbers into a simple **summary table or bar chart** so directors read the insight in 3 seconds!

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Turning PowerPoint slides into **"teleprompter essays"** packed with 12-pt paragraphs that you read aloud word-for-word with your back to the audience.
> - Picking a **Pie Chart** when numbers are **independent totals NOT summing to 100%** (`e.g. comparing January vs February vs March revenue — use a Bar/Line graph instead!`).
> - Guessing fake statistics during Q&A instead of **admitting honestly when you will verify and follow up**!

## Practice

```questions
Q: Distinguish between **verbal** and **non-verbal** presentation, giving TWO examples of each. (6)
A: **Verbal presentation** uses spoken or written language to convey ideas (e.g. **clear voice modulation, structured spoken explanations, executive summaries/handouts**). **Non-verbal presentation** communicates through visual aids and body cues without spoken words (e.g. **open posture/eye contact, graphs/tables, clean PowerPoint layouts, posters**).
---
Q: Recommend the best **visual aid (`table, pie chart, line graph, or flowchart`)** for: *(a)* showing the step-by-step approval workflow of a customer loan; *(b)* tracking monthly electricity bills over one year; *(c)* displaying exact Rand costs of five products side by side. (3)
A: *(a)* **Flowchart**; *(b)* **Line graph**; *(c)* **Table**.
---
Q: Suggest THREE practical strategies a presenter should follow **during Q&A** after a business presentation. (6)
A: 1. **Listen actively without interrupting** and rephrase/clarify complex questions politely. 2. **Stay calm and non-defensive** even if challenged sharply, answering briefly with verified facts. 3. **Never fabricate unknown data** — commit honestly to verifying and sending exact figures afterward.
```
