> **What you'll learn:** the **three CAPS production systems** (*Mass, Batch, Job production*), the **four steps of Production Planning** (*planning, routing, scheduling, loading*), and the **four steps of Production Control** (*dispatching, follow-up, inspection, corrective action*) (*CAPS Business Operations · Paper 1*).
>
> **Prerequisites:** Chapter 16 (`aligning production with marketing demand`).
>
> **Why this matters:** Paper 1 Section B (`15–30 marks` on Production!) loves testing **which production system fits a business scenario** AND punishing learners who **mix up the 4 Production Planning steps (`before machines start`) with the 4 Production Control steps (`while & after machines run`)**!

## The idea in plain language

Imagine running a bakery in Polokwane. You do not bake **1 single wedding cake** using the exact same assembly line that bakes **20 000 standard white loaves a day**!
1. First, you pick the **right Production System** (`Job, Batch, or Mass`).
2. Next, **BEFORE switching on the ovens**, you run **Production Planning** (`Planning -> Routing -> Scheduling -> Loading`).
3. Finally, **ONCE baking begins**, you run **Production Control** (`Dispatching -> Follow-up -> Inspection -> Corrective Action`) so every batch finishes on time without burnt crusts!

## Part 1: The Three Production Systems (`Mass vs Batch vs Job`)

| Production System | How Goods Are Made | Typical Products | Advantages & Disadvantages |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Job Production** | **One unique, custom item** crafted from start to finish specifically to **one client's exact specification** (`often skilled craftspeople`). | Custom wedding gown, portrait painting, custom steel gate, bridge construction. | **Pros:** Maximum quality/customisation, high pride, high profit per item. **Cons:** Slowest speed, **highest cost per unit**, relies heavily on rare skilled labour! |
| **2. Batch Production** | Limited **groups (`batches`) of identical items** move step-by-step through stages before machinery/recipe resets for the next batch! | 500 chocolate muffins then 500 bran muffins; school uniforms by size; bottled craft sauces. | **Pros:** Balances variety + moderate unit cost; less downtime risk than continuous lines. **Cons:** Machine cleaning/re-tooling downtime between batches; storage needed between batches. |
| **3. Mass (`Continuous / Flow`) Production** | **Non-stop assembly line** churning out thousands/millions of **standardised identical products** using specialised automated machines. | Bottled sparkling water, bricks, standard white bread, flat-pack nails, car assembly. | **Pros:** **Lowest cost per unit (`economies of scale`)**, blazing output speed, consistent specs. **Cons:** Huge upfront factory cost, **inflexible** if tastes shift, **one breakdown stops the whole line**, boring repetitive tasks for workers! |

## Part 2: Production Planning vs Production Control (`Exact 4 + 4 Sequence!`)

Never blend these two lists together — **Planning happens on paper BEFORE production starts; Control monitors execution DURING & AFTER production!**

```compare
title: Production Planning (Before) vs Production Control (During & After)
left: 4 Steps of Production Planning (`Before Start`)
- **1. Planning (`What & How Much`):** Forecast quantities needed, choose production system, and verify raw materials, tools, and staff skills are ready.
- **2. Routing (`Which Path`):** Map the exact **logical sequence of steps/workstations** raw materials must travel from cutting -> sewing -> packing (*eliminates wasted backtracking!*).
- **3. Scheduling (`When & How Long`):** Set exact **start dates, finish times, and deadlines** for every batch/operation (`Gantt-style factory timetable`).
- **4. Loading (`Who & Which Machine`):** Assign specific **quantities of work to each machine and worker** up to safe capacity (*prevents overloading Machine A while Machine B sits idle!*).
right: 4 Steps of Production Control (`During & After`)
- **1. Dispatching (`Go!`):** Issue official **written work orders, blueprints, and raw-material release slips** giving operators permission to start!
- **2. Follow-Up (`Expediting`):** Track live work-in-progress hourly/daily to catch machine jams, missing parts, or bottlenecks **before deadlines slip**!
- **3. Inspection (`Quality Check`):** Test raw materials, mid-stage samples, and finished goods against **quality/weight/safety standards** (`removing defects early!`).
- **4. Corrective Action (`Fix Root Cause`):** Recalibrate broken machines, retrain staff, adjust routing/timings, or swap bad suppliers immediately when inspection or follow-up finds errors!
```

## Worked example: Classifying Systems & Fixing a Factory Bottleneck (`Case Study`)

**Case Study:** *StyleWear Clothing makes **(A)** 10 000 identical white hospital coats per month on continuous machines, AND **(B)** a separate small studio sewing 200 navy blazers before switching patterns to 200 grey blazers. Recently, shirts piled up behind Machine 3 while Machine 4 sat empty (`Problem 1`), and defective crooked seams reached packed retail boxes (`Problem 2`).*

1. *Identify the **production system** in `(A)` and `(B)` (`2`).* -> **(A) Mass production** (`high-volume identical hospital coats`); **(B) Batch production** (`200 navy then reset for 200 grey`).
2. *Which **Production Planning step** prevents `Problem 1` (`Machine 3 overloaded while Machine 4 idle`)? (`2`)* -> **Loading** (`assigning balanced workloads across machines according to capacity`) alongside **Routing**!
3. *Which **TWO Production Control steps** fix `Problem 2` (`defective seams reaching boxes`)? (`4`)* -> **Inspection** (`checking seams mid-process BEFORE final packing`) followed by **Corrective action** (`recalibrating the sewing needle tension or retraining the operator immediately`)!

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Swapping **Routing** (`physical sequence of machines/steps`) with **Scheduling** (`time/dates timetable`) or **Loading** (`assigning workload to each machine/worker`).
> - Placing **Dispatching** (`issuing start orders`) under *Planning* — CAPS places **Dispatching** as the first step of **Production Control**!
> - Saying *"Mass production has zero disadvantages"* — high capital cost, total line stoppage if one machine fails, and inflexibility to sudden style changes are huge risks!

## Practice

```questions
Q: Contrast **job production** and **mass production** in terms of **(a) product variety**, **(b) unit cost**, and **(c) worker skill needed**. (6)
A: *(a)* **Job:** Unique customised one-offs; **Mass:** Standardised identical goods. *(b)* **Job:** Highest unit cost; **Mass:** Lowest unit cost (`economies of scale`). *(c)* **Job:** Highly skilled multi-purpose craftspeople; **Mass:** Specialised semi-skilled operators performing repetitive tasks.
---
Q: Briefly explain the difference between **routing**, **scheduling**, and **loading** in production planning. (6)
A: **Routing** determines the exact sequence of operations/workstations raw materials follow. **Scheduling** fixes start and completion dates/times for each operation. **Loading** assigns specific work quantities to individual machines and workers without exceeding capacity.
---
Q: Why are **follow-up** and **corrective action** vital even after a factory has completed careful **production planning**? (4)
A: Real factories hit unexpected breakdowns, power cuts, late deliveries, or operator errors: **follow-up** spots bottlenecks early during the shift, and **corrective action** fixes root causes immediately (`retraining, machine repair, extra shifts`) before customer delivery deadlines or quality fail!
```
