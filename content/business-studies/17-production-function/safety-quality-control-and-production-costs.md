## The idea in plain language

Running a fast assembly line means nothing if **machines injure workers (`shutting down the plant under OHS inspectors!`)**, **faulty goods get returned by angry retailers**, or **you charge R55 per item while costing R62 to make!**

2. **Quality Control (`SABS & TQM`)** — keeping products reliable and defect-free.

## Part 1: Safety Management (`OHS Act 85 of 1993` & Safety Policies)

Under South Africa's **OHS Act 85 of 1993**, safety is a **shared legal duty (`BOTH employer AND employee!`)**:

```compare
title: Employer Duties vs Employee Duties Under the OHS Act
left: Employer Legal Duties (`Management`)
- Provide and maintain **safe machinery, ventilation, lighting, fire exits, and non-slip floors**.
- Supply **free Personal Protective Equipment (`PPE` — hard hats, goggles, ear plugs, safety boots)**!
- Train staff in local languages / clear **visual safety signs**, appoint trained **Health & Safety Representatives (`when >20 employees`)**, and display the OHS Act summary cleanly.
right: Employee Legal Duties (`Workers`)
- **Wear supplied PPE properly** at all times (`never remove machine safety guards to speed up!`).
- Obey reasonable safety instructions, report **unsafe faults or hazards immediately** to supervisors/safety reps, and **never work intoxicated or act recklessly**.
```

- **Key Components of a Workplace Safety Policy:** Emergency evacuation routes & assembly points, first-aid kit locations (`trained first-aiders`), hazardous chemical storage (`MSDS`), machine lockout rules during repairs, and accident reporting (`linking to **COIDA** in Chapter 19!`).

## Part 2: Quality Control (`SABS & Quality Management Systems`)

- **Quality Control (`Inspection`):** Checking raw inputs, semi-finished goods, and final outputs (`sampling or 100% check`) to catch defects before shipping.
- **Quality Assurance / Quality Management System (`QMS`, e.g. `ISO 9001` & `TQM`):** Designing defect-prevention into **every process and employee habit** (`not just catching broken products at the back door!`), often using small worker **Quality Circles** meeting weekly to eliminate errors!
- **SABS (`South African Bureau of Standards`):** South Africa's national statutory standards body! Products passing rigorous independent lab tests and factory audits earn the trusted **SABS Approved mark (`SABS Mark of Approval`)** — boosting buyer trust, safety compliance (`plugs, helmets, cement`), and export credibility!

## Part 3: Production Cost Calculations (`CAPS Formulas`)

Memorise these four exact formulas:
1. **Fixed Costs (`FC`):** Stay constant regardless of how many units you make (`factory rent, permanent manager salaries, machine insurance, depreciation`).
2. **Variable Costs (`VC`):** Change directly with output volume (`raw materials, direct factory piece-wages, packaging boxes, machine electricity per unit`).
   $$\text{Total Variable Cost} = \text{Variable Cost per Unit} \times \text{Number of Units Produced}$$
3. **Total Cost of Production (`TC`):**
   $$\text{Total Cost} = \text{Fixed Costs} + \text{Total Variable Cost}$$
4. **Unit Production Cost (`Cost per Unit`):**
   $$\text{Unit Cost} = \frac{\text{Total Cost of Production}}{\text{Number of Units Produced}}$$

## Worked example: Full Production Costing Calculation (`Paper 1 Exam Style`)

**Scenario:** *Karoo Candles (`Pty) Ltd* manufactures **2 000 scented candles** in April with these monthly costs:*
- Factory workshop rent: `R9 500`
- Permanent supervisor salary: `R14 000`
- Business insurance: `R1 500`
- Soy wax & wicks: `R18.00 per candle`
- Glass jars & labels: `R11.50 per candle`
- Packaging box & direct packing labour: `R4.50 per candle`

Calculate **(a) Total Fixed Costs**, **(b) Total Variable Costs**, **(c) Total Cost of Production**, **(d) Unit Cost per Candle**, and **(e) Selling Price if applying a 40% cost-plus mark-up** (`10 marks`):

1. **Total Fixed Costs (`FC`):**
   `R9 500 + R14 000 + R1 500` = **`R25 000.00`**
2. **Variable Cost per Candle:**
   `R18.00 + R11.50 + R4.50` = `R34.00 per candle`
   **Total Variable Costs (`VC` for 2 000 units):**
   `2 000 × R34.00` = **`R68 000.00`**
3. **Total Cost of Production (`TC`):**
   `R25 000 + R68 000` = **`R93 000.00`**
4. **Unit Cost per Candle:**
   `R93 000 ÷ 2 000` = **`R46.50 per candle`**
5. **Selling Price (`40% mark-up on unit cost`):**
   `R46.50 × 1.40` = **`R65.10 per candle`**

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Classifying **factory rent or permanent manager salary** as *Variable Cost*, or forgetting to **multiply per-unit raw materials by Total Units Produced** before adding Fixed Costs!
> - Thinking **Quality Control** and **Quality Assurance (`QMS/TQM`)** mean the same thing — **Quality Control** inspects finished/in-process batches to *catch* defects; **Quality Assurance / QMS (`ISO 9001`)** builds systematic processes across the whole firm to *prevent* defects before they occur!
> - Claiming **ONLY management** is responsible for safety under the OHS Act — **employees also carry strict legal duties** to wear PPE, follow safety rules, and report hazards!

## Practice

```questions
Q: State TWO legal responsibilities of an **employer** and TWO responsibilities of an **employee** under the **OHS Act 85 of 1993**. (4)
A: **Employer:** (1) Supply necessary **PPE free of charge** and maintain safe machinery/workplaces; (2) Train workers on hazards and display clear safety signs. **Employee:** (1) Wear provided PPE properly and follow safety instructions; (2) Report unsafe equipment or hazards immediately without tampering with guards.
---
Q: Explain TWO benefits to a South African manufacturer of earning the **SABS (`South African Bureau of Standards`)** approval mark. (4)
A: 1. Independent testing proves products meet strict safety and durability standards, winning **instant consumer and retail buyer trust**. 2. Improves **export competitiveness** and lowers costly legal recalls or defective returns.
---
Q: **Mpho Crafts** produces **500 beaded lamps** per month. Fixed costs are **R15 000/month** (`rent & admin`), and variable costs (`beads, wire, bulbs, direct labour`) are **R55.00 per lamp**. Calculate: *(a)* Total production cost; *(b)* Unit cost per lamp; *(c)* New unit cost if production doubles to **1 000 lamps** (`fixed costs stay R15 000`). (6)
A: *(a)* `15 000 + (500 × 55) = 15 000 + 27 500 =` **`R42 500.00`**. *(b)* `42 500 ÷ 500 =` **`R85.00 per lamp`**. *(c)* `Total = 15 000 + (1 000 × 55) = R70 000`; `Unit cost = 70 000 ÷ 1 000 =` **`R70.00 per lamp`** (`unit cost drops by R15.00 due to fixed costs spreading across more units!`).
```
