> **What you'll learn:** what a **theory** is in academic **Religion Studies**, how **Functionalist theory** (*social cohesion, meaning, comfort*) and **Conflict theory** (*power, class inequality, oppression AND liberation*) explain religion's social roles without claiming a faith is "true" or "false", and the difference between **morality** (lived right/wrong behaviour) and **ethics** (systematic reasoning behind moral rules) across religions.
>
> **Prerequisites:** academic, non-confessional approach and mutual interdependence of religion and society.
>
> **Why this matters:** Paper 1 and Paper 2 love asking learners to **apply Functionalist or Conflict theory to a real-world case study** (like a charity kitchen, a royal coronation, or apartheid churches)! Mixing up the two theories — or treating them as arguments *against* God's existence — costs big essay marks.

## The idea in plain language

In everyday speech, people say *"That's just a theory!"* meaning a wild guess. In academic scholarship, a **theory** is a **tested, organised framework explaining WHY and HOW something works**!

Sociologists study religion like looking at a busy community festival through **two different camera lenses**:
1. **Lens 1 — Functionalist Theory:** Focuses on **glue and harmony** (*How does religion keep society stable, united, and hopeful?*).
2. **Lens 2 — Conflict Theory:** Focuses on **power and struggle** (*Who benefits, who gets silenced, or how do oppressed groups challenge injustice using faith?*).

## Part 1: Functionalist Theory vs Conflict Theory

```compare
title: Functionalist Theory vs Conflict Theory of Religion
left: Functionalist Theory (e.g. Émile Durkheim)
- **Core Question:** What helpful **social functions** does religion perform for individuals and society?
- **Key Functions:**
  1. **Social Cohesion:** Shared rituals, feasts, and symbols bind strangers into one supportive community (*ubuntu*, *ummah*, *sangha*).
  2. **Meaning & Comfort:** Gives courage and dignity during crisis, grief, illness, and death (`funerals`).
  3. **Moral Order:** Teaches shared values (`honesty, charity, respect for elders`) keeping communities cooperative.
- **Criticism / Blind Spot:** Can overlook how religion sometimes silences minorities, women, or poor workers!
right: Conflict Theory (e.g. Karl Marx / Critical Sociologists)
- **Core Question:** How does religion interact with **social class, gender, race, and political power**?
- **Key Insights:**
  1. **Justifying Inequality:** Powerful elites can misuse religious teaching (*"obey your rulers; suffer quietly now for reward in heaven"*) to keep peasants/workers docile (*Marx's "opium of the people"*).
  2. **Challenging Injustice (`Liberation`):** Conversely, oppressed people ALSO draw on faith to resist tyranny (e.g. **US Civil Rights**, **South African Liberation Theology / Kairos Document** against apartheid!).
- **Criticism / Blind Spot:** Can reduce deep sincere spiritual devotion merely to economics or power politics!
```

### Crucial Academic Rule (Never Forget This!)
**Sociological theories describe how human institutions behave in society — they CANNOT prove or disprove spiritual claims about God, Nirvana, or ancestors!** You can be a devout believer AND recognise when corrupt leaders misuse religion for power (`Conflict lens`), OR recognise how festivals unite neighbourhoods (`Functionalist lens`).

## Part 2: Morality and Ethics in Religion

```steps
title: Morality vs Ethics — and how religions ground moral choices
step: **1. Morality vs Ethics:** **Morality** refers to **actual standards and daily habits of right and wrong** (*"Do not steal; care for elderly parents"*). **Ethics** is the **systematic philosophical/theological reflection** asking *WHY* an action is right or wrong and how to resolve tough dilemmas!
step: **2. Sources of Religious Ethics:** Sacred scriptures (**Torah, Bible, Qur'an, Bhagavad Gita, Guru Granth Sahib**), exemplary founders/prophets (**Jesus, Prophet Muhammad, the Buddha, Guru Nanak**), **ancestral/communal wisdom (`Ubuntu`)**, and **reason/conscience**!
step: **3. Universal Golden Rule Across Traditions:** Nearly every major religion teaches **reciprocity**: *treat others as you wish to be treated* — grounded in compassion (*karuna* in Buddhism), justice (*tzedakah/zakat* in Judaism & Islam), *ubuntu* (*"umuntu ngumuntu ngabantu"* in ATR), and love of neighbour (*Christianity*).
step: **4. Common Tension Today:** Religions often unite strongly on charity, honesty, and family care — yet debate fiercely inside and across traditions on modern medical/social ethics (`abortion, euthanasia, gender roles, wealth inequality`).
```

## Worked example: Applying Functionalist AND Conflict Lenses to One Event

**Case Study:** *During a severe drought in a rural district, religious leaders organise a multi-faith day of prayer and distribute food parcels donated by local businesses. However, a wealthy landowner insists farmworkers should accept starvation wages as "God's will for the humble."*

1. *Analyse the food parcels and joint prayer using **Functionalist Theory**. (2)*
   - Religion builds **social solidarity, emotional resilience in disaster, and practical charitable care**, uniting community members across denominations to help vulnerable families survive.
2. *Analyse the landowner's statement using **Conflict Theory**. (2)*
   - A powerful economic elite is **misusing religious language (*"God's will"*)** as ideology to silence workers' demand for fair wages and preserve class inequality!

```tryit
title: Test your grasp of Functionalist vs Conflict vs Ethics
prompt: Answer these three quick checks:
1. A sociologist argues: *"Weekly communal worship strengthens neighbourhood trust and cuts loneliness."* Which theory is this?
2. Why is it **wrong** to write *"Conflict theory proves all religions are lies"*?
3. What is the difference between **morality** and **ethics**?
hint: Check **Cohesion (`Functionalist`)**, **sociological vs truth claims**, and **rules/conduct vs moral reasoning**!
answer: 1. **Functionalist theory** (focuses on social cohesion and community wellbeing).
2. Sociological theories analyse **social functions and power relations** in society — they do NOT measure or disprove metaphysical/spiritual truth claims!
3. **Morality** is lived standards of right/wrong conduct; **ethics** is the systematic study and reasoning behind moral principles and dilemmas.
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Thinking **Functionalist theory** means *"religion works well so religion is true"* or **Conflict theory** means *"religion is evil/false"* — both are **analytical social-science lenses**, not theological verdicts!
> - Describing **Conflict theory** only as *"Marx hated religion"* and forgetting how oppressed communities also use religious prophecy to **fight injustice** (*e.g. anti-apartheid churches*)!
> - Treating **morality** and **ethics** as identical without noting that ethics involves *reasoning systematically* through principles and dilemmas.

## Practice

```questions
Q: What does **Functionalism** say are THREE social functions of religion in human communities? (3)
A: 1. Creating **social cohesion and belonging** through shared rituals. 2. Providing **meaning, comfort, and hope** during grief, crisis, or major life transitions. 3. Reinforcing shared **moral norms** that promote cooperation and order.
---
Q: Using **Conflict theory**, explain TWO opposite ways religion can relate to power in society. (4)
A: 1. **Supporting status-quo power:** Dominant elites can use religious teachings to justify inequality, monarchy, caste, or passive obedience among the poor. 2. **Fueling resistance/liberation:** Oppressed communities can draw on prophetic justice teachings to challenge colonialism, slavery, or apartheid.
---
Q: Give TWO examples of how **Ubuntu** in African Traditional Religion or **Zakat / Langar** in Islam/Sikhism connect **belief** to **practical ethics**. (2)
A: In **ATR (`ubuntu`)**, human dignity is relational (*"I am because we are"*), requiring hospitality and care for neighbours/orphans; in **Islam (`zakat`)** and **Sikhism (`langar`)**, belief in God's justice directly requires sharing wealth with the poor and feeding everyone equally regardless of status.
```
