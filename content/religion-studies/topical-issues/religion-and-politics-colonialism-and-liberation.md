> **What you'll learn:** diverse **religious views on political participation**, how **religion and politics influence each other in both directions**, how religion was entangled with **colonialism and imperialism**, and how religious ethics inspired **liberation and democratic transformation** (including in South Africa).
>
> **Prerequisites:** the five religion–state models and sociological theories of religion (**Functionalism vs Conflict**).
>
> **Why this matters:** Paper 2 questions often ask how the **same religion** can both justify oppression (*e.g. colonial domination or apartheid theology*) and inspire courageous resistance (*e.g. Liberation Theology, Desmond Tutu, Mahatma Gandhi, Islamic anti-oppression ethics*)!

## The idea in plain language

Can believers stay out of politics? Even **"staying out of politics"** is a political choice — because silence leaves unjust laws untouched!

Across world religions, believers hold a spectrum of political attitudes:
1. **Active prophetic engagement:** Challenging injustice, corrupt rulers, and poverty as a sacred duty (*e.g. Hebrew prophets, Desmond Tutu, Gandhi's Satyagraha*).
2. **Civic obedience with conscience:** Paying taxes and respecting lawful government (`Romans 13`, Islamic duty of social order), **unless** state law commands injustice (*"obey God rather than human rulers"*).
3. **Withdrawal / quietism:** Focus primarily on inner detachment, prayer, or monastic peace away from party politics.

## Part 1: Two-Way Influence Between Religion and Politics

Religion and politics shape each other continuously — never in a single direction:

| Direction | How It Happens | Concrete Historical / Modern Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Religion -> Politics** | Religious values shape constitutions, anti-slavery laws, liberation struggles, voter mobilisation, and moral critiques of corruption. | - **William Wilberforce & Quaker abolitionists** campaigning against the Atlantic slave trade.<br>- **South African Liberation Theology, SACC, Muslims Against Apartheid, and Hindu/Jewish anti-apartheid activists** opposing racial injustice. |
| **Politics -> Religion** | Political rulers regulate, fund, ban, split, or co-opt religions — or impose ideological tests on clergy! | - **National Party apartheid legislation** dividing mission churches and banning prophetic anti-apartheid leaders.<br>- **Stalin's USSR** closing/demolishing churches and mosques (`state suppression`).<br>- Politicians quoting selective verses at election rallies to claim divine endorsement. |

## Part 2: Religion, Colonialism and Imperialism

Why do historians say European colonialism arrived with **"the Bible and the gun"** (or **"Gold, God, and Glory"**)? Because **religion and empire frequently reinforced each other — yet also created internal contradictions**:

```steps
title: How religion intersected with colonialism and imperialism
step: **1. Theological justification of conquest:** Imperial powers framed colonisation as a *"civilising mission"* to bring Christianity and European culture to indigenous peoples, treating **African Traditional Religion** and indigenous spiritualities as "superstition" or "heathenism".
step: **2. Land, schools, and language:** Mission stations built clinics and taught literacy (training early African leaders like **John Tengo Jabavu, Sol Plaatje, and Albert Luthuli**!), YET colonial authorities also used mission reserves and education to weaken traditional authority and draw people into wage labour.
step: **3. Co-option of religious leaders:** Colonial rulers rewarded compliant churches/clerics with land and state funding while suppressing independent prophets and **African Initiated Churches (Ethiopian & Zionist movements)** who demanded African spiritual autonomy (`"Africa for Africans"`).
step: **4. Birth of anti-colonial conscience:** Ironically, colonised people read the very **sacred scriptures** brought by missionaries — especially **Exodus** (liberation from slavery) and **universal human equality** — and turned them directly against colonial hypocrisy!
```

## Part 3: Religion as a Force for Liberation and Transformation

```compare
title: Two Faces of Faith in South African Political History
left: Apartheid Theology (Conflict Theory Alert)
- Some state-aligned churches and politicians misused selective biblical texts (`such as the Tower of Babel or Noah's sons`) to argue that God ordained **racial separation ("apartheid")**.
- Critics worldwide — including South African theologians like **Beyers Naudé**, **Allan Boesak**, and **Archbishop Desmond Tutu** — condemned this as **heresy** (`1982 Ottawa Declaration & 1985 Kairos Document`)!
right: Liberation & Transformation Movements
- **Kairos Document (1985):** South African theologians rejected both *"State Theology"* (blessing oppression) and *"Church Theology"* (cheap reconciliation without justice), calling for **"Prophetic Theology"** standing with the oppressed.
- **Multi-faith anti-apartheid solidarity:** Imam Abdullah Haron (murdered in detention, 1969), Maulana Farid Esack, Albert Luthuli (Nobel Peace Prize, 1960), Helen Suzman / Rabbi Ben Isaacson, and **Ubuntu-inspired TRC healing** after 1994!
```

### Global Parallel Examples (Great for Essays!)
- **Hindu & Multi-faith Anti-Colonialism (`Mahatma Gandhi`):** Rooted non-violent resistance (**Satyagraha**, *"truth-force"*, and **Ahimsa**, non-harm) first tested in South Africa (`1893–1914`) before leading India's independence struggle.
- **Latin American Liberation Theology (`Gustavo Gutiérrez`):** Taught God's **"preferential option for the poor"** — scripture read from the standpoint of exploited workers and peasants.
- **US Civil Rights Movement (`Rev. Dr Martin Luther King Jr`):** Anchored constitutional equality demands in Christian love (*Agape*), Black church organising, and Gandhian non-violence.

## Worked example: Answering a "Religion Can Harm OR Liberate" Essay

**Prompt (50 marks):** *"Religion has been both an instrument of oppression and a catalyst for liberation in South Africa."* Discuss this statement critically.

1. **Introduction (Stance):** Fully agree that **historical evidence supports both sides**: selective religious doctrines were used to legitimise colonialism and apartheid, **yet** prophetic traditions across Christianity, Islam, Hinduism, Judaism, and African ethics proved decisive in dismantling apartheid and guiding reconciliation.
2. **Oppression side (Evidence):** Mission-colonial disrespect for African Traditional Religion; state-sanctioned **Apartheid Theology** justifying racial separation as God's will.
3. **Liberation side (Evidence):** **Ethiopian AIC movement** reclaiming African leadership; **Albert Luthuli**, **Beyers Naudé**, **Imam Abdullah Haron**, **Desmond Tutu**, and the **1985 Kairos Document** distinguishing *State Theology* from *Prophetic Theology*.
4. **Sociological evaluation (link to Topic 2!):** Show how **Conflict Theory** explains ruling groups weaponising religion for control, while **oppressed believers reclaim liberation ethics** from within those very texts.
5. **Conclusion:** Religion's political impact depends not on sacred texts alone, but on **who interprets them and in whose interest power is wielded**.

```tryit
title: Spot the Three Theologies in the 1985 Kairos Document
prompt: In the 1985 South African **Kairos Document**, theologians criticised two distorted approaches and called for a third. Match each description:
1. Uses scripture selectively to tell citizens to blindly obey an unjust government as "ordained by God".
2. Preaches vague "peace and forgiveness" without confronting apartheid injustice, land theft, or state violence.
3. Reads sacred scripture from the perspective of the oppressed and demands justice, truth, and structural change.
hint: Match to **State Theology**, **Church Theology**, and **Prophetic Theology**.
answer: 1. **State Theology**; 2. **Church Theology**; 3. **Prophetic Theology**.
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Writing as if **all missionaries hated Africans** or **all Christians supported apartheid** — distinguish carefully between **colonial/State Theology** and **liberation/Prophetic voices** like **Beyers Naudé, Albert Luthuli, and the SACC**.
> - Describing **only Christianity** in political essays — Grade 11 CAPS rewards multi-faith depth (`Imam Abdullah Haron, Gandhi's Satyagraha, Jewish & Ubuntu/ATR values`)!

## Practice

```questions
Q: Explain TWO ways **colonialism** negatively affected indigenous religious traditions in Africa. (4)
A: 1. Colonial powers and some missionaries dismissed **African Traditional Religion** as "heathen superstition", discouraging initiation, ancestral veneration, and indigenous healing. 2. Western education and law privileged colonial languages and Christian institutions, sidelining indigenous spiritual authorities.
---
Q: Using ONE example, show how **politics influences religion** and ONE example of **religion influencing politics**. (4)
A: **Politics -> Religion:** Under apartheid, the South African state used security laws and bans to silence critical clergy and promote compliant "State Theology". **Religion -> Politics:** Religious leaders such as Archbishop Desmond Tutu, Imam Abdullah Haron, and the SACC mobilised sanctions, marches, and moral pressure that helped end apartheid.
---
Q: Explain why the **same sacred tradition** can be used both to defend oppression and to fight for liberation. (4)
A: Sacred texts can be interpreted through different social lenses: powerful groups (*Conflict Theory*) select obedience or hierarchy texts to protect privilege (**State Theology**), whereas oppressed communities and prophetic reformers highlight justice, equality, and exodus/liberation themes (**Prophetic Theology / Satyagraha**).
```
