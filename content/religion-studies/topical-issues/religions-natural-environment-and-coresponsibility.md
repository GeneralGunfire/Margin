> **What you'll learn:** how **the natural environment shapes religion** and how **religions shape human care (or exploitation) of nature**, key CAPS scientific-ethical links (**the greenhouse effect, climate change, deforestation, and alternative energy**), **stewardship vs dominion vs deep interconnectedness** across world faiths, and **inter-religious co-responsibility** for global quality of life.
>
> **Prerequisites:** symbolism of nature (`water, fire, mountains, land`) and inter-religious dialogue.
>
> **Why this matters:** Topic 3 closes with Units 13–14 — linking **environmental crisis** and **multi-faith action**. Paper 2 essays love asking whether religions help cause ecological damage (misreading *"dominion"*) or offer our strongest moral antidote (**stewardship & Ubuntu**)!

## The idea in plain language

Look closely at every major religion's calendars, symbols, and ethics, and you realise something remarkable: **human spirituality grew up inside nature** — rivers, desert skies, seasonal harvests, sacred groves, and rains.

Yet today humanity faces an unprecedented crisis: **global warming, biodiversity loss, water scarcity, and pollution**. In Grade 11 CAPS, you explore **two big questions**:
1. **How does nature shape religion, and how does religion shape our treatment of nature?**
2. **How can diverse religions combine their moral authority, networks, and resources to protect Earth and human dignity together?**

## Part 1: Two-Way Influence Between Religion and the Environment

```compare
title: Environment <-> Religion Mutual Influence
left: How the Environment Shapes Religion
- **Geography & symbols:** Desert faiths (`Judaism, Islam`) prize **cool water, oasis gardens, and fasting discipline**; river/forest cultures (`Hinduism, ATR, Buddhism`) revere **Ganga, sacred mountains, rain ceremonies, and ancestral groves**.
- **Seasonal festivals:** Passover/Easter (`spring`), Diwali (`autumn harvest lamps`), and African **first-fruits rituals (`Umkhosi wokweshwama`)** follow ecological cycles!
right: How Religion Shapes the Environment
- **Ethical restraint or exploitation:** Sacred taboos protected forests, springs, and totem animals (`ATR, Jain/Buddhist Ahimsa`); conversely, misinterpreting texts to justify unlimited extraction accelerates damage.
- **Diet & consumption:** Vegetarianism/moderation (`Hinduism, Buddhism, Bahá'í simplicity`) lowers ecological footprints.
```

## Part 2: Environmental Challenges & CAPS Scientific-Ethical Context

To write strong essays, connect **scientific facts** directly to **religious ethics**:

| Challenge | What Happens Physically (`CAPS`) | Why Religious Ethics Cares |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The Greenhouse Effect & Global Warming** | Burning **fossil fuels (`coal, oil, gas`)** traps excess infrared heat via **$\text{CO}_2$ and methane**, driving extreme droughts, floods (`e.g. KZN floods`), and rising sea levels. | **Environmental justice:** Poor communities contributed least to emissions yet suffer first and worst from crop failure and displacement! |
| **Deforestation & Habitat Loss** | Clearing forests removes carbon sinks and wipes out species biodiversity. | Destroys what faiths view as **sacred creation** (`God's handiwork` / interdependent web of life). |
| **Alternative & Renewable Energy** | Shifting toward **solar, wind, hydro, and energy efficiency** cuts greenhouse emissions while expanding clean access for rural communities. | **Stewardship in action:** Churches, mosques, and temples installing **solar roofs** and divesting pension funds from heavy polluters. |

## Part 3: Multi-Faith Teachings on the Environment (`Stewardship & Interconnectedness`)

| Tradition | Key Ecological Concept & Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| **African Traditional Religion (`ATR`)** | **Ubuntu & cosmic kinship:** Humanity belongs *inside* the community of ancestors, living people, unborn generations, land, and animals (*Ukama / relationality*). Sacred groves, rivers, and totem animals carry strict conservation taboos. |
| **Christianity** | **Stewardship (`Genesis 2:15`):** Humans are **caretakers ("till and keep")**, NOT ruthless owners! Modern critics (`Lynn White Jr, 1967`) warned that misreading *"subdue/dominion"* (`Genesis 1:28`) fuelled industrial exploitation; theologians (`Pope Francis's Laudato Si'`) reply true dominion means servant-care. |
| **Islam** | **Khalifah (`Trusteeship`) & Mizan (`Balance`):** God appointed humans as **trustees (`khalifah`)** of Earth (*Qur'an 6:165*) and forbade waste (*israf*) or upsetting cosmic **balance (`mizan`)**. Water and greenery are acts of ongoing charity (*sadaqah jariyah*). |
| **Judaism** | **Tikkun Olam (`Repairing the World`) & Bal Tashchit (`Do Not Destroy`):** Deuteronomy's ban on needlessly cutting fruit trees grounds ethical ecology, alongside **Shabbat** (weekly rest from consuming/producing) and **Tu BiShvat** (New Year of the Trees). |
| **Hinduism** | **Ahimsa (`Non-violence`) & Dharma:** Earth (*Bhudevi*) is revered as sacred mother; rivers, trees, and animals share the same divine presence, encouraging simple living and widespread **vegetarianism**. |
| **Buddhism** | **Pratītyasamutpāda (`Dependent Origination`) & Compassion:** Nothing exists independently; harming rivers or forests ultimately harms ourselves. Greed/overconsumption is a root spiritual poison (`tanha`). |
| **Bahá'í Faith** | **Harmony of Science & Religion + Unity of Humanity:** Nature reflects God's attributes; solving climate change requires global spiritual unity beyond narrow nationalism. |

## Part 4: Inter-Religious Co-Responsibility & Resources for Quality of Life

Why must religions work **together** (`Unit 14`) rather than alone on climate change, poverty, HIV/AIDS, and peace?

```steps
title: How religions pool resources for human & ecological quality of life
step: **1. Moral authority & behavioural reach:** Over 80% of humanity identifies with a religious community — sermons, Khutbahs, and teachings reach billions of households every week far faster than policy memos.
step: **2. Infrastructure & human capital:** Religious bodies run **schools, clinics, soup kitchens, youth groups, burial societies, and community halls** (`e.g. SAFCEI — Southern African Faith Communities' Environment Institute`).
step: **3. Co-responsibility principle:** No single religion owns the atmosphere, rivers, or poverty crisis; saving our shared home requires **joint advocacy** (*e.g. multi-faith declarations before UN COP climate summits*).
step: **4. Healing & resilience:** Faith partnerships tackle **food insecurity, gender-based violence, drug abuse, and pandemic care** without forcing recipients to convert!
```

## Worked example: Answering the "Does Dominion Cause Destruction?" Critique

**Prompt (50 marks):** *Historian Lynn White Jr argued that religious beliefs about human "dominion" over Earth caused the ecological crisis. Evaluate this claim from multiple religious perspectives.*

1. **Introduction (Balanced Stance):** Acknowledge that **arrogant misinterpretations** of *"dominion"* did excuse colonial/industrial exploitation (*partially true*), yet argue strongly that **authentic teachings across Christianity, Islam, Judaism, Hinduism, Buddhism, and ATR** condemn greed and teach **stewardship, trusteeship (`khalifah`), and cosmic kinship**.
2. **Evidence supporting the critique:** Industrial nations historically invoked *"subdue the Earth"* (`Genesis 1:28`) as limitless permission to mine, pollute, and clear indigenous lands; fossil-fuel lifestyles clash with ecological limits.
3. **Counter-evidence from diverse faiths:**
   - **Christianity & Judaism:** *Genesis 2:15* ("care for and guard"), *Bal Tashchit* ("do not waste/destroy"), and *Laudato Si'* ("cry of the earth and cry of the poor").
   - **Islam:** *Khalifah* (accountable trustee) and *Mizan* (ecological balance).
   - **ATR, Hinduism, Buddhism:** Totem taboos, *Ubuntu* across unborn generations, *Ahimsa*, and *dependent origination*.
4. **Conclusion:** Economic greed and fossil-fuel consumerism — NOT faith itself — drive climate breakdown, and **multi-faith co-responsibility (`SAFCEI`)** is one of humanity's vital tools for clean energy and justice.

```tryit
title: Match the Ecological Concept to Its Religion
prompt: Identify the religion and explain how each term encourages environmental care:
1. **Khalifah & Mizan**
2. **Bal Tashchit & Tikkun Olam**
3. **Dependent Origination & Ahimsa**
hint: Think **Islam**, **Judaism**, and **Buddhism / Hinduism**.
answer: 1. **Islam** — humans are accountable **trustees** (`khalifah`) obligated to protect Earth's **balance** (`mizan`). 2. **Judaism** — prohibition against needless waste/destruction (`Bal Tashchit`) and duty to repair the world (`Tikkun Olam`). 3. **Buddhism / Hinduism** — all life is **interconnected** (`dependent origination`) and deserves **non-harm** (`Ahimsa`).
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Writing an essay that sounds like a **Geography-only summary of global warming** without weaving in **religious ethics, sacred texts/concepts, and interfaith action (`SAFCEI`)**.
> - Blaming *all* religion for pollution without showing how **consumer greed** and **misuse of dominion** differ from **stewardship (`Genesis 2:15`)**.

## Practice

```questions
Q: Explain ONE way the **natural environment shapes religion** and ONE way **religion shapes the environment**. (4)
A: **Environment -> Religion:** Sacred seasons, agricultural festivals (`e.g. harvest/spring`), and symbols (`water, desert, mountains, rivers`) emerge directly from local geography and climate. **Religion -> Environment:** Religious teachings on stewardship (`khalifah`), sacred groves/totems in ATR, and dietary restraint (`Ahimsa`) encourage conservation and limit waste.
---
Q: Define **inter-religious co-responsibility** and name TWO resources religions possess to improve quality of life. (4)
A: **Inter-religious co-responsibility** means distinct religions partnering equally to address shared social and ecological crises (climate change, poverty, injustice) without seeking converts. Two resources are **vast community networks/trust** (schools, clinics, halls) and **moral authority** capable of shifting daily habits (`water, energy, generosity`).
---
Q: Suggest THREE practical actions a local multi-faith coalition in South Africa can take to cut greenhouse gas emissions and build climate justice. (6)
A: 1. Install **solar panels and rainwater tanks** at shared churches, mosques, synagogues, and temples. 2. Run joint community education campaigns on **reducing waste, planting indigenous trees, and supporting renewable energy**. 3. Advocate to local municipalities for safe public transport and flood-resilient housing for vulnerable low-income neighbourhoods.
```
