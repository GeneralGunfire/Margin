## The idea in plain language

When diverse religions live side by side in trade ports, empires, or democratic cities like Johannesburg and Cape Town, they **never stay isolated**:
- Some religions actively seek to share their message across all nations (**missionary**).
- Others focus on preserving covenant, ancestry, or community heritage rather than recruiting outsiders (**non-missionary**).
- Along the way, faiths **borrow, adapt, clash, or cooperate** — and scholars use **seven precise concepts** to tell those interactions apart!

## Part 1: Missionary vs Non-Missionary Religions

| Category | Core Goal | Religions Named in CAPS |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Missionary Religions** | Believe their spiritual message is universal and actively invite people of all cultures to join. | **Christianity, Islam, Buddhism, Bahá'í Faith** |
| **Non-Missionary Religions** | Tied closely to birth, lineage, community, or regional heritage; generally do NOT organise campaigns to convert outsiders. | **African Traditional Religion, Judaism, Hinduism** *(note: individuals may still study or adopt them in some modern branches, yet organized proselytising missions are absent!)* |

### Two Classic Examples of Strong Mutual Influence (`CAPS Focus`)
1. **Sikhism (`Punjab, 15th–16th C`):** Founded by **Guru Nanak** in a region where **Hinduism and Islam** lived closely together. While Sikhs affirm Sikhism as a distinct revealed faith (*One God, rejection of caste, equality, Langar community kitchen*), scholars note historical interaction with both devotional Hindu *Bhakti* and Sufi Islamic mysticism in Punjab.
2. **African Initiated Churches (`AICs`, e.g. Zionist & Ethiopian Churches):** Grew in South Africa where **Christianity** met **African Traditional Religion** — preserving Christian scripture/baptism while incorporating African drumming, dreams/prophecy, spiritual healing, and communal warmth!

## Part 2: Seven Crucial Interaction Concepts (Do NOT Confuse Them!)

```steps
title: Precise CAPS definitions for Paper 1 short & source questions
step: **1. Mission:** An organised religious effort to serve and share beliefs with others (`education, healthcare, preaching`).
step: **2. Evangelism:** Enthusiastically sharing the "Good News" (`gospel`) of one's faith by invitation and personal witness.
step: **3. Proselytisation:** **Aggressive, manipulative, or coercive pressure** to convert someone away from their own faith (e.g. tying food aid or jobs to conversion) — often condemned in interfaith ethics!
step: **4. Revitalisation:** Renewing and reforming a religion **from within** to recover spiritual vitality or adapt to modern crises (`e.g. Christian Reformation, Islamic revival movements`).
step: **5. Ecumenism:** Dialogue, cooperation, and unity **AMONG DIFFERENT DENOMINATIONS WITHIN THE SAME RELIGION** (especially Catholic, Orthodox, and Protestant churches working together — e.g. *World Council of Churches*, *SACC*!).
step: **6. Syncretism:** Blending elements from **TWO OR MORE DIFFERENT RELIGIONS** into a combined practice or new synthesis. *(Beware: outsiders sometimes use "syncretism" negatively — in academic study, describe it neutrally as religious blending!)*
step: **7. Religious Colonialism / Imperialism:** Imposing one's religion alongside **political, military, or cultural domination** over colonised peoples, devaluing indigenous beliefs as "heathen".
```

## Part 3: Attitudes to Other Religions & Inter-Religious Dialogue

How do religions interpret one another when they meet? Scholars identify three main attitudes:

```compare
title: Exclusivism vs Inclusivism vs Pluralism
left: Exclusivism & Inclusivism
- **Exclusivism:** *"Only my religion possesses truth/salvation; all others are mistaken."* Makes open dialogue very hard (often breeds hostility).
- **Inclusivism:** *"My religion holds the fullest truth, YET other religions contain genuine rays of truth and goodness."* Leaves room for respectful friendship.
right: Pluralism & Authentic Inter-Religious Dialogue
- **Pluralism:** *"Different religions are valid, distinct paths responding to the Ultimate Reality."* Encourages equal dignity!
- **Goals of Inter-Religious Dialogue:** **Listen genuinely to understand** (NOT secretly trying to convert the other person!), dismantle stereotypes, protect religious freedom, and work together on poverty, peace, and ecology!
```

## Worked example: Distinguishing Ecumenism, Syncretism & Dialogue in a Scenario

**Scenario:** *In a South African township, Methodist, Anglican, and Catholic congregations hold a joint winter soup kitchen (**Action A**). Meanwhile, the local mosque, synagogue, temple, and church form a neighbourhood Peace Forum to stop xenophobic violence (**Action B**).*

1. *Which CAPS concept describes **Action A**, and why? (2)* -> **Ecumenism** — different denominations *within one religion* (Christianity) cooperating in unity!
2. *Which concept describes **Action B**, and why? (2)* -> **Inter-religious dialogue / co-operation** — *distinct world religions* partnering respectfully for shared community peace without trying to convert one another.

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Confusing **Ecumenism** (*unity WITHIN one religion*, e.g. Christian churches) with **Inter-religious dialogue** (*cooperation BETWEEN different religions*, e.g. Islam + Hinduism + Christianity)!
> - Calling **every friendly invitation to church/mosque** *"proselytisation"* — proselytisation specifically implies **pressuring or manipulating** someone, whereas **evangelism/mission** can be respectful and peaceful.
> - Thinking **inter-religious dialogue** means *"everyone must blend into one single religion"* — real dialogue respects each religion's unique differences while building mutual understanding!

## Practice

```questions
Q: Classify the following as **missionary** or **non-missionary** religions according to CAPS: *Islam, Judaism, Buddhism, African Traditional Religion*. (4)
A: **Missionary:** Islam, Buddhism. **Non-missionary:** Judaism, African Traditional Religion.
---
Q: Explain the difference between **ecumenism** and **syncretism**, giving one example of each. (4)
A: **Ecumenism** is cooperation among branches **within the same religion** (e.g. Catholic, Anglican, and Methodist churches partnering in the SACC). **Syncretism** is the blending of beliefs/practices from **two or more distinct religions** into a combined form.
---
Q: State TWO essential ground rules for successful **inter-religious dialogue**. (2)
A: 1. Participants listen genuinely to **understand**, never to ambush or covertly convert each other. 2. Every faith is treated with **equal dignity**, while participants remain honest about both shared values and real doctrinal differences.
```
