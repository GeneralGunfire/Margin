> **What you'll learn:** the difference between **short-, medium-, and long-term life goals**, the **five practical steps of SMART goal-setting**, **problem-solving and perseverance** when setbacks hit, **prioritising major life goals** (*family, marriage, parenting, career, relationships*), and how **personal values shape everyday choices and long-term direction** (*CAPS Term 1 · LO Paper Section B & C*).
>
> **Prerequisites:** Grade 10 self-awareness and personal values basics.
>
> **Why this matters:** Life Orientation **Section C (`30 marks`)** rarely asks you to just define *"short-term goal"* — it drops you into a stressed Grade 11 learner's story and asks you to **diagnose why their vague dream failed, rebuild a clear goal plan, and show how their values guide tough sacrifices**!

## The idea in plain language

*"I want to be rich and successful one day"* is **a wish, not a goal** — because it has no deadline, no measurable target, and zero action for Tuesday night!

When your **personal values** (`honesty, family care, independence, health, service`) steer your **goal plan**, every small daily decision (`skipping a distraction, saving R50, asking a teacher for help`) lines up behind a future you genuinely want to build.

## Part 1: Short-, Medium- & Long-Term Goals (`The Goal Ladder`)

| Timeframe | Realistic Horizon | Grade 11 Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Short-term goal** | **Days to 6 months** (`this week or this term`) | Pass Term 1 Maths above 65%; stick to a 3-day fitness routine; finish LO Source Task 4 days early! |
| **Medium-term goal** | **6 months to ~2–3 years** (`Grade 11 -> post-Matric year 1`) | Achieve a Bachelor's NSC pass with 32 APS; secure a learnership/bursary application; earn driver's licence. |
| **Long-term goal** | **3 to 10+ years** | Complete nursing/engineering qualification; build financial independence; establish a supportive family home. |

*Notice how **short-term goals act as the rungs leading UP to medium- and long-term goals** — skip the bottom rungs and the top stays out of reach!*

## Part 2: Steps in Goal-Setting (`SMART + Action + Perseverance`)

```steps
title: Five steps to plan and achieve meaningful life goals
step: **1. Clarify your values & prioritise:** Ask *"What genuinely matters to ME — not just what wins likes online?"* Rank competing priorities carefully across **career, relationships, family, health, and future marriage/parenting** so one area does not wreck all others!
step: **2. Make every goal SMART:**
- **S**pecific (*exact target: "raise Physical Sciences from 48% to 65%"*)
- **M**easurable (*how you check progress: past-paper marks*)
- **A**chievable (*stretching yet realistic given your current baseline*)
- **R**elevant (*aligned with your career & personal values*)
- **T**ime-bound (*exact deadline: "by June exam"*)
step: **3. Break into weekly action steps:** Turn *"65% in Science"* into *"solve 4 mechanics problems every Tuesday & Thursday at 17:00"*.
step: **4. Apply problem-solving when obstacles hit:** Expect barriers (`load-shedding, failed test, noisy home, lack of transport`). Identify the obstacle calmly, brainstorm realistic workarounds (`study at school library, download offline PDFs`), and pick the best fix!
step: **5. Practise perseverance & persistence:** **Perseverance** means continuing steadily *despite* setbacks or slow results; **persistence** means refusing to quit after one bad mark while smartly adjusting failing study methods (`not stubbornly repeating the same mistake!`).
```

## Part 3: How Personal Values Shape Choices & Prioritising Life Goals

```compare
title: Values -> Choices vs Competing Life Priorities
left: How Personal Values Guide Choices
- A learner valuing **integrity** refuses leaked exam tips or plagiarism even when anxious.
- A learner valuing **family responsibility** turns down an expensive night out so younger siblings have quiet study help and grocery money stays safe.
- A learner valuing **long-term independence** chooses afternoon skills classes over fleeting status trends!
right: Prioritising Major Life Goals (`Family, Career, Relationships`)
- **Timing matters:** Choosing a **career/education foundation first** (`or pacing relationships responsibly`) equips young adults emotionally and financially **before** taking on **marriage or parenting** responsibilities!
- **Balance prevents regret:** Success in work at the cost of destroying mental health or trusted family relationships leaves life lopsided.
```

## Worked example: Rebuilding a Vague Goal Plan (`LO Section C Style`)

**Scenario:** *Kagiso (17) wants to become an **electrical technician** one day (`Long-term`), yet spends every afternoon gaming, scored 38% in Mathematics in Grade 10, and feels hopeless saying: "I'll never qualify anyway, why bother trying?"*

1. *Explain ONE reason Kagiso's mindset lacks **perseverance**, and show how **problem-solving** breaks the cycle (`4`).* -> He interprets one low 38% mark as permanent failure instead of an obstacle to fix. Applying **problem-solving**, he can diagnose which Grade 10 topics failed (`algebra/equations`), ask his teacher or a peer study group for targeted help, and practise daily!
2. *Rewrite ONE **short-term SMART goal** for Term 1 (`4`).* -> **"I will complete 30 minutes of Grade 11 Maths algebra practice four afternoons a week after school and attend Wednesday extra help so my Term 1 Maths test rises from 38% to at least 52% by March."** (`Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound!`).

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Writing goals with **no time limit or measurement** (*"study harder"*, *"do better"*) — Section C markers look specifically for **specific actions + measurable indicators + clear deadlines**!
> - Confusing **perseverance** (`continuing steadily through hardship while adjusting smartly`) with **blind stubbornness** (`ignoring feedback when a method isn't working`).
> - Ignoring **relationships, marriage, and parenting** when asked to discuss prioritising life goals (`CAPS specifically includes these alongside career!`).

## Practice

```questions
Q: Distinguish between **short-term**, **medium-term**, and **long-term** goals, giving ONE Grade 11 example of each connected to one career dream. (6)
A: **Short-term (`weeks to 6 months`):** Raise Biology score from 55% to 68% in Term 1 via weekly summaries. **Medium-term (`6 months to 3 years`):** Earn a Bachelor's NSC pass and gain admission into a Nursing degree. **Long-term (`3–10 years`):** Qualify as a registered nurse and lead a community primary-care clinic.
---
Q: Explain TWO ways **personal values** influence a Grade 11 learner's daily choices and life goals. (4)
A: 1. Values act as an **internal compass** deciding how you spend free time and friendships (`e.g. valuing health over peer status avoids risky binge drinking`). 2. Values keep motivation steady during setbacks (`e.g. valuing family dignity fuels persistent study toward stable work`).
---
Q: Suggest THREE practical **problem-solving steps** a learner facing noisy home conditions can use instead of giving up on their academic goals. (6)
A: 1. **Identify controllable options** (`stay 1 hour later at the school library/classroom after final bell`). 2. **Communicate respectfully with family** to negotiate a quiet 90-minute evening study window. 3. **Adapt study timings** (`wake 45 minutes earlier while the house is quiet and minds are fresh`).
```
