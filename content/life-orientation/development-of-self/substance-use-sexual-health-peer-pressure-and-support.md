> **What you'll learn:** how **risky behaviours** (*substance use/alcohol/vaping, unprotected sexual activity, STIs and HIV, unintended teenage pregnancy, peer coercion, and overwhelming distress/teenage suicide risk*) affect long-term well-being, **practical ways to resist negative peer pressure confidently**, and **confidential, non-judgmental support channels** in South Africa (*CAPS Term 3 · LO*).
>
> **Prerequisites:** Lesson 121 (`balanced lifestyle and personal responsibility`).
>
> **Why this matters:** LO Section B & C scenarios often describe a teenager pulled by peer pressure into drinking or unsafe sex, OR a friend showing silent signs of severe emotional distress — asking you for **realistic, compassionate, practical guidance without preaching or shaming**!

## The idea in plain language

Teenage years bring **strong social pressure** alongside **real adult-scale consequences**. Nobody wakes up planning to develop addiction, drop out of school, or hurt inside silence — most risky traps start with **one unguarded moment** (`"Just one drink"`, `"Everyone else is doing it"`, or `"I'm a burden if I ask for help"`).

Knowing the facts calmly **before** pressure hits — plus having **exact phone numbers and exit scripts ready** — protects your health, your dignity, and your friends' lives.

## Part 1: Understanding Key Risks & Making Informed Decisions

| Risk Area | Factual Risks (`Health, Legal & Future`) | Protective & Responsible Actions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Substance Use (`Alcohol, Tobacco/Vaping, Cannabis, Nyaope/Drugs`)** | Slows judgment (`driving/consent risks`), damages developing teen brains and lungs, causes addiction dependency, hurts school focus, triggers legal/disciplinary consequences. | Set pre-decided limits (**zero alcohol/drugs under 18**); travel home **only with sober drivers**; exit unsafe parties immediately (`blame strict parents if needed!`); seek confidential recovery support early. |
| **Unprotected Sexual Activity, STIs & HIV** | Risks **Sexually Transmitted Infections (`STIs`)**, **HIV transmission**, and **unintended teenage pregnancy** (`interrupting schooling, housing, and career readiness`). | Practise **clear informed consent** (`nobody owes sexual access in exchange for gifts, lifts, or grades!` — legal age of consent in SA is 16, and sex under coercion is always abuse); **delay sexual debut**, use **barrier protection (`condoms`) consistently & dual protection**, and get regular confidential clinic testing & **ART/PrEP advice**. |
| **Overwhelming Distress & Suicide Risk** | Persistent hopelessness, severe bullying, family conflict, trauma, or untreated depression can leave a young person feeling trapped (`NOT personal weakness or "attention-seeking"`!). | **Take every warning sign seriously** (`withdrawal, giving away belongings, saying "everyone would be better off without me"`). Listen warmly **without lecturing**, keep them safe with a trusted adult, and connect immediately to professional crisis helplines! |

## Part 2: Handling Negative Peer Pressure (`Practical Exit Strategies`)

Saying **"no"** to friends you actually like is hard! Replace awkward silence with **confident, calm strategies**:

```steps
title: Five practical ways to refuse peer pressure without starting a fight
step: **1. Use a clean "broken-record" refusal:** *"No thanks, I'm good with water tonight"* (`keep repeating calmly without over-apologising`).
step: **2. Blame external rules as an easy shield:** *"My coach/dad checks in randomly and I'll lose my spot — not worth it."*
step: **3. Suggest a better alternative:** *"Let's grab food / watch the game at my place instead."*
step: **4. Pair up with one aligned friend (`Buddy system`):** Agree *before* arriving at any event that you look out for each other and leave together if things turn messy!
step: **5. Walk away immediately from coercion:** Real friends respect your boundaries; anyone insulting you for refusing alcohol, drugs, or sex is proving they value your compliance more than your safety.
```

## Worked example: Responding to Peer Pressure & A Struggling Friend (`Section C`)

**Scenario:** *At a Grade 11 birthday braai, older teens hand **Sipho** a bottle of vodka and mock him for drinking soda (`Part A`). Inside, his close friend **Lerato** whispers quietly that she hasn't slept in days, feels worthless since her exams slipped, and wishes she wouldn't wake up (`Part B`).*

1. *Advise **Sipho** on TWO calm strategies to resist peer pressure (`Part A`, `4`).* ->
   - **Confident boundary + humour/exit:** State firmly *"No thanks, I'm driving/training tomorrow"* (`or use the buddy system to step outside and go home safely if mocking escalates`).
   - **Re-evaluate the social circle:** Recognise true friendship never requires risking alcohol poisoning or injury to belong.
2. *Explain **THREE urgent, compassionate steps** Sipho must take for **Lerato** (`Part B`, `6`).* ->
   - **Listen without judging or dismissing:** Never say *"snap out of it"* — let her know she matters and is not alone.
   - **Involve a trusted adult immediately (`never keep suicide danger a secret!`):** Tell Lerato's parent, school counsellor, or trusted teacher straight away while staying beside her calmly.
   - **Connect to professional crisis care:** Call **SADAG (`0800 567 567`)** or **Childline (`116`)** with her right away so trained clinicians help!

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Writing preachy moral insults (`"bad girls get pregnant"` / `"weak teens do drugs"`) in LO Section C essays — examiners mark **factual consequences, empathy, practical refusal strategies, and real support steps**, NOT shaming!
> - Saying *"HIV is an instant death sentence"* — outdated myth! Modern **antiretroviral treatment (`ART`)** allows long, healthy lives when started promptly at local clinics.
> - Suggesting *"just tell your sad friend to cheer up and ignore it"* when clear depression/suicide warnings appear — always link to **trusted adults + SADAG/Childline/clinic care**!

## Practice

```questions
Q: Explain TWO ways **alcohol or drug use** increases a teenager's vulnerability to **other** life risks (`e.g. road safety and sexual health`). (4)
A: 1. Alcohol and drugs impair **reaction speed, judgment, and coordination**, sharply raising car/pedestrian crash and injury risk. 2. Intoxication lowers awareness of boundaries and **consent**, increasing the risk of unprotected sex, STIs/HIV, unintended pregnancy, or sexual assault.
---
Q: Give THREE practical assertive refusal techniques a Grade 11 learner can use when friends pressure them to skip school or vape. (6)
A: 1. **Firm calm "No" + repeat** without lengthy arguments (*"Not for me, thanks"*). 2. **Change the activity or location** (`walk toward safe peers/class or suggest grabbing snacks instead`). 3. **Have a pre-arranged exit plan** (`call a parent or trusted sibling immediately when uncomfortable`).
---
Q: A classmate tells you privately that life feels pointless and they thought about ending their life. Outline THREE responsible steps you should take right away. (6)
A: 1. **Stay calm, listen attentively without judging**, and reassure them they matter. 2. **Do NOT keep it secret** — alert a trusted adult immediately (`school counsellor, LO teacher, parent/guardian`) so they are not left alone. 3. Help contact a crisis service such as **SADAG (`0800 567 567`)**, **Childline (`116`)**, or a local clinic/hospital emergency unit.
```
