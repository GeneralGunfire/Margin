> **What you'll learn:** the **types of relationships** (`family, friendships, romantic, formal/authority, digital/online`) and their **changing nature**, how relationships either **contribute to or harm personal well-being**, **rights and responsibilities** inside healthy relationships, **social and cultural views** shaping relationship norms, and **critically evaluating the media's impact on beliefs about relationships** (*CAPS Term 1 · LO*).
>
> **Prerequisites:** Lesson 111 (`personal values and goals`).
>
> **Why this matters:** LO Section B & C scenarios frequently examine **controlling or toxic friendships/dating**, **online social-media pressure (`filters, ghosting, sexting, fake "relationship goals"`)**, and **how respecting boundaries protects both emotional and academic well-being**!

## The idea in plain language

Healthy relationships are like clean oxygen: they give you **confidence, encouragement, honest accountability, and safety to grow** into your best self!
Harmful relationships are like carbon monoxide: they slowly drain your **self-esteem, study focus, friendships, and dignity** — often disguised as *"loyalty"* (`"If you really loved me, you'd share your password / ignore your friends"`).

## Part 1: Types of Relationships & Their Changing Nature

- **Family (`parents, guardians, siblings, elders`):** Shift in Grade 11–12 from child-dependency toward **mutual negotiation and growing independence** (`balancing house rules with emerging adult trust`).
- **Friendships & Peer Groups:** Can evolve as values and goals mature — outgrowing a friendship that pulls you toward dangerous shortcuts is **normal growth, not betrayal**!
- **Romantic / Dating Relationships:** Move from casual crushes toward deeper mutual respect, consent, and clear boundaries (`must NEVER replace schoolwork, family, or personal safety!`).
- **Formal / Mentor Relationships (`teachers, coaches, employers`):** Built on professional boundaries, respect, and guidance.
- **Online / Social Media Connections:** Fast, convenient — yet easier to misunderstand tone, conceal fake identities, or blur privacy boundaries!

## Part 2: Healthy (`Contributing`) vs Harmful (`Detrimental`) Relationships

```compare
title: Spot the Difference — Healthy Support vs Harmful Control
left: Relationships That Build Well-Being
- **Mutual respect & equality:** Both voices matter; neither insults, shames, or bullies the other.
- **Honest open communication & trust:** You can disagree calmly without fear of revenge or silent manipulation.
- **Healthy boundaries & space:** Partners/friends celebrate your study goals, family ties, and private time!
- **Accountability & encouragement:** Good friends challenge bad choices respectfully and cheer your progress.
right: Relationships That Harm Well-Being
- **Control, jealousy & isolation:** Checking your phone secretly, telling you what to wear, or cutting off your friends/family.
- **Emotional manipulation & guilt-tripping:** Threatening self-harm or breakup unless you obey unfair demands.
- **Physical, verbal, or sexual coercion:** Violating **consent** (`anything less than clear, willing, sober agreement is NOT consent!`), mocking body/intelligence, or sharing private photos.
- **Codependent pressure:** Encouraging cheating, substance abuse, or truancy as a "loyalty test".
```

## Part 3: Rights, Responsibilities, Culture & Media Influence

### Rights & Responsibilities Go Together!
| Every Person's Right in a Relationship | Matching Personal Responsibility |
| :--- | :--- |
| Right to **physical & emotional safety** | Treat others with patience; **never intimidate, hit, or insult** |
| Right to **privacy & bodily autonomy (`consent`)** | Respect others' private messages, boundaries, and **"No"** immediately |
| Right to **be heard & express feelings** | **Listen empathetically** without mocking or interrupting |
| Right to **personal growth & friendships** | Support partner/friends' studies and own goals without possessive jealousy |

- **Social & Cultural Views:** Different South African families and cultural/religious traditions hold varied expectations around **dating age, gender roles, parental involvement, *lobola* / courtship customs, and elder respect**. Healthy critical reflection honours **cultural roots that affirm dignity (`Ubuntu`)** while firmly challenging **any custom or peer norm used to excuse abuse or inequality**!
- **Media's Impact on Beliefs About Relationships (`Critique Carefully!`):**
  - Movies, music videos, and TikTok/Instagram influencers frequently glamourise **instant perfection (`zero conflict`), possessive jealousy framed as "romance", hyper-materialism (`status gifts buying love`), or unreal beauty ideals**.
  - **Media literacy rule:** Compare media fantasy against **real-life trust, shared respect, and emotional safety**!

## Worked example: Advising a Peer Facing Social-Media & Dating Pressure (`Section C`)

**Scenario:** *Ayanda (`17`) notices her boyfriend gets angry whenever she studies with male classmates, demands her Instagram password *"to prove trust"*, and quotes viral couples online claiming "real love means sharing everything 24/7". Ayanda's Term 1 marks drop.*

1. *Identify **TWO harmful relationship behaviours** and **ONE media myth** in the scenario (`3`).* -> **Harmful behaviours:** Isolating her from study peers (`possessive control`) + demanding her private Instagram password (`violating privacy`). **Media myth:** Viral social clips equating **zero privacy/24-7 surveillance** with *"true love"*.
2. *Advise Ayanda on **THREE practical steps** to protect her rights and well-being (`6`).* ->
   - **Assert clear boundaries:** Explain calmly that trust respects **privacy (`keep passwords personal`)** and supports **educational friendships**!
   - **Test his reaction:** Healthy partners accept respectful boundaries; if anger, threats, or guilt-tripping escalate, **end the unsafe relationship** with trusted adult/family support.
   - **Seek trusted guidance:** Talk openly with a school counsellor, Life Orientation teacher, or parent to rebalance study routines safely.

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Saying *"all social media is toxic"* — balanced LO answers recognise social platforms also connect supportive study groups and distant family, **provided learners think critically** about edited fantasies!
> - Listing relationship **rights** (`to be respected, to privacy`) without pairing them with matching **responsibilities** (`to respect others' privacy and boundaries too`).
> - Confusing **normal changing friendships** as we grow with deliberate cruelty — growing in different study/career directions is natural.

## Practice

```questions
Q: Explain TWO ways relationships can **change naturally** between Grade 8 and Grade 11, and how learners should handle those shifts positively. (4)
A: 1. **Parent–teen relationships** shift toward greater personal autonomy and responsibility (`negotiating curfews and study independence constructively`). 2. **Friendships** shift as learners discover distinct academic goals and values (`maintaining politeness toward old peers while investing energy in supportive, goal-aligned friends`).
---
Q: Contrast **three signs of a healthy relationship** with **three warning signs of a harmful relationship**. (6)
A: **Healthy:** (1) Respectful two-way communication; (2) Honoured personal privacy and clear consent; (3) Mutual encouragement of school and family goals. **Harmful:** (1) Possessive jealousy/isolation from friends; (2) Threats, insults, or guilt-tripping; (3) Demanding passwords or pressuring risky behaviour as proof of loyalty.
---
Q: Critically discuss TWO ways **popular media (`TV, music videos, social platforms`)** distorts young people's beliefs about healthy romantic relationships. (4)
A: 1. Media frequently romanticises **jealous control and dramatic conflict** as "passion", blinding teens to emotional abuse. 2. Curated influencer reels link love strictly to **expensive gifts and flawless looks**, masking the real work of communication, honesty, and mutual respect.
```
