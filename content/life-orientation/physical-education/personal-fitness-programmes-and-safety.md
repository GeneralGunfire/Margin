>
> **Prerequisites:** Lesson 121 (`balanced lifestyle & sports nutrition`).
>
> **Why this matters:** In Grade 11 LO, Physical Education is **worth `20%` (`20 marks`) of every single term's mark** (`through your PET`), **AND** fitness concepts (`FITT, components of fitness, warm-up/cool-down physiology, injury prevention`) appear directly in written June and November exam papers!

## The idea in plain language

Starting a fitness plan by sprinting `10 km` on Day 1 without stretching or running shoes is the fastest way to pull a hamstring and quit by Day 3! A proper **personal fitness programme** measures where your body stands today (`baseline fitness`), picks **clear health or sport targets**, increases load **gradually**, and protects joints with smart warm-up, hydration, and recovery.

## Part 1: Health-Related vs Skill-Related Fitness Components (`Know Both Tables!`)

Examiners love testing whether you can classify real movements accurately:

```compare
title: Health-Related vs Skill-Related Fitness (`CAPS Physical Education`)
left: Health-Related Fitness (`Needed for Daily Long-Term Health`)
- **Cardiovascular / Aerobic Endurance:** Heart and lungs delivering oxygen smoothly during sustained movement (`jogging, swimming, skipping, brisk walking`).
- **Muscular Strength:** Maximum force a muscle exerts in one effort (`push-ups, carrying heavy loads, squats`).
- **Muscular Endurance:** Muscles repeating sub-maximal contractions without tiring fast (`1-minute plank, repeated lunges, sit-ups`).
- **Flexibility:** Full, pain-free range of motion around a joint (`hamstring/shoulder stretches` — prevents tears!).
- **Body Composition:** Healthy ratio of lean muscle, bone, and water to body fat (`supported by regular movement + whole nutrition`).
right: Skill-Related Fitness (`Boosts Sport & Quick Coordination`)
- **Agility:** Changing body direction rapidly under full control (`shuttle runs, netball/rugby dodging cones`).
- **Balance (`Static & Dynamic`):** Holding posture steady while still or moving (`landing cleanly after a jump, standing crane pose`).
- **Coordination:** Eyes, hands, and feet working together seamlessly (`dribbling a soccer ball, catching a cricket ball`).
- **Power:** Combining **Strength x Speed** explosively (`vertical jump for rebound, shot-put throw, sprint start`).
- **Reaction Time & Speed:** How quickly you respond to a whistle/signal (`reaction time`) and cover distance fast (`speed`).
```

## Part 2: Designing Your Programme with the `FITT` Principle

Before writing a 6-week personal fitness plan, apply **FITT** to match your realistic starting level:

| FITT Element | Plain Meaning | Safe Grade 11 Beginner Example (`16–17 yrs`) | Progression Rule (`Don't Overtrain!`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **F — Frequency** | *How often* you train per week. | `3–4` structured sessions per week (`with 1–2 rest/light-walk days`). | Add `1` extra day only after 3–4 steady weeks. |
| **I — Intensity** | *How hard* your heart and muscles work (`talk test` or `% max heart rate`). | **Moderate** (`can still speak short sentences without gasping`: brisk jog, circuit training). | Increase pace or reps gradually (`~5–10%` per week). |
| **T — Time** | *How long* each active session lasts. | `30–45 minutes` total (`5–8 min warm-up + 20–30 min main set + 5–7 min cool-down`). | Build duration before pushing sprint speed. |
| **T — Type** | *Which specific exercises* match your goal. | Cycling/jogging/skipping (`cardio`), bodyweight squats/push-ups (`strength`), yoga/static stretching (`flexibility`). | Mix **aerobic + strength + mobility** to avoid overuse injuries! |

## Part 3: Warm-Up, Cool-Down, Hydration & Safety Rules (`T1 & T3`)

```steps
title: Five non-negotiable physical activity safety habits
step: **1. Dynamic Warm-Up (`5–8 min BEFORE main exercise`):** Raise body temperature and joint lubrication gradually using **light jogging, arm circles, high knees, and gentle leg swings** — never bounce violently into cold deep stretches (`ballistic stretching tears cold fibres!`).
step: **2. Safe Footwear, Clothing & Surface Check:** Wear breathable clothing, supportive closed lace-up takkies/trainers on flat obstacle-free ground (`remove glass, stones, loose gravel, or wet slippery puddles first!`), and check medical readiness (`asthma inhaler on hand if prescribed`).
step: **3. Correct Technique Before Heavy Load:** Keep spine neutral during planks/squats, land softly on bent knees from jumps, and breathe steadily (`never hold your breath lifting weights`).
step: **4. Hydration & Heat Safety:** Drink **clean water before, in small sips during, and after exercise**; in summer heat (`>30 deg C`), train early morning or shaded areas and stop immediately if dizzy, nauseous, or experiencing chest pain!
step: **5. Static Cool-Down (`5–7 min AFTER exercise`):** Walk slowly until heart rate drops, then hold **gentle static stretches (`20–30 seconds` per muscle group without bouncing)** to flush waste products and preserve flexibility.
```

## Part 4: How Grade 11 `PET` (`Physical Education Task`) Is Marked (`20 Marks / Term`)

Learners often confuse why someone super athletic still gets `12/20` while a steady beginner scores `18/20`:

| PET Component | Marks (`CAPS`) | What Teachers Actually Assess |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Criterion 1: Frequency of Participation** | **`10` marks** | Consistent attendance, correct PE kit, punctual warm-up, sustained active effort across **every scheduled PE lesson** of the term *(missing multiple sessions without a valid medical note drops this sharply!)*. |
| **Criterion 2: Outcome of Movement Performance** | **`10` marks** | Observed **technique, posture, improvement over the term, teamwork, and safe execution** during two formal movement observation check-ins (`e.g. fitness circuit form in T1/T3; game skills & officiating in T2/T4`). |

## Worked example: Fixing a Unsafe Fitness Plan (`Section B / Exam Theory`)

**Scenario:** *Kabelo (`16`) wants stronger arms and faster `100 m` sprint times. His plan is: **"Monday to Sunday (`7 days`), sprint maximum speed for `45 minutes` straight on concrete in school dress shoes with zero warm-up, then skip water so I stay light."***

1. *Identify **FOUR safety or FITT errors** in Kabelo's programme (`4`).* ->
   - **Zero rest days (`Frequency`):** Training `7 days/week` at max effort causes overtraining, shin splints, and muscle breakdown.
   - **Dangerous Intensity/Time mismatch:** Nobody can sprint max-speed continuously for `45 minutes` — sprints need short work bursts + recovery intervals.
   - **Unsafe footwear & zero warm-up:** Hard leather school shoes on concrete with cold muscles dramatically raise Achilles, knee, and ankle injury risk.
   - **Skipping water (`Dehydration risk`):** Deliberate fluid restriction impairs muscle power, causes cramps, and risks heat exhaustion.
2. *Rewrite a safe **Tuesday/Thursday/Saturday** session fixing his **FITT** and **safety** (`4`).* ->
   - **Warm-up (`8 min`):** Proper lace-up running trainers on grass/track — light jog + dynamic skips + leg swings.
   - **Main (`25 min`):** `6 x 60 m` controlled accelerations with `90 sec` walk recovery (`Power & Speed`) + `3 sets` of `10–12` push-ups & `30 sec` planks (`Muscular Strength/Endurance`).
   - **Hydration & Cool-down (`7 min`):** Sip water between sets; finish with slow walk + `25 sec` static calf/hamstring/shoulder stretches; keep **Sunday as full rest**.

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Skipping **PE kit or sitting out** thinking *"PET doesn't count toward Promotion"* — **PET makes up `20 marks` out of `100` every term**, meaning skipping PE can pull a `70%` LO average down into the `50s`!
> - Confusing **Muscular Strength** (`heavy maximum force for few reps`) with **Muscular Endurance** (`lighter force sustained over many reps or minutes, e.g. plank/distance cycling`).
> - Confusing **aerobic cardiovascular exercise** (`sustained rhythmic oxygen-using movement`) with **static stretching** (`flexibility`).

## Practice

```questions
Q: Define the **FITT principle** (`all four letters`) and give ONE concrete Grade 11 example for improving **cardiovascular endurance**. (6)
A: **F — Frequency:** how many sessions per week (`e.g. 3 days/week`); **I — Intensity:** how hard the effort is (`e.g. brisk pace where talking in short sentences is possible`); **T — Time:** duration per session (`e.g. 30 minutes`); **T — Type:** specific activity chosen (`e.g. brisk jogging, cycling, or jump-rope circuits`).
---
Q: Distinguish between **power**, **agility**, and **muscular endurance**, giving ONE sporting example for each. (6)
A: **Power** (`skill-related`) combines strength and speed explosively (`e.g. vertical jump to block a volleyball spike`). **Agility** (`skill-related`) is rapid, controlled change of body direction (`e.g. sidestepping defenders in netball/rugby`). **Muscular endurance** (`health-related`) is a muscle's ability to repeat contractions without rapid fatigue (`e.g. holding a 90-second plank or rowing steadily for 15 minutes`).
---
Q: Explain how a learner's **20-mark Term PET score** is calculated and give TWO practical safety steps teachers require before any outdoor PE lesson begins. (4)
A: **PET (`20 marks`):** `10 marks` for **frequency of active participation** (`consistent attendance, PE kit, full engagement`) + `10 marks` for **outcome of movement performance** (`technique, skill improvement, and safe execution`). **Two pre-lesson safety steps:** (1) Inspect the field/court for holes, broken glass, stones, or slippery water; (2) Conduct a progressive **dynamic warm-up** and confirm appropriate flat sports shoes and hydration access.
```
