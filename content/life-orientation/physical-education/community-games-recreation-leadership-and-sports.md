> **What you'll learn:** the **social, cultural, and fitness value of South African indigenous and playground games** (*morabaraba, diketo, jukskei, kho-kho, kgati, dibeke, three-tins*), designing **modified or self-invented community games** for inclusive participation, **leading recreational and relaxation group activities** (*stress relief, social cohesion*), and mastering **umpiring, refereeing, fixture administration, and risk management** (*CAPS Term 2 & Term 4 · LO*).

>
> **Why this matters:** Term 2 and Term 4 Physical Education shift from solo fitness into **teamwork, cultural heritage, leadership, and officiating**! Both PET observations and written LO exams test how you adapt games for **zero-cost equipment, mixed-ability or differently abled peers**, and uphold **fair play when officiating peers**.

## The idea in plain language

You do **not** need a R5 million stadium or imported gear to build agility, strategic thinking, and community unity! Across South Africa, **indigenous and playground games** turn a piece of chalk, ten pebbles, a rope, or an old tennis ball into high-energy **cardio, coordination, hand-eye precision, and cultural pride** — while teaching teenagers how to **lead, referee fairly, and organise fixtures** without conflict.

## Part 1: Traditional & Indigenous South African Games (`Culture Meets Fitness`)

| Game | Heritage & Simple Gear | How It Works (`Core Rules`) | Fitness & Cognitive Skills Developed |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Diketo** | Traditional SA coordination game; `1` small circle + `10` smooth pebbles + `1` larger *gusha* (`toss stone`). | Toss the *gusha* upward, scoop stones smoothly out and back into the circle *before* catching the falling stone without touching left-behind pebbles. | **Hand-eye coordination, fine motor timing, reaction time, focus**. |
| **Kgati (`Rope Jumping`)** | Widely played across SA schools; `1` long skipping rope held by two turners. | Individual or group jumpers leap rhythmically while chanting tempo sequences (`1–2` jumpers weaving in/out without tripping). | **Cardiovascular endurance, agility, rhythm, teamwork, muscular endurance**. |
| **Dibeke (`Skittle / Dodge-Run Ball`)** | SA running/dodging team game; `1` light soft ball + chalk bases/zones. | Attackers kick/pass and dash across safe zones while defenders pass and aim below the waist to tag runners out (`no headshots or tackling!`). | **Agility, sprint speed, peripheral vision, reaction time, team communication**. |
| **Three Tins (`Kani / Blikkies`)** | Township/rural street classic; `3` stacked empty tins + `1` soft tennis ball. | One striker rolls/throws to knock the stack down and races to restack all 3 tins (`calling "safe!"`) while fielders try to tag runners with the ball (`below waist`). | **Speed, dodging agility, throwing accuracy, teamwork under pressure**. |
| **Morabaraba (`Strategic Board`)** | Indigenous strategy game (`12` cows/tokens each on chalk/cardboard `24`-intersection board). | Place and slide tokens to form **"mills" (`3 in a row`)** and capture opponent cattle — sharp tactical foresight! | **Strategic thinking, patience, emotional regulation, mental stamina** (`great low-impact recreational leadership!`). |
| **Jukskei / Kho-Kho** | **Jukskei** (`wooden pins/targets pitched accurately across sand/grass`); **Kho-Kho** (`high-speed relay tag between seated alternating line chasers`). | *Jukskei:* knock target peg cleanly (`precision`). *Kho-Kho:* sprint-tag runners around poles and tap seated teammates (`"Kho!"`) to swap chasers instantly. | *Jukskei:* **accuracy, balance, focus**; *Kho-Kho:* **explosive acceleration, agility, relay discipline**. |

## Part 2: Adapting & Inventing Inclusive Community Games (`Modified Sport`)

A great Grade 11 PE leader adapts rules so **nobody sits excluded on the sidelines** (`whether due to lack of equipment, mixed gender, beginners vs club stars, or physical disabilities`):

```compare
title: Rigid Exclusive Sport vs Inclusive Modified Community Game
left: What Excludes Learners (`Fix These!`)
- Hard leather balls thrown fast at beginners or mixed-age groups (`fear of injury`).
- Eliminating knocked-out learners completely to sit bored for `25 minutes`.
- Requiring expensive spiked boots, full rugby kits, or formal grass pitches.
right: Smart Modifications (`CAPS Inclusive Design`)
- **Equipment swap:** Use soft foam/tennis balls, chalk lines on tar/sand, water-filled plastic bottle cones, or bell-balls for visually impaired peers!
- **Keep everyone active (`Zero Permanent Elimination`):** Eliminated players immediately become **side-line rebounders, scorekeepers, timers, or re-enter via 5 jumping jacks**.
- **Handicap / Fair Rules:** *"Everyone must touch the ball before scoring"*, **two-bounce rule** for wheelchair/beginner tennis, or **walking-only netball** for mixed-fitness groups.
```

## Part 3: Leading Recreation & Relaxation Activities (`Stress & Social Cohesion`)

Physical Education also covers **low-stress recreational and relaxation leadership** (`especially valuable around exams!` — connecting back to Lesson 121/312):
- **Group Relaxation & Mobility:** Leading a `15-minute` **guided breathing, neck/shoulder release, gentle Tai-Chi/flow stretching, or mindful walking circle** lowers exam cortisol and helps anxious teammates reset calmly.
- **Recreational Leadership Habits:** Explain rules clearly in **`<90 seconds`** (`demonstrate once visually`), divide balanced teams quickly (`count off 1–4 instead of embarrassing "last picked"` captains!), rotate roles so quiet learners also lead warm-ups, and celebrate **effort & sportsmanship** over scoreboards.

## Part 4: Umpiring, Officiating & Event Administration (`T2 & T4`)

When you step up as an **umpire, referee, linesperson, or fixture organiser** in Term 2/4 PET, you practise civic leadership and administrative precision:

```steps
title: Five pillars of confident, fair officiating & match administration
step: **1. Know & State the Modified Rules Upfront (`Pre-Match Toss`):** Brief both captains on boundary lines, safe contact limits (`e.g. waist-down tags only`), and scoring *before* the first whistle.
step: **2. Clear Signals, Positioning & Confident Whistle:** Blow short crisp whistles for minor infringements (`long firm whistle for stop/score`), use clear hand signals, and move alongside play so you never guess from 40 m away.
step: **3. Stay Neutral & Calm Under Pressure (`Emotional Control`):** Call fouls consistently on **both** friends and rivals; address arguing calmly to captains only (*"Captain, my decision stands — let's play on"*), never shouting insults back.
step: **4. Simple Fixture & Score Administration:** Draw a clean **round-robin or knockout table** (`Team A vs B, C vs D, time slots, 3 pts win / 1 pt draw / 0 loss`), track time honestly, and sign final scores with both captains.
step: **5. Post-Match Risk & Equipment Wrap-Up:** Collect all bibs/cones/balls (`accountability!`), inspect for any twisted ankles/scrapes (`first-aid log`), and prompt both teams to **shake hands**.
```

## Worked example: Designing a Modified Inclusive Game + Fixture (`8 marks`)

**Task (`Term 2 PET Leadership`):** *Your Grade 11 PE group (`16 learners`, mixed fitness levels, `1` learner using a forearm crutch, only `2` soft tennis balls and chalk available) must run a `20-minute` safe, high-energy tournament.*

1. *Design **ONE modified indigenous/community game** fitting the equipment and keeping **all `16` learners** moving safely (`4`).* -> **Modified 4-Zone Dibeke (`Walking/Slow-Jog Edition`):** Chalk `4` short safe base zones on a smooth swept courtyard; split into **`2` teams of `8`** (`rotational 4-minute halves`). Use **soft tennis balls** with **strict waist-below gentle passes (`no high throws, no tackling`)**. Include a **designated protected runner-partner zone** or **crutch-friendly seated/walk-pass wicket role** where every teammate contributes passes and safe dashes; tagged runners do `5` gentle heel-raises at the side zone and **rejoin on the next round** (`zero boredom!`).
2. *Explain **TWO officiating & administrative steps** your learner-leaders must run (`4`).* ->
   - **Pre-game risk & boundary audit:** Sweep pebbles/gravel off the courtyard, clearly chalk court lines `2 m` away from brick walls, and brief both teams on waist-below soft tagging before starting.
   - **Fair scoring & rotating referee:** Assign `2` rotating umpires (`1` referee + `1` scorer/timer) to log points cleanly on a clipboard table (`3 win / 1 draw`), blow consistent whistles, and rotate roles at halftime so everyone plays AND officiates.

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Dismissing indigenous games (`morabaraba, diketo, kgati`) as *"just kids' games"* — in CAPS LO they build **measurable agility, cardiovascular fitness, strategic logic, and South African cultural heritage**.
> - Umpiring silently without hand signals or letting loud friends talk you into changing fair calls — **consistency and calm neutrality** are what earn respect as a referee!
> - Designing complex tournaments (`16-team 40-minute playoffs`) for a `35-minute` PE period — keep fixtures realistic (`e.g. 4 teams playing 5-minute mini-matches`).

## Practice

```questions
Q: Describe **TWO South African indigenous or community games** and name **TWO distinct fitness or cognitive benefits** gained from each. (6)
A: 1. **Kgati (`Skipping Rope`):** Players jump rhythmically inside a turning long rope while chanting sequences; improves **cardiovascular endurance**, **agility**, and **group rhythm/timing**. 2. **Morabaraba:** A `24`-intersection strategy board game where players form lines of three ("mills") to capture opponent tokens; strengthens **strategic planning**, **concentration**, and **calm emotional control**.
---
Q: Explain **THREE practical modifications** a PE leader can make so a traditional ball game becomes **safe and inclusive** for beginners and differently abled peers. (6)
A: 1. **Adapt equipment & speed:** Replace hard balls with soft foam/tennis balls and lower target zones (`waist-below tags only`). 2. **Remove permanent elimination:** Give "tagged-out" learners quick `10-second` re-entry tasks (`or side-line scoring/passing stations`) so everyone stays active for the full period. 3. **Modify movement/touch rules:** Require every teammate to touch the ball before scoring, or allow walking/protected zones so players of varying mobility contribute equally.
---
Q: Outline **FOUR responsibilities** of a fair **umpire and event administrator** running a Grade 11 inter-class sports fixture. (4)
A: 1. Inspect the playing surface for hazards (`glass, holes, wet patches`) and confirm first-aid access beforehand. 2. Brief both teams clearly on rules, boundaries, and foul penalties before kick-off. 3. Enforce rules **consistently and neutrally** using clear whistle blasts and hand signals without favouring friends. 4. Maintain accurate **timekeeping, fixture schedules, and signed match scores**, and ensure all equipment is returned safely afterwards.
```
