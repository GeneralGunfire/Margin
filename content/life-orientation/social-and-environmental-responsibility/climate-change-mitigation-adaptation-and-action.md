> **What you'll learn:** the **human and natural causes of climate change**, **unequal impacts on South African development, health, food security, and vulnerable groups**, the clear difference between **mitigation (`reducing causes`) and adaptation (`coping safely with effects`)**, and how to design a **practical school or community environmental service plan** (*CAPS Term 2 · LO*).
>
> **Prerequisites:** Lesson 411 (`environmental degradation and resource depletion`).
>
> **Why this matters:** Examiners constantly catch learners confusing **mitigation** with **adaptation**! They also test how climate shocks (`droughts, heatwaves, flash floods`) deepen poverty and food prices unless youth lead realistic, local action instead of vague slogans.

## The idea in plain language

Weather is what happens **this afternoon** (`"it is raining in Durban today"`); **climate** is the **30-year average pattern** of temperature, rainfall, and seasons. **Climate change** means long-term shifts in those global patterns — driven faster since the Industrial Revolution by **greenhouse gases** trapping outgoing heat like a thicker blanket around Earth.

In South Africa, climate change doesn't hit everyone equally: when severe drought dries dams or sudden cloudbursts flood low-lying valleys, **subsistence farmers, informal settlements, elderly residents, and outdoor workers** suffer first and longest.

## Part 1: Causes & Unequal Impacts in South Africa

| Driver / Dimension | Specific Mechanism (`CAPS`) | Impact on People, Health & Economy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fossil Fuel Combustion** | Burning coal, petrol, and diesel for electricity (`coal power stations`), transport, and factories releases **carbon dioxide (`CO2`)**. | Accelerates global warming, worsens respiratory illness (`smog, asthma`), and disrupts rainfall belts. |
| **Deforestation & Land Misuse** | Clearing indigenous forests/wetlands for timber or uncontrolled development destroys natural **carbon sinks** (`trees/soil absorbing CO2`) and natural flood sponges. | Increases flash flooding, topsoil loss, and loss of rural shade/firewood. |
| **Methane & Waste** | Rotting organic waste in poorly managed landfills and intensive livestock farming release **methane (`CH4`)**, a potent heat-trapping gas. | Intensifies extreme heat days and drives up greenhouse warming faster in the short term. |
| **Agricultural & Food-Price Shock** | Prolonged drought in maize/fruit regions plus hailstorms and heat stress wipe out harvests and livestock grazing. | Staple food prices (`maize meal, vegetables`) spike, deepening household hunger and rural unemployment. |
| **Water Scarcity & Disease** | Lower dam inflows alongside warmer standing water increase water restrictions and mosquito/waterborne disease risk (`cholera, malaria range shifts`). | Clinics face higher caseloads while households queue at water tankers or spend income buying bottled water. |

## Part 2: Mitigation vs Adaptation (`Exam Goldmine!`)

Think of a leaking roof during a heavy storm: **fixing the broken tiles** stops more rain entering (**mitigation**), while **placing buckets and moving furniture** protects your family from the water already inside (**adaptation**). South Africa needs **BOTH** simultaneously!

```compare
title: Mitigation vs Adaptation — Never Mix Them Up!
left: Mitigation (`Tackling the ROOT CAUSES — cutting emissions`)
- **Clean & efficient energy:** Switching lights/appliances off when unused, solar geysers, rooftop PV, and clean public transport instead of single-occupant cars.
- **Waste & consumption reduction:** Repairing, reusing, composting food scraps (`preventing landfill methane`), and cutting single-use plastics.
- **Protecting carbon sinks:** Planting indigenous trees (`spekboom, local shade species`), halting wetland destruction, and conservation farming.
right: Adaptation (`Living SAFELY with unavoidable climate impacts`)
- **Water-wise living:** Rainwater harvesting tanks, fixing dripping taps immediately, greywater garden irrigation, and drought-tolerant indigenous plants.
- **Disaster & heat readiness:** Clearing stormwater drains *before* rainy season, shade shelters at schools, early-warning SMS alerts, and safe flood evacuation plans.
- **Resilient agriculture & housing:** Drought-resistant crop varieties, heat-insulated roofs, and avoiding building on floodplains.
```

## Part 3: Designing a Practical Community Action Plan (`Responsibility in Action`)

In Grade 11 LO, *"plant 1 million trees"* earns 0 marks because it is **unrealistic and unowned**. Good **community service projects** fit your real school/ward resources:

```steps
title: Five-step youth climate & environmental action blueprint
step: **1. Audit one local problem (`Observe & measure`):** Walk your school grounds or neighbourhood block (`e.g. blocked stormwater drains causing playground flooding, leaking taps wasting litres daily, or unrecycled paper piling up`).
step: **2. Set a concrete, measurable target:** Instead of *"stop pollution"*, set *"Install 2 labelled paper-and-bottle bins per Grade 11 corridor and harvest rainwater from the hall gutter into a JoJo tank for the school food garden by Term 3"*.
step: **3. Build partnerships (`Don't work in isolation`):** Request permission from the **Principal/SGB**, ask **ward councillors or municipal waste teams** for bin collection, and consult local nurseries/NGOs for indigenous seedlings.
step: **4. Assign roles, safety gear & timeline:** Issue gloves/tongs for clean-ups, schedule 30-minute class rotations during breaks, and log weekly litres/kilograms diverted so progress stays visible.
step: **5. Educate & sustain:** Present results briefly at assembly and mentor Grade 10 learners to inherit the project next year — turning one-day clean-ups into **lasting civic habits**!
```

## Worked example: Section C — Advising a Municipality & School (`10 marks`)

**Scenario:** *A semi-rural town faces a two-year drought causing crop failures and rising clinic visits for waterborne illness after residents fetch untreated river water, followed by sudden flash floods clogging unmaintained drains.*

1. *Distinguish **mitigation** from **adaptation** using ONE exact action from the town's crisis (`4`).* ->
   - **Mitigation** reduces greenhouse gas emissions causing long-term climate warming — e.g., **installing solar water pumps and municipal solar streetlights** instead of diesel generators.
   - **Adaptation** helps residents cope safely with climate impacts already occurring — e.g., **distributing household rainwater tanks, boiling/purifying emergency water, and clearing stormwater culverts before storms**.
2. *Explain **TWO ways** this climate cycle worsens **social inequality** in the town (`4`).* ->
   - Wealthier households can afford boreholes, backup tanks, and store-bought food, while **low-income families rely on polluted river water and face steep staple food price rises**.
   - **Farm workers and informal vendors lose wages** when droughts destroy crops, pushing youth out of school or into food insecurity.
3. *Propose **ONE realistic Grade 11 school contribution** (`2`).* -> Run a weekly **student-led rainwater-garden and drain-clearing audit** around school boundaries alongside a **safe water-boiling awareness poster campaign** in local languages.

```tryit
title: Mitigation or Adaptation? Classify Fast!
prompt: Label each action strictly as **MITIGATION** or **ADAPTATION** (`and give 1 brief reason`):
1. Fixing a school borehole with solar panels instead of a petrol generator.
2. Teaching households to plant drought-resistant sorghum and harvest roof rainwater.
3. Replacing plastic bottles with reusable flasks and composting tuckshop food peels.
hint: Ask yourself: Does it stop heat-trapping gases (`Mitigation`), or help people survive drought/floods (`Adaptation`)?
answer: 1. **Mitigation** (replaces fossil fuel emissions with clean solar energy). 2. **Adaptation** (adjusts farming and water storage to survive dry spells). 3. **Mitigation** (reduces plastic production emissions and landfill methane).
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Writing *"climate change happens because of the ozone hole"*! **Depletion of the ozone layer** (`UV rays from CFCs`) and **greenhouse global warming** (`CO2/methane trapping infrared heat`) are **separate environmental issues** — do NOT confuse them!
> - Labelling *"building flood walls"* or *"planting drought-resistant crops"* as **mitigation** — both cope with climate impacts (`adaptation`), they do not cut emissions!
> - Suggesting personal eco-actions only (`"turn off your phone charger"`) while ignoring **systemic/community action** (`municipal maintenance, waste collection, tree preservation, policy advocacy`).

## Practice

```questions
Q: Define **climate change** and explain **TWO human activities** that accelerate it in South Africa. (5)
A: **Climate change** is a long-term shift in regional and global average temperatures, rainfall, and extreme-weather patterns. **Two human drivers:** (1) **Burning fossil fuels** (`coal-fired electricity and vehicle petrol/diesel`) releases carbon dioxide that traps heat in the atmosphere; (2) **Deforestation and poor landfill management** destroy carbon-absorbing vegetation while decaying organic waste emits methane.
---
Q: Using TWO examples, show why **poor and rural communities** often experience harsher climate impacts than wealthy suburbs despite emitting fewer greenhouse gases. (4)
A: 1. **Food & income vulnerability:** Rural farmworkers and subsistence growers depend directly on steady rain, so droughts destroy livelihoods and push up basic food prices. 2. **Infrastructure gaps:** Informal and low-income settlements often lack storm drains, shade trees, insulated housing, or backup water tanks, leaving families exposed to flash floods, heatwaves, and contaminated water.
---
Q: Contrast **mitigation** and **adaptation** and propose ONE school-based example of each. (4)
A: **Mitigation** tackles the root causes of climate change by reducing greenhouse gas emissions (`e.g. installing LED lighting and composting tuckshop organic waste so it emits less methane`). **Adaptation** adjusts daily systems to stay safe amid climate impacts already arriving (`e.g. harvesting roof rainwater into JoJo tanks for toilets and food gardens during droughts`).
```
