> **What you'll learn:** **how learning actually takes place inside the brain** (*encoding, active recall, spaced repetition*), the exact CAPS difference between **study styles (`personal preferences`)** and **study strategies (`active task-based methods`)**, practical high-yield **study techniques** (*SQ3R, Pomodoro, flashcards, concept maps, teaching aloud*), and **realistic weekly time management** (*CAPS Term 2 · LO*).
>
> **Prerequisites:** Grade 10 note-making basics and Lesson 111 (`SMART goals`).
>
> **Why this matters:** Re-reading a textbook ten times with a highlighter **feels productive yet produces terrible exam marks (`illusion of competence`)**! Term 2 LO exams ask you to **distinguish study styles from strategies** AND **diagnose why a hard-working learner still forgets work in test conditions**!

## The idea in plain language

Two Grade 11 learners both spend **3 hours** studying Physical Sciences tonight:
- **Learner A** reads Chapter 4 quietly three times while texting every 8 minutes, feeling confident because the words look familiar.
- **Learner B** puts the phone in another room, closes the book after 20 minutes, writes out every formula from memory (**active recall**), fixes mistakes, and solves 4 unseen past-paper questions (**retrieval practice**)!

Guess who remembers it on Friday morning? **Learner B** — because the brain strengthens memories **only when forced to pull information OUT (`retrieval`)**, not when lazily staring at words!

## Part 1: Study Styles vs Study Strategies (`Exam Trap!`)

Never use these two CAPS terms interchangeably:

```compare
title: Study Style vs Study Strategy (CAPS Distinction!)
left: Study Style (`Your Natural Preference`)
- **Meaning:** How you naturally prefer to take in and process information (`a personal preference — NOT an excuse to avoid active practice!`).
- **Examples:**
  - **Visual:** Prefers diagrams, colour-coded mind maps, flowcharts, graphs.
  - **Auditory:** Learns cleanly by explaining aloud, podcasts, group discussion.
  - **Kinaesthetic / Tactile:** Learns by doing experiments, sketching models, moving around while reciting.
  - **Reading / Writing:** Prefers structured summaries, lists, written definitions.
right: Study Strategy (`Task-Specific Action Method`)
- **Meaning:** A deliberate **step-by-step technique chosen to match the specific subject and task** (`every effective learner combines multiple strategies regardless of style!`).
- **Examples:**
  - **SQ3R (`Survey, Question, Read, Recite, Review`)** for heavy theory textbooks (`History, Business, Tourism`).
  - **Spaced repetition & Flashcards** for definitions/Afrik rules.
  - **Timed past-paper problem solving** for Maths & Physical Sciences!
  - **Mind mapping & Comparison tables** for multi-factor essays.
```

## Part 2: How Effective Learning Takes Place (`Brain Science Made Practical`)

```steps
title: Five high-impact study habits backed by learning science
step: **1. Focus cleanly (`Pomodoro 25/5 or 45/10`):** Silence phone notifications (`switching back and forth cuts memory formation by ~40%`). Work intensely for **25–45 minutes**, then take a real **5–10 minute screen-free stretch/water break**!
step: **2. Use SQ3R on dense chapters:** **Survey** headings/summaries first -> turn headings into **Questions** -> **Read** actively seeking answers -> **Recite** answers with the book CLOSED -> **Review** 24 hours & 7 days later!
step: **3. Active Recall beats passive highlighting (`Always test yourself!`):** Close notes and write/sketch everything you remember on blank paper; check gaps with a different pen colour; revisit weak spots first!
step: **4. Space it out (`Spaced Repetition`):** 3 × 30 minutes across Monday, Wednesday, and Saturday beats one exhausted 90-minute Sunday night cram session **every single time**!
step: **5. Interleave subjects (`Mix Practice`):** Don't spend 4 hours straight on only one formula type — alternate between Maths problems and History/Business recall so your brain learns **how to recognise which tool fits which question**!
```

## Part 3: Practical Weekly Time Management (`Avoiding Burnout`)

A realistic weekly study timetable includes **THREE essentials** learners often omit (`and examiners deduct marks if missing!`):
1. **Fixed commitments first:** School hours, transport time, chores, meals, sports practice.
2. **Balanced homework + revision slots (`5–6 days/week`):** Tackle hardest/weakest subjects (`e.g. Maths/Science`) when brain energy is **highest**, not at `23:30`!
3. **Sleep (`7.5–9 hours`), breaks & social/rest time:** Skipping sleep destroys memory consolidation (`the brain locks daytime learning into long-term memory DURING deep sleep!`).

## Worked example: Diagnosing a Broken Study Routine (`Section B/C`)

**Scenario:** *Keitumetse (`Grade 11`) studies 4 hours nightly from `20:00 to 00:00` by highlighting every sentence in her History textbook while scrolling TikTok during slow parts. She sleeps 5 hours a night and still blanks out during tests.*

1. *Identify **THREE ineffective habits** in Keitumetse's routine (`3`).* ->
   - **Passive highlighting (`no active recall`)** creates false familiarity without training exam retrieval.
   - **Multitasking with social media** fragments attention.
   - **Studying past midnight (`only 5 hours sleep`)** sabotages memory consolidation and daytime focus!
2. *Recommend **THREE concrete changes** matching **SQ3R / Time Management** (`6`).* -> Shift start time earlier (`e.g. 16:30–19:30` in focused `40-min` phone-free blocks for **7.5–8 hours sleep**); apply **SQ3R (`close textbook and recite/write cause-effect steps from memory`)**; do **15 minutes weekly spaced review** every Saturday morning instead of passive re-reading!

```tryit
title: Study Style vs Strategy Check
prompt: Classify each as either a **Study Style** or a **Study Strategy**, and explain why simply "reading notes five times" fails:
1. Using the **SQ3R method** (`Survey, Question, Read, Recite, Review`) to master Tourism SADC attractions.
2. Preferring **visual diagrams and flowcharts** over plain audio lectures.
hint: Deliberate step-by-step method vs personal sensory preference; passive familiarity vs active retrieval!
answer: 1. **Study Strategy** (`structured method applied to a reading task`). 2. **Study Style** (`visual preference`). Merely re-reading creates **recognition fluency** (`"oh yes, I saw that page"`), NOT **exam recall** when facing blank lines under time pressure!
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Writing *"my study style is cramming the night before"* — cramming is a **poor emergency habit (`bad strategy`)**, NOT a study style!
> - Designing an **"impossible robot timetable"** (`studying 16:00–23:00 non-stop with zero dinner, zero chores, zero breaks, zero sleep`) in exam tasks — markers look for **realistic sustainable schedules** balancing study, rest, chores, and exercise!
> - Studying **only favourite easy subjects** first while putting off difficult topics until exhausted.

## Practice

```questions
Q: Distinguish clearly between a **study style** and a **study strategy**, giving ONE example of each. (4)
A: A **study style** is a learner's natural sensory preference for taking in information (`e.g. visual preference for diagrams or auditory discussion`), whereas a **study strategy** is a deliberate, task-specific technique used to learn effectively (`e.g. SQ3R reading method, flashcards with spaced repetition, or timed past-paper drills`).
---
Q: Explain TWO reasons why **active recall (`testing yourself with books closed`)** produces better exam results than **re-reading and highlighting**. (4)
A: 1. Active recall forces your brain to **retrieve and reconstruct information from scratch**, exactly mirroring what you must do in an exam room! 2. It **exposes weak gaps immediately**, stopping false confidence (`illusion of competence`) so you fix misunderstandings before test day.
---
Q: State THREE practical rules a Grade 11 learner should follow when building a realistic **weekly study timetable**. (6)
A: 1. Schedule **school, transport, meals, chores, and at least 7.5–8 hours of sleep** first so the plan matches real life. 2. Tackle **demanding subjects (`like Maths/Science`) early** when energy is high, using focused phone-free blocks (`25–45 min + short breaks`). 3. Leave **one weekly buffer/review slot** for unexpected homework or spaced revision!
```
