> **What you'll learn:** comparing South Africa's **main post-school funding routes** (**NSFAS, merit/company bursaries, scholarships, study loans, learnerships, and SETA programmes**), understanding **contractual and financial obligations** (*repayment with interest, work-back service ties, academic pass conditions*), and spotting **fake funding scams** (*CAPS Term 1 · LO*).
>
> **Prerequisites:** Lesson 511 (`NSC admission tiers & APS`).
>
> **Why this matters:** Tuition, textbooks, transport, and accommodation cost tens of thousands of rands per year — yet many qualified learners miss out simply because they miss early **bursary deadlines**, sign **predatory loans** without reading interest clauses, or fall for **fake WhatsApp scholarship fees**!

## The idea in plain language

Funding your studies is never *"free magic money"*: **every single rand comes with rules and obligations** attached! Some funding obligates you to **keep passing modules (`NSFAS / scholarships`)**, some obligates you to **work for a sponsor after graduating (`company bursaries`)**, and others obligate you to **repay principal plus compound interest (`study loans`)**.

## Part 1: Comparing Study Funding Options (`CAPS Term 1`)

| Funding Type | Who Funds It? | Repayment / Work Obligation | Academic & Eligibility Focus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **NSFAS (`National Student Financial Aid Scheme`)** | SA Government (`DHET`) for public **Universities & TVET Colleges**. | **No cash repayment** for qualifying full bursary recipients *provided you pass required modules* (`typically 50%+ progress` — check current NSFAS rules each year; *"missing middle"* loan schemes carry distinct terms!). | Combined **household income thresholds** (`plus SASSA grant auto-qualification` / disability support) and South African citizenship. |
| **Company / Corporate Bursary** | Banks, mines, engineering firms, retail groups, or government departments (`e.g. Funza Lushaka for Teaching`). | **Work-back contract (`service obligation`):** You usually must work for the sponsor for `1 year per year funded` (`or repay the full amount if you fail or break contract!`). | Targets **scarce skills** (`STEM, teaching, accounting, health`) using strong **Grade 11/12 marks** and sometimes financial need. |
| **Scholarship** | Universities, foundations, sporting/arts bodies, or international trusts. | **No cash repayment & usually no work-back tie**, provided you maintain **high merit standards** (`e.g. Dean's List average or sports squad commitment`). | Pure **academic excellence (`80%+`), leadership, or athletic/artistic talent** — fiercely competitive! |
| **Study / Student Loan** | Commercial banks (`FNB, Standard Bank, Absa, Nedbank, Capitec`) or specialist lenders (`Fundi`). | **MUST be repaid in full PLUS interest!** While studying, a **parent/guardian (`surety/guarantor`)** usually pays monthly interest; full capital repayments start after graduating (`or immediately if you drop out!`). | Requires a **creditworthy surety with stable income** (unless backed by institutional guarantee schemes) + proof of university registration. |
| **Learnership & SETA Funding** | **Sector Education and Training Authorities (`SETAs`)** (`e.g. MerSETA, FASSET, BANKSETA, CHIETA`) + host employers. | **No tuition debt!** You receive a **monthly stipend (`allowance`)** while splitting time between accredited classroom theory and real workplace training (`leading to an NQF qualification`). | Ideal **earn-while-you-learn** route for **TVET/Matric youth** seeking practical artisan, IT, finance, or logistics experience! |

## Part 2: Navigating Contractual & Moral Obligations

```compare
title: What You Sign Matters (`Financial & Academic Obligations`)
left: Before Signing Any Contract
- **Calculate total loan cost:** Ask *"What is the interest rate (`prime vs prime + 3%`), initiation fee, and total rand amount I will repay over 5 years?"*
- **Check work-back geographic clauses:** Would a provincial teaching or mining bursary require you to relocate to a rural district for 3–4 years? Accept willingly — breaking contract triggers immediate debt recovery!
- **Verify fail-module penalties:** Many bursaries convert grants into **repayable debt** if you drop out or fail more than the permitted modules.
right: Avoiding Scams & Self-Funding Smartly
- **Zero upfront "admin fee" rule:** **Legitimate bursaries, NSFAS, and SETAs NEVER ask you to eWallet/PayShap money** to "unlock" an application or guaranteed seat!
- **Stack lawful support:** Combine **early Grade 11 applications** (`using Grade 11 final marks`), part-time holiday work/tutoring, university **merit fee rebates (`automatic discounts for 4+ distinctions`)**, and affordable **TVET/distance study (`UNISA`)** if living costs are high.
```

## Part 3: Application Action Checklist (`Grade 11 -> Grade 12`)

```steps
title: How to secure funding without panic
step: **1. Prepare clean certified documents early:** Keep clear PDF scans of your **SA ID**, **parents'/guardians' IDs and proof of income (`payslips, SASSA letter, or sworn affidavit if unemployed`)**, and **Grade 11 Term 4 report**.
step: **2. Create a clean professional email address:** Use `firstname.lastname@...` — never apply to corporate bursaries using joke nicknames!
step: **3. Apply to 5–8 sources (`Don't rely on ONE option!`):** Apply to **NSFAS** *and* **2–4 corporate/provincial bursaries** *and* **university residence/merit aid** *and* **1–2 SETA learnerships** as backup.
step: **4. Track every deadline & reference number:** Store confirmation emails in a dedicated folder and check spam weekly for interview invitations (`many corporate bursaries interview shortlisted Grade 11/12 applicants!`).
```

## Worked example: Choosing Between Three Real Offers (`8 marks`)

**Scenario:** *Zanele (`household income R180,000/yr`) has `78%` in Maths and `74%` in Physical Sciences and receives three options for a BEd (`Maths & Science Teaching`):*
- **Option A:** *Funza Lushaka Bursary (`covers tuition, accommodation, books + stipend; requires teaching in a public school 1 year per year funded`).*
- **Option B:** *Bank Study Loan at `prime + 2%` interest (`uncle acts as surety`).*
- **Option C:** *WhatsApp message offering a "Guaranteed R90,000 Scholarship — pay R350 registration fee via eWallet before Friday".*

1. *Identify and explain which option is a **fraudulent trap** (`2`).* -> **Option C** is an advance-fee scam: genuine bursaries/scholarships **never demand eWallet/cash "registration fees"** over WhatsApp!
2. *Compare **Option A (`Funza Lushaka`)** vs **Option B (`Bank Loan`)** and advise Zanele (`6`).* ->
   - **Option A (`Bursary`):** Ideal financially — covers **full study + living costs (`tuition, res, books`)** with **zero debt or interest**, provided Zanele passes her modules and honours her **public-school teaching work-back obligation** (which fits her chosen career directly!).
   - **Option B (`Bank Loan`):** Leaves Zanele and her uncle burdened with **high compound interest** and monthly repayments even before she secures her first teaching salary.
   - **Recommendation:** Accept **Option A**, keep **NSFAS** as backup if needed, and protect her academic average every semester so the bursary never converts into debt.

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Thinking **bursaries and scholarships are identical**! While both cover study costs without cash loan repayments (if passed), **scholarships** are strictly **merit/talent-based** (`usually no work-back tie`), whereas **bursaries** commonly factor **financial need and/or scarce-skill work-back service contracts**.
> - Assuming **"a study loan is easy money because you pay later"** — interest compounds from day one, requires a **creditworthy surety**, and stays owed **even if you fail and drop out**!
> - Believing **NSFAS automatically pays for private colleges** — NSFAS strictly funds eligible students at **SA public universities and public TVET colleges**.

## Practice

```questions
Q: Distinguish clearly between a **bursary**, a **study loan**, and a **SETA learnership** in terms of **funding source** and **learner obligations**. (6)
A: A **bursary** (`government/company`) pays study costs and usually obligates the student to **pass modules and/or work for the sponsor** after graduating (`work-back contract`). A **study loan** (`bank/lender`) lends tuition money that **must be repaid with interest** (`requiring a surety`). A **SETA learnership** (`Sector Education & Training Authority + employer`) combines accredited training with real work experience while paying the learner a **monthly stipend without tuition debt**.
---
Q: Explain **TWO financial obligations** a Grade 11 learner must understand before asking a relative to sign as **surety** on a bank study loan. (4)
A: 1. **Shared legal debt risk:** If the student fails, drops out, or cannot find work after graduating, the **surety becomes legally responsible** for repaying the full remaining loan plus interest (risking blacklisting or asset attachment). 2. **Immediate monthly interest:** Most student loans require the surety to pay **monthly interest payments immediately while the student is still studying**, straining the relative's monthly budget.
---
Q: Give **TWO red flags** of fake bursary/college scams and explain why **Grade 11 Term 4 marks** are critical for real funding applications. (4)
A: **Red flags:** (1) Requesting upfront **eWallet/cash "admin/unlock" fees**; (2) Unverified WhatsApp/social-media links lacking an official `.gov.za`, `.ac.za`, or registered corporate domain and DHET/SAQA registration. **Why Grade 11 marks matter:** Most universities, NSFAS early cycles, and corporate bursaries open early in Grade 12 **before** Matric results exist — making Grade 11 final marks the deciding proof for provisional selection!
```
