> **What you'll learn:** how Grade 11 History Papers 1 and 2 work (`150 marks`, 3 questions of `50 marks` each), how to answer every **source-based question type** (extraction, definition in context, comparison, usefulness, reliability/bias, 8-mark paragraph), and how to score top-band marks on **50-mark essays** using **PEEL**, a clear **stance**, and sustained **line of argument**.
>
> **Prerequisites:** basic reading comprehension and chronological sequencing.
>
> **Why this matters:** In every Grade 11 History exam, **every single 50-mark Section A question** tests source skills and **every 50-mark Section B essay** is graded on a **7-level Content × Presentation matrix**. Mastering the exact mark-scheme rules unlocks marks across *every* historical topic from Russia to Apartheid!

## The idea in plain language

Historians do NOT memorise dates just to recite lists — they work like **detectives** weighing witnesses (**Sources**) and building a reasoned case (**Essays**):
- **Paper 1 (3 hours, 150 marks):** Communism in Russia, Capitalism in the USA, Ideas of Race.
- **Paper 2 (3 hours, 150 marks):** Nationalisms (two chosen case studies) and Apartheid South Africa (1940s–1960s).
- In **each paper**, you answer **three 50-mark questions**: at least **1** (and at most 2) from **Section A (Source-Based)** and at least **1** (and at most 2) from **Section B (Essay)** — usually two Source-Based + one Essay, OR one Source-Based + two Essays.

## Part 1: Decoding Source-Based Questions (Section A — 50 marks)

Notice the mark notation next to questions — e.g. **`(2 x 2) (4)`** means **TWO separate valid points**, worth **2 marks each**!

```steps
title: How to handle the 5 hardest Section A question types
step: **1. Extraction & Definition in Context (`1 x 2`, `2 x 1`):** Quote or paraphrase directly from the named source (`Source 1A`) — NEVER use outside memory when the prompt says *"According to the source..."*! For definitions (*"Define capitalism in your own words"*), explain the concept accurately WITHOUT copying the source line verbatim.
step: **2. Perspective / Purpose / Bias (`1 x 2`, `2 x 2`):** Read the **italicised attribution line** at the top/bottom of EVERY source (*who wrote it, what role they held, when, and why*)! A government poster written during war aims to persuade/inspire, not give neutral criticism.
step: **3. Usefulness vs Reliability (NEVER confuse them!):**
- **Useful:** *Can a historian learn something valuable from this source?* Say **"Yes, to a large/certain extent..."** AND explain **WHAT specific evidence** it provides (even biased propaganda is useful for proving what a regime wanted citizens to think!). Add one limitation for balance if worth 4 marks!
- **Reliable:** *Can we trust the factual accuracy?* Judge **author motive, first-hand access, propaganda purpose, exaggeration, and corroboration**!
step: **4. Comparing Sources (`2 x 2`):** Explicitly link **BOTH sources in every bullet**: *"Source 1A shows X, **and this is supported by / contradicted by** Source 1B which states Y."* Never write two disconnected summaries!
step: **5. Paragraph Question (8 marks — ~8 lines):** Uses a **3-level holistic rubric** (`0–3 weak`, `4–6 competent`, `7–8 strong`). Synthesise **multiple sources PLUS your own historical knowledge** to directly answer the paragraph's focal question!
```

## Part 2: Writing a 50-Mark History Essay (Section B)

Markers grade essays on two axes (**Content** × **Presentation**):
1. **Stance & Line of Argument (`LOA`):**
   - Look carefully at the prompt wording!
     - *"Critically discuss..."* / *"To what extent..."* -> **YOU MUST TAKE A CLEAR STANCE** (*"To a large extent..."*, *"While X played a role, Y was primarily responsible..."*)! In DBE provincial memos, failing to take a stance on a *"To what extent"* prompt severely caps your essay!
     - *"Explain why..."* -> Argue the key causes logically without narrating random events.
2. **PEEL Paragraphs (~4–5 body paragraphs):**
   - **Point:** Topic sentence linking back to your stance (never *"Next in 1924..."*).
   - **Evidence:** Specific laws, dates, leaders, figures, and policies.
   - **Explain:** *How/why* that evidence proves your argument!
   - **Link:** Echo the prompt keywords cleanly.
3. **Conclusion:** Restate your stance in fresh words, summarise your main analytical threads, and add **ZERO brand-new facts**.

```compare
title: Chronicle/Storytelling Essay (caps at ~20–24/50) vs Analytical Essay (40+/50)
left: Narrative storytelling trap
- *"First Lenin died in 1924. Then Stalin fought Trotsky. Then Stalin made the First Five-Year Plan in 1928 and collectivised farms in 1929. Then millions died in the famine..."*
- Lists events in time order **without answering the question** (`"To what extent were Stalin's Five-Year Plans a success?"`).
right: Analytical Line of Argument
- *"Stalin's Five-Year Plans succeeded to a significant extent in transforming the USSR into an industrial superpower capable of surviving World War II; however, they failed in human terms by inflicting famine, terror, and severe consumer shortages."*
- Every paragraph weighs **industrial success** against **human/social cost** tied straight back to that thesis!
```

## Worked example: Answering a "Reliability" & "Comparison" Source Question

**Source 1A:** Excerpt from a 1933 Soviet state newspaper boasting that Ukrainian collective farms celebrated record grain harvests and overflowing granaries.
**Source 1B:** Private 1933 diary of an aid worker describing starving villagers searching empty fields during grain requisitions.

1. *Comment on the **reliability** of Source 1A regarding agricultural conditions in 1933. (2 x 2) (4)*
   - **Point 1:** Source 1A is **unreliable** because it was published by a **state-controlled Soviet newspaper** under Stalin's censorship, whose purpose was propaganda to portray collectivisation as a triumph.
   - **Point 2:** Furthermore, it is contradicted by independent eyewitness evidence (Source 1B) showing severe **famine and grain requisitioning** in Ukraine in 1933.
2. *How does Source 1B **contradict** Source 1A? (1 x 2) (2)*
   - Source 1A claims collective farms enjoyed **overflowing granaries and abundance**, whereas Source 1B reveals **widespread starvation and empty fields** during the exact same year.

```tryit
title: Upgrade a weak "Usefulness" answer
prompt: Question: *Explain to what extent a **1929 New York newspaper cartoon** showing ruined investors jumping out of windows is **useful** to a historian studying the Wall Street Crash. (2 x 2) (4)*
A learner wrote: *"It is NOT useful because it is exaggerated and biased."*
Explain why the learner loses marks and write a strong 2-point answer!
hint: Remember: **exaggeration affects RELIABILITY, NOT usefulness** for understanding public shock/mood!
answer: **Why weak:** Cartoons rarely depict literal reality, yet they remain **highly useful** for showing **public panic and attitudes** at the time!
**Strong answer:**
1. It is **useful** because it is a contemporary (1929) visual source revealing the **scale of shock, panic, and financial despair** caused by the Wall Street Crash.
2. **However, its usefulness has limitations** because cartoons rely on **exaggeration/satire** rather than giving verified statistics on actual suicides or bank failure numbers.
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Ignoring the **source attribution** (author, date, context) — that tiny caption holds the answer to bias, purpose, and reliability questions!
> - Saying a biased source is **"useless"** — a biased source is often **extremely useful** as proof of what an author, political party, or government believed or wanted citizens to feel!
> - Writing `"To a certain extent"` without explaining *which part was true* and *which part failed*! Always define your stance concretely.

## Practice

```questions
Q: What does the mark notation **`(2 x 2) (4)`** on a History source-based question tell you to write? (2)
A: **Two distinct valid points**, each developed clearly enough to earn **2 marks** (4 marks total).
---
Q: Why must an **8-mark paragraph question** use **both** the addendum sources **and** your own historical knowledge? (2)
A: The 3-level holistic rubric rewards candidates who **synthesise evidence from multiple sources alongside accurate historical context** in a structured argument, rather than merely copying lines from one source.
---
Q: What happens to your mark if an essay prompt asks *"To what extent..."* and you write pure chronological facts without taking a **stance**? (2)
A: Your essay fails to maintain a **line of argument** directly answering the question, keeping both Content and Presentation capped in lower rubric bands regardless of how many dates you memorised.
```
