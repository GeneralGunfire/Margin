## The idea in plain language

A **state** is a set of borders, courts, and tax collectors. A **nation** is a group of people who *feel* they belong together through shared history, language, symbols, or political loyalty (**nationalism**).

Political theorist **Benedict Anderson (1983)** famously called nations **"imagined communities"**: you will never meet 99.9% of your fellow citizens, yet shared newspapers, anthems, flags, and common struggles make you *imagine* yourselves as one family!

### How Modern Nationalism Spread (Europe -> Africa)
- **French Revolution (1789):** Replaced loyalty to a king with loyalty to *"the nation"* and citizens' rights.
- **Revolutions of 1830 & 1848 + Unifications:** People rose against old empires; separate states united into **Italy (`1861`, Cavour/Garibaldi)** and **Germany (`1871`, Bismarck)**.
- **New Imperialism & Berlin Conference (`15 Nov 1884 – 26 Feb 1885`):** European powers carved Africa into artificial colonial borders without consulting Africans — accidentally uniting diverse African peoples inside new colonies against shared foreign rule!

## Rise of African Nationalism in South Africa (`1902–1948`)

1. **White Union & Land Dispossession (`1910–1913`):** After the **South African War (`1899–1902`, Treaty of Vereeniging `31 May 1902`)**, the **Union of South Africa (`31 May 1910`)** handed political power to a white minority and left Africans largely voteless. The **Natives Land Act (`June 1913`)** barred Black South Africans from buying or renting land outside tiny scheduled reserves (**~7–8%** of land in 1913, expanded to **~13%** by the 1936 Act), forcing men into cheap migrant mine labour!
2. **Early Moderate Unity (`Politics of Petition`):**
   - **Natal Indian Congress (`1894`):** Founded by **Mahatma Gandhi** (`satyagraha` passive resistance from 1908).
   - **African Political Organisation (`APO`, Dr Abdullah Abdurahman):** Defended Coloured voting/civil rights (1909 London delegation).
   - **SANNC (`8 January 1912`, Bloemfontein — renamed `ANC` in `1923`):** Leaders like **John Dube**, **Pixley ka Isaka Seme**, and **Sol Plaatje** united across ethnic lines (*"We are one people"*) using polite deputations to Britain and newspaper *Abantu Batho*.
3. **1920s–1930s Worker Militancy & Women's Protest:**
   - **Bantu Women's League (Charlotte Maxeke)** and **1913/1918 anti-pass protests** in the Free State.
   - **Industrial and Commercial Workers' Union (`ICU`, 1919, Clements Kadalie)** mobilised tens of thousands of urban/farm workers; **1920 African mineworkers' strike** (~70 000 strikers); **SACP** and trade unions.

## WWII Turning Point & Two Strands of African Nationalism (`1940s–1959`)

```steps
title: How World War II transformed cautious petitions into mass action
step: **1. Atlantic Charter (`14 August 1941`) & *African Claims* (`1943`):** Allied leaders Roosevelt & Churchill promised self-determination against tyranny. ANC President **Dr A.B. Xuma** responded with ***African Claims in South Africa* (`1943`)**, demanding **universal franchise (`one person, one vote`)** and an end to land/pass discrimination!
step: **2. Returning Soldiers & 1945 Manchester Congress:** Black SA soldiers served abroad (without equal weapons or pensions), while **Fifth Pan-African Congress (Manchester, Oct 1945)** inspired faster anti-colonial urgency.
step: **3. ANC Youth League (`1944`):** **Anton Lembede, Nelson Mandela, Walter Sisulu, and Oliver Tambo** impatiently pushed elders aside (`Dr James Moroka` replaced Xuma in **1949**) with a militant **Programme of Action (1949)** — strikes, boycotts, and civil disobedience!
step: **4. Mass Alliances vs Africanist Split (`1955–1959`):** Under the **Congress Alliance** (`ANC, SAIC, SACPO, COD, SACTU`), delegates adopted the non-racial **Freedom Charter (`Kliptown, 25–26 June 1955`)**: *"South Africa belongs to all who live in it, black and white."* Disagreeing with multi-racial alliances and communist influence, **Robert Sobukwe** led Africanists to break away and found the **Pan Africanist Congress (`PAC`, April 1959)**!
```

```compare
title: Two Strands of African Nationalism in SA
left: Inclusive Non-Racial Nationalism (Charterist / ANC)
- Rooted in **Xuma (`1943`), Programme of Action (`1949`), Congress Alliance (`1955 Freedom Charter`)**, later **UDF (`20 Aug 1983`)**.
- Welcomed Indian, Coloured, and progressive white allies fighting a common non-racial struggle (*"South Africa belongs to all who live in it"*).
- Guided **1994 democratic transition (`27 April 1994`)**, **Truth and Reconciliation Commission (`1995`)**, and **1996 Constitution**!
right: Exclusivist / Africanist Tradition (Lembede -> PAC)
- Emphasised **African psychological self-reliance** and liberation led **solely by Africans** without white/communist direction (*"Africa for the Africans"*).

```

## Worked example: Explaining "Why did it take ~40 years for African nationalism to become a mass movement?"

**Essay argument points:**
1. **Early elite deference (`1912–1930s`):** Early SANNC leaders were mission-educated lawyers, teachers, and chiefs trusting British liberalism and polite petitions rather than mass strikes.
2. **Regional/ethnic & rural divisions + 1930s repression:** Migrant labour and pass laws divided urban from rural workers; ICU collapsed by 1930; Hertzog's 1936 laws silenced opposition temporarily.
3. **1940s catalyst (`Urbanisation + WWII + Youth League`):** Wartime factory growth doubled urban Black populations (**1946 mineworkers' strike**), while the **1944 Youth League** and **1949 Programme of Action** finally linked intellectuals with mass urban workers!

```tryit
title: Untangle 3 common Topic 4 chronology traps
prompt: Spot and fix the factual error in each sentence:
1. *"The 1945 Manchester Pan-African Congress led Dr Xuma to write African Claims in 1943."*
2. *"The 1913 Natives Land Act immediately allocated 13% of South Africa's land to Black reserves."*
3. *"The PAC broke away from the ANC in 1944 under Nelson Mandela."*
hint: Check **1943 vs 1945**, **1913 vs 1936**, and **1944 ANCYL vs 1959 PAC**!
answer: 1. **1943 came BEFORE 1945!** The **1941 Atlantic Charter** directly inspired **Xuma's 1943 *African Claims*** (and the **1945 Manchester Congress** reinforced post-war Pan-African momentum).
2. The **1913 Land Act** set aside **~7–8%** of land; the **1936 Native Trust and Land Act** expanded the ceiling to **~13%**.
3. **Mandela, Lembede, Sisulu, and Tambo** founded the **ANC Youth League in 1944**; **Robert Sobukwe** led the **PAC breakaway in April 1959**!
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Calling the **Berlin Conference (`1884–1885`)** the "Congress of Berlin" (`1878` was a separate European Balkan treaty!).
> - Claiming **all** African nationalists wanted to exclude white South Africans — contrast the **Freedom Charter's non-racial inclusive nationalism (`1955`)** carefully with **PAC Africanism (`1959`)**!
> - Mixing up **1943 *African Claims*** with the **1955 Freedom Charter** — both demanded democracy, yet *African Claims* was the ANC's 1943 wartime response to the Atlantic Charter, whereas the *Freedom Charter* united the wider multi-racial Congress Alliance in 1955.

## Practice

```questions
Q: How does **Benedict Anderson's** concept of an **"imagined community"** apply to the **1912 founding of the SANNC**? (2)
A: Despite never meeting most people across distant provinces and speaking different languages, SANNC founders used shared newspapers (*Abantu Batho*), conferences, and opposition to Union exclusions to unite South Africans into a single national movement (*"We are one people"*).
---
Q: Explain TWO ways **World War II (1939–1945)** accelerated **African nationalism** in South Africa. (4)
A: 1. The **1941 Atlantic Charter**'s promise of self-rule inspired **Xuma's *African Claims* (1943)** demanding full voting and human rights. 2. Wartime factory expansion accelerated Black urbanisation, giving rise to militant strikes (e.g. **1946 mineworkers' strike**) and the **1944 ANC Youth League** pushing mass defiance.
---
Q: Why did **Robert Sobukwe** and the **Africanists** break away to form the **PAC in April 1959**? (2)
A: They objected to the **Freedom Charter's** multi-racial **Congress Alliance** (`"South Africa belongs to all who live in it"`), arguing white/communist influence diluted African leadership and land restoration.
```
