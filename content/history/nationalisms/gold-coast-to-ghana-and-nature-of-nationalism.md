> **What you'll learn:** how anti-colonial nationalism grew in the British colony of **Gold Coast** (*NCBWA 1919/1920, cocoa boycott 1937–1938*), how **WWII**, the **Atlantic Charter**, and the **1948 Accra riots** opened the door for **Kwame Nkrumah** and the **Convention People's Party (`CPP`, June 1949)** to lead **Ghana** to independence (**`6 March 1957`** — the first sub-Saharan African colony freed from European rule!), Nkrumah's **Pan-Africanism** and post-independence challenges (`1966 coup`), and Topic 4's overarching synthesis: **when nationalism is inclusive/positive vs exclusive/destructive**.
>
> **Prerequisites:** European colonialism (`Berlin Conference 1884–1885`) and Pan-Africanism basics.
>
> **Why this matters:** Topic 4 closes by zooming out from specific countries to answer the Grade 11 Big Question: **When is nationalism a liberating force for unity, and when does it turn oppressive, racist, or violent?** Ghana (`1957`) gives a brilliant contrast alongside South Africa, Europe, and Bosnia (`1992`)!

## The idea in plain language

In **1901**, Britain ruled the West African territory of **Gold Coast** (named for gold exports and wealthy cocoa farms) through **Indirect Rule** — using loyal local chiefs while shutting educated Africans and ordinary workers out of real power.

In just **ten years (`1947–1957`)**, mass nationalist mobilisation led by **Kwame Nkrumah** forced Britain out peacefully without a protracted civil war, inspiring liberation movements across the entire continent (including South Africa!). Yet Ghana's post-1957 story also shows how even unifying nationalism can drift toward **one-party authoritarianism** once independence is won.

## Step 1: Early Gold Coast Protest (`1919–1948`)

1. **Coastal Elite Protest (`NCBWA`, 1919–1920):** Educated professionals formed the **National Congress of British West Africa** (first conference in **Accra, March 1920**) calling for elected representation — though still elitist and cut off from rural farmers.
2. **Cocoa Hold-Up (`1937–1938`):** Furious at European trading companies fixing low cocoa prices, Gold Coast farmers organised a massive **cocoa boycott**, proving rural economic solidarity worked!
3. **World War II Catalyst & Accra Riots (`28 February 1948`):**
   - Returning Gold Coast veterans who had fought for Britain against fascism (inspired by the **1941 Atlantic Charter** and **Marcus Garvey's** Pan-African pride) faced unemployment and broken promises.
   - On **28 February 1948**, police fired on a peaceful ex-servicemen's march toward Christiansborg Castle in Accra (sparking riots and ~29 deaths nationwide) -> Britain arrested **UGCC** ("Big Six") leaders, turning them into national heroes!

## Step 2: Kwame Nkrumah, `CPP` & Independence (`1947–1966`)

```steps
title: How Kwame Nkrumah won "Self-Government Now"
step: **1. From UGCC (`1947`) to Mass CPP (`12 June 1949`):** After studying in the USA and helping organise the **1945 Manchester Pan-African Congress**, Nkrumah returned as General Secretary of moderate party **UGCC (`Aug 1947`)**. Impatient with UGCC's cautious slogan (*"Self-government in the shortest possible time"*), Nkrumah split to launch the mass **Convention People's Party (`CPP`, June 1949)** demanding **"Self-Government Now!"** — recruiting women traders, youth, and trade unions!
step: **2. Positive Action (`January 1950`):** Inspired by Gandhi's non-violence, Nkrumah launched **Positive Action** — strikes, boycotts, and civil disobedience. Britain jailed Nkrumah; while he sat in prison, the CPP won the **1951 elections** by a landslide, forcing Britain to release him as **Leader of Government Business / Prime Minister (`1952`)**!
step: **3. Independence (`6 March 1957`) & Republic (`1 July 1960`):** Renamed **Ghana** (honouring the ancient West African empire), it became the **first sub-Saharan African colony to win independence** (`6 March 1957`), inspiring *"Uhuru"* (freedom) across Africa and hosting All-African Peoples' Conferences.
step: **4. Post-Independence Shadows (`1958–1966`):** Nkrumah poured funds into schools, healthcare, and the Akosombo Dam — **yet** cocoa prices dropped, debt soared, and Nkrumah passed the **Preventive Detention Act (`1958`)** jailing rivals without trial, declared a one-party state, and built a personality cult until overthrown by a **military coup on `24 February 1966`** (died in exile, 1972).
```

## Topic 4 Synthesis: Positive vs Negative Features of Nationalism

```compare
title: Inclusive (Beneficial) vs Exclusive (Destructive) Nationalism
left: Positive / Inclusive Nationalism
- **Unites diverse citizens** around shared civic rights, freedom from foreign/colonial rule, and equality (**civic nationalism**).
- **Examples:** Ghana's early **CPP unity (`1949–1957`)**; South Africa's **Freedom Charter (`1955`)** and **1994 rainbow-nation constitution (`Act 108 of 1996`)** + **1995 Rugby World Cup** unity; **Pan-African solidarity** against colonialism.
right: Negative / Exclusive Nationalism
- Defines the nation by **strict racial, ethnic, or religious purity**, scapegoating "outsiders" as threats — often leading to repression, war, or ethnic cleansing (**ethnic/chauvinist nationalism**).
- **Examples:** **Nazi Germany's `Herrenvolk`** racism; **1948 Apartheid**; extreme exclusionary clashes in post-Cold War Eastern Europe after the **USSR dissolved (`December 1991`)**, notably the **Bosnian War (`April 1992`)** ("ethnic cleansing")!
```

### Vocabulary Check for Topic 4 Essays
- **Pan-Africanism:** Movement promoting unity, solidarity, and liberation among all African peoples and the African diaspora.
- **Neo-colonialism:** Indirect economic/political control by former colonial powers or multinational corporations even after formal flag independence (warned against by Nkrumah!).
- **Uhuru:** Swahili word for *freedom / independence* symbolising 1950s–1960s African decolonisation.

## Worked example: Assessing Nkrumah's Legacy (Balanced Essay Stance)

**Question:** *Critically evaluate Kwame Nkrumah's contribution to Ghana and African nationalism. (Short stance)*

- **Positive achievements:** Transformed anti-colonialism into a disciplined **non-violent mass movement (`CPP`, Positive Action)**, achieved historic independence (**6 March 1957**), expanded education/infrastructure, and championed continental **Pan-Africanism**.
- **Negative limitations:** Responded to economic strain (falling cocoa prices, debt) and regional opposition by banning rivals, **detaining critics without trial (`1958`)**, and fostering authoritarian rule that ended in the **1966 military coup**.

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Describing **all nationalism as evil** (or all nationalism as heroic) — Topic 4 explicitly asks you to distinguish **inclusive, liberating nationalism** from **exclusive, oppressive nationalism**!
> - Forgetting **Gold Coast's cocoa farmers (`1937–1938`) and ex-servicemen (`1948`)** — independence was driven from below by ordinary workers and soldiers, not Nkrumah acting alone.
> - Dating the **collapse of the USSR to `1990`** — Gorbachev resigned and the Soviet Union formally ended in **December 1991**.

## Practice

```questions
Q: Why was **Ghana's independence on `6 March 1957`** a landmark moment beyond West Africa? (2)
A: As the **first sub-Saharan African British colony** to achieve independence, Ghana shattered colonial myths that Africa was "unready" to self-govern and inspired liberation movements across the continent (including South Africa) through Nkrumah's **Pan-Africanism**.
---
Q: What was Nkrumah's **Positive Action (`January 1950`)** campaign, and how did it backfire on British colonial authority? (3)
A: Modeled on non-violent resistance, **Positive Action** used coordinated strikes, boycotts, and civil disobedience to demand immediate self-rule. Although Britain jailed Nkrumah, imprisonment boosted his popularity so sharply that the **CPP swept the 1951 elections**, forcing Britain to release him as Prime Minister.
---
Q: Distinguish between **inclusive civic nationalism** and **exclusive ethnic nationalism**, giving ONE Grade 11 historical example of each. (4)
A: **Inclusive nationalism** unites people across race/religion around equal citizenship and freedom from oppression (e.g. SA's **1955 Freedom Charter / 1994 transition** or **1957 Ghana**). **Exclusive nationalism** restricts belonging by blood/ethnicity and oppresses minorities (e.g. **Apartheid Afrikaner nationalism**, **Nazi Germany**, or **1992 Bosnian ethnic cleansing**).
```
