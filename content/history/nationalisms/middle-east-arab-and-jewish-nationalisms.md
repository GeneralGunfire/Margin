> **What you'll learn:** the parallel origins of **Jewish nationalism (Zionism)** and **Arab/Palestinian nationalism** up to 1917, how **conflicting British WWI promises** (*Hussein–McMahon correspondence, Sykes–Picot 1916, Balfour Declaration 2 Nov 1917*) fuelled unrest in Mandate Palestine, the **1947 UN Partition Plan** and **1948** (*Israeli "War of Independence" vs Palestinian An-Nakba*), regional wars (**1956 Suez, 1967 Six-Day War, 1973 Yom Kippur**), and key diplomacy (**Camp David 1978 / Egypt–Israel Treaty 1979, First Intifada 1987, Oslo Accords 1993**).
>
> **Prerequisites:** concept of competing nationalisms over one shared territory.
>
> **Why this matters:** History exams test **multi-perspectivity** above all here — presenting BOTH **Israeli/Jewish** and **Palestinian/Arab** historical experiences fairly, accurately, and neutrally without taking partisan sides!

## The idea in plain language

The Middle East conflict is **NOT** simply an ancient religious feud that stayed unchanged for 3 000 years — it is primarily a **modern 20th-century clash between two nationalisms** claiming self-determination in the **exact same historic land** (Palestine / Eretz Israel):
- **Jewish Nationalism (Zionism):** Born out of centuries of European **antisemitism and pogroms**, arguing Jewish safety required a sovereign homeland in their ancient biblical homeland.
- **Arab & Palestinian Nationalism:** Born among Arab peoples resisting **Ottoman Turkish rule** and then **European colonialism**, demanding full national independence and protection of Palestinian homes and land.

## Origins & Conflicting World War I Promises (`1897–1947`)

1. **Zionism (`1897`):** Following violent antisemitism in Russia and the Dreyfus Affair in France, **Theodor Herzl** convened the **First Zionist Congress in Basle (`29–31 August 1897`)**, launching the modern political goal of a Jewish homeland in Ottoman Palestine.
2. **Arab Nationalism (`1913` & `1915–1916`):** Arab leaders met at the **First Arab National Congress (`1913`)** seeking autonomy from the Ottoman Empire. During WWI, Britain promised **Sharif Hussein** Arab independence in exchange for revolting against the Ottomans (**Hussein–McMahon Correspondence**)!
3. **Contradictory British Promises:**
   - **Sykes–Picot Agreement (`1916`):** Secretly carved Ottoman Arab lands into British and French spheres of control!
   - **Balfour Declaration (`2 November 1917`):** Britain publicly declared support for *"the establishment in Palestine of a **national home for the Jewish people**"*, while stating *"nothing shall be done which may prejudice the civil and religious rights of existing non-Jewish communities"*!
4. **British Mandate (`1920s–1930s`):** Under a League of Nations Mandate, growing Jewish immigration (accelerating drastically in the 1930s as refugees fled **Nazi Germany**) clashed with Palestinian farmers fearing dispossession -> **1921 & 1929 clashes**, Jewish defence militia **Haganah**, the **1936–1939 Arab Revolt**, and Britain's 1937 Peel partition proposal.

## `1947–1949`: Partition, `14 May 1948` & Two Perspectives

Exhausted after WWII, Britain handed Palestine to the **United Nations**. On **29 November 1947**, the UN passed **Resolution 181 (Partition Plan)** proposing independent **Jewish** and **Arab** states with Jerusalem internationalised. Jewish leaders accepted; Palestinian and Arab leaders rejected dividing the land.

Fighting erupted immediately; on **14 May 1948**, **David Ben-Gurion** proclaimed the **State of Israel**, and neighbouring Arab armies invaded the next day (`1948 Arab–Israeli War`), ending in **1949 armistices** expanding Israeli territory beyond the UN line, while Jordan held the West Bank/East Jerusalem and Egypt held Gaza.

```compare
title: Two Contrasting Historical Perspectives on 1948
left: Israeli Perspective ("War of Independence")
- Viewed **14 May 1948** as hard-won survival and national rebirth in the ancestral homeland just three years after the **Holocaust**.
- Celebrates building a democratic haven against invading neighbouring armies.
right: Palestinian Perspective ("An-Nakba" — The Catastrophe)
- Viewed 1948 as **catastrophic dispossession**: roughly **700 000+ Palestinians** fled or were expelled from hundreds of villages, becoming permanent **refugees** (`UN Resolution 194, 11 December 1948` affirmed their right of return or compensation).
```

## Key Wars, Diplomacy & Turning Points (`1956–2000`)

```steps
title: Major turning points in Arab–Israeli & Palestinian history
step: **1. Suez Crisis (`1956`) & PLO (`1964`):** Egyptian President **Gamal Abdel Nasser** championing pan-Arab nationalism **nationalised the Suez Canal on `26 July 1956`**, triggering an Oct–Nov invasion by Britain, France, and Israel (halted by US/USSR pressure). In **1964**, the **PLO** was formed (first chairman **Ahmad Shukeiri**; **Yasser Arafat** became chairman in **1969**).
step: **2. Six-Day War (`5–10 June 1967`) & UN Resolution 242 (`22 Nov 1967`):** In a six-day war, Israel captured the **Sinai & Gaza** (from Egypt), **West Bank & East Jerusalem** (from Jordan), and **Golan Heights** (from Syria). **UN Resolution 242** called for Israeli withdrawal from occupied territories in exchange for secure peace (`land for peace`).
step: **3. Yom Kippur War (`6–25 October 1973`) & Camp David (`1978–1979`):** Egypt and Syria launched a surprise attack on 6 Oct 1973, shocking Israel and prompting **Camp David Accords (`17 September 1978`)** mediated by US President Jimmy Carter, leading to the **Egypt–Israel Peace Treaty (`26 March 1979`)** returning Sinai to Egypt.
step: **4. Intifada (`Dec 1987`) & Oslo Accords (`13 September 1993`):** Frustrated under occupation, Palestinians launched the grassroots **First Intifada (1987–1993)**. In **1988**, the PLO recognised Israel's right to exist; secret negotiations in **Norway** produced the **Oslo Accords**, signed in **Washington D.C. on `13 September 1993`** (creating the Palestinian Authority for interim self-rule) — though unresolved borders, settlements, refugees, and Jerusalem fuelled breakdown and a Second Intifada from 2000.
```

## Worked example: Answering a "Conflicting Promises" Source Question

**Question:** *Explain why historians argue Britain's WWI diplomacy (`1915–1917`) laid the foundation for long-term conflict in Palestine. (2 x 2) (4)*

1. **Incompatible commitments:** Britain promised Arabs independence (`Hussein–McMahon`), secretly planned Franco-British imperial partition (`Sykes–Picot, 1916`), and promised Zionists a Jewish *"national home"* in Palestine (**Balfour Declaration, `2 Nov 1917`**) — three mutually contradictory pledges over the same region!
2. **Unresolved tension under the Mandate:** Trying to satisfy both Jewish immigration (especially fleeing Nazi Europe) and Palestinian aspirations created violent clashes (`1929`, `1936 Revolt`) that Britain eventually abandoned to the UN in 1947.

```tryit
prompt: Fix the errors in these statements:
1. *"The Oslo Accords were signed in Oslo, Sweden in 1979."*
2. *"Yasser Arafat founded the PLO in 1964 as its very first chairman."*
3. *"The Six-Day War happened in 1973 when Egypt nationalised the Suez Canal."*
hint: Check **Norway vs Washington (`13 Sep 1993`)**, **1964 vs 1969 (`Ahmad Shukeiri`)**, and **`1956` vs `1967` vs `1973`**!
answer: 1. The **Oslo Accords** were negotiated secretly in **Norway** (not Sweden!) and signed in **Washington on `13 September 1993`** (**1979** was the Egypt–Israel Peace Treaty after the **1978 Camp David Accords**!).
2. The **PLO was founded in 1964** with **Ahmad Shukeiri** as its first chairman; **Yasser Arafat** became chairman in **1969**.
3. Nasser nationalised the Suez Canal in **`1956`**; the **Six-Day War** occurred in **`5–10 June 1967`**; the **Yom Kippur War** occurred in **`6–25 October 1973`**.
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Saying the Oslo Accords were signed in *"Oslo, Sweden"* — Oslo is in **Norway**, and the formal ceremony took place in **Washington D.C. (`13 September 1993`)**!
> - Taking a **one-sided partisan stance** in essays — DBE markers expect balanced empathy explaining **both** Israeli security/self-determination motives **and** Palestinian loss (*An-Nakba*) and statehood aspirations!
> - Mixing up **Camp David Accords (`17 Sep 1978`)** with the **Egypt–Israel Peace Treaty (`26 Mar 1979`)** or **UN Resolution 181 (`1947`)** with **Resolution 242 (`1967`)**.

## Practice

```questions
Q: Why do **Israelis** and **Palestinians** remember **1948** by two sharply different names? (3)
A: Israelis call it the **War of Independence** celebrating the survival and establishment of a Jewish state (**14 May 1948**) after the Holocaust; Palestinians call it **An-Nakba ("The Catastrophe")** mourning the loss of homeland and displacement of hundreds of thousands of refugees (**UN Resolution 194**).
---
Q: What territories did Israel occupy during the **Six-Day War (`5–10 June 1967`)**, and what principle did **UN Resolution 242 (`22 Nov 1967`)** propose? (3)
A: Israel occupied the **Sinai Peninsula and Gaza Strip** (Egypt), **West Bank and East Jerusalem** (Jordan), and **Golan Heights** (Syria). **UN Resolution 242** proposed *"land for peace"* — Israeli withdrawal from territories occupied in 1967 alongside Arab recognition of Israel's right to live securely in peace.
---
Q: Why is the **Balfour Declaration (`2 November 1917`)** viewed as deeply controversial in Middle East history? (2)
A: Britain publicly backed a *"national home for the Jewish people"* in Palestine while also promising not to harm the rights of existing Palestinian Arab residents — contradicting earlier British pledges of Arab independence and ignoring the political wishes of Palestine's Arab majority.
```
