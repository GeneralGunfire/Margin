> **What you'll learn:** the historical roots of **Afrikaner nationalism** (*Great Trek, Boer Republics, South African War, GRA 1875*), how economic hardship (**"Poor White problem"**, Great Depression, **Carnegie Commission 1927–1932**) fused with cultural mobilisation (**Broederbond `1918`, FAK, Volkskapitalisme, Dutch Reformed Church, Christian National Education, `1938` Eeufees**) to turn scattered Dutch/Afrikaans speakers into a disciplined **`Volk`**, and how **D.F. Malan's Purified National Party (`1934`)** rode exclusive nationalism to victory in **May 1948**.
>
> **Prerequisites:** Benedict Anderson's *"imagined community"*, 1929 Great Depression impact on SA, and African nationalism contrast.
>
> **Why this matters:** Paper 2 almost universally compares **African nationalism** and **Afrikaner nationalism**! You must explain how Afrikaner nationalists deliberately engineered **language, churches, banks, schools, and shared historical myths (`1938 Eeufees`)** to capture political and economic power by **1948**.

## The idea in plain language

In **1910**, Afrikaans-speaking whites did **not** think of themselves as one unified, prosperous bloc: Cape Afrikaners differed sharply from Transvaal farmers, English-speaking British firms owned almost all mines, banks, and shops, and tens of thousands of rural Afrikaners fell into deep urban poverty during droughts and the Great Depression (**"Poor White problem"**).

Afrikaner nationalist leaders solved this through **careful cultural and economic nation-building** — turning **language, Calvinist religion, economic co-operatives (`volkskapitalisme`), and memory of the Great Trek** into a tightly bound **exclusive `Volk` (`white Afrikaans-speakers`)**.

## Phase 1: Cultural & Language Foundations (`1875–1933`)

1. **Historical Memory (`Great Trek` & `South African War`):** Nationalist memory drew heavily on the **Great Trek (`c. 1835–1846`)**, the **Battle of Blood River (`16 Dec 1838`)**, and bitter memories of British scorched-earth tactics and **concentration camps** during the **South African War (`1899–1902`)** — promoting a belief that Afrikaners were a *"chosen people"* fighting British cultural domination (`Milner's anglicisation`).
2. **First & Second Language Movements:**
   - **Genootskap van Regte Afrikaners (`GRA`, Paarl, `14 August 1875`, S.J. du Toit):** Published *Die Afrikaanse Patriot* promoting Afrikaans instead of formal High Dutch.
   - **Second Language Movement (`1900–1930`):** Won recognition of **Afrikaans as an official language alongside English in `1925`** and published the **Afrikaans Bible translation in `1933`** — giving the *Volk* a shared literary and sacred language (`"imagined community"`!).
3. **Afrikaner Broederbond (`5 June 1918`):** Founded secretly as *Jong Zuid-Afrika* (`renamed Broederbond 1919`), this elite male secret society recruited teachers, clergy, editors, and civil servants to coordinate cultural bodies (**FAK**), newspapers (*Die Burger*, *Die Transvaler*, *Die Huisgenoot*), and **Christian National Education (`CNE`)**!

## Phase 2: Class, Race & `Volkskapitalisme` (`1924–1948`)

```steps
title: How economics and the Great Depression radicalised Afrikaner nationalism
step: **1. Hertzog's "Civilised Labour" & Colour Bar (`1924–1926`):** After Prime Minister J.B.M. Hertzog's Pact government took office (`1924`), laws like the **Industrial Conciliation Act (`1924`)**, **Wage Act (`1925`)**, and **Mines and Works Amendment Act (`1926`)** protected skilled/semi-skilled jobs on railways (`SAR&H`), steelworks (`ISCOR`), and mines for white workers ahead of Black workers!
step: **2. Great Depression & Carnegie Commission (`1927–1932`):** Drought and the 1929 crash drove ~300 000 rural Afrikaners into city slums competing with Black jobseekers. The **1932 Carnegie Commission on Poor Whites** warned of "racial mixing" and pushed racially segregated upliftment rather than class unity across colour lines!
step: **3. Volkskapitalisme (`1939 Volkskongres` & `Reddingsdaadbond`):** Afrikaner businessmen and the Broederbond urged ordinary Afrikaners to **save inside Afrikaner-owned banks and insurers (`Volkskas`, `Sanlam`, `Santam`)** so *"Afrikaner capital"* could rescue poor white workers into middle-class loyalty!
step: **4. Religion (`Dutch Reformed Church`) & `Volksmoeder`:** Drawing on Dutch neo-Calvinist **Abraham Kuyper's** idea of distinct *"spheres"*, DRC ministers framed racial separation as Christian duty, while mothers (**volksmoeders**) were celebrated as guardians of Afrikaner culture in the home.
```

## Phase 3: The `1934` Split, `1938` Eeufees & `1948` Victory

- **1934 Political Split:** When Hertzog merged with Jan Smuts (`United Party`), **Dr D.F. Malan** broke away to form the **Purified National Party (`Gesuiwerde Nasionale Party`)**, accusing Hertzog of betraying Afrikaner purity and working too closely with English capital.
- **1938 Eeufees (Great Trek Centenary):** Re-enacted ox-wagons crossed South Africa drawing massive crowds in traditional *Voortrekker* dress, culminating when the foundation stone of the **Voortrekker Monument** was laid on **16 December 1938** — turning a divided group into an emotional national mass movement!
- **May 1948 Election:** Exploiting white fears over Black wartime urbanisation (`"swart gevaar"` propaganda) and uniting with Havenga's Afrikaner Party, **Malan's National Party** won a narrow 5-seat parliamentary majority (despite winning ~40% of total votes) — opening the door to **Apartheid**.

```compare
title: African Nationalism vs Afrikaner Nationalism (Classic Paper 2 Comparison!)
left: African Nationalism (Mainstream / Charterist)
- **Goal:** End colonial/minority discrimination and expand **universal equal citizenship**.
- **Membership trajectory:** Moved across ethnic divisions (`SANNC 1912`) toward **non-racial inclusive solidarity** (`Freedom Charter 1955`).
- **Role of religion/culture:** Used diverse faiths and civic rights arguments (`African Claims 1943`) to challenge oppression.
right: Afrikaner Nationalism (1920s–1948)
- **Goal:** Protect minority ethnic identity against **English economic power** AND **Black majority competition**.
- **Membership boundary:** Strictly **exclusive ethnic boundary** (`white Afrikaans-speakers` only — excluding even Black Afrikaans speakers!).
- **Result in power:** Captured the state in **1948** and used **apartheid laws** to entrench racial privilege!
```

## Worked example: Applying "Imagined Communities" to Afrikaner Nationalism

**Exam question:** *Explain how Afrikaner nationalists constructed an "imagined community" between 1918 and 1938. (Essay/Paragraph guide)*

1. **Print media & standard language:** *Die Burger*, *Die Huisgenoot*, official recognition of **Afrikaans (1925)**, and the **1933 Afrikaans Bible** let people across distant farms and towns read and pray in one shared national voice.
2. **Coordinated organisations (`Broederbond & FAK`):** Connected teachers, churches, youth movements (*Voortrekkers*), and trade unions around shared Christian National symbols.
3. **Shared ritual & historical myth (`1938 Eeufees`):** Ox-wagon processions and the **Voortrekker Monument** turned distant ancestors' trek into emotional national unity.

```tryit
title: Connect economics & culture in 1930s Afrikaner Nationalism
prompt: Explain in 3–4 clear lines how the **Great Depression** and **1938 Eeufees** worked together to strengthen Malan's **Purified National Party (`1934`)** before 1948.
hint: Link **Poor Whites + Carnegie Commission (`1932`) + Volkskapitalisme** with the **emotional cultural unity of `1938`**!
answer: The Great Depression deepened the **Poor White crisis** documented by the **1932 Carnegie Commission**, pushing poor Afrikaners to rely on **Volkskapitalisme** (`Volkskas`, `Reddingsdaadbond`, white job protection) rather than cross-racial class solidarity. When the **1938 Eeufees** ox-wagon trek united rich and poor Afrikaners emotionally around shared heritage, **Malan's Purified National Party** successfully captured both working-class and middle-class Afrikaner loyalty ahead of **1948**.
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Saying **all Afrikaans-speakers were included in Afrikaner nationalism** — Malan's movement strictly defined the *Volk* as **white** Afrikaans-speakers, systematically excluding Coloured Afrikaans-speakers!
> - Treating Afrikaner nationalism as purely cultural/linguistic and ignoring **economic class conflict** (English control of mining/commerce + the **Poor White** urban crisis + *Volkskapitalisme*).
> - Confusing **Hertzog (1924)** with **Malan (1934 / 1948)** — Malan broke away from Hertzog in 1934 precisely because Malan demanded a purer, more exclusive Afrikaner nationalist project!

## Practice

```questions
Q: Why did the **Afrikaner Broederbond (`1918`)** create bodies like the **FAK** and promote **Volkskapitalisme**? (3)
A: To coordinate Afrikaner schools, churches, newspapers, and cultural clubs (**FAK**), and to mobilise Afrikaner savings into Afrikaner-owned banks/businesses (**Volkskapitalisme / Reddingsdaadbond**) so Afrikaners could break English economic dominance and uplift Poor Whites within an exclusively ethnic movement.
---
Q: How did **1925** and **1933** strengthen Afrikaner cultural nationalism? (2)
A: In **1925**, Afrikaans gained recognition as an **official language** alongside English (replacing Dutch), and in **1933**, the complete **Afrikaans Bible** was published — standardising the language across churches, schools, and homes.
---
Q: Why did the **1932 Carnegie Commission** and the **1938 Eeufees** help turn exclusive nationalism into political power in 1948? (3)
A: The Carnegie study turned Poor White poverty into a racialised ethnic rescue mission, while the **1938 Eeufees** ox-wagon trek forged intense emotional unity across class lines behind **D.F. Malan's Purified NP**, paving the way for the **May 1948** *"swart gevaar"* election win.
```
