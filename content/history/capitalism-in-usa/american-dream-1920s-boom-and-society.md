> **What you'll learn:** the core features of **American capitalism** (*private ownership, profit motive, free market, laissez-faire*), why the **USA boomed in the 1920s** after WWI (*assembly-line mass production, hire purchase, margin buying*), who the **"Roaring Twenties"** left behind (farmers, African Americans, immigrants), and the **hidden structural weaknesses** simmering underneath apparent prosperity before October 1929.
>
> **Prerequisites:** basic contrast between communism (state planning) and capitalism (private markets).
>
> **Why this matters:** Examiners love asking whether 1920s America was *truly* prosperous for everyone — AND why the **1929 Crash** triggered a deep **Great Depression** instead of a brief market dip! Every root cause sits right here inside the 1920s boom.

## The idea in plain language

While Lenin and Stalin replaced markets with state control in Russia, the **United States** went in the opposite direction: **pure free-market capitalism**. Under Republican presidents **Warren Harding** (*"return to normalcy"*) and **Calvin Coolidge** (*"the business of America is business"*), Washington stepped back (**laissez-faire**): cut taxes on the wealthy, raised tariffs against foreign imports, crushed labour strikes, and celebrated the **American Dream** — the belief that anyone working hard (*rugged individualism*) could rise from rags to riches!

On the surface, **1920s America looked dazzling** (cars, jazz, radio, skyscrapers). Underneath, the boom rested on **unequal wages, overproduction, and borrowed money**.

## Why the USA Boomed in the 1920s

1. **World War I Advantage (1914–1918):** While European factories lay ruined in debt, the USA entered WWI late (**6 April 1917**), sold food and weapons to the Allies at huge profit, and became the world's leading industrial and creditor nation — then turned **isolationist** (the Senate rejected the Treaty of Versailles on **19 March 1920**, staying out of the League of Nations).
2. **Mass Production & Henry Ford:** Moving **assembly lines** turned luxury items into everyday goods — **Ford's Model T ("Tin Lizzie")** price plunged so ordinary workers could afford cars, driving demand for steel, rubber, glass, petrol, and roads.
3. **Electricity, Radio & Advertising:** Electrified homes bought refrigerators, radios, and vacuum cleaners, fuelled by modern nationwide advertising.
4. **Buy Now, Pay Later (`Hire Purchase` & `Speculation on the Margin`):** Consumers bought goods on credit instalments, while millions bought company shares **on the margin** — putting down just **~10% cash** and borrowing the rest from stockbrokers!

## Who Was Left Out? (`Society in the Roaring Twenties`)

| Group | Glossy Myth ("Roaring Twenties") | Harsh Everyday Reality |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Farmers & Rural Workers** | Modern tractors and booming towns. | **Overproduction** after European farms recovered caused crop prices to crash; millions fell into mortgage debt and left farms! |
| **African Americans** | Great Migration north to cities; vibrant **Harlem Renaissance** jazz & literature. | **Jim Crow segregation** and lynching in the South; racist housing/job barriers in the North; resurgence of the **Ku Klux Klan (KKK)**! |
| **Immigrants & Unions** | *"Land of opportunity"* for all newcomers. | **Red Scare** fear of communism; **Immigration Act of 1924** imposed strict racist quotas slashing Southern/Eastern European & Asian entry; anti-union crackdowns. |
| **Women** | Won vote (1920); *flappers* broke fashion/social rules; consumer appliances. | Still barred from most leadership/professions and paid far less than men for identical work. |
| **Prohibition (1920–1933)** | 18th Amendment banned alcohol to cure social ills. | Backfired into illegal **speakeasies, bootlegging**, and violent organised crime (**Al Capone**) until repealed in December 1933. |

## The 5 Hidden Faultlines Beneath the Boom

```compare
title: Surface Boom vs Hidden Structural Weaknesses (Pre-1929)
left: Visible 1920s Prosperity
- Dow Jones share prices soared almost continuously (1924–1929).
- Factories churned out cars, radios, and appliances faster than ever.
- Unregulated banks eagerly lent money to investors.
right: Hidden Time-Bombs Underneath
- **1. Unequal Wealth Distribution:** Top 5% held roughly a third of national income while worker wages stagnated — ordinary families couldn't keep buying factory output without debt!
- **2. Overproduction:** Warehouses filled up with unsold cars and grain.
- **3. Credit & Margin Speculation:** Both households AND banks rested on fragile borrowed money.
- **4. High Tariffs (`Fordney–McCumber`):** Shut out foreign imports — so European countries slapped retaliatory tariffs on US farm/factory exports!
- **5. Weak Unregulated Banks:** Thousands of small banks had zero federal deposit insurance.
```

## Worked example: Explaining "Was the American Dream real in the 1920s?"

**Exam question:** *Using historical evidence, evaluate whether all Americans benefited equally from the 1920s economic boom. (Short structured argument)*

- **Who benefited:** Industrial shareholders, urban middle-class professionals, corporate monopolies (*US Steel*, *General Motors*), and white suburban households enjoyed rising consumption, electricity, and stock gains.
- **Who was excluded:** **Farmers** suffered falling prices and foreclosures throughout the 1920s; **African Americans** faced Jim Crow terror, KKK violence, and wage discrimination; **low-paid factory workers and recent immigrants** faced anti-union repression and the 1924 quota restrictions. **Conclusion:** Prosperity was real yet **deeply uneven and debt-driven**.

```tryit
title: Explain "Buying Shares on the Margin" in plain English
prompt: A factory clerk in 1928 has **$100** cash and wants to buy **$1 000** worth of Radio Corporation stock. Explain:
1. How **buying on the margin** lets them do this.
2. What happens instantly if that stock drops from **$1 000** to **$600**!
hint: Remember: the broker lent **$900** — and wants that loan repaid regardless!
answer: 1. The investor pays **10% ($100)** upfront and borrows the remaining **90% ($900)** from a broker/bank, expecting share prices to keep rising.
2. If prices fall to **$600**, the investor loses their entire $100 deposit AND still owes the broker **$300** — triggering panic **margin calls** forcing everyone to dump shares at once!
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Saying **everyone in the USA was rich in the 1920s** — farmers entered depression *years before 1929*, and wealth inequality was extreme!
> - Confusing **laissez-faire** (*government stays OUT of business regulation*) with the **New Deal** (*government intervenes actively*).
> - Writing that **Prohibition ended the Depression in 1931** — Prohibition lasted **1920 to December 1933** and repeal happened *during* FDR's first year in office!

## Practice

```questions
Q: Define **laissez-faire capitalism** and **rugged individualism** as practised by US presidents in the 1920s. (3)
A: **Laissez-faire** means minimal government interference in the economy (low taxes on business, little banking/labour regulation). **Rugged individualism** was the belief that individuals should succeed through personal effort without government welfare handouts.
---
Q: How did **high US tariffs** in the 1920s hurt American farmers and manufacturers even before 1929? (2)
A: High tariffs blocked European goods from entering the US market, leaving European nations unable to earn dollars to repay war debts — and provoking **retaliatory tariffs** that closed foreign markets to surplus US crops and goods.
---
Q: Name THREE social groups who largely missed out on 1920s prosperity and give one reason for each. (3)
A: 1. **Farmers** (crop overproduction and falling prices/foreclosures). 2. **African Americans** (Jim Crow segregation, low wages, KKK intimidation). 3. **Southern/Eastern European immigrants** (restricted by the 1924 Immigration Act quotas and exploited in low-wage sweatshops).
```
