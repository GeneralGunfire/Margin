> **What you'll learn:** how global racism (`Jim Crow USA, Australia, Nazi Germany`) and **post-1910 South African segregation** (`1913 Land Act, 1923 Urban Areas Act, Hertzog's 1924–1926 colour bar, 1936 Acts`) laid the foundation for apartheid, why **D.F. Malan's National Party** won in **May 1948** (`Fagan vs Sauer` commissions), how **apartheid differed from earlier segregation**, and how key **1949–1959 laws** engineered social, residential, educational, political, and economic separation.
>
> **Prerequisites:** Afrikaner nationalism (`1934–1948`) and African land/labour dispossession (`1910–1936`).
>
> **Why this matters:** Paper 2 essays frequently ask: **"Did 1948 mark a brand-new system, or merely intensify existing segregation?"** Knowing both the **1910–1948 continuities** AND the **systematic, rigid legal machinery of 1949–1959** lets you score top marks on both essays and law-table source questions!

## The idea in plain language

Racial discrimination in South Africa did **not** start in May 1948. Between **1910 and 1948**, British-aligned and Afrikaner-led governments alike built a system of **segregation**: white minority political power, migrant mine labour, pass controls, and tiny rural reserves.

What changed in **1948** was **systematisation, ideological rigidity, and total legal reach**: Malan's National Party turned messy, flexible segregation into **apartheid** (*"apartness"*) — a state-enforced cradle-to-grave racial grid backed by police power and homeland fragmentation.

## Step 1: Global Context & Segregation After Union (`1910–1948`)

- **Global 1920s–1930s Context:** Segregation in South Africa developed alongside **Jim Crow laws and KKK terror** in the USA, **White Australia** immigration/assimilation policies, **sterilisation laws** in Europe/USA, and **Nazi racial laws** in Germany — *except* South Africa **expanded** legalised white supremacy right after **1948** while the rest of the post-WWII world moved toward decolonisation and the **UN Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948)**!
- **Key Pre-1948 Segregation Laws:**
  - **1913 Natives Land Act:** Restricted Black ownership/tenancy to **~7–8%** of South Africa's land (forcing rural families into wage/migrant labour).
  - **1923 Native (Urban Areas) Act:** Declared cities *"white areas"* where Black South Africans were tolerated only as temporary workers under **pass laws**.
  - **1924–1926 Colour Bar (`Hertzog`):** **Industrial Conciliation Act (`1924`)** excluded pass-bearing Black workers from recognised trade unions; **Wage Act (`1925`)** & **Mines and Works Amendment Act (`1926`)** reserved skilled jobs for whites.
  - **1936 Acts (`Hertzog`):** **Representation of Natives Act** stripped Black voters from the Cape common roll; **Native Trust and Land Act** raised maximum reserve land to **~13–13.6%**.

## Step 2: Why the National Party Won in May 1948 (`Fagan vs Sauer`)

During WWII (`1939–1945`), factory expansion drew hundreds of thousands of Black workers into towns (`Alexandria, Sophiatown`), relaxing pass enforcement temporarily:
- **United Party (`Smuts`) — Fagan Commission (`1946`):** Concluded Black urbanisation was permanent and urged pragmatic relaxation of pass controls.
- **Herstigte/Purified NP (`Malan`) — Sauer Commission (`1947`):** Demanded strict total **apartheid**, intensified influx control, and cheap controlled labour for white farmers!
- **May 1948 Election:** Smuts's United Party looked complacent and fractured, while **Malan's HNP allied with Havenga's Afrikaner Party**, campaigned fiercely on *"swart gevaar"* (white fear of Black majority rule), and won a **5-seat parliamentary majority despite winning only ~40% of total votes** (aided by rural seat weighting!).

## Step 3: Legalising the Apartheid State (`1949–1959`)

```steps
title: Core Apartheid Laws Grouped by Sphere of Control
step: **1. Social & Classification (`Personal Life`):** **Prohibition of Mixed Marriages Act 55 (`1949`)** & **Immorality Amendment Act 21 (`1950`)** outlawed interracial marriage and relationships. **Population Registration Act 30 (`1950`)** classified every South African rigidly into racial categories (*White, Native/Bantu, Coloured*, later *Asian*) — tearing families apart (`e.g. Sandra Laing` case!). **Reservation of Separate Amenities Act 49 (`1953`)** enforced segregated beaches, buses, benches, and hospitals (*explicitly allowing unequal facilities*).
step: **2. Residential & Land (`Space & Movement`):** **Group Areas Act 41 (`1950`)** zoned cities strictly by race, triggering devastating forced removals (*Sophiatown, District Six, Cato Manor*). **Native Resettlement Act** & **Natives (Abolition of Passes) Act (`1952` `dompas`)** tightened urban influx control.
step: **3. Education & Culture (`Mind Control`):** **Bantu Education Act 47 (`1953`, Verwoerd)** shifted Black schools out of mission control into a central state syllabus explicitly designed to train Black youth for manual labour, NOT equality! **Extension of University Education Act (`1959`)** barred Black students from open white universities (`UCT, Wits`) into segregated ethnic colleges.
step: **4. Political & Labour Control (`Power`):** **Suppression of Communism Act 44 (`1950`)** defined "communism" so broadly the minister could ban ANY anti-apartheid activist or group! **Separate Representation of Voters Act (`1951`, forced through via Senate-packing in `1956`)** removed Coloured voters from the Cape roll. **Bantu Authorities Act (`1951`)** & **Promotion of Bantu Self-Government Act (`1959`)** laid the blueprint for ethnic **Bantustans** to strip Black South Africans of SA citizenship! **Native Labour (Settlement of Disputes) Act (`1953`)** denied Black unions legal strike rights.
```

## Worked example: Linking Laws to Impact (`Source/Paragraph Practice`)

**Question:** *Explain how the **Population Registration Act (1950)** and **Group Areas Act (1950)** worked together to destroy multiracial urban communities. (2 x 2) (4)*

1. **Population Registration Act 30 (`1950`):** Assigned every individual a fixed racial identity document — without this master classification register, racial zoning and marriage bans could not be policed!
2. **Group Areas Act 41 (`1950`):** Used those official racial categories to declare vibrant mixed neighbourhoods like **Sophiatown** and **District Six** *"white areas"*, forcibly bulldozing homes and removing Black, Indian, and Coloured families to distant townships.

```tryit
title: Match 3 famous Apartheid Acts to their exact year & sphere
prompt: Match each law to its **Year** (`1949`, `1950`, `1953`) and explain ONE direct consequence:
1. `Bantu Education Act 47` | 2. `Group Areas Act 41` | 3. `Prohibition of Mixed Marriages Act 55`
hint: Check **Social (`1949`)**, **Residential (`1950`)**, and **Educational (`1953`)**!
answer: 1. **Bantu Education Act 47 (`1953`, Educational):** Closed most mission schools and enforced an inferior curriculum aimed at restricting Black learners to subordinate labour roles.
2. **Group Areas Act 41 (`1950`, Residential):** Forced racial segregation of towns/cities, uprooting established communities (`Sophiatown, District Six`).
3. **Prohibition of Mixed Marriages Act 55 (`1949`, Social):** Criminalised marriage across racial lines, marking the first major statutory pillar of National Party apartheid.
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Saying the **1913 Natives Land Act gave 13%** of the land — `1913` allocated **~7–8%**; the **1936 Native Trust and Land Act** expanded it to **~13–13.6%**!
> - Thinking the **Suppression of Communism Act (1950)** banned *only* communists — its vague wording let the state ban **non-communist trade unionists, liberal critics, and nationalist leaders** simply by "naming" them!
> - Describing **apartheid as identical to pre-1948 segregation** — always show *both* continuity (passes, land dispossession) *and* radical intensification (Population Registration, Bantustan denationalisation, Bantu Education, Group Areas).

## Practice

```questions
Q: Contrast the **Fagan Commission (`1946`)** and the **Sauer Commission (`1947`)** ahead of the **May 1948 election**. (3)
A: The **Fagan Commission** (United Party) recognised Black urbanisation as permanent and advised relaxing pass/influx controls; the **Sauer Commission** (National Party) recommended reversing integration via rigid **apartheid**, tighter influx control, and separate ethnic development.
---
Q: Why is the **Population Registration Act 30 of 1950** often called the "cornerstone" of apartheid legislation? (2)
A: Because it classified every resident by race on a national register, providing the legal definition needed to enforce **Group Areas, Mixed Marriages, Separate Amenities, and Bantu Education**.
---
Q: How did the National Party remove **Coloured voters** from the Cape common voters' roll despite initial Supreme Court opposition? (2)
A: After the **1951 Separate Representation of Voters Act** was struck down for lacking a two-thirds joint sitting majority, the NP enlarged/packed the **Senate** (`1955`) to secure the required majority and validate the removal in **1956**.
```
