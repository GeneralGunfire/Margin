>
> **Prerequisites:** 1950s Defiance Campaign, Freedom Charter (`1955`), and PAC breakaway (`April 1959`).
>
> **Why this matters:** Topic 5's finale is one of the most heavily examined topics in Grade 11 Paper 2 AND links straight into Grade 12 History! ## The idea in plain language

Throughout the 1950s, anti-apartheid leaders tried **peaceful protests, petitions, boycotts, and non-violent defiance**. Every time peaceful protests grew larger, the apartheid government answered with **harsher police laws, mass arrests, and bullets** — finally outlawing the ANC and PAC completely in **1960**.

By **1961**, leaders like Nelson Mandela concluded that **non-violence alone could not defeat a state that answered peaceful marches with gunfire** — turning **1960–1964** into the decisive shift toward **underground armed resistance, exile, and global isolation**.

## Step 1: State Repression in the 1950s (`1953–1961`)

1. **Crushing Defiance (`1953`):** After the 1952 Defiance Campaign, Parliament passed the **Public Safety Act (`1953`)** (allowing States of Emergency and detention without trial) and **Criminal Law Amendment Act (`1953`)** (heavy fines, jail, and whipping for civil disobedience!).
2. **The Treason Trial (`5 December 1956 – 29 March 1961`):** Police arrested **156 Congress Alliance leaders** (`Mandela, Sisulu, Albert Luthuli, Helen Joseph, Lilian Ngoyi`) charging them with *"high treason"* for advocating the **Freedom Charter**! After a marathon four-year trial tying leaders down in court, **all remaining accused were acquitted on 29 March 1961** because the state failed to prove violent intent!

## Step 2: Sharpeville (`21 March 1960`) — The Turning Point

After breaking away from the ANC in **April 1959**, **Robert Sobukwe's PAC** launched a peaceful nationwide **anti-pass campaign on `21 March 1960`**: protesters left passes at home and presented themselves politely at police stations for arrest (*"No bail! No defence! No fine!"*).

- **Sharpeville Massacre (`21 March 1960`):** Outside Sharpeville police station (Vereeniging), nervous police panicked and opened fire with submachine guns on an unarmed crowd — killing **69 people** (many shot in the back while fleeing) and wounding ~180! Across Cape Town (Langa), marches erupted too.
- **Immediate State Clampdown:**
  - **30 March 1960:** Government declared a **State of Emergency** across 122 districts, detaining thousands (`including Sobukwe`).
  - **7–8 April 1960 (`Unlawful Organisations Act`):** **Both the ANC and PAC were banned!**
  - **Whites-Only Republic (`5 October 1960` referendum -> `31 May 1961`):** Facing furious condemnation from newly independent Commonwealth nations over Sharpeville, Prime Minister **H.F. Verwoerd** withdrew South Africa's Commonwealth application (March 1961) and declared South Africa an isolated **Republic on `31 May 1961`**.

title: How 1960–1961 forced the shift to Underground Armed Struggle
step: **1. Non-violence met with bullets (`21 March 1960`):** Peaceful anti-pass marches resulted in 69 dead at Sharpeville (`and shootings in Langa`).
step: **2. Legal opposition banned (`April 1960`):** Outlawing the ANC and PAC made peaceful open mass organising illegal.
step: **3. Republic leaves the Commonwealth (`31 May 1961`):** Verwoerd rejected all appeals for a non-racial National Convention after a May 1961 stayaway.
step: **4. Launch of `MK` (`16 December 1961`) & `Poqo`:** Underground leaders formed **Umkhonto we Sizwe (`MK`, "Spear of the Nation", led by Mandela)** targeting **sabotage against infrastructure/installations (`pylons, government offices`) while strictly avoiding loss of life** initially — whereas the PAC's underground wing **Poqo** targeted armed insurrection against collaborators and whites.
```

## Step 3: Liliesleaf Raid (`1963`) & the Rivonia Trial (`1963–1964`)

- **Mandela Captured (`5 August 1962`):** After secretly touring Africa for military training/aid (`"Black Pimpernel"`), Mandela was arrested near **Howick** and sentenced to 5 years for incitement and leaving SA illegally.
- **Liliesleaf Farm Raid (`11 July 1963`):** Police raided the secret **MK High Command headquarters at Liliesleaf Farm** in Rivonia, Johannesburg, seizing *Operation Mayibuye* documents and arresting **Walter Sisulu, Govan Mbeki, Raymond Mhlaba, Ahmed Kathrada, Denis Goldberg**, and others (later joined by **Mandela** in custody!).
- **Rivonia Trial (`9 October 1963 – 12 June 1964`):**
  - Ten accused faced **sabotage charges** (**NOT** treason — though the state demanded the death penalty!).
  - **Mandela's Dock Statement (`20 April 1964`):** Rather than pleading for mercy, Mandela turned the courtroom into a moral indictment of apartheid: *"I have cherished the ideal of a democratic and free society... It is an ideal for which I am prepared to die."*
  - **Verdict (`12 June 1964`):** Eight accused (including **Mandela, Sisulu, Mbeki, Mhlaba, Kathrada, Motsoaledi, Mlangeni, Goldberg**) received **life imprisonment** (`Robben Island`; Goldberg to Pretoria Central!).
- **Aftermath Inside South Africa (`1964–1969`):** With internal leadership jailed or forced into exile (**Oliver Tambo** rebuilt the external ANC mission), intense police repression silenced overt mass protest temporarily during a 1960s economic boom — until **Black Consciousness (`Steve Biko`)** and **1976 Soweto** revived resistance!

title: Treason Trial (1956–1961) vs Rivonia Trial (1963–1964) — NEVER confuse them!
left: Treason Trial (`5 Dec 1956 – 29 Mar 1961`)
- **Who:** **156 Congress Alliance leaders** arrested after the **1955 Freedom Charter**.

right: Rivonia Trial (`9 Oct 1963 – 12 Jun 1964`)

```

## Step 4: "Apartheid" Becomes an International Word

While the rest of the world adopted the **UN Declaration of Human Rights (`1948`)** and decolonised Asia and Africa (`Ghana 1957`, *"Wind of Change"*), South Africa uniquely wrote rigid racial inequality into modern law — turning **"apartheid"** into a globally condemned term (`"polecat of the world"`):
- **UN Security Council Resolution 134 (`1 April 1960`):** First Security Council resolution condemning South Africa right after Sharpeville, followed by voluntary arms embargoes (`1963`), sports boycotts, trade/academic boycotts, and **OAU (`1963`)** isolation!

## Worked example: Explaining "Why did the ANC turn to armed struggle in 1961?"

**Essay argument points:**
1. **Failure of 50 years of peaceful methods:** Petitions (`1912–1940`), Defiance (`1952`), Freedom Charter (`1955`), and the Women's March (`1956`) only produced **harsher laws (`1953 Public Safety Act`) and mass arrests (`1956 Treason Trial`)**.
2. **Sharpeville Massacre (`21 March 1960`):** Shooting 69 unarmed protesters proved the state would kill peaceful marchers.
3. **Banning of ANC & PAC (`April 1960`) & Republic (`May 1961`):** Closing every legal avenue forced underground activists to launch **MK (`16 Dec 1961`)** using targeted **sabotage** to pressure the regime toward negotiation without mass bloodshed.

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Writing **`31 March`** instead of **`21 March 1960`** for Sharpeville (commemorated annually as **Human Rights Day**!).
> - Mixing up the **1956–1961 Treason Trial** (156 accused, ALL acquitted) with the **1963–1964 Rivonia Trial** (10 accused, 8 sentenced to life for **sabotage**).
> - Claiming **MK initially targeted civilians** — Mandela and the MK High Command specifically restricted early **1961 sabotage** to empty government buildings, power pylons, and installations to minimise civilian deaths.

## Practice

```questions
Q: Why is **21 March 1960 (`Sharpeville Massacre`)** considered the decisive turning point in anti-apartheid resistance? (3)
A: Police killed **69 unarmed anti-pass protesters** (organised by the PAC), triggering a **State of Emergency (`30 March`)**, the **banning of the ANC and PAC (`7–8 April 1960`)**, and **UN Resolution 134** — convincing liberation leaders that legal non-violent protest was exhausted and leading to underground armed wings (**MK** & **Poqo**) in **1961**.
---
Q: Distinguish between the **1956–1961 Treason Trial** and the **1963–1964 Rivonia Trial** on **(a) charges** and **(b) outcome**. (4)
A: **(a) Charges:** The Treason Trial charged **156 Congress Alliance activists with High Treason** over the Freedom Charter; the Rivonia Trial charged **10 MK leaders with sabotage** after the **11 July 1963 Liliesleaf Farm raid**. **(b) Outcome:** All Treason Trial accused were **acquitted (`29 March 1961`)**; **8 Rivonia accused received life imprisonment (`12 June 1964`)**.
---
Q: What did **UN Security Council Resolution 134 (`1 April 1960`)** signal for South Africa's standing in the world? (2)
A: Passed days after Sharpeville, it formally condemned apartheid killings and urged South Africa to abandon racial separation — launching growing diplomatic isolation, arms/sports boycotts, and South Africa's **1961 exit from the Commonwealth**.
```
