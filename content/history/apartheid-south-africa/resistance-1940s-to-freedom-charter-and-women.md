> **What you'll learn:** how anti-apartheid resistance transformed from polite petitions (`1912–1930s`) into militant **non-violent mass mobilisation (`1940s–1956`)** — grassroots boycotts & the **1946 mineworkers' strike**, the **1949 Programme of Action**, **1952 Defiance Campaign**, **Congress of the People & Freedom Charter (`25–26 June 1955`)**, and **FEDSAW & the 1956 Women's March (`9 August 1956`)** — set alongside global protests (`US Civil Rights`).
>
> **Prerequisites:** apartheid laws (`1949–1953`) and 1940s ANC Youth League / *African Claims*.
>
> **Why this matters:** Topic 5 Paper 2 questions heavily test **how resistance tactics evolved** (`petition -> boycott/stayaway -> planned civil disobedience -> broad multi-racial Congress Alliance`) AND the vital **leadership role of women and Indian/Coloured organisations**, not just the ANC!

## The idea in plain language

Between **1912 and 1940**, South African resistance movements wrote respectful letters, signed petitions, and sent delegations to London — and saw their rights shrink anyway.

From the mid-1940s onward, a younger generation (**ANC Youth League, 1944**) allied with Indian, Coloured, trade-union, and women's organisations decided: **if polite requests are ignored, ordinary people must disrupt unjust laws openly through coordinated mass non-violent defiance!**

## Phase 1: Grassroots Turning Points (`1942–1950`)

- **Community & Worker Action (`1942–1946`):** Ordinary people acted before national politicians caught up! **Alexandra bus boycotts (`1943, 1944`)** forced bus fare hikes down; **Marabastad protests (`1942`)** challenged pay/conditions; **over 60 000 African mineworkers struck (`August 1946`)** for living wages before police crushed the strike violently.
- **Multi-Racial Unity (`1947` Doctors' Pact):** **Dr A.B. Xuma** (ANC) signed a joint pact with **Dr Yusuf Dadoo** (TIC) and **Dr Monty Naicker** (NIC), inspired by Gandhi's *satyagraha* (`1946` Indian Passive Resistance against the "Ghetto Act") and joined by the **Non-European Unity Movement (`1943`)**.
- **1949 Programme of Action (`December 1949`):** Led by the ANC Youth League (**Mandela, Sisulu, Tambo, Lembede's legacy** under President **Dr James Moroka**), the ANC officially shifted from petitions to **boycotts, strikes, civil disobedience, and stayaways** — beginning with **1 May 1950 Freedom Day** (police shot 18 protesters) and **26 June 1950 National Day of Protest**.

## Phase 2: The 1952 Defiance Campaign & `1955` Freedom Charter

```steps
title: From the Defiance Campaign (1952) to Kliptown (1955)
step: **1. Defiance Campaign (`26 June 1952 – early 1953`):** Jointly launched by the **ANC and SA Indian Congress (`SAIC`)**, with **Nelson Mandela** as Volunteer-in-Chief! Over **8 000 disciplined volunteers** deliberately broke six unjust laws (entering `"Whites Only"` railway waiting rooms, breaking curfews, refusing passes) and willingly went to jail without resistance to clog courts and win global attention. ANC membership soared from ~7 000 to ~100 000!
step: **2. Building the Congress Alliance (`1953–1955`):** To unite everyone against apartheid, the **ANC** joined **SAIC** (Indian), **SACPO** (Coloured), **Congress of Democrats (`COD`)** (progressive white allies), and **SACTU** (non-racial trade unions) — inviting ordinary citizens across townships and farms to send in their demands (*"Let us speak together of freedom!"*).
step: **3. Congress of the People (`Kliptown, 25–26 June 1955`):** Thousands of delegates (estimates range around several thousand — historian accounts differ between ~2 844 and ~8 000) gathered at **Kliptown, Soweto** to adopt the **Freedom Charter** clause by clause before armed police surrounded the field and seized documents! Its core promise: *"South Africa belongs to all who live in it, black and white... The people shall govern!"*
```

## Phase 3: Women's Resistance (`FEDSAW` & `9 August 1956`)

Women were NOT side characters in 1950s resistance — they led some of the largest, bravest confrontations of the decade!
- **Why Women Mobilised:** Apartheid extended **passes (`dompas`) to African women**, threatening women's freedom to live with their families in towns, run informal beer/trade livelihoods, and avoid instant arrest.
- **FEDSAW (`17 April 1954`):** Founded by **Lilian Ngoyi, Helen Joseph, Ray Alexander, Frances Baard**, and **Sophie Williams**, the **Federation of South African Women** drafted the *Women's Charter* demanding equal rights AND end to passes.
- **Union Buildings March (`9 August 1956`):** Following a smaller 1955 protest, roughly **20 000 women of all races** marched peacefully to the Union Buildings in Pretoria carrying over 100 000 signed anti-pass petitions, standing in silent protest for 30 minutes before singing the fierce anthem: ***"Wathint' abafazi, wathint' imbokodo!"*** (*"You strike a woman, you strike a rock!"*) — commemorated today as **National Women's Day**!
- *(Also note ongoing rural & urban women's struggles, including **1959 Cato Manor** protests against municipal beerhalls and forced removals.)*

```compare
title: Two Complementary Forms of 1950s Non-Violent Protest
left: Defiance Campaign (1952)
- **Tactic:** Deliberately breaking selected petty apartheid laws by trained volunteers courting arrest.
- **Impact:** Multiplied ANC membership (~100 000), united Black & Indian activists, yet provoked harsh new laws (`Public Safety Act 1953`).
right: Congress of the People & Women's March (1955–1956)
- **Tactic:** Mass democratic consultation (**Freedom Charter**) and disciplined mass marches (**20 000 women** at Union Buildings).
- **Impact:** Gave the liberation struggle a shared democratic blueprint and proved mass non-racial solidarity — provoking the **1956 Treason Trial** arrests!
```

## Global Context: International Civil Rights Parallels (`1950s–1960s`)

South African resistance did not occur in isolation — it mirrored global struggles against racism and authoritarianism:
- **US Civil Rights Movement:** **Rosa Parks (`1955`)** Montgomery Bus Boycott and **Dr Martin Luther King Jr.'s** non-violent civil disobedience (`1963 March on Washington`) challenged Jim Crow laws just as the Defiance Campaign challenged petty apartheid.
- **1960s Youth & Rights Protests:** Global anti-Vietnam War demonstrations, second-wave feminism, and **1968 student uprisings** (`France, Mexico City, Tokyo`) — contrasting starkly with state repression in apartheid South Africa and Soviet-bloc Eastern Europe!

## Worked example: Analysing the Freedom Charter as a Historical Source

**Source snippet (Freedom Charter, June 1955):** *"South Africa belongs to all who live in it, black and white, and no government can justly claim authority unless it is based on the will of all the people."*

1. *How does this extract contrast with the **National Party's apartheid ideology** AND with later **PAC Africanism (`1959`)**? (2 x 2) (4)*
   - **Vs Apartheid:** The National Party claimed South Africa belonged to white minority rule with Black people confined to ethnic Bantustans, whereas the Freedom Charter declared **equal shared ownership and democratic consent (`"will of all the people"`)**.
   - **Vs PAC (`1959`):** While the Charter welcomed `"black and white"` inside a non-racial alliance, Sobukwe's Africanists rejected sharing ownership/leadership with white/communist partners before African land restoration.

```tryit
title: Avoid 3 classic Topic 5 resistance traps
prompt: Spot and correct the mistake in each statement:
1. *"Only the ANC wrote and adopted the Freedom Charter in 1955."*
2. *"During the 1952 Defiance Campaign, Nelson Mandela led violent sabotage attacks on railway lines."*
3. *"The 20 000 women marched to the Union Buildings on 9 August 1956 mainly to protest Bantu Education."*
hint: Check **Congress Alliance**, **1952 peaceful non-violence vs 1961 MK**, and **anti-pass (`dompas`) laws for women**!
answer: 1. The **Freedom Charter (`25–26 June 1955`)** was adopted by the **Congress Alliance** (`ANC, SAIC, SACPO, COD, SACTU`) — not the ANC alone!
2. The **1952 Defiance Campaign** was **strictly non-violent** civil disobedience; sabotage (**MK**) began only **after Sharpeville** in **December 1961**!
3. The **9 August 1956 Women's March** protested **the extension of pass laws (`dompas`) to African women** (organised by **FEDSAW**).
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Calling the **Defiance Campaign (`1952`)** violent — it was strictly **non-violent**; armed struggle began **nearly a decade later (`16 December 1961`)**!
> - Saying the **Freedom Charter was adopted solely by the ANC** — always name the multi-racial **Congress Alliance** (`ANC, SAIC, SACPO, COD, SACTU`).
> - Leaving **women (`FEDSAW, Lilian Ngoyi, Helen Joseph, 9 August 1956`)** out of 1950s resistance essays!

## Practice

```questions
Q: Why did the **1949 ANC Programme of Action** mark a sharp break with earlier SANNC/ANC tactics (`1912–1940`)? (2)
A: Earlier leaders relied on respectful **petitions, deputations, and appeals to Britain**; the **1949 Programme of Action** (driven by the Youth League) switched to **mass strikes, boycotts, stayaways, and civil disobedience**.
---
Q: How did the **1952 Defiance Campaign** strengthen the anti-apartheid movement even though apartheid laws remained in place? (3)
A: 1. It united African and Indian activists (`ANC + SAIC`) in disciplined non-violent defiance (`8 000+` volunteers arrested). 2. ANC membership surged dramatically (~7 000 to ~100 000). 3. It attracted international media and UN attention to apartheid's injustice.
---
Q: Why did **FEDSAW** organise the **9 August 1956 Women's March** to the Union Buildings, and what iconic slogan emerged? (3)
A: Roughly **20 000 women of all races** marched peacefully to protest **extending pass laws (`dompas`) to African women**; their song coined the enduring slogan ***"Wathint' abafazi, wathint' imbokodo!"*** (*"You strike a woman, you strike a rock!"*).
```
