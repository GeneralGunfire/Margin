> **What you'll learn:** why **Joseph Stalin** abandoned the NEP in 1928–1929, how **Collectivisation** (*kolkhoz, sovkhoz, dekulakisation*, and the **1932–1933 famine**) crushed the peasantry, how **Gosplan's Five-Year Plans** turned the USSR into an industrial giant at vast human cost, and how the **Great Terror (1934–1938)**, **Gulags**, **propaganda**, and **women's roles** reshaped Soviet life up to **1941**.
>
> **Prerequisites:** NEP vs War Communism and Stalin's victory over Trotsky.
>
> **Why this matters:** Topic 1 essays in Paper 1 almost ALWAYS centre on **Stalin** (*"To what extent were Stalin's Five-Year Plans a success?"* or *"Stalinism was a betrayal — or continuation — of Lenin's communism"*). You need **both** the industrial achievements **and** the devastating human costs to argue a balanced, top-band essay!

## The idea in plain language

By **1928**, Stalin saw a lethal danger: the USSR was still overwhelmingly peasant and trailed industrialised capitalist powers by *"fifty or a hundred years"*. His slogan was stark: **"Either we make good this difference in ten years, or they crush us."**

Stalin scrapped the NEP and launched a **state-command revolution from above** resting on three pillars:
1. **Collectivisation (Agriculture):** Merge small private peasant plots into giant state-controlled farms so the state could seize cheap grain to feed factory cities and export for foreign machinery.
2. **Five-Year Plans (Industry):** Centralised targets set by **Gosplan** to build coal, steel, dams, tractors, and railways at breakneck speed.
3. **Totalitarian Terror & Propaganda:** Destroy all real or imagined rivals (*kulaks*, old Bolsheviks, generals) while building an intense **cult of personality** around Stalin.

## Pillar 1: Collectivisation (1929–1930s) & Famine

- **Kolkhoz vs Sovkhoz:** Peasants were forced onto **kolkhozy** (collective farms pooling land, tools, and animals under state quotas) or **sovkhozy** (state-owned farms paying wages). Machine Tractor Stations (**MTS**) controlled modern machinery and political loyalty.
- **"Liquidate the Kulaks as a Class":** Richer peasants (**kulaks**) — and anyone resisting collectivisation — had land confiscated and were shot or deported to freezing **Gulag** labour camps. Millions of peasants slaughtered their own livestock and burned crops rather than hand them over!
- **Human catastrophe (1930 & 1932–1933 Famine):** Grain procurement quotas stayed brutally high even after poor harvests and lost draught animals, triggering catastrophic famine across **Ukraine, Kazakhstan, and the North Caucasus** — historians estimate **many millions died** while the USSR continued exporting grain!
- **Outcome:** By 1941 nearly all farmland was collectivised, giving the state reliable grain supply for cities — though agricultural productivity stayed weak for decades.

## Pillar 2: The Five-Year Plans (`Gosplan`)

| Plan | Dates | Core Focus & Achievements | Severe Human & Consumer Costs |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **First Plan** | **1928–1933** | **Heavy industry:** coal, iron, steel, electricity (Dnieper Dam, **Magnitogorsk** steel city, Stalingrad tractor works). | Chaotic unrealistic quotas; tiny consumer goods; freezing barracks; forced Gulag labour (e.g. White Sea Canal). |
| **Second Plan** | **1933–1937** | Continued heavy industry + **railways, chemicals, electricity**, and some consumer goods; **Stakhanovite** productivity propaganda (`Alexei Stakhanov`). | Severe penalties for lateness or "wrecking"; internal passports tied workers to factories. |
| **Third Plan** | **1938–1941** | **Armaments, tanks, and aircraft** as Nazi threat grew — interrupted when **Germany invaded the USSR on 22 June 1941**! | Acute housing shortages and consumer austerity persisted. |

```compare
title: Evaluating Stalin's Five-Year Plans — Success vs Failure (Essay Matrix!)
left: Major Economic & Strategic Successes
- Transformed a backward peasant nation into the **world's second-largest industrial power** behind the USA inside one decade.
- Built steel, tractor, and tank factories far east of the Urals — **crucial for surviving Hitler's 1941 Operation Barbarossa invasion!**
- Wiped out open urban unemployment while expanding **literacy, engineering colleges, and technical skills**.
right: Severe Human, Social & Efficiency Failures
- **Appalling living standards:** cramped communal apartments, chronic shortages of shoes, clothing, and food; falling real wages in the early 1930s.
- **Quality sacrificed for quantity:** managers faked figures or made unusable boots/machinery to avoid arrest.
- Built partly on **slave labour in Gulags** and **famine extraction** from collectivised villages.
```

## Pillar 3: The Great Terror (`1934–1938`), Propaganda & Women

```steps
title: How Stalin's Great Terror operated
step: **1. Spark — Kirov's Murder (1 December 1934):** Popular Leningrad party boss Sergei Kirov was assassinated (many historians suspect Stalin's involvement). Stalin used it as a pretext for sweeping emergency arrests.
step: **2. Moscow Show Trials (1936–1938):** Former top Bolsheviks — **Kamenev, Zinoviev, Bukharin** — were tortured into absurd public confessions of spying for Trotsky and Nazi Germany, then executed.
step: **3. NKVD Purges (`Yezhovshchina`, 1936–1938) & Red Army Purge (1937):** Secret police (**NKVD** under Nikolai Yezhov, who was himself later purged!) arrested millions across factories, minorities, and the **Red Army officer corps** — fatally weakening Soviet leadership right before WWII!
step: **4. Gulags, Censorship & Cult of Personality:** Millions were sent to **Gulag** forced-labour camps (historians' death-toll estimates vary widely); textbooks were rewritten, photos airbrushed to erase Trotsky, and Stalin worshipped as infallible *"Father of Nations"*.
```

### Women & World War II Context
- **Women under Stalin:** Rapid industrialisation drew millions of women into factories and engineering (rising from **28%** of industrial workers in 1927 to **~40%** by 1937!), supported by workplace creches — **yet** Stalin's *"Great Retreat"* banned abortion (1936) and made divorce harder to reverse falling birth rates!
- **Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact (23 August 1939):** Stalin signed a temporary non-aggression pact with Nazi Germany to buy time and split Eastern Europe; **Germany broke the pact** by launching **Operation Barbarossa on 22 June 1941**.

## Worked example: Framing a "Stalin vs Lenin" or "Success of Plans" Essay Intro

**Essay Prompt:** *"Stalin's Five-Year Plans succeeded in modernising the USSR, but at an unacceptable human cost." Critically discuss this statement.*

**Model Introduction (~5 lines):**
> To a large extent, Stalin's Five-Year Plans achieved a remarkable industrial transformation while simultaneously inflicting devastating human suffering. Between 1928 and 1941, state-directed planning via Gosplan turned an agricultural nation into a heavy-industrial superpower capable of withstanding Nazi invasion. Yet this rapid modernisation relied on forced collectivisation, the 1932–1933 famine, harsh labour discipline, consumer deprivation, and mass terror. Evaluating both economic output and human welfare reveals that Soviet industrial success was inseparable from state brutality.

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Saying **Stalin broke the pact with Germany in 1942** — **Hitler broke it on 22 June 1941**!
> - Writing an essay that **only lists Five-Year Plan steel/coal factories** without evaluating living conditions, quality issues, collectivisation famine, and Gulag labour (or vice versa).
> - Quoting one single famine or Gulag casualty figure as exact — always note *"historians estimate several million..."* because Soviet secrecy obscured exact totals.

## Practice

```questions
Q: Distinguish between a **kolkhoz** and a **sovkhoz**, and explain why many peasants resisted collectivisation in 1929–1930. (3)
A: A **kolkhoz** was a collective farm pooling village land and livestock under state quotas; a **sovkhoz** was a direct state-owned farm paying wages. Peasants (especially *kulaks*) resisted because they lost land, livestock, and profits, leading many to burn crops and slaughter cattle rather than surrender them.
---
Q: Give TWO major industrial achievements and TWO major human/social costs of Stalin's **Five-Year Plans (1928–1941)**. (4)
A: **Achievements:** Rapid growth in heavy industry (coal, steel, electricity — e.g. Magnitogorsk, Dnieper Dam) and an industrial/armaments base east of the Urals vital to surviving WWII. **Costs:** Acute shortage of housing and consumer goods, harsh factory discipline/Gulag slave labour, and famine linked to forced grain extraction.
---
Q: How did the **1937 Red Army Purge** affect the USSR when Germany attacked in June 1941? (2)
A: Executing or imprisoning thousands of experienced commanders left the Soviet military **badly leadership-starved and disorganised**, contributing to catastrophic early Soviet defeats in 1941.
```
