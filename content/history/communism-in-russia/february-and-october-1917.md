> **What you'll learn:** the political, economic, and military causes that collapsed Tsardom by **1917**, why **February 1917** (*8–15 March N.S.*) was spontaneous and created **Dual Power**, why the **Provisional Government** failed, and how **Lenin's April Theses** and **Trotsky's planning** turned **October 1917** (*7–8 November N.S.*) into a decisive Bolshevik takeover.
>
> **Prerequisites:** 1905 Revolution, Bolshevik vs Menshevik split, and Julian (O.S.) vs Gregorian (N.S.) dates.
>
> **Why this matters:** Paper 1 frequently sets **source-based questions AND essays** comparing February vs October 1917 (*"Why did the Provisional Government fail?"* or *"Was October a popular revolution or a calculated Bolshevik coup?"*).

## The idea in plain language

Russia did **NOT** have one single 1917 revolution — it had **TWO separate revolutions eight months apart**:
1. **February Revolution (March N.S. 1917):** An **unplanned, spontaneous street uprising** driven by freezing bread queues and war exhaustion that forced **Tsar Nicholas II to abdicate**, leaving Russia split between a weak **Provisional Government** and the workers'/soldiers' **Petrograd Soviet**.
2. **October Revolution (November N.S. 1917):** A **deliberately planned Bolshevik seizure of power** led by **Vladimir Lenin** and **Leon Trotsky** when the Provisional Government lost all remaining credibility.

## Long-term & Immediate Causes of 1917

1. **Failed Reform & Repression (1906–1914):** Prime Minister **Pyotr Stolypin** paired modest peasant land reforms with brutal hangings (*"Stolypin's necktie"*) until his assassination (**14 September 1911 N.S.** / 1 Sept O.S.). Strikes surged again after the **1912 Lena Goldfields massacre**.
2. **Disaster in World War I (1914–1917):** Millions of poorly armed peasant soldiers suffered catastrophic defeats, shortages of boots and rifles, and massive casualties.
3. **Tsar at the Front & Rasputin Scandal:** In **1915**, Nicholas II made a fatal error by taking personal command of the army — tying every defeat directly to his own name! Back in Petrograd, **Tsarina Alexandra** (distrusted as German-born) leaned on the notorious mystic **Grigori Rasputin**, until conservative nobles murdered Rasputin in **December 1916** (*night of 16–17 Dec O.S. / 29–30 Dec N.S.*) trying to save the monarchy.
4. **Economic Collapse in Petrograd:** Railways broke down; bread and firewood vanished in bitter winter cold; hyperinflation wiped out workers' wages.

## Revolution #1: February 1917 (Spontaneous Collapse)

```steps
title: How Tsardom fell in 8 days (O.S. -> N.S.)
step: **23 Feb O.S. (8 Mar N.S.) — International Women's Day:** Textile women workers marched for bread in Petrograd, joined rapidly by metalworkers until ~200 000–300 000 strikers filled the streets.
step: **26–27 Feb O.S. (11–12 Mar N.S.) — Garrison Mutiny:** Ordered to shoot crowds, soldiers refused, shot officers, and joined the protesters! Duma politicians formed a **Provisional Government** (first led by Prince **Georgy Lvov**, later **Alexander Kerensky** from July), while workers/soldiers revived the **Petrograd Soviet**.
step: **2 Mar O.S. (15 Mar N.S.) — Abdication:** Generals and politicians told Nicholas II he had lost the army; Nicholas II abdicated for himself and his haemophiliac son. Over **300 years of Romanov rule** ended without a single organised Bolshevik plan!
```

### Why the Provisional Government Failed (**Dual Power** Trap)
The Provisional Government had legal authority, **yet the Petrograd Soviet held real street power** over railways, post, and troops! Under **Soviet Order Number 1** (*1 March O.S. / 14 March N.S.*), soldiers obeyed orders ONLY if approved by the Soviet.

Three fatal blunders doomed the Provisional Government:
1. **Kept fighting World War I:** Honoured Allied treaties instead of ending the slaughter (June 1917 offensive collapsed in mass desertions).
2. **Postponed Land Reform:** Told desperate peasants to wait for an elected Constituent Assembly — peasants seized gentry estates anyway!
3. **Delayed Elections:** Allowed food shortages and economic chaos to deepen while losing support to radicals.

## Lenin's Return & the Road to October

- **April Theses (3 April O.S. / 16 April N.S. 1917):** Smuggled back from Swiss exile in a sealed German train, **Lenin** stunned even his own party by demanding **NO support for the Provisional Government**, immediate withdrawal from WWI, and power transferred straight to the soviets under the irresistible slogan: **"Peace, Land, Bread!"** and **"All Power to the Soviets!"**
- **July Days (July 1917):** Premature armed protests failed; Kerensky blamed the Bolsheviks, jailed Trotsky, and forced Lenin into hiding in Finland.
- **Kornilov Affair (late August O.S. / early Sept N.S. 1917):** General Kornilov marched troops toward Petrograd; panicked, **Kerensky armed the Bolshevik Red Guards** to defend the capital! The coup fizzled, making the Bolsheviks look like saviours of the revolution and giving them majorities in the **Petrograd and Moscow Soviets** by September.

## Revolution #2: October 1917 (Planned Takeover)

```compare
title: February 1917 vs October 1917
left: February (March N.S.) 1917
- **Trigger:** Spontaneous bread strikes and International Women's Day protests.
- **Role of Bolsheviks:** Minimal (Lenin in Switzerland; Stalin/others caught off guard).
- **Outcome:** Ended Tsardom -> created **Dual Power** (Provisional Gov + Soviet).
right: October (November N.S.) 1917
- **Trigger:** Deliberately planned overnight insurrection directed by **Trotsky's Military-Revolutionary Committee**.
- **Event (25–26 Oct O.S. / 7–8 Nov N.S.):** Red Guards seized bridges, telegraph offices, railways, and the **Winter Palace** (blank shot from cruiser *Aurora*) with minimal bloodshed.
- **Outcome:** Bolshevik **Sovnarkom** took sole power, issuing immediate **Decrees on Peace and Land**.
```

### Immediate Aftermath (Late 1917 – Early 1918)
- **Dissolution of the Constituent Assembly:** When long-awaited elections returned a **Socialist Revolutionary (SR)** majority instead of a Bolshevik majority, the Assembly met once on **5 January O.S. (18 January N.S.) 1918** and Red Guards shut it down the very next day!
- **Secret Police (`Cheka`):** Set up in December 1917 to crush counter-revolutionaries.
- **Treaty of Brest-Litovsk (3 March 1918):** Fulfilling *"Peace"*, Lenin signed a brutal treaty with Germany losing huge western territory (Ukraine, Baltics, coal/iron) to exit WWI — sparking furious opposition and Civil War.

## Worked example: Answering a "Was October a popular revolution or a coup?" essay angle

**Prompt:** *Explain why the Bolsheviks succeeded in seizing power in October 1917. (Essay plan)*

- **Stance:** The Bolshevik takeover succeeded through a **combination of Provisional Government collapse AND disciplined Bolshevik strategy** — though it operated far more as a **planned minority coup** in Petrograd than a nationwide democratic mandate.
- **Pillar 1 (Provisional Gov failures):** Unpopular continuation of WWI, delayed land reform, and the Kornilov blunder destroyed Kerensky's legitimacy.
- **Pillar 2 (Lenin's vision & slogans):** *"Peace, Land, Bread"* directly answered war-weary soldiers', peasants', and workers' exact demands.
- **Pillar 3 (Trotsky's organisation):** Securing Soviet majorities and using the Military-Revolutionary Committee to capture vital infrastructure cleanly overnight.

```tryit
title: Spot the common 1917 myth!
prompt: Read this student sentence and identify **2 factual errors**:
> *"In February 1917, Lenin led an armed Red Guard attack on the Winter Palace to overthrow Tsar Nicholas II, and then Rasputin took command of the Russian army."*
hint: Check **WHO was even in Russia** in Feb 1917, **WHICH event** attacked the Winter Palace, and **WHAT happened to Rasputin** in Dec 1916!
answer: 1. **February 1917 was spontaneous**, and **Lenin was still in exile in Switzerland** (he only returned in April 1917!). The Winter Palace seizure happened during the **October Revolution** (Nov N.S.).
2. **Rasputin was murdered in December 1916** by Russian nobles — it was **Tsar Nicholas II** who took personal command of the army in 1915!
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Saying **Lenin overthrew the Tsar in October 1917** — the Tsar abdicated in **March N.S. (February O.S.) 1917**; October overthrew **Kerensky's Provisional Government**!
> - Saying **the Tsar ordered Rasputin's murder** — conservative nobles (including Prince Yusupov) murdered Rasputin in December 1916 *against* the royal couple's wishes.
> - Thinking the Bolsheviks won a democratic majority across Russia — they lost the **Constituent Assembly elections** (~24% of votes) and dissolved it by force in January 1918!

## Practice

```questions
Q: What was **Dual Power** in Russia between March and October 1917, and how did **Order Number 1** weaken the Provisional Government? (3)
A: **Dual Power** meant two rival authorities existed simultaneously: the middle-class **Provisional Government** (formal authority) and the **Petrograd Soviet** (mass worker/soldier loyalty). Under **Order Number 1**, troops obeyed military commands ONLY if endorsed by the Soviet, stripping the government of reliable armed control.
---
Q: Why did the **Kornilov Affair** (August/September 1917) mark a decisive turning point for the Bolsheviks? (3)
A: Panicked by General Kornilov's apparent coup attempt, Prime Minister **Kerensky released Bolshevik prisoners and armed the Red Guards** to defend Petrograd. When Kornilov's advance collapsed, the Bolsheviks emerged as armed heroes of the revolution and soon won majorities in the Petrograd and Moscow Soviets.
---
Q: What price did Lenin pay in the **Treaty of Brest-Litovsk (3 March 1918)**, and why did he accept it? (2)
A: Russia surrendered vast western territories (including parts of Ukraine, Poland, and the Baltic region) containing rich farmland and industry to Germany; Lenin accepted it because exiting World War I immediately was essential to save the fragile Bolshevik regime.
```
