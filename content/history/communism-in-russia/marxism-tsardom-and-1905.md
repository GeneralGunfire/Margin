## The idea in plain language

In **1848**, German thinkers **Karl Marx** and Friedrich Engels published *The Communist Manifesto* (followed by *Das Kapital* in **1867**). Marx argued that industrial **capitalism** split society into two clashing classes:
- **Bourgeoisie:** factory owners and capitalists who own the *means of production* (land, mines, machinery) and keep profits.
- **Proletariat:** industrial workers who own only their labour and sell it for low wages.

Marx predicted that industrialised countries like Britain or Germany would experience a **workers' revolution**, overthrow private property, establish a temporary **dictatorship of the proletariat** (*socialism*), and eventually reach pure **communism** — a stateless, classless society where wealth is shared by all (*"from each according to ability, to each according to need"*).

**The giant historical twist:** The first successful Marxist-inspired revolution did **NOT** happen in industrialised Britain or Germany — it happened in **1917 Russia**, an overwhelmingly **agricultural peasant empire** ruled by an absolute monarch (**Tsar**)!

## Russia under the Tsars before 1905

- **Autocracy:** Tsar **Nicholas II** (Romanov dynasty) claimed absolute divine right to rule — no elected parliament, strict censorship, and secret police (*Okhrana*) crushing dissent.
- **Land & Peasant Misery:** Roughly **80%+** of the population were poor peasants. Although **Tsar Alexander II emancipated the serfs on 3 March 1861 (N.S.)** (*19 February O.S.*), peasants had to pay crushing *redemption payments* for poor land strips; after Alexander II was assassinated (**13 March 1881 N.S.**), Tsar Alexander III and Nicholas II halted reforms and tightened repression.
- **Late, Rapid Industrialisation:** From the 1890s, foreign loans built railways and crowded factories in St Petersburg and Moscow, creating a small, militant urban **proletariat** suffering 12–14 hour days and appalling slums.
- **Marxist Split (1903):** Founded in **1898** (in Minsk), the **Russian Social-Democratic Labour Party (RSDLP)** split in **1903** into two rival factions:

## The 1905 Revolution: Dress Rehearsal for 1917

### Causes & Spark
1. **Russo-Japanese War (1904–1905):** Humiliating military and naval defeats by Japan exposed Tsarist corruption and incompetence, driving up food prices and unemployment at home.
2. **Bloody Sunday — 22 January 1905 N.S. (9 January O.S.):** Father Georgy Gapon led tens of thousands of unarmed workers carrying icons and a respectful petition for better pay and an elected assembly to the Winter Palace in St Petersburg. Imperial troops opened fire, killing and wounding hundreds. Faith in the Tsar as *"Little Father"* shattered permanently.

### Course & Outcome of 1905
- **Mass Strikes & Mutinies:** Strikes paralysed cities and railways; peasants burned landlord estates; sailors mutinied on the battleship *Potemkin*.
- **First Soviets:** Workers formed elected **soviets** (councils) to coordinate strikes — the **St Petersburg Soviet** (chaired briefly by **Leon Trotsky**) tested worker self-government for the first time.
- **October Manifesto (30 October 1905 N.S. / 17 October O.S.):** Cornered by a general strike, Nicholas II promised civil liberties (speech, assembly) and an elected parliament (**Duma**). This split moderate middle-class reformers (*Octobrists*, *Kadets*) from radical workers!
- **Reaction & Fundamental Laws (1906):** Once troops returned from the Far East, the Tsar crushed the **December Moscow uprising**, issued the **Fundamental Laws** keeping an **imperial veto** over the Duma, and dismissed uncooperative Dumas repeatedly.

### Why 1905 mattered for 1917
Although the Tsar survived 1905, three decisive lessons carried straight into 1917:
1. Workers discovered the power of **general strikes and soviets**.
2. Middle-class liberals learned the Tsar could **not** be trusted to share genuine power.
3. Leon Trotsky and Lenin revised theory (**Permanent Revolution** / worker–peasant alliance) to argue Russia did not need to wait for full Western capitalism.

## Worked example: Explaining why Marx's theory clashed with Russian reality

**Exam question:** *Why did traditional Marxists doubt a socialist revolution could succeed in Russia around 1900? (2 x 2) (4)*

1. **Lack of an industrial majority:** Marx believed socialism required a large, educated **urban working class (proletariat)** produced by mature capitalism, whereas early-1900s Russia was overwhelmingly **feudal and peasant-based**.
2. **Absence of bourgeois democracy:** Marx expected capitalism and a middle-class liberal democracy to develop first before workers took power, whereas Russia remained under **Tsarist autocracy** with weak political rights.

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Saying Marx founded the Bolshevik party or ruled Russia — Marx died in **1883**; he was a German philosopher/economist, NOT a Russian politician!
> - Saying the RSDLP was formed in 1861 (1861 was **serf emancipation**; the Marxist party was founded in **1898** and split in **1903**).
> - Thinking the **October Manifesto (1905)** turned Russia into a true democracy — the Tsar's **1906 Fundamental Laws** preserved his supreme autocratic veto over the Duma!

## Practice

```questions
Q: Identify the two opposing classes in Karl Marx's theory of **class struggle** and explain their relationship. (3)
A: The **bourgeoisie** (owners of factories, capital, and land) and the **proletariat** (propertyless wage workers). Marx argued the bourgeoisie exploit the proletariat's labour for profit, creating irreconcilable conflict ending in revolution.
---
Q: How did **Lenin's Bolsheviks** differ from the **Mensheviks** after the 1903 split? (2)
A: Lenin wanted a **small, disciplined vanguard party** of professional revolutionaries ready to seize power rapidly alongside peasants, whereas the Mensheviks favoured a **broad, democratic mass party** waiting for capitalism and middle-class democracy to mature first.
---
Q: Give TWO reasons why the **1905 Revolution** is called a "dress rehearsal" for 1917. (2)
A: 1. Workers created **soviets** (workers' councils) and proved the power of mass strikes. 2. Bloody Sunday permanently destroyed trust in the Tsar, while the Tsar's broken Duma promises convinced revolutionaries that reform from above would fail.
```
