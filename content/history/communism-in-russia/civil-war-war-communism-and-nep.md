> **What you'll learn:** how the Bolsheviks survived the **Russian Civil War (1918–1921)**, why **War Communism** caused economic collapse, famine, and the **Kronstadt Mutiny (March 1921)**, how **Lenin's New Economic Policy (NEP)** rescued food production via a strategic compromise with capitalism, how **Lenin adapted Marxism**, and why **Stalin beat Trotsky** after Lenin died on **21 January 1924**.
>
> **Prerequisites:** October 1917 Revolution and Treaty of Brest-Litovsk.
>
> **Why this matters:** Topic 1 source-based and essay papers regularly test **Lenin's economic shifts** (*"Compare War Communism and the NEP"* or *"Did the NEP betray communism?"*) AND **how Lenin's one-party vanguard laid the groundwork for Stalin's dictatorship**.

## The idea in plain language

Winning Petrograd in October 1917 was only half the battle. Within months, the Bolsheviks faced a brutal **three-year Civil War (1918–1921)** against anti-Bolshevik forces, foreign armies, and starving cities. To survive, Lenin swung the economy **twice**:
1. **First swing — War Communism (1918–1921):** Total emergency state control (seize peasant grain, ban private trade) to feed the Red Army — which won the war yet **ruined the economy**.
2. **Second swing — New Economic Policy / NEP (March 1921):** *"One step backward to take two steps forward"* — temporarily allowed **small private trade and peasant sales** so Russia wouldn't starve or revolt!

## Part 1: The Russian Civil War (1918–1921)

- **The Reds (Bolsheviks):** Led militarily by **Leon Trotsky**, who turned the **Red Army** into a disciplined force using strict conscription, former Tsarist officers watched by political commissars, and central railways (`Moscow` to `Petrograd`).
- **The Whites:** A loose coalition of monarchists, liberals, landlords, and anti-Bolshevik socialists — backed half-heartedly by Britain, France, Japan, and the USA (fearing the spread of communism and furious over Brest-Litovsk).
- **Red Terror & Romanovs:** The **Cheka** executed political rivals without trial; **Tsar Nicholas II and his entire family were executed at Ekaterinburg on the night of 16–17 July 1918** so the Whites could never rescue a monarchic symbol.
- **Why the Reds won:** Unified single command (`Trotsky`), central industrial heartland and rail network, ruthless **War Communism** supply priority, while the Whites fought without a shared political goal and alienated peasants by threatening to restore landlord estates!

## Part 2: War Communism vs the NEP (1921)

```compare
title: War Communism (1918–1921) vs New Economic Policy (NEP, 1921–1928)
left: War Communism (Total State Control)
- **Agriculture:** Armed squads forcibly **requisitioned surplus grain** from peasants without fair pay.
- **Industry & Trade:** ALL factories nationalised; **private trade banned**; wages paid in rations; labour conscription.
- **Outcome:** Supplied the Red Army, **BUT** peasants hid/stopped planting grain -> industrial output collapsed, **1921 famine** killed millions, and loyal **Kronstadt naval base sailors mutinied (March 1921)**!
right: New Economic Policy / NEP (Tactical Compromise)
- **Agriculture:** Grain requisitioning replaced by a **fixed grain tax** — peasants could **sell remaining surplus for profit**!
- **Industry & Trade:** Small factories/shops (<20 workers) returned to private ownership (**Nepmen** traders & richer **kulak** farmers thrived), while state kept **"commanding heights"** (banks, mines, heavy industry, railways).
- **Outcome:** Grain harvests and consumer goods recovered by 1926, **BUT** hardline Bolsheviks saw Nepmen/kulaks as a **betrayal of Marxism**!
```

### Key turning point: March 1921 (`Kronstadt` + `Tenth Party Congress`)
Even after winning the Civil War, starving workers struck in Petrograd and sailors at the **Kronstadt** naval base — once the *"pride and glory of the Revolution"* — rebelled demanding free soviets and an end to Bolshevik dictatorship! Trotsky crushed Kronstadt brutally, **yet Lenin immediately announced the NEP** at the **Tenth Party Congress (March 1921)** — proving he would concede *economic* freedom briefly while keeping **total one-party political control** (banning even internal party factions!).

## Part 3: Lenin's Interpretation of Marxism & Early Soviet Society

How did **Marxism–Leninism** reshape Karl Marx's original ideas?
1. **Skipped full capitalism:** Argued a peasant empire could leap into socialism led by a **worker–peasant alliance**.
2. **Vanguard party & Democratic Centralism:** One disciplined elite party ruled on behalf of workers — no opposition parties allowed!
3. **Dictatorship of the Proletariat -> One-Party Police State:** Through the *Cheka* and ban on factions, temporary worker rule hardened into permanent party control.
4. **Formation of the USSR:** Treaty creating the **Union of Soviet Socialist Republics (USSR)** approved on **30 December 1922** *(not 1923!)*.
5. **Social changes (Women):** Early Bolshevik decrees established legal equality for women, civil marriage, easier divorce, abortion access, and the women's department (**Zhenotdel**) — though everyday poverty remained harsh.

## Part 4: Lenin's Death (21 Jan 1924) & Why Stalin Won

When Lenin died on **21 January 1924** after disabling strokes (warning in his suppressed **Testament** that Stalin was *"too rude"* and should be removed as General Secretary!), a fierce leadership struggle erupted between **Leon Trotsky** and **Joseph Stalin**:

```steps
title: How Joseph Stalin outmanoeuvred Leon Trotsky (1924–1929)
step: **1. Control of the Party Machine (`General Secretary`):** While Trotsky led the Red Army heroically, Stalin quietly appointed thousands of loyal regional officials (`apparatchiks`) who owed their jobs to him and packed party congress votes!
step: **2. Ideological Clash — "Socialism in One Country" vs "Permanent Revolution":** Trotsky urged spreading risky world revolutions immediately; Stalin promised **stabilising and building Russia first ("Socialism in One Country")** — far more appealing to an exhausted party!
step: **3. Tactical Alliances (`Troika`) & Image:** Stalin allied with **Kamenev and Zinoviev** to isolate Trotsky (and painted himself as Lenin's loyal mourner/disciple while Trotsky missed Lenin's funeral!). Once Trotsky was expelled/exiled (1929; murdered in Mexico in 1940), Stalin turned and removed Kamenev, Zinoviev, and Bukharin too!
```

## Worked example: Answering "Was the NEP a betrayal of communism?"

**Exam question:** *To what extent did Lenin's New Economic Policy abandon communist principles? (Short argument)*

- **Where it compromised (Capitalist features):** Re-introducing **profit incentives, private small businesses, wage differentials, and Nepmen traders** directly contradicted Marxist bans on private enterprise and class inequality.
- **Why Lenin defended it as tactical socialism (NOT abandonment):** The Soviet state **never surrendered the "commanding heights"** (heavy industry, banks, railways, foreign trade) or **one-party political rule**. Lenin framed it as a **temporary breathing space** to rebuild agricultural production before moving back to full state planning.

```tryit
title: Spot the cause-and-effect chain (1918–1924)
prompt: Arrange these 4 events in exact **chronological order** and briefly connect each to the next in one sentence:
`(A) Tenth Party Congress adopts NEP` | `(B) Kronstadt Mutiny` | `(C) War Communism grain requisitioning` | `(D) Treaty forming the USSR`
hint: Economic pain -> rebellion -> tactical policy U-turn -> union of Soviet republics!
answer: **Order:** **(C) -> (B) -> (A) -> (D)**.
*(War Communism requisitioning (1918–21) triggered famine and the **Kronstadt Mutiny** (Mar 1921); that rebellion forced Lenin to adopt the **NEP** at the Tenth Party Congress (Mar 1921); recovery helped stabilise the state into the **USSR** on **30 December 1922**.)*
```

> **Common mistakes**
>
> - Saying **the NEP permanently ended communism in Russia** — it was a **temporary mixed-economy compromise (1921–1928)** replaced by Stalin's Five-Year Plans!
> - Dating the creation of the **USSR to 1923** — the Union Treaty was approved on **30 December 1922**.
> - Explaining Stalin's victory as just *"Trotsky was lazy"* — Stalin won systematically through his role as **General Secretary** (patronage appointments), **Socialism in One Country**, and divide-and-rule party alliances!

## Practice

```questions
Q: Name THREE reasons why the **Red Army** defeated the **Whites** in the Russian Civil War (1918–1921). (3)
A: 1. Unified leadership under **Leon Trotsky** with disciplined Red Army conscription and commissars. 2. Control of Russia's **central heartland, factories, and railway hub** around Moscow/Petrograd. 3. **White disunity** (monarchists, liberals, and socialists distrusted one another and alienated peasants).
---
Q: Why was the **Kronstadt Mutiny (March 1921)** such a shock to Lenin? (2)
A: The Kronstadt sailors had been **fiercely loyal revolutionary heroes in 1917**; their revolt over starvation and repression proved War Communism had alienated even the Bolsheviks' core supporters, forcing the urgent switch to the **NEP**.
---
Q: Contrast **Trotsky's "Permanent Revolution"** with **Stalin's "Socialism in One Country"**. (2)
A: Trotsky argued Russia could only survive if **revolutions spread rapidly across Europe and the world**; Stalin argued the USSR could **build a strong, self-sufficient socialist state on its own** first without waiting for foreign revolutions.
```
